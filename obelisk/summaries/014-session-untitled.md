# Chapter 14

Date: 2026-10-25

## Summary
The morning after completing their perimeter scout, the party arrived at the crumbling gates of Cragmaw Castle disguised as pilgrims of the Jagged Tooth, a religion they knew to be entirely fabricated. Seven towers of different sizes and heights rose above the fortress, their upper stories in various states of collapse. A short flight of steps led to a terrace and the wreckage of the main doors, watched by arrow slits in the round entrance towers. Their cover story was that they had rescued Gorl on the road and wanted shelter for a few days before continuing home. Gorl, brutally maimed during his captivity, rode in a cart. Veyra struggled to lift it up the steps, dropping it without breaking it; Sister Maela Dawnforge pushed her aside, hauled it up with Pip’s assistance, and returned the pushing to Veyra.

The commotion drew a challenge from an unseen guard: “Stop there.” Then came the questions: “Is that Gorl? Where’s Nosk?” Zenvon Forepot claimed they were worshippers of the Jagged Tooth returning Gorl to the castle. His own deception was shaky, but his companions’ convincing demeanor secured entry. A gap-toothed goblin named Polt, dragging his left leg and carrying nine black-feathered arrows, escorted them toward the priestess. Sister Maela identified his limp as an old battle injury. Despite that limp, Polt deliberately lifted his foot over a line in the dirt, revealing a hidden tripwire to the observant party.

Polt brought them to the northern tower shrine, where a stone altar covered in blood-stained black cloth held a golden chalice and censer. Three goblins in robes over armor knelt there, including Lhupo. Although the party considered having a religious-looking companion lead the exchange, Forepot remained their spokesman because of his stronger deception skills. Lhupo welcomed them as blessed pilgrims, then asked where they had heard of the Tooth. Forepot invented a famine-stricken village, a wandering messenger whose prayers brought rain, and a grateful population converted to the Jagged Tooth. Asked what the deity required, he described livestock offerings twice weekly and an annual sacrifice of a villager. With Veyra helping and a natural 20 on his deception check, he persuaded Lhupo so completely that, for that moment, she believed her invented deity truly existed—and Forepot was its prophet.

That apparent triumph immediately became dangerous. Lhupo asked whether the Tooth had revealed who killed her people at the cave, naming individual goblins and recalling their distinctive habits and appearances. The party recognized the dead as goblins they had killed earlier in the adventure, revealing Lhupo’s connection to the Cragmaw goblins, a fourth group Gorl had not disclosed. Forepot deflected by claiming the Tooth had stopped answering their prayers a month earlier. They had come to Lhupo’s shrine to regain its favor after meeting Gorl on the road. Lhupo accepted the explanation, though she was disappointed that her prophet could not identify her people’s killers.

Lhupo sent the party through heavy curtains into a dark ruined chapel, telling them to kneel at a cracked stone brazier and await a sign. Angelic figures overlooked the floor from the upper walls, and Sister Maela recognized the place as a former shrine to a good deity. Hissing came from the ceiling. Forepot chose to appear frightened while keeping his hands away from his weapons, chanting, “Jagged tooth, help me, Jagged tooth help me.” The others initially stayed still with him. At Maela’s urging, he searched the unlit brazier; with her assistance, he found a glint of gold and recovered a devotional statue of an elf. Veyra examined it without detecting magical properties. Lhupo treated the discovery as revelation, crying that the Jagged Tooth lived and that Forepot was truly blessed.

When Lhupo knelt and asked what the Tooth required, Forepot pretended to consult the statue. He first asked what she wanted. Her answer was King Grol’s death and his tooth as a trophy. Forepot then claimed that Grol was a small concern beside a greater matter involving a kidnapped person and an obelisk. Lhupo identified a dwarf prisoner in the eastern chambers, revealed the route to Grol’s quarters, and later disclosed that a Drow had come seeking the dwarf. Forepot declared that Grol must be defeated and the dwarf freed as an offering to the Tooth. Lhupo’s delighted, salivating response revealed that she expected to eat the prisoner. Forepot warned that the offering was sacred, not supper, and persuaded her to accept the restriction.

Lhupo also revealed the castle’s factional arrangement. King Grol maintained cooperation through payment, while both the goblins and the Red Mask hobgoblins resented him. The Red Masks numbered eight, were led by Thrak, and carried longswords and longbows. They were a disciplined remnant that had fled defeats elsewhere rather than a full tribe. Lhupo showed their positions and identified Grol’s great wolf, Snarl. Forepot initially considered postponing action until tomorrow, but one of the shrine goblins had already departed to announce that the prophet wanted Grol dead. Word was spreading, and delay became difficult.

The companions debated their options. Pip Thistlewick favored overwhelming force but insisted on an exit strategy, warning that their temporary allies might turn on each other and catch the party in the fighting. Sister Maela suggested extending the religious deception to the hobgoblins. Veyra proposed retrieving the dwarf while the goblins and hobgoblins slaughtered one another, then searching the bodies for treasure and magic. Forepot adopted the plan’s use of factional conflict while emphasizing that rescuing the dwarf and escaping came first. Lhupo feared her people would be sent to their deaths, so Forepot promised the party’s help. She agreed that her goblins would charge only after the party killed Thrak.

A hobgoblin named Vorl arrived with Grol’s summons. The party learned that the entrance watchers had reported their arrival, so the king already knew he had visitors. Passing through a ruined storage tower, Forepot discovered a concealed exit that might serve as an escape route. Gorl then became an immediate liability: terrified of being abandoned, he looked ready to scream and expose them. After briefly considering a sleeping potion, the party knocked him unconscious and concealed him behind barrels. Both Vorl and Lhupo approved of the treatment.

Vorl announced the party to King Grol, while Lhupo remained behind, uninvited. The king’s quarters held thick fur carpets, old trophies, a glowing brazier, a large bed, and a round table. On the floor near the table lay Gundren Rockseeker, badly beaten and unconscious. Looming over him were King Grol, the huge unleashed wolf Snarl, and a white-haired, gray-skinned Drow woman in gray robes. Gundren’s unconsciousness spared the party the immediate danger of him recognizing them and exposing their cover. The session ended before the audience with Grol began.

  - Vex, with three claw scars, who bit anyone who came close.
  - Mog, with one white eye, who hoarded pebbles he believed were dragon eggs.

- Forepot explains that they hope to spread the Jagged Tooth religion across the continent and seek success in that mission, as well as returning Gorl.

## NPCs
### Gorl
A bugbear previously captured and brutally maimed by the party. He rides in their cart as the supposed rescued traveler whose return supports their pilgrim disguise. The DM notes that he curiously admires their observance of the promise not to kill him despite their treatment of him.

Gorl belongs to King Grol’s tribe. His inability to fight would expose him to contempt among other bugbears. He becomes visibly terrified when the party considers leaving him behind, creating a risk that he will scream and reveal their deception. The party knocks him unconscious and hides him behind barrels in the storage tower.

### Polt
A gap-toothed goblin guard with a limp and a quiver of nine black-feathered arrows. Sister Maela identifies his dragging left leg as an old battle injury.

Polt escorts the party toward Lhupo after their admission. His deliberate step over a tripwire reveals the entrance trap’s location. He answers the party’s thanks with, “Don’t get too excited,” before taking them to the priestess.

### Lhupo
A goblin priestess who invented the Jagged Tooth religion and desperately wants evidence that her god exists. Forepot’s account of distant worshippers first persuades her that he is a prophet; the golden elf statue then becomes a divine sign in her eyes.

She belongs to the Cragmaw goblins and remembers the party’s earlier cave victims by name and personal traits. She asks whether the Tooth knows who killed them, unaware that she is speaking to their killers.

Lhupo wants King Grol dead and asks for his tooth. She provides the king’s location, the Red Masks’ positions and leadership, information about the Drow’s interest in the dwarf, and the identity of Snarl. Her enthusiasm for receiving the dwarf as an offering includes an appetite for eating him, which Forepot restrains through religious authority.

She fears sacrificing her tribe against the better-trained hobgoblins and agrees to attack only after the party kills Thrak. A goblin has already spread news of the prophet’s demand for Grol’s death, making it difficult to delay action. Lhupo remains behind when the party is summoned to the king.

### King Grol
A mighty bugbear who rules Cragmaw Castle and belongs to Gorl’s tribe. He maintains the factions’ cooperation through payment. Lhupo’s information leaves the party with the impression that both goblins and hobgoblins resent him.

His guards report the newcomers’ arrival, and he sends Vorl to summon them. He holds Gundren Rockseeker captive and is with Snarl and a Drow visitor when the party reaches his quarters. His conversation with the party has not yet begun.

### Thrak
Leader of the eight-member Red-Mask Kin stationed in the castle. The group is disciplined, equipped with longswords and longbows, and consists of survivors who fled defeats elsewhere.

Thrak’s death is Lhupo’s prerequisite for committing her goblins against the Red Masks. The party learns his location but does not confront him this session.

### Snarl
King Grol’s large, snarling pet wolf. Lhupo identifies him during the intelligence gathering. He is present beside Grol and the Drow over the captive Gundren, without a leash or other restraint.

### Gundren Rockseeker
The dwarf the party has come to rescue. He lies unconscious and badly beaten on the floor near the table in King Grol’s quarters.

A Drow visitor also seeks him. Forepot fears that Gundren’s recognition of the party could expose their disguise, but his unconscious state prevents that immediate complication.

### Vorl
A hobgoblin messenger who arrives at the shrine with King Grol’s demand to see the visitors. He escorts the party through the storage tower and announces them to the king.

Vorl approves when the party knocks Gorl unconscious. He remains behind the party at the final audience setup. The DM explains that the hobgoblins view the goblins and their religion with contempt, so Lhupo’s recognition of Forepot as a prophet gives him no assured authority with Vorl’s people.

### The Drow
A mysterious woman with white hair, gray skin, and gray robes. Lhupo reports that she came to the castle seeking the dwarf.

She is present in King Grol’s quarters, standing over Gundren alongside the king and Snarl. Her name, affiliation, and precise purpose remain unrevealed.

## Locations
### Cragmaw Castle
A fortress with seven crumbling towers of differing sizes and heights, their upper stories in various states of collapse. A short staircase reaches the main terrace and sundered entrance doors. Round towers with arrow slits command the approach.

A hidden tripwire guards the entrance. The castle houses goblins, Red Mask hobgoblins, and bugbears whose cooperation depends on Grol’s payments despite mutual hostility. The entrance watchers report visitors to the king.

### Northern Tower Shrine
Lhupo’s place of worship, containing a stone altar draped in blood-stained black cloth with a gold chalice and censer arranged atop it. Goblins kneel in robes worn over armor.

Two heavy-curtained southern archways lead into the ruined chapel. The shrine is where Forepot establishes his prophetic status, learns about the factions, and negotiates Lhupo’s conditional support.

### Ruined Chapel
A high, narrow former chapel or shrine to a good deity, identified by Sister Maela. Angelic figures sculpted along the upper walls overlook the floor.

A cracked stone brazier stands between the curtained northern archways. It is unlit when the party searches it and discovers the golden elf statue. Hissing comes from the ceiling, but its source is not identified. Lhupo uses the chamber as a place to seek answers from the Jagged Tooth.

### Ruined Storage Tower
A nearly collapsed tower containing rotting crates and ancient barrels from its former use as a provisions store.

A heavy curtain obscures a crumbling area to the south, an intact door leads east, and a northern passage through rubble ends at a canvas screen. Forepot discovers a concealed exit here. The party hides the unconscious Gorl behind barrels before entering the king’s quarters.

### King Grol's Quarters
A furnished living space with thick fur carpets, old wall trophies, and a brightly burning brazier of coals. A large bed stands to the north, while a round table and several chairs occupy the south near the door.

Gundren lies unconscious on the floor near the table, with Grol, Snarl, and the Drow looming over him. Snarl is free to roam. The party arrives under escort at the session’s close; Lhupo has not been invited.
