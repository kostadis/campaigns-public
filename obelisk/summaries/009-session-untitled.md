# Chapter 09

Date: 2026-08-02

## Summary
The session opened with the party reaching 3rd level — a level-up owed since Chapter 4 — and Zenvon committing to the Arcane Trickster archetype, which handed him the *Mage Hand* and *Minor Illusion* he would lean on for the rest of the night. No long rest had been taken. Before diving back in, the DM walked through the open loose threads: the four bugbears in the barracks (who might "bring back up or worse, tell people"), the still-living Nothic in the crevasse and its treasure, and the Dendrar family who needed escorting out.

The party returned to the prison cells, where the Dendrar family had barricaded themselves in terror at the sight of the skeletal guardians standing watch outside. Zenvon called out to them — "Hey guys, it's me. I came back for you. It's all safe now. You can let go of the door. Let me help you." — and the sound of furniture scraping across stone was followed by the door swinging open. The skeletons, recognizing the red cloaks, stepped aside and retreated to their resting positions without a sound, and the captives were free at last.

Mirna Dendrar, a human woman with two teenage children — thirteen-year-old Nars and eighteen-year-old Nilsa — was overjoyed to see her rescuers. Zenvon, uncertain how to roleplay the moment, offered them food and water first, noting they looked starved. Mirna explained that a couple of days ago the Redbrands had murdered her husband, Thel, and had intended to kill the rest of the family as well. She had bought time by lying about wealthy relatives in Neverwinter who would pay a ransom, hoping someone would come. When she pressed for news of Thel, Zenvon hedged, then failed a Deception roll — Mirna knew he was lying about the body's whereabouts. Zenvon asked Sister Maela to explain Thel's fate gently. The GM added an aside: "She avoids saying probably being torn to shreds and eaten alive by a monster." *This describes wording Maela avoided, not a claim that Thel was eaten alive or that the party witnessed it.* Mirna, satisfied and relieved by the honesty, admitted her Neverwinter story had been a bluff to buy time. In gratitude, she told the party of a valuable emerald necklace hidden behind a section of storage shelves in her family's old herb and alchemy shop in the southeast parcel of the ruined town of Thundertree, insisting they take it as their own if they ever passed that way. She also asked that they bring Thel's body back if they ever could. Zenvon agreed to try; the request remains open.

The party escorted the Dendrar family through the ruins of Tresendar Manor and out through a door leading up and out the front of the building, delivering them safely to the outskirts of Phandalin. With the family secure, the adventurers turned back toward the cellars to deal with the remaining threats lurking below. Moving stealthily through the apothecary and into the cavern beyond, they could hear the distant, wet sounds of the Nothic feeding on the bodies of the fallen. Zenvon used *Minor Illusion* to conjure the convincing sound of Glasstaff working in his workshop, and the creature — recognizing its master's presence — fell silent and crept away to investigate, leaving the crevasse unguarded.

Zenvon descended into the steep fissure and discovered a hidden chest resting in the darkness below. Using *Mage Hand* to open it from a safe distance — wary of traps, mimics, and "monsters whose only purpose in life is to teach you don't touch" — he found 160 silver pieces and 120 gold, five malachite gems, three potions — healing, climbing, and growth, and a finely crafted magical longsword — Talon, the blade of the long-dead knight Aldith Tresendar, though nobody in the party recognised it or learned its name. Pip was delighted, and the party quietly agreed the sword belonged in the hands of their most capable fighter. With the treasure secured, they turned their attention to the bugbear barracks.

The party crept to the barracks door and listened — Sister Maela pushing Zenvon aside to catch several gruff voices barking commands at a goblin in the goblin tongue. Zenvon used *Mage Hand* to ease the door open, and the party stepped inside wearing their red cloaks. A miserable goblin named Droop promptly fainted from terror at the sight of them. The lead bugbear, a brute named Nosk, laughed at Zenvon's attempt to assert authority, sneering "leave it to a Redbrand to think he's in charge."

Zenvon quickly realized that the bugbears served the Black Spider directly rather than Glasstaff, answering to an entirely different chain of command. Rather than back down, he leaned into the confrontation, rage-baiting Nosk with pointed questions about what the Black Spider would think of his behavior. The gambit worked almost too well — Nosk, furious and flustered, blurted out that the Black Spider was at **Cragmaw Castle** before the color drained from his face as he realized what he had done. "It don't matter that now you know, because now I kill you," Nosk snarled. The room erupted into violence.

The fight was brutal and desperate. Zenvon cast *Thunderwave*, and the blast slammed into two of the bugbears, but the creatures were powerful and resilient, and Nosk answered with a crushing blow from his morning star that dealt 14 points of damage and left Zenvon barely standing. Sister Maela channeled divine energy to keep him on his feet, but another bugbear landed a second vicious strike that nearly finished the job. The party was outmatched, and the realization came quickly — they needed to run.

Pip slammed the barracks door shut to buy precious seconds while Zenvon poured flask after flask of oil across the threshold and down the hallway. Using *Mage Hand*, he ignited the oil just as the bugbears smashed through the shattered door, and the hallway erupted in flames. The fire scorched the brutes for five points each round and turned the slick, oil-soaked floor into treacherous terrain, slowing their pursuit to a crawl. The party dashed through the tunnels toward the manor entrance, Sister Maela dragging a struggling Veyra behind her.

As they sprinted past the crevasse, the Nothic emerged from the shadows and watched them flee. It called itself Ssarnak, and as Veyra ran past, it leaned close and whispered something that stopped her cold — her teacher was not dead; he was not in a place where dead is easy. Veyra went pale and tried to turn back, desperate to speak with the creature, but Sister Maela physically grappled her and held her fast. Maela shut and locked a door during the retreat. The bugbears broke through after three rounds, allowing time for the party to escape the manor into the cool night air of Phandalin.

In the quiet aftermath, Veyra turned to Pip and asked him to describe the black stone he had once sold — how it felt to hold. Zenvon made a choice he had been deferring since the road: he told Veyra about the black stone in his pocket and produced it. Her blue crystal flared even brighter in response, the two objects resonating with some unseen magical force. Veyra recalled her advisor, Orryn Voss, and his theory that some kind of device was controlling people — theories that had once sounded "like the kind of thing people believe, but isn't." But with the second glowing rock, the two dead, elongated-skull goblins in the crevasse, matching Sildar's description of his interrogators, and the Nothic's whispered revelation, it was all beginning to feel connected. Zenvon kept his growing belief to himself, but he trusted her more than he had before. Sister Maela, meanwhile, wore a sour expression the party could not fully read.

The party returned to the Stonehill Inn, bloodied and exhausted, where Toblen Stonehill offered them a free night's stay in gratitude for ridding the town of the Redbrands. Zenvon paid ten silver for food sent up to the room. After a long and much-needed rest, they met with Sildar Hallwinter, presenting him with the Black Spider's letter — recovered from Glasstaff's desk back in Chapter 7 — which confirmed Iarno Albrek had been working for the Black Spider all along. Sildar was appalled by the betrayal and paid the party the remaining 150 gold pieces of their agreed reward. He then offered a new bounty for Iarno's capture, preferring him brought back alive to face trial in Neverwinter, though he admitted quietly that he "would not be upset if he came back dead." Zenvon argued that capturing the Black Spider was the more important prize, and Sildar conceded the point, noting that Iarno had likely fled straight to his master and the party might "get two birds with one stone."

The party then paid a visit to the Shrine of Luck, where a scholarly acolyte named Sister Garaele praised them — "the new heroes of Phandalin" — before making a request of her own. She had previously attempted to approach a powerful undead banshee named Agatha to ask about the location of a legendary spellbook belonging to the mage Bowgentle, but had barely escaped with her life. She asked the party to bring Agatha a jeweled silver comb as a gift to win her favor, offering three potions of healing in return. Veyra confirmed that Bowgentle appeared in her research notes — a good sign that he was real — and Zenvon accepted the task, though he made clear there were other pressing matters to attend to first ("a P0, but not everything," in the players' shorthand).

Their final stop was the Miner's Exchange, where the Guildmaster Halia Thornton greeted them with a smile and congratulations on defeating the Redbrands. Her expression cooled noticeably when she learned Glasstaff had escaped rather than died, and she quickly pivoted to asking whether the party had found any correspondence among his personal effects, claiming she wanted to ensure local trade would no longer be disrupted. She offered a hundred gold pieces for any letters they had recovered. Zenvon rebuffed her, suggesting the information was worth far more, and the two parted on carefully polite terms — but the party walked away convinced that Halia Thornton knew far more about the Redbrands than she was letting on.

## Locations
### Tresendar Crypt *(Chapter 8 — transited, not fought this session)*
An underground chamber thick with the smell of ancient stone and decay, housing stone sarcophagi and guarded by skeletal remains. The crypt fight happened in Chapter 8; this session the party merely passed through, the surviving skeletons standing down for the red cloaks.

### Redbrand Slave Pens
A long room divided by iron bars into filthy, straw-lined cells used by the Redbrands to hold their captives.

### Tresendar Manor Cellar
A large subterranean space beneath the manor ruins featuring a great cistern, stone steps, a landing, and storage racks for kegs and barrels.

### Tresendar Manor
An ancient, abandoned manor on the eastern edge of Phandalin, standing on a low hillside among woods and thickets — described in the read-aloud as "more a castle than a house." Its cellars have been converted into a fortified hideout for the Redbrand ruffians. An upper door leads out the front of the building to the surface.

### Thundertree
A ruined town overrun by undead, mentioned by Mirna Dendrar as the former location of her family's herb and alchemy shop and a hidden emerald necklace. Mirna's family fled it "after the undead overran the place." The necklace lies in the southeast parcel of town.

### Tresendar Manor Crevasse
A natural cavern featuring a steep side fissure five to ten feet wide and twenty feet deep, spanned by two arched wooden bridges, flanked by two rough stone columns supporting a 20-foot ceiling, and smelling of decaying flesh. Home to a Nothic and a hidden treasure chest.

### Bugbear Barracks
A foul-smelling room within the Tresendar Manor hideout containing four roughly built wooden bunks with heaped blankets, dirty dishes, and the stench of unwashed bodies and rotten meat. Site of a brutal skirmish with four bugbears, led by Nosk. One burned to death in the oil fire during the party's retreat; **three survive, all wounded**, and were last heard hammering on the locked manor door.

### Stonehill Inn
A bustling inn in Phandalin where the party rests for free after their victory over the Redbrands, courtesy of Toblen Stonehill. Elsa, Tuck, Freda and Narth are among the patrons present; Sildar Hallwinter sits at a table.

### Shrine of Luck
A small shrine in Phandalin constructed from stones taken from nearby ruins, dedicated to Tymora, the goddess of good fortune. Tended by Sister Garaele.

### Miner's Exchange
A trading post and unofficial records office in Phandalin where local miners have their finds weighed and claims registered, run by Halia Thornton. Described as "a great place to meet people who spend a lot of time in the countryside surrounding Phandalin."

### Agatha's Lair
The dangerous home of the banshee Agatha, near the ruined village of Conyberry in the countryside east of Phandalin. **GM-only note for future sessions:** Agatha traded Bowgentle's spellbook to the necromancer Tsernoth over a century ago; its current whereabouts remain unconfirmed. She answers one question, and her account of the transfer provides a lead to Tsernoth rather than the book itself.
