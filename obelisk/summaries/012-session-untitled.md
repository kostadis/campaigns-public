# Chapter 12

Date: 2026-10-09

## Summary
Over a final meal at Old Owl Well, Zenvon asked Hamun Kost to honor his promise and reveal the location of Cragmaw Castle. The Red Wizard obliged without hesitation — "I think that that is an excellent next destination for you. I think it's a fabulous place for you to go" — taking the party's map himself and drawing a precise line across it with a practiced hand to the castle's exact location. As the meal wound down, the conversation turned (in a private aside, kept out of Hamun's hearing at first) to the outstanding matter of Sister Garaele's request — a visit to Agatha the Banshee's lair. The party obsessed over whether they should go. Zenvon was entirely unwilling to go ("zombies are fine, but…" no banshees), but Pip pointed out that a banshee capable of answering one question could answer others — "if a creature knows some information, it may know other information" — suggesting they use their single question for their own purposes. Sister Maela objected that the comb wasn't theirs to spend: she didn't want to be "the moral poopy pants," but repurposing Sister Garaele's gift might be viewed as, well, not good. Sister Maela was also blunt about the danger itself: "the only good banshee is a dead banshee," which set off Veyra insisting the term "undead" was prejudicial and that she preferred "recently unalived." Veyra became highly excited at the prospect of asking about her lost mentor, but quickly grew conflicted, torn because the original question was about a spellbook and she, as a wizard, felt the weight of that loss — "Spellbook. My mentor. Spellbook. My mentor." Then she suddenly hit an equilibrium point, stopped talking, pulled out her notebook and scribbled equations, arrows, and circles — weighing a lost spellbook against the desperate need to find her missing mentor, Orryn Voss — then threw up her hands in frustration and said nothing more on the matter. Zenvon ultimately floated the idea with Hamun directly, explaining the comb and the question. Hamun, quoting the first edition of the Thayan Necronomicon: *The Undead and You* (inventing the great wizard's name on the spot), warned the party gravely: ignore the wailing — it is merely a lure to draw you into the ethereal plane — do not look upon her, and above all else, do not let her touch them. A banshee, he explained, is a pissed-off spirit that feels betrayed in some profound way; approached with the appropriate respect and decency, the comb might even work. Asked the chances of things going south, he replied: "Well, if I was in charge, zero. But you, Zenvon." He declined to accompany them, citing his very complex archaeological procedure, thanked them for a wonderful dinner and for handling his bugbear problem, and pointedly gestured outside to make clear they were not getting space in his luxurious tent. The party resolved to return to Phandalin before making any further decisions — Zenvon preferring to rest between quests.

The journey back was quiet and uneventful, though the weight of unfinished business hung over the group. They arrived in town in the middle of the day.

Their first stop was the Townmaster's Hall, where Harbin Wester sat behind his desk with the air of a man who considered himself very important. Zenvon informed him that Wyvern Tor had been cleared, and Harbin, after a moment of transparent hesitation, claimed the deal had been for one hundred gold pieces. Zenvon immediately caught him out on the deception — "Are you sure, Mr. Harbin Wester?" — and handed the matter to Pip, who had negotiated the original deal. Pip, polite but immovable, recited the entire history: Harbin had offered 110, Pip had demanded 140, and they had settled on 120. Harbin said only "yes," produced the full one hundred and twenty gold pieces, pulling the additional twenty from his own private purse, and dropped the pile in front of them with a single word — "go." When Zenvon told him he didn't have to be that rude, Harbin answered with exaggerated politeness — "you're so right, you're such noble heroes" — and declared he had important Townmaster activities to attend to. The party left Harbin to his tic-tac-toe game, Zenvon pointedly staring at the board on the way out.

Next, the group made their way to the Lionshield Coster, where the proprietor Linene Graywind greeted them with the enthusiasm of someone who recognized good customers when she saw them. She presented Pip with a beautifully engraved, gem-studded scabbard — "a work of art, Mr. Thistlewick," at a mere 100 gold — and pitched an extravagant suit of plate mail that had "arrived in town just the other day" for 1,500 gold, bundling the pair at 1,550 as a personal sacrifice that would surely bankrupt her: her final offer. Pip and Sister Maela ultimately settled on breastplates at 400 gold pieces each — Pip judged the plate-mail bundle too much to spend on one person's gear ("after all, we're a team here"), and unlike scale mail at 50 gold, a breastplate would not spoil their stealth. Pip noted he'd take the armor to the smithy for customization, observing — to Linene's theatrical shock ("Markup!") — that the Coster carried quite a markup over local artisans. As the party was leaving, Linene promised to bring in finer goods from Waterdeep — "the purest and finest potions" — now that the roads were clear enough, and the party flush enough, to make it worthwhile. Zenvon then ran back with a specific request: potions and magical items suited for dealing with a banshee. Linene raised an eyebrow — why would he want to deal with a banshee? "Let's just say it's for a friend," Zenvon offered — but she said she would see what she could do, Mr. Zenvon.

At Edermath Orchard, Daran greeted the party warmly — "well, you're back!" — asked whether they'd kept their heads on a swivel as he'd advised, and asked for a full account of what they had found at Old Owl Well. Zenvon began to describe Hamun Kost's zombie army in frank detail — "I guess we made a friend there" — and Daran's alarm mounted with each revelation: "You had a meal with a red wizard?" "An army of zombies? And you said there's no threat!" At which point Pip quietly pulled Zenvon aside and suggested a more diplomatic version of events: a few zombies for archaeological digs and servant work, awful but not town-directed — "I'm just throwing that out there, boss, but you're in charge." Taking the hint ("You're right, Pip. I guess sometimes I tend to overspeak"), Zenvon revised the story: the Red Wizard was raising his zombies to settle a feud within his own family, a revenge against his brother, and had no intention of turning them on Phandalin. Daran accepted this with cautious relief: his real fear had been Thayans pulling out Netheril magic, and a zombie army raised for some intra-Thayan nonsense was better than that. As a reward for their service, he presented the party with a pair of enchanted boots that enhanced the wearer's speed and agility — he had no more pairs, so someone had to be chosen — and after a brief discussion, everyone agreed they belonged on Zenvon's feet, the party's rogue and halfling both. Veyra mused that the boots were somehow related to something, though she couldn't say what.

On the way to the Stonehill Inn the party spotted Sister Garaele, but exhaustion — or perhaps avoidance — won out, and they waved her off with a promise to speak later ("we're super tired from the quest, so we're just gonna retire" — it's not you, it's us). They awkwardly reached the inn as the afternoon light faded — the downside of a small town — slipping past Sildar Hallwinter without a word, having no news for him and no appetite for another twenty-minute lecture, and collapsing into well-earned rest. Morning came with the smell of breakfast — and then something far less welcome. As the four companions descended the stairs into the common room, they found it occupied by a group of bugbears with unmistakably singed fur, the very same ones who had fled Tresendar Manor weeks before, now come with reinforcements. One of them held Toblen Stonehill face-down on the floor with a knife over his head, while their leader, Nosk, did the talking. Two goblins blocked the exit to the street. Nosk's opening words: "You have embarrassed me." His bosses were not at all pleased that the party was still alive and breathing. His message was simple: drop your weapons, come quietly, and Toblen walks free — "I personally don't care if he's fed to the goblins, but I suspect you do." Then they'd be tied up and led out the back door.

Zenvon studied Nosk carefully and saw through the performance. The bugbear had no intention of keeping his word: Nosk had been humiliated — the party had destroyed the Tresendar operation and hadn't even had the decency to kill him — and he meant to tie the party up and haul them to Cragmaw Castle, and they would not arrive in one piece. When Zenvon pressed him on how he could guarantee the other townsfolk's safety, Nosk conceded, "You have a good point. That is something I did not think about." Working quickly — and waving off Veyra's offer to simply kill him ("I could kill him. I got the spells") — Zenvon negotiated a partial disarmament, convincing Pip and Sister Maela to lay down their weapons as a show of good faith. Nosk sized up the situation, aware there were dangerous people in town and this could go sideways quickly, and agreed. When a cackling bugbear moved to bind them — well beyond what had been agreed — Zenvon stared Nosk down with cold authority until the bugbear stepped back. Nosk then demanded that Pip, "the fighter," be tied up before Toblen could go free. Zenvon protested — "that's not what we agreed, and you said you're a man of word, and now you're raising so many questions in my mind" — and a second read of the bugbear showed the rest of the plan: let Toblen run as a distraction, then jump the party while they were distracted. Then, just as Nosk signaled Toblen to run, Zenvon conjured a perfect illusion of himself across the room. The tavern erupted into chaos: a goblin loosed an arrow straight through the phantom, exposing the trick, but the distraction had bought precious seconds. Veyra unleashed a crackling volley of magical energy that dropped one goblin where it stood. Zenvon scooped up the discarded weapons and flung them back to his companions, then slipped under a table beside Veyra, unseen. Pip reclaimed his blade and charged — swinging twice and missing both times. Sister Maela called upon Dumathoin and wrapped the entire party in a divine blessing. The enemy answered: a goblin's bow turned on Maela, another goblin closed with a sword, and a bugbear's blow took Pip for seven points of damage. Veyra's second volley left another foe hanging on by a single hit point. By the time play paused, Nosk himself had taken a well-placed arrow from Zenvon's shortbow — twelve points of damage from hiding, sneak attack and all — and was bleeding heavily. The session stopped there, mid-fight, with the battle still undecided.

### Harbin Wester
The pompous Townmaster of Phandalin who claims the deal was for 100 gold before Zenvon catches him out and Pip reminds him the deal was for 120 — reciting the whole negotiation: 110 offered, 140 demanded, 120 settled. He pulls the additional gold from his own private purse, drops the pile with a curt "go," and when chided for rudeness answers with dripping sarcasm: "you're so right, you're such noble heroes, I should be so much more polite to you." He dismissively returns to playing tic-tac-toe — "important Town Master activities… it's a process."

### Linene Graywind
The sharp-witted owner of the Lionshield Coster who pitches a 100-gold engraved scabbard and a 1,500-gold suit of plate mail as a 1,550-gold "bankruptcy" bundle before selling breastplate armor to Pip and Sister Maela at 400 gold each. Shocked — shocked! — at Pip's talk of markup, she nonetheless promises to procure specialized potions and magical goods from Waterdeep ("the purest and finest potions brewed in Waterdeep"), including items effective against banshees, now that the roads are safer and she knows well-moneyed adventurers are in town. She asks no further questions when Zenvon claims the banshee business is "for a friend" — "Fine. I'll see what I can do, Mr. Zenvon."

### Daran Edermath
A retired adventurer in Phandalin who rewards the party for scouting the Thayan presence at Old Owl Well, pleased his "head on a swivel" advice served them. Initially alarmed by talk of a meal with a Red Wizard and an army of zombies — "And you said there's no threat!" — he is relieved to hear (via Zenvon's invented family-feud story) that the wizard is pursuing intra-Thayan revenge rather than meddling with Netherese magic. He gifts the party the Boots of Striding and Springing as promised, apologizing that he has no more pairs.

### Sildar Hallwinter
A figure staying at the Stonehill Inn who is waiting for information from the party. The party chooses to ignore him as they have nothing new to report — and no wish to endure another twenty-minute lecture.

### Toblen Stonehill
The innkeeper of the Stonehill Inn, happy to see the party return, who is later held face-down at knifepoint by Nosk's bugbears during the morning ambush. He makes a run for the door during the chaos of Zenvon's illusion, past the two goblins blocking the exit, and runs out of the inn.

### Nosk
The bugbear leader with singed fur who seeks revenge for the party's actions at Tresendar Manor — they destroyed his operation and "didn't even have the decency to kill him," and his bosses at Cragmaw Castle are displeased the party still breathes. He orchestrates a hostage situation at the Stonehill Inn, declaring himself "a bugbear of my word" while planning to disarm, bind, and betray the party en route to Cragmaw Castle. Shrewd enough to know there are dangerous people in town and that the standoff could go sideways, he nonetheless loses control of it and is severely wounded — 12 points from Zenvon's sneak attack — when combat breaks out.

### Pip Thistlewick
A companion who handles negotiations with Harbin Wester with polite, relentless precision, upgrades his old chain mail to a breastplate (declining a plate-mail bundle as too much for one person's gear — "we're a team here"), needles Linene about her markup, and coaches Zenvon on when to stop volunteering information about zombie armies. He is briefly disarmed during the hostage negotiation — shooting Zenvon an *are you crazy?* look — before rejoining the fight once Zenvon returns his sword, though both of his first swings miss.

### Sister Maela Dawnforge
A cleric companion who asserts clerical authority on the undead ("it's the clerics who are the experts on the undead, not the wizards"), judges that "the only good banshee is a dead banshee," and serves as the party's conscience on the comb — refusing to be "the moral poopy pants" while pointing out the comb isn't theirs to spend. She purchases a breastplate for protection, is briefly disarmed during the hostage standoff (dropping her mace only after waiting for Nosk to agree), and blesses her allies during the tavern brawl.

### Veyra
A wizard companion who debates the ethics of calling the undead 'recently unalived' ("Well, why not call them not dead? Aren't we alive?"), has a moment of internal conflict over her mentor Orryn Voss versus a spellbook — scribbling equations until she throws up her hands — and offers, mid-hostage-crisis, "I could kill him. I got the spells." She senses the Boots of Striding and Springing are "related somehow to something," and in the tavern ambush uses magical bolts of energy to strike down a goblin, then a second volley that leaves another foe at a single hit point, fighting from cover beneath a table.

## Locations
### Old Owl Well
The site of an archaeological dig where the party dines with the Red Wizard Hamun Kost amidst his undead servants, and where Hamun marks Cragmaw Castle on the party's map. The party is welcome to spend the night — outside, pointedly, not in Hamun's luxurious tent.

### Cragmaw Castle
The party's next destination, marked on their map by Hamun Kost, where Gundren Rockseeker is believed to be held and where Nosk threatens to take the party. Both Nosk's bugbears and Iarno answer to the Black Spider at Cragmaw Castle (Nosk let it slip in Chapter 10), who, furious that the party survived, dispatched the bugbears to Phandalin.

### Agatha's Lair
The home of a banshee that Sister Garaele requested the party visit to trade a silver comb for information — at worst half a day's journey back, by Zenvon's guess. Hamun Kost warns the party about the dangers of visiting; the party defers the trip rather than attempt it unprepared.

### Phandalin
A small frontier town — two stores in all — where the party returns to collect rewards, resupply, and rest, and which becomes the site of a bugbear ambush. Small enough that avoiding Sister Garaele requires conspicuous, awkward sneaking.

### Townmaster's Hall
The administrative center of Phandalin where Harbin Wester manages town affairs — and his tic-tac-toe game — and reluctantly pays the party their 120 gold piece reward, the last 20 from his own purse.

### Lionshield Coster
A merchant post in Phandalin owned by Linene Graywind, stocking armor and adventuring gear — unlike Barthen's Provisions, which carries neither weapons nor armor — where the party purchases breastplates and requests future magical supplies from Waterdeep, including anti-banshee items.

### Edermath Orchard
The home and orchard of Daran Edermath, a retired adventurer, where the party reports on Old Owl Well and receives the Boots of Striding and Springing.

### Stonehill Inn
A local inn in Phandalin where the party seeks rest, which becomes the site of a tense hostage standoff and skirmish when bugbears and goblins ambush them in the morning — Toblen pinned on the common-room floor, two goblins at the street exit, and a back-door escape route in the bugbears' plan.

## Items
### Jeweled Silver Comb
A beautiful silver comb given to the party by Sister Garaele, intended to be offered to Agatha the Banshee in exchange for an answer to a question — specifically, one about a spellbook. The party still has not used it. Pip floated spending its "free answer" on the party's own questions; Sister Maela objected that the comb isn't theirs to redirect.

### World Map
The party's navigational map, on which Hamun Kost personally draws a line marking the precise location of Cragmaw Castle during their dinner at Old Owl Well.

### Breastplate Armor
Sturdy chest protection purchased by Pip and Sister Maela from the Lionshield Coster at 400 gold pieces each, to provide better defense without the stealth penalty of the 50-gold scale mail alternative. Pip plans to have his customized — at the smithy, where the markup is kinder.

### Decorative Scabbard
A finely engraved, gem-encrusted sheath for a sword offered to Pip by Linene Graywind at the Lionshield Coster for 100 gold pieces — "a work of art, Mr. Thistlewick" — pitched as part of a 1,550-gold bundle with plate mail that the party declined.

### Boots of Striding and Springing
Magical footwear (uncommon) gifted to the party by Daran Edermath as a reward for investigating Old Owl Well — his only pair. They enhance the wearer's movement speed (walking speed of 30 feet while worn), and the party agrees they should go to Zenvon, the group's rogue and halfling. Veyra suspects the boots are "related somehow to something," though she cannot say what.

## Spells
### Minor Illusion
Zenvon cast this spell to create a duplicate of himself in the tavern common room at the exact moment Toblen bolted for the door — drawing an arrow from a goblin that passed clean through the phantom, exposing the trick to Nosk ("that was a good try") but buying the party its opening.

### Magic Missile
Veyra cast glowing darts of magical energy twice: the first volley instantly struck down one of the goblins blocking the tavern door, and the second, from cover beneath a table, left another enemy clinging to a single hit point.

### Bless
Sister Maela called upon divine power during the tavern skirmish to grant her allies increased accuracy and resolve throughout the fight — a d4 bonus to every party member's attacks.
