# Chapter 10 The Wizard of the Old Owl Well

Date: 2026-08-21

## Summary

With the party still at the Miner's Exchange, Zenvon reviewed every open lead: Old Owl Well, Wyvern Tor, Agatha's Lair, Thundertree, and the main pursuit of the Black Spider at Cragmaw Castle. The precise location of Cragmaw Castle remained unknown. The party could pursue the main quest immediately, but side work promised experience, power, magical items, and potentially useful intelligence. Zenvon chose Old Owl Well in part because, as he put it, “since I've recently gained Magic powers,” he wanted to investigate the ancient magical site.

The DM also reminded the party that unfinished business could produce later consequences. The surviving bugbears were unaccounted for, Halia's real motives remained uncertain, and the manor itself had not been revisited after the escape. Zenvon knowingly chose to leave those threads unresolved, curious about what the surviving bugbears might do next.

Before leaving, the party visited Daran Edermath at his orchard on the edge of town, informing the retired adventurer that they intended to set out for the Old Owl Well the following morning. Daran greeted them as the heroes of Phandalin and proudly congratulated them for dealing with the Redbrands. Zenvon modestly answered that it had happened almost by accident: “we didn't intend to, but it was, I guess, God's plan.”

Daran warned that the farther east they traveled, the less settled the country would become. Much of the north had been torn apart by wars, demons, gods, and elemental catastrophes, while caravans near Wyvern Tor had recently suffered attacks. He described two possible routes: the road, where the party might occasionally meet another traveler, or a shorter path through the hills, where they were more likely to encounter creatures that considered the hills their territory.

When invited to accompany the party, Daran briefly considered it before declining with the hard-earned wisdom of retirement: “you know, one of the things about being an old adventurer is you're an old adventurer because you choose to stop adventuring.” Asked if he had anything useful to contribute, he instead launched into a lengthy recounting of his own adventuring days, dispensing advice that amounted largely to keeping one's back straight, one's sword sharpened, and one's eyes open. Zenvon took careful notes on all of it, which pleased the old man enormously, while Pip struggled to keep a straight face beside him. Zenvon's patience earned him a one-time +1 bonus to an Investigation or Survival check.

When Zenvon asked about the Netheril Empire — the ancient civilization Daran had once mentioned in connection with the watchtower — the old adventurer grew more serious. He described a civilization of staggering power: flying ships, magic that surpassed anything modern wizards could achieve, and artifacts of terrible potency scattered across the north when the empire fell. Modern wizardry reached the ninth level of power, he said, while stories claimed the Netherese had mastered magic of the twelfth level.

A leader named Karsus had reached too far and caused the Weave itself to break, nearly destroying magic and bringing the empire crashing down. Daran cautioned that the familiar story of a great empire destroyed by hubris sounded almost too much like a morality play, but he agreed with Zenvon that old stories generally contained some grain of truth. Whatever the exact history, Netheril had left magical trinkets across the north, and rumors of one could attract every magic-user, scavenger, and power-hungry seeker in the region.

Daran dismissed stories of cursed Netherese artifacts as old wives' tales, though he qualified the reassurance. With any powerful magical object, no one could be certain who had made it, why it had been made, or what purpose it was intended to serve. Even an uncursed artifact could cause disaster “in hands that are not capable of handling those magical things.” Ultimately, what he wanted from the party was an answer: whether a threat to Phandalin was brewing at Old Owl Well. Anything worth recovering would be a welcome second prize.

That evening, the party returned to the Stonehill Inn, where Toblen Stonehill welcomed them back as the toast of the town and called for Elsa to pour Zenvon a beer. Over drinks, Zenvon told Toblen of their plan to travel northeast to the Old Owl Well and asked whether they should detour through the ruins of Conyberry. Toblen questioned why any adventurer would deliberately enter an abandoned town and warned that ruins beside a road had a way of attracting “people who are dangerous to the unwary.”

An Insight check confirmed that Toblen had never visited Conyberry and was expressing personal fears rather than firsthand intelligence. His historical information was sound, however: Conyberry had been devastated during the Spellplague and its remaining inhabitants were later killed by Uthgardt raiders. Toblen also reminded the party that the route to Agatha's lair passed through the ruins, should they later undertake Sister Garaele's errand. The party took his concerns in stride and retired for a long and much-needed rest.

The next morning, Sildar Hallwinter caught them before they could depart, inviting them to sit for breakfast. He laid out a new offer from the Lords' Alliance: five hundred gold pieces for whoever could locate Cragmaw Castle and drive out the band leaders within. Pip's eyes lit up at the figure, and he nudged Zenvon to make sure they at least learned the amount. After hearing the offer, Zenvon quietly pulled Pip aside and reminded him that divided four ways, the reward amounted to only 125 gold pieces each — and the risk was considerable. Pip composed himself and told Sildar, “it's a chunk of change, but we're… you know, we'll keep that under advisement, Sildar.”

Sildar added that the Lords' Alliance would “look very kindly” on whoever dealt with Cragmaw Castle, implying favors and opportunities beyond the monetary reward. Before they left, he noticed the magical sword recovered from Tresendar Manor and remarked, “my, my, my, that is a fine, noble sword, Pip.” Zenvon's Insight check of 20 revealed that the comment was aimed specifically at Pip: Sildar appeared to be suggesting that success might open a place for him in the Lords' Alliance. Zenvon was uncertain whether Pip understood the hint and chose not to explain it yet.

The party set out along the Triboar Trail, covering roughly thirty miles during the first day. The road was quiet — unnervingly so — and along the way they passed the remains of a caravan amid signs of a bandit attack. The evidence confirmed that traffic along the trail had been targeted, though whether the attackers were based at Wyvern Tor, were goblins or Redbrands, or represented some new faction remained unknown. No travelers passed them that day, though the trail was still in use; the region's major flow of trade ran north-south along the High Road rather than east-west.

The journey also reinforced how narrow civilization's reach had become. Neverwinter and its immediate environs marked the practical boundary of settled country, while the hinterlands had been ravaged by barbarians, orcs, goblins, meddling gods, wars, the Spellplague, and “wizards who should have known better.”

By the following day, the hills rose around the party and Old Owl Well came into view: a crumbling watchtower surrounded by mounds of rubble, with a colorful tent pitched in the middle of its courtyard and no one in sight. Zenvon sensed an eerie atmosphere and instructed the others to tiptoe through the main entrance toward the well.

The stillness lasted only a moment before twelve zombies began to shamble out of the tower. Among the undead laborers were a sun-peeled former sentinel and a figure still wearing a singed Red Wizard acolyte's robe. Sister Maela immediately explained that zombies were mindless and did not speak; someone else had raised and controlled them. Looking toward the tent, she concluded that a necromancer was probably present and warned that anyone capable of commanding so many corpses wielded evil, dangerous, and powerful magic. Pip cautioned, “Boss, I don't know if we want to fight all these zombies.”

Rather than draw steel, Zenvon tried to provoke the zombies' master into revealing himself. “Hey, Zombie Lord, are you afraid to face us? And that's why… is that why you're sending the zombies?” he shouted toward the tent. The taunt worked. A wizard stepped outside and demanded, “WHAT IS THE MEANING OF THIS?” He had a shaved, tattooed head, and Veyra hurriedly consulted her notebook before identifying the markings as symbols of a necromancer.

The wizard introduced himself as Hamun Kost. Zenvon introduced Pip, Sister Maela, and Veyra and assured Hamun that the party had come only to talk. Hamun described himself as a wizard conducting an archaeological dig at the old watchtower, but initially refused to explain why the site interested him. When Zenvon claimed responsibility for keeping the citizens of Neverwinter and Phandalin safe, Hamun laughed hysterically and assured the “dear boy” that he had no desire to hurt anyone nearby.

Zenvon promised that Hamun's secret would remain with the party if he explained his work. Hamun answered with cold practicality: “My dear boy, if I wanted my secrets to be safe, I would kill you.” He then identified himself as a Red Wizard of Thay, a title Zenvon understood carried enough power and reputation that it was not to be treated lightly.

Hamun nevertheless offered to demonstrate that he could be a positive member of the local community by trading information. While his zombies formed a protective ring around him in a deliberate show of force, he asked Zenvon what he most wished to know. Zenvon named the location of Cragmaw Castle. Hamun claimed to know it and offered the information in exchange for one of two favors.

The first option was to deal with marauders at Wyvern Tor: several bugbears and an ogre had scouted Hamun's camp and appeared inclined to cause trouble. Hamun insisted that he could handle them himself but did not want to raise still more zombies merely to conduct his business. The second option was to ask the banshee Agatha for the name of the wizard who originally built the tower at Old Owl Well. When Sister Maela asked why he did not question Agatha himself, Hamun admitted that he did not want to be killed by a banshee.

The party withdrew briefly to discuss the choice. Zenvon initially regarded visiting Agatha as the more suicidal option, reasoning that they were already familiar with bugbears. Sister Maela enthusiastically agreed, declaring that she would choose fighting ogres and bugbears over chatting with a banshee every day and “twice on the 10th day.” The party accepted the Wyvern Tor task. Neither Hamun nor the party remarked on it, but the job was one they had already been paid to take: Harbin Wester's 120-gp contract on the same marauders had been sitting unstarted since Phandalin.

Hamun was pleased and promised to have the location of Cragmaw Castle ready when they returned, perhaps accompanied by a fine meal. When Zenvon asked that the zombies not attack on their return, Hamun advised them not to walk into someone's home without calling out first. Zenvon pointed out that the wizard's home was technically the tent and that they had never entered it, but Hamun replied that his zombies were simply protective and asked the party to shout before approaching.

The trek to Wyvern Tor wound through rugged terrain. Zenvon's first attempt to locate the marauder camp failed, prompting Veyra to observe, “I don't think you know where you're going, buddy.” On his second attempt, Zenvon rolled a natural 20, found the camp, and discovered a shortcut. He silently stared at Veyra and pointed ahead as if to say, “trust me this time,” earning a simple “well done.”

The faint smell of smoke hung in the air as the party climbed a rugged ridge on the lower slopes of the hill. Fifty yards away, a cave mouth opened at the bottom of a ravine. Crouched behind a boulder twenty yards outside the cave, one of the Axe-Biter Marauders kept watch: a tall, furry bugbear with large ears, its eyes scanning the slopes with the bored vigilance of a creature that had not yet been given reason to be alarmed. The party remained out of the sentry's sight, studying the cave and preparing for what came next.

## Locations

### Tresendar Manor

A ruined estate in Phandalin containing a secret Redbrand hideout, prison cells, an apothecary, bugbear barracks, skeletal guardians, and a dangerous crevasse inhabited by the Nothic Ssarnak. The party rescued the Dendrar family from its cells, recovered treasure from the crevasse, and escaped after a brutal battle with Nosk's bugbears. Three of the four bugbears survived the oil fire. Where they went afterwards is unknown — the party never returned to find out — and they remain an unresolved danger.

### Stonehill Inn

A local tavern and inn in Phandalin where the party recovered from its wounds, received a free night's stay for its heroics, gathered local lore from Toblen, and shared breakfast with Sildar Hallwinter. Toblen calls the adventurers the heroes of Phandalin, while Elsa serves their drinks. Zenvon also spent ten silver pieces to have food sent up after the escape from Tresendar Manor.

### Shrine of Luck

A temple in Phandalin where Sister Garaele hailed the party as the town's new heroes and requested help approaching the banshee Agatha. She supplied a jeweled silver comb intended to win Agatha's favor and offered three potions of healing in exchange for information about Bowgentle's spellbook.

### Miner's Exchange

The headquarters of Phandalin's local guild, where Halia Thornton manages trade and attempted to purchase correspondence recovered from Glasstaff for 100 gold pieces. Her concern about Glasstaff's escape and keen interest in his letters made the party suspicious. The party also reviewed its current quest log here and chose Old Owl Well as its next destination.

### Edermath Orchard

A cottage and orchard on the edge of Phandalin, home to retired adventurer Daran Edermath. The party found Daran clearing underbrush, received warnings about the eastern wilderness and Wyvern Tor, and listened to an extended lecture on adventuring posture, vigilance, and old exploits. Daran also recounted the legendary history of Netheril and the dangerous artifacts left after its collapse.

### Triboar Trail

A road running east from Phandalin through increasingly unsettled country. The party traveled roughly thirty miles along it and found an abandoned caravan bearing evidence of an attack. Although no other travelers appeared that day, signs showed that the route remained in use. Regional trade is heavier on the north-south High Road, and civilization becomes increasingly sparse east of Neverwinter.

### Conyberry

A ruined town abandoned following devastation during the Spellplague and later attacks by Uthgardt raiders. Toblen warned that roadside ruins can attract people dangerous to unwary travelers, though an Insight check revealed that he had never personally visited the town. The route to Agatha's lair also passes through Conyberry.

### Old Owl Well

The crumbling ruins of an ancient Netherese watchtower in the rugged hills northeast of Phandalin. Mounds of rubble enclose a courtyard in which Hamun Kost has erected a colorful tent and begun an archaeological dig. Twelve zombies — the Kost Wardens — serve as laborers and a defensive cordon; among them are Warden Dreth, a sun-peeled former sentinel, and Warden Holl, a dead acolyte still wearing a singed robe. Hamun expects visitors to call out before entering so he can restrain or redirect his protective undead.

### Wyvern Tor

A rugged ridge on the lower slopes of the hills featuring a hidden ravine and the camp of the Axe-Biter Marauders. The party is under two separate commissions to clear it: Harbin Wester's 120-gp contract and Hamun Kost's favour. The full module roster is the orc chief Brughor Axe-Biter, four bugbears, and the ogre Gog. The party detects smoke from the camp and finds a cave mouth roughly fifty yards below its position. A lone bugbear sentry guards the entrance from behind a boulder twenty yards outside the cave.

## NPCs

### Mirna Dendrar

A human woman rescued from the Redbrand cells with her children, 13-year-old Nars and 18-year-old Nilsa. The Redbrands murdered her husband, Thel, and planned to kill the rest of the family; Mirna delayed them by inventing wealthy relatives in Neverwinter who would pay a ransom. She rewarded the party with the location of a hidden emerald necklace in her family's abandoned alchemy shop in Thundertree and asked them to recover Thel's body if possible.

### Ssarnak

A Nothic living in the caverns beneath Tresendar Manor that feeds on corpses and recognizes Glasstaff as its master. Zenvon distracted it with an illusion of Glasstaff working. As the party fled, Ssarnak whispered to Veyra that her teacher was not dead and was “not in a place where dead is easy,” causing her to stop and attempt to return to the crevasse.

### Nosk

The leader of the Scarlet Fist, the Black Spider's four-bugbear reinforcement group sent to support the Redbrands and intimidate Phandalin. He mocked Zenvon's attempt to command him, accidentally revealed that the Black Spider was at Cragmaw Castle, and then initiated a brutal battle. His morning-star strike dealt 14 damage and nearly brought Zenvon down.

### Sildar Hallwinter

A member of the Lords' Alliance who paid the remaining 150 gold pieces for proof of Iarno Albrek's betrayal. He wants Iarno captured alive for trial but would accept his death. He later offered 500 gold pieces for locating Cragmaw Castle and driving out its band leaders, adding that the Lords' Alliance would look kindly upon whoever succeeded. His pointed admiration of Pip's “fine, noble sword” suggested that he may be considering Pip for future membership.

### Sister Garaele

A scholarly acolyte at the Shrine of Luck who previously tried to approach Agatha and barely escaped with her life. She asked the party to present the banshee with a jeweled silver comb and inquire about the location of the legendary spellbook of Bowgentle, offering three potions of healing as payment.

### Halia Thornton

The guildmaster of the Miner's Exchange who congratulated the party on defeating the Redbrands but grew cold upon learning Glasstaff escaped. She quickly asked about correspondence recovered from his belongings and offered 100 gold pieces for it, claiming an interest in securing local trade. Zenvon judged the information more valuable and refused, leaving both sides carefully polite and the party deeply suspicious.

### Daran Edermath

A retired adventurer who tends an orchard on the edge of Phandalin. He congratulated the heroes, warned them about the ruined eastern lands and caravan attacks near Wyvern Tor, and declined to accompany them because old adventurers survive by choosing to stop adventuring. His ten-minute lecture on posture, sharp swords, vigilance, and dubiously relevant exploits earned Zenvon a one-use bonus for listening patiently. Daran also recounted the legends of Netheril, Karsus, the breaking of the Weave, and powerful artifacts scattered across the north.

### Harbin Wester

The Townmaster of Phandalin, mentioned by Daran as having reported several caravan attacks near Wyvern Tor. Daran had not followed all the details of Harbin's reports. Harbin is also the source of the party's standing 120-gp contract to clear the Wyvern Tor marauders, accepted in Phandalin and still unstarted at the top of this session.

### Toblen Stonehill

The friendly proprietor of the Stonehill Inn who welcomes the adventurers as the heroes of Phandalin. He warned them against entering Conyberry, describing it as a Spellplague-blasted ruin later destroyed by Uthgardt raiders. An Insight check revealed that his warnings were based more on caution and imagination than personal experience. He wished the party luck when it departed.

### Elsa

A barmaid at the Stonehill Inn. At Toblen's request, she poured Zenvon a beer when the party returned to announce its journey.

### Pip Thistlewick

A party companion and the current wielder of the magical longsword recovered from Tresendar Manor. He became visibly excited by Sildar's offer of 500 gold for Cragmaw Castle, nudged Zenvon to ask the exact amount, and called him “boss.” After Zenvon reminded him that the reward would be split four ways, Pip smoothly tempered his enthusiasm and told Sildar the party would keep the offer under advisement. He may not have realized that Sildar's praise of his noble sword hinted at possible membership in the Lords' Alliance.

### Veyra

A scholarly companion carrying a blue crystal that resonates with Zenvon's black stone. Ssarnak's revelation about her missing teacher nearly drew her back into Tresendar Manor. She recalled her adviser Orryn Voss and his theory about a device controlling people. At Old Owl Well, she consulted her notebook and identified Hamun Kost's head tattoos as necromantic symbols. She later mocked Zenvon's initial failure to navigate Wyvern Tor but conceded “well done” when he found a shortcut.

### Sister Maela Dawnforge

A companion who helped keep Zenvon alive during the fight with Nosk and physically restrained Veyra during the escape from Tresendar Manor. At Old Owl Well, she explained that zombies are mindless corpses raised by a necromancer and warned that commanding twelve of them implied considerable magical power. When Hamun offered two tasks, she strongly advocated fighting the bugbears and ogre rather than speaking with Agatha.

### Hamun Kost

A Red Wizard of Thay and necromancer with distinctive tattoos across his shaved head. He commands the twelve Kost Wardens at Old Owl Well, using them as archaeological laborers and as a conspicuous defensive cordon. Hamun is confident enough to tell Zenvon that he would simply kill the party if secrecy required it, but he insists that he has no interest in harming nearby settlements. He knows the location of Cragmaw Castle and will reveal it if the party either eliminates the marauders at Wyvern Tor or asks Agatha for the name of Old Owl Well's original builder. He claims he could handle the marauders himself but does not wish to raise additional zombies, and he candidly fears being killed by Agatha. He promises information and possibly a fine meal when the party returns.

### Hul

A tall, furry bugbear with large ears keeping watch outside the marauder cave at Wyvern Tor. It is hunkered behind a boulder approximately twenty yards from the cave mouth and has not yet noticed the party on the ridge. It is one of four bugbear rank-and-file serving the orc chief Brughor Axe-Biter alongside the ogre Gog.

## Items

### Unidentified Magical Longsword

A finely crafted magical longsword discovered in a hidden chest beneath the crevasse in Tresendar Manor. Zenvon opened the chest remotely with Mage Hand, and the party agreed that its most capable fighter, Pip, should wield the weapon. The sword is Talon, the blade of the long-dead knight Aldith Tresendar, but the party has not identified its name or provenance. Sildar Hallwinter later called it a “fine, noble sword,” using the compliment to hint at future opportunities for Pip within the Lords' Alliance.

### Jeweled Silver Comb

A gift provided by Sister Garaele to be presented to the banshee Agatha in order to win her favor and obtain information about the location of the mage Bowgentle's legendary spellbook. Hamun Kost subsequently offered a second question for Agatha: the name of the wizard who originally built the tower at Old Owl Well.

### Black Stone

A mysterious object carried secretly by Zenvon since the road. After the party's escape from Tresendar Manor, he finally showed it to Veyra; her blue crystal flared brightly, and the two objects resonated with an unseen magical force. The reaction, together with the altered goblins and Ssarnak's revelation, lends credibility to Orryn Voss's theory that some kind of device is controlling people.

Cast by Zenvon during the brutal fight in the bugbear barracks, blasting two bugbears. The spell did not overcome the creatures' resilience, and Nosk answered with a 14-damage morning-star strike that left Zenvon barely standing.
