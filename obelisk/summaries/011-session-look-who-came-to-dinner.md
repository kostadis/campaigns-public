# Chapter 11 Look who came to dinner

Date: 2026-09-05

## Summary
The party crested the rugged ridge of Wyvern Tor with the faint smell of cookfire smoke hanging in the cool air. Their work here served two purposes: clearing the marauders for Harbin Wester and fulfilling their agreement with Hamun Kost, who had promised information leading to Cragmaw Castle. At the bottom of a rocky ravine, a lone bugbear leaned on a salvaged pike beside a cave mouth, his milky, half-blind eye scanning the terrain with bored disinterest.

Zenvon initially considered shooting the sentry with his shortbow, but Pip suggested observing him first. A Stealth check of 25 brought Zenvon close enough to study the guard. The cave was too dark to inspect from that position, though the smell of a cookfire suggested more occupants inside. When the companions tried to advance, their failed group stealth alerted the sentry to something south of his post.

The party hatched a layered plan. Zenvon used Mage Hand to throw stones and redirect the guard’s attention. He initially hoped to draw out one or two additional enemies, but Pip warned that they could summon far more opposition than they could handle. Distance also mattered: Pip had roughly sixty feet of open ground to cross before he could attack. Veyra ultimately stepped into the open clutching her notebook, silently impersonating a bewildered student. The bugbear muttered, “Just a tourist!” with the appetite of a predator sizing up dinner.

Only Veyra gained the opening surprise attack. Her first Magic Missile dealt 6 damage; Zenvon’s shortbow missed, and he moved along the ridge and hid. Sister Maela charged in with her mace, determined to prevent an alarm, and Veyra’s next volley struck harder. Pip finished the sentry with his enchanted sword. Zenvon felt sorry for the creature’s blind eye, prompting sympathy from Maela and a reminder from Veyra that she had been its prospective lunch. At Maela’s urging, they concealed the body in bushes.

Inside the firelit cave waited four bugbears, a hulking ogre, and a single orc. Zenvon and Maela found little through their investigation, but Pip recognized the important detail: the physically weakest creature sat beside the fire while the others listened. The orc was the leader. Drawing on his military experience—and remembering a previous retreat from four bugbears—Pip advised killing the boss to break the gang’s morale.

Zenvon proposed hiding behind an illusory version of the dead sentry. After checking Minor Illusion’s limitations, the party settled on a stationary image rather than a moving escort. The concealment gave them advantage on their approach, and Zenvon and Sister Maela gained surprise. Zenvon’s opening attacks badly wounded Brughor Axe-Biter; after Maela missed, Zenvon struck again in the normal initiative order. His short sword unbalanced the orc, and his scimitar cut Brughor’s throat.

The four bugbears fled. Zenvon chose to let them escape, and Pip stopped Veyra from attacking one as it passed. Gog, the ogre, stayed to fight. His furious grief revealed that Brughor had meant much more to him than a commanding officer. He denounced the fleeing bugbears and threatened to eat the party alive.

Sister Maela called down sacred fire, Pip pressed Gog with Talon, and Veyra supported them with fire and magical darts. Zenvon tried True Strike for the first time, landing a shortsword attack followed by a natural-20 scimitar strike. With Gog nearly finished, Veyra asked whether to expend her last spell; Zenvon told her to make it count, and her Magic Missile dealt 15 damage.

Zenvon then attempted diplomacy, rolling a natural 20 for a total Persuasion result of 22. He asked why the raiders had been bothering Hamun Kost. Gog broke down over Brughor, explaining that Brughor ordered attacks but failing to recognize Hamun’s name. Zenvon decided the interrogation had yielded nothing useful and called for the killing to resume. Pip delivered the final blow. Maela found the abrupt transition from negotiation to execution cold, though she admitted she would have skipped the negotiation herself.

The cave held modest treasure: silver, electrum, and three perfume vials. More intriguing was a charcoal drawing of an oddly shaped goblin with a crystal jutting from its skull. The crystal was part of the drawing, not an object embedded in the wall. Veyra’s blue crystal flared faintly, and she consulted her notes about Sildar’s account of a strange goblin with an elongated head.

Veyra began connecting the goblins, crystals, bugbears, and her missing mentor in a sprawling diagram. Maela teased that an arrow was missing, sending her searching for another connection. Zenvon could not determine whether the theory made sense, but suggested that Hamun and Cragmaw Castle might supply answers. Veyra drew a castle and aimed her arrows at it. A brief disagreement over rewarding Pip’s killing blow ended amicably when Zenvon gave Pip his own share of the loot while everyone else retained their normal portions.

At Old Owl Well, Hamun welcomed their success and insisted on serving dinner before discussing information. His undead former apprentice, Warden Holl, waited on the party beside a pleasant fire. Hamun’s cultivated hospitality did little to ease Zenvon’s distrust.

Mention of the crystal-headed goblin abruptly erased Hamun’s relaxed manner. He demanded the exact details, then claimed not to know what they meant. Zenvon could not read him, but Pip caught a flash of excitement when the black obelisk came up. Zenvon restricted what they disclosed and kept Veyra’s blue crystal secret.

Hamun described himself as an expert archaeologist specializing in the Netheril Empire and its ancient language. He identified Pip’s sword, Talon, as the blade of Aldith Tresendar, the Black Hawk, whose name came from his black armor and billowing cape. Hamun offered to advise them on Netherese discoveries. Veyra, barely containing her questions, obeyed Zenvon’s instruction to stay quiet.

The evening ended without Hamun handing over the route to Cragmaw Castle. The party still has the name and no road to it. The session also concluded with advancement to level 4, leaving Zenvon to choose a feat or ability score improvement and one additional prepared spell.

## NPCs
### Pip Thistlewick
A military-minded companion who turns observations into practical tactics. He recommends studying the sentry, warns against drawing out unknown reinforcements, and identifies Brughor’s authority from his position by the fire and the others’ attention.

Pip wields the +1 sword Talon and delivers the killing blows on both the sentry and Gog. His Action Surge against Gog produces a missed follow-up attack. He also restrains Veyra from attacking fleeing bugbears, honoring the party’s plan.

He discovers the disappointing unlocked treasure chest and receives Zenvon’s personal share after the loot dispute. At dinner, he notices Hamun’s concealed excitement about the black obelisk and learns Talon’s history.

### Sister Maela Dawnforge
A cleric companion who combines compassion with ruthless practicality. She strikes the sentry with her mace, urges the party to conceal the body, and praises Zenvon for caring about a creature they killed.

She gains surprise in the cave but misses Brughor. Against Gog, her first Sacred Flame deals 10 damage, while later attempts are resisted. She warns the bugbears to leave and accepts their retreat.

Maela calls Zenvon cold for moving from negotiation to killing so abruptly, while admitting she would have killed Gog without negotiating. Her missing-arrow joke exploits Veyra’s obsessive theorizing; when Veyra proudly supplies a castle-shaped answer, Maela resorts to prayer to hold her tongue.

### Veyra
A scholarly companion whose notebook serves as both research tool and ambush prop. She poses silently as a lost student and is the only companion to gain surprise against the sentry.

Her recorded Magic Missile volleys deal 6 and 12 damage to the sentry; against Gog, she contributes a 1-damage Fire Bolt and a final 15-damage Magic Missile. Pip must remind her to let a fleeing bugbear go.

Her blue crystal flares at the charcoal drawing. She consults notes about Sildar, recalls earlier strange-goblin clues, and builds a theory connecting the crystals, bugbears, her mentor, and Cragmaw Castle. Zenvon cannot determine whether her connections are sound.

She objects to kill bonuses replacing equal treasure shares. At dinner, she obeys Zenvon’s instruction to conceal her crystal despite an almost overwhelming desire to question Hamun.

### Hul
A bored guard with a milky film over one eye, leaning on a salvaged pike beside a boulder outside the cave. He notices the companions’ attempted approach but does not immediately identify them.

Mage Hand distractions and Veyra’s lost-student act draw him into the ambush. His tourist remark carries unmistakably predatory intent. Pip kills him, and the party conceals his body in bushes.

### Brughor Axe-Biter
The orc leader of the Wyvern Tor band. Despite being physically weaker than the ogre and bugbears, he commands their attention from his place beside the fire.

Pip identifies him as the gang’s organizer and recommends attacking him first. Zenvon’s opening strikes leave him barely alive before the subsequent lethal attack. His death causes all four remaining bugbears to flee.

Gog’s grief establishes that Brughor was also his romantic companion. According to Gog, Brughor gave the orders to attack.

### Gog
A massive ogre deeply attached to Brughor Axe-Biter. He refuses to retreat, berates the fleeing bugbears, and fights with a great club while threatening to eat the party alive.

Zenvon’s successful Persuasion attempt draws out tears and the explanation that Brughor ordered the raids. Gog does not recognize Hamun Kost’s name and would need a description to identify him. He reveals nothing useful to Zenvon’s satisfaction, and Pip kills him after the questioning ends.

### Hamun Kost
A Red Wizard of Thay and necromancer who commissioned the clearing of Wyvern Tor in exchange for information about Cragmaw Castle. His promised directions remain undelivered at the session’s end.

Hamun enjoys fine food and cultivated hospitality, insisting on dinner before business and employing his undead apprentice as waiter. The crystal-headed goblin drawing abruptly changes his demeanor, though he claims not to understand it.

He professes expertise in Netheril’s archaeology and language, identifies Talon, and offers advice on future Netherese discoveries. Pip notices his suppressed excitement at mention of the black obelisk. Zenvon neither establishes whether he is lying nor trusts him enough to disclose Veyra’s crystal.

### Warden Holl
Hamun Kost’s former apprentice, now an undead servant who waits on the party at Old Owl Well. His appearance as a Red Wizard distinguishes him from the more ordinary-looking zombies.

Hamun remarks that Holl serves him better in death than in life. Per the established GM ruling, he is distinct from Warden Dreth.

## Locations
### Wyvern Tor
A rugged ridge and ravine on the lower slopes of a hill. On arrival, the cave mouth lies approximately fifty yards from the party, with the sentry’s boulder another twenty yards outside the entrance.

Cookfire smoke betrays occupation before the party can inspect the interior. The cave houses Brughor, Gog, and four additional bugbears; its firelit gathering place exposes their hierarchy. Nearby bushes conceal the slain sentry.

Inside are an unlocked treasure chest and a crude charcoal drawing of a goblin with a crystal in its skull. Four bugbears escape after Brughor’s death.

### Old Owl Well
An ancient site occupied by Hamun Kost and his zombie servants. The undead include his former apprentice, a guard-like figure, and several peasant-like bodies.

The camp accommodates a surprisingly fine dinner beside a fire. Its civilized comforts coexist with undead service and Hamun’s unsettling interest in the party’s discoveries. It remains the place where the party must collect the promised directions to Cragmaw Castle.
