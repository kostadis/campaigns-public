# Chapter 08 Deception

Date: 2026-07-18

## Summary

The air in the Tresendar Crypts was thick with the smell of ancient stone and decay as the party squared off against a cluster of skeletal guardians. One of the undead creatures raised a shortbow and loosed an arrow at the group, but the shot went wide — missing "spectacularly," in the DM's own words — clattering harmlessly against the far wall. Zenvon Forepot pressed forward and struck at the nearest skeleton. His first swing missed, but his second connected with a natural twenty, drawing first blood in the grim underground chamber for eight points of damage. Then Sister Maela Dawnforge stepped to the front of the group, raised her holy symbol high, and called upon the power of her dwarven god with a booming command: "BE GONE! In the name of my dwarven god!" Her divine presence washed over the undead like a wave, and two of the six skeletons recoiled in supernatural terror — their wisdom saving throws both failing the DC — fleeing through the nearest door and vanishing into the darkness beyond.

With the frightened skeletons gone, the remaining undead fought on, and one of them landed a vicious blow against Zenvon with its short sword, dealing eleven points of damage and leaving him with only four hit points remaining. Pip Thistlewick and Veyra coordinated their attacks with practiced efficiency: Pip cut down the skeleton he had already wounded and spent his Action Surge to strike a second for six points, while Veyra — having already expended two prior castings of Magic Missile — used her last remaining slot here, the glowing darts picking apart bone and sinew. Zenvon switched to his scimitar for Nick, missed his first swing, and landed the second for twelve points to destroy another. With Zenvon backing away at four hit points, the last skeleton turned on Pip instead and caught him for six — and it was Pip, not Zenvon, whom Sister Maela healed, laying her hands on him and channeling ten hit points of divine restoration via Cure Wounds (2d8+2). Zenvon closed the fight with his dagger, rolling a 24 to hit and dealing 8 points of damage, before the party turned their attention to the three stone sarcophagi resting in the chamber.

As they searched the ancient remains, the two skeletons that had fled earlier crept back into the room. Upon noticing the red cloaks the party wore, however, the creatures simply stood at attention, recognizing the garments as a sign of allegiance and choosing not to attack. The DM noted, in a moment of candid rules correction, that the skeletons should not have attacked the cloaked party to begin with and applied the detail retroactively. Digging through the sarcophagi, Zenvon's fingers closed around a jeweled gold bracelet nestled among the bones of a human woman — a valuable find worth 140 gold pieces.

It was then that the party heard it — muffled cries drifting through the northeastern door. An investigation check confirmed what the voices were: "Please, help us! Please, help us!" — desperate and frightened captives. The cleric turned to Zenvon with a firm look and reminded him that they were the good guys, and that the helpless deserved their protection. Zenvon relented — "I'll do this for you, Sister M" — and the party moved toward the door. When they pushed it open, they found a long room divided by iron bars into filthy cells lined with straw, the hinge doors secured by chains and padlocks. Two human women were locked in one cell and a young boy was confined in another — all captives of the Redbrands. Before the party could act, however, a group of Redbrand ruffians who had been lying in wait lunged forward, attempting to catch the adventurers off guard. Their ambush failed entirely, the ruffians rolling so poorly that no one was caught by surprise, and the fight was on.

Zenvon darted forward and drove his dagger into the nearest ruffian for 5 points before slipping back out of reach using his Cunning Action to disengage. Sister Maela swung her hammer with righteous fury, cracking bone and sending one of the bandits reeling with 6 points of damage. Pip cut down a wounded ruffian with a single decisive strike for 6 points, killing him outright. Veyra unleashed a bolt of fire that scored 8 points of damage and drove another to within a hit point of death — this time, the DM allowed, the damage did sound the way it looked. Sister Maela then called down a column of sacred radiant flame: the ruffian blew his saving throw, took 5 points of radiant damage, and died. The last surviving ruffian, seeing his companions fall one by one, turned and bolted from the room.

Pip gave chase while Zenvon and Sister Maela stayed behind to free the prisoners. Zenvon worked quickly, picking the locks on the iron bars with a dexterity check, and the young boy burst free with delight. Sister Maela unlocked the remaining cell to release the captive women. But Pip's sprint after the fleeing bandit came to a sudden, painful halt — the floor gave way beneath his feet, and he plunged twenty feet into a hidden pit trap, landing hard in the darkness below. The DM rolled poorly for the fall damage; Pip survived with only 3 points taken. Still, the bandit had gained precious ground.

Zenvon rushed to the edge of the pit and improvised: pressing his whip into service as a makeshift rope, he hauled Pip back up to safety. Veyra attempted to catch the fleeing ruffian with a bolt of fire but missed entirely, scorching the wall instead. Sister Maela mended Pip's injuries with another touch of divine magic — 6 more hit points restored — and the party pressed on.

They entered a large cellar below the manor. A read-aloud from the module described the space: a five-foot landing fifteen feet above the cellar floor, stone steps descending in two short flights, a door beneath the stairs to the north, a great stone cistern dominating the western wall, and racks of kegs and barrels lining the room. The party noted the cistern but did not search it yet. An investigation roll of 19 at the door on the upper landing revealed voices murmuring on the other side — several of them. The fleeing ruffian had clearly warned his companions.

Zenvon considered his options for only a moment before a bold plan took shape. He threw open the door and strode in wearing his red cloak, projecting the confidence of a man who belonged there. The bandits paused and looked at the cloak: "Wait! You're one of us!" They turned on their own comrade: "What do you mean they attacked you, man? Are you sure these are the guys that attacked you?" The ruffian jabbed back defiantly — "Do I look like I'm the kind of person who would lie? That is a killing machine! Kill him!" — but Zenvon seized the moment, pointing at the fleeing ruffian and accusing him of being a traitor who had let enemies into the hideout: "We are one of you guys... look at this guy, this guy has turned a traitor. He was trying to let someone in, so that's why I wanted to catch him and eliminate him. So he's the bad guy." The DM declared the performance "very clever — extremely clever" and called for a deception roll with advantage. The ruffian failed his counter-persuasion roll. One of the bandits, named Dosa Rook, immediately believed it and declared, "About time somebody cleaned house — I'm going to cut this rat into several pieces." A second, more skeptical bandit was not fully convinced: "Hmm, traitor. Funny. Nobody sent word down, but it's not very convincing when he's covered in blood and you're covered in blood." He announced he would need to go verify the story.

Zenvon offered to accompany him and lure him toward the waiting party on the stairs. The moment the skeptical bandit came within reach, Pip struck him for 6 points with his sword — and the ruse unraveled in an instant. A chaotic brawl erupted. Pip finished the bandit he had struck. Zenvon drove his dagger home for 12 points of damage, killing Dosa Rook. When the dust settled, only a bandit named Wick was left standing — beside the injured but still-living Redbrand ruffian who had originally fled. Wick made his calculation quickly: he cut down the wounded ruffian himself, then threw up his hands and begged for mercy.

Wick was willing to talk, and Zenvon pressed him hard. The thug described Glasstaff as a dangerous wizard — not merely a figurehead: "I've seen him drop a man with a spell, no weapon drawn, nobody messes with him twice." He claimed that the dungeon beneath the manor had multiple levels, and that the subterranean depths were deeply unsettling in a way that went beyond ordinary danger — "There's something really wrong over there that messes with your head, I'm told." He added that Glasstaff answered to somebody, that he had overheard the wizard complaining about orders from town and references to "the boss's business" — someone in Phandalin, Wick believed, though the sentence trailed off before he could finish the thought. When Zenvon spoke the name "Black Spider," Wick visibly flinched, his face going pale — but when pressed, he retreated behind ignorance: "Look, dude, I just carry crates, alright? You start asking about the boss's boss, that's how people end up carrying less." Under further duress, Wick offered one final piece of information — that the Redbrands had previously "handled" a family called the Dendrars. The DM confirmed quietly that this was the same family the party had just freed from the cells.

Zenvon weighed his options. Wick had made a show of wanting to join the party in exchange for gold, and a high insight roll confirmed what Zenvon already suspected: the man was lying. He wanted the coin and the first opportunity to disappear — "not that he'll betray you, but that he wants to get out of here," the DM noted, adding that Wick's "of course I'm with you, boss" rang hollow. There was no loyalty to be bought, only a thug looking for an exit. Zenvon made his decision, and Wick met his end in the cellar. Only then did the party turn to the cistern, and the search paid: a waterproof satchel hung from a submerged rope attached to the south wall, about two feet below the water's surface, holding a potion of healing and a potion of invisibility. They looted the fallen bandits as well — bodies carrying 16 silver and 7 gold on one; 12 silver and 5 gold on another; and 20 copper pieces, 11 electrum pieces, and two garnets worth 10 gold pieces each on the third.

With the barracks cleared and the treasure secured, the party made their way back to the cells where the freed captives were still waiting. The two skeletal guardians from the crypts had drifted back to the doorway, standing passive in the presence of the red cloaks. On the other side of the door, however, the captives had barricaded themselves in — terrified by the skeletons just outside. Zenvon called out to them: "Hey guys, it's me. I came back for you. It's all safe now. You can let go of the door. Let me help you." The sound of furniture scraping across stone followed, and then the door swung open. The skeletons, seeing Zenvon step inside, retreated to their resting positions without a sound. The barracks were cleared, but the party still had unfinished business in the hideout.

## Locations

### Tresendar Crypts
An ancient burial site located beneath Tresendar Manor, featuring three stone sarcophagi and false columns in an atmosphere of heavy decay. Skeletal guardians armed with shortbows and short swords stand watch here. The skeletons are keyed to ignore those wearing red Redbrand cloaks — a detail the DM caught and applied mid-session, noting the initial skeleton attack should not have occurred.

### Slave Pens
A long room partitioned into three areas by iron bars, as read aloud by the DM: "filthy straw lines the floors of these cells, the hinge doors of which are secured by chains and padlocks." Two human women were held in the southern cell and a young boy in the northern cell. All three are members of the Dendrar family.

### The Cellar
A large subterranean room described by the DM as having a five-foot landing fifteen feet above the main floor, stone steps descending in two short flights, a door beneath the stairs to the north, a great stone cistern dominating the western wall, and walls lined with kegs and barrels. The barrels contain food stores. A waterproof satchel was hidden two feet below the cistern's surface, attached to a rope on the south wall.

### The Pit Trap
A concealed twenty-foot-deep hole hidden in the hallway connecting the slave pens to the cellar, designed to catch intruders. Pip fell into it while sprinting after a fleeing Redbrand ruffian, taking 3 points of damage. Once visible, the party walked around it without difficulty.

### The Barracks
A room adjoining the cellar landing where multiple Redbrand ruffians were stationed. The fleeing ruffian retreated here to raise the alarm. It was the site of Zenvon's deception gambit and the subsequent brawl. The room was not described in physical detail in this session.

## NPCs

### Pip Thistlewick
A capable fighter who accompanies the party, contributing significantly to combat by killing multiple Redbrand ruffians and pursuing a fleeing bandit. He used Action Surge during the crypt fight to land a second strike in the same round. His pursuit of the fleeing ruffian ended abruptly when he fell twenty feet into a pit trap, taking 3 points of damage. He was pulled out by Zenvon using a whip as a rope and healed twice by Sister Maela during the session.

### Sister Maela Dawnforge
A dwarven cleric who serves as the party's healer and moral compass. She turned several skeletons with a booming invocation — "BE GONE! In the name of my dwarven god!" — and cast Cure Wounds twice across the session, both times on Pip (10 hit points during the crypt fight; 6 hit points after the pit fall). She urged the party to rescue the captives and called down Sacred Flame to finish a Redbrand ruffian in the slave pens. The DM noted mid-session that her sidekick stat block was missing Turn Undead and applied the ability anyway, stating that "clerics that can't turn undead are kind of pointless."

### Veyra
A spellcaster who supported the party with Magic Missile (her last remaining casting expended in the crypt fight) and Fire Bolt for the remainder of the session. She disengaged from melee rather than risk her 4 hit points in close combat. Mindless undead guardians armed with shortbows and short swords that haunt the Tresendar Crypts. Six rose against the party.
