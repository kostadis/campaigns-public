# Chapter 07 It's Nothing Personal, You See

Date: 2026-07-16

## Summary
As the session resumed deep beneath the ruins of Tresendar Manor, the party remained face to face with the Nothic — a gaunt, one-eyed creature of terrible hunger and terrible knowledge. The creature had just laid bare a secret it had plucked from Pip Thistlewick's mind: Pip had won a strange blackstone shard in a dice game near Neverwinter, then sold it to a passing peddler bound for Phandalin. Since parting with the shard, Pip had been haunted by dreams of a broken black stone buried beneath the earth and whispers that called his name. He wanted to find the peddler, buy or steal the shard back, and discover why it was haunting him. Everyone could hear the creature, but only Sister Maela understood its Undercommon and could translate.

The Nothic made its terms simple: it was hungry, and it would let the party live if they gave it one of their own. While the party considered the offer, Veyra investigated the cavern and discovered dead goblins with elongated skulls, dressed in filthy, haphazard rags. The creature was loose, with no chains restraining it. Questioned about the remains, the Redbrand prisoner Rondar said he had never seen a goblin like that. He knew only that someone had ventured into the lower area and never returned.

Zenvon negotiated through Maela, asking what the party would receive for such a sacrifice and why they should trust the creature. The Nothic promised to eat only one of them and let the others continue exploring. Its reasoning was practical: “I like living, and why would I kill you if I've been fed?” The party reached a cold agreement — Rondar would pay the price. But Rondar had been listening, and he bolted, screaming for help. Pip tried to knock him down and failed. Zenvon's first attack missed, but his second left the fleeing prisoner barely standing.

Sister Maela judged Rondar a crook, thief, and scoundrel, and dismissed the killing as impersonal. Rondar protested that he could hardly see his own execution that way. Maela acknowledged his point without accepting it, then killed him with her mace. The delighted Nothic dragged the body away. Zenvon wondered whether the creature might grant wishes; the DM explained that its reward was the right to remain alive. Maela urged the party onward before it wanted another meal.

The group headed west, continuing their search for the Redbrands' boss. At a southern door, Zenvon heard bugbears laughing and talking, along with a goblin. At a northern door, he heard humans gambling. Both doors were closed but unlocked. Pip warned that an attack could leave them trapped between the two groups. Zenvon's initial plan was to rush inside, close the door, and take each human prisoner at swordpoint. After Pip questioned the practicality of immediately subduing four fighters, Zenvon revised the plan: beat them down first, then question them.

Four tough warriors in scarlet cloaks sat around a table heaped with coins and trinkets, unprepared for the intrusion. Pip led the charge with his declaration that he had never liked the crooked kind. Veyra cast Magic Missile from outside the room, Zenvon fought with his blades, and Sister Maela finished a wounded enemy with her mace. The fighting included several misses on both sides. A ruffian shouted for the bugbears; a bandit trying to escape through Veyra struck her for four damage. Her second Magic Missile split between the remaining enemies and killed them both.

With everyone dead, Zenvon closed the door and confronted Veyra about losing their chance to interrogate a survivor. She defended herself: “they were about to kill me.” His persuasion only partially convinced her to leave enemies alive in future. The party found 75 copper pieces and 55 silver pieces, with a four-way split agreed as the bookkeeping rule.

A bugbear came to investigate, knocking and asking whether everything was alright. Zenvon improvised a bandit's voice, despite admitting he did not know exactly how the dead men had sounded. His deception worked: the bugbear walked away and shut its own door. All four companions donned the fallen Redbrands' cloaks. Following Pip's suggestion, they stacked barrels against the bugbears' door and continued north.

Beyond a short succession of doors lay a wizard's workshop, crowded with bubbling alchemical apparatus, parchment, and strange books. Listening along the way yielded little except the sound of the Nothic digesting. Zenvon invited Veyra into what he called her playground. Initially uncertain, she examined the equipment and notes and identified an unsuccessful attempt to brew a potion of invisibility. Most of the apparatus had little value, but three bottles — Mercury, Dragon Bile, and Powdered Nightshade — were worth 25 gold pieces each.

Sister Maela found Urmon's journal, written in Dwarvish. It described the Lost Mine of Phandelver, the Forge of Spells, and Lightbringer, a magic mace commissioned by the priests of Lathander from mages working with the gnomes and dwarves of the Phandelver Pact. The mace had been lost when Wave Echo Cave and its mine vanished from history.

Pressing east, the party entered a scarlet-draped bedchamber with a desk, bed, and wooden chest. An open secret door suggested someone had just fled. They pursued immediately. Veyra spotted the fleeing wizard and asked Zenvon whether she should kill him — a direct response to their earlier conversation. He told her to stop his escape without killing him. Her Fire Bolt hit for five damage, and Pip's javelin dealt another five.

Recognized from Sildar's description as Iarno Albrek, the wizard used movement and teleportation magic to gain distance. Zenvon demanded that he surrender, but Iarno shouted instead at the Nothic: “I pay you to protect this entrance, not to feast on whatever bodies I presented to you.” Zenvon's shortbow shot missed, and Iarno resisted Sister Maela's Sacred Flame. He escaped through the cavern route toward the party's original entrance. When the Nothic approached asking for more food, Zenvon directed it to the four bodies in the common room.

Returning to Iarno's quarters, the party found workshop supply orders addressed to apothecaries and alchemists, along with a letter bearing a spider-shaped wax seal. The letter ordered Iarno to capture the party if possible, kill them if necessary, and deliver their dwarven maps. It confirmed his connection to The Spider, the mysterious figure already associated with the goblins and a wider network of bandit gangs. The evidence could be valuable to Sildar, and the DM connected Iarno to Gundren Rockseeker's capture. The chest yielded gold and gemstones, including carnelians and peridots, and the party divided the spoils before moving on.

In the finished northern end of the cavern, among barrels, crates, packing straw, and tools, Zenvon discovered another secret door. Beyond it stood a locked eastern door, which he picked open to reveal an armory. Racks of weapons and a dozen spare red cloaks suggested preparations on a larger scale. Pip concluded that someone was trying to build an army. Carrying everything would be impractical, so the party selected useful pieces and could return for the rest after clearing the hideout. Zenvon chose a short sword decorated with semi-precious stones; Pip exchanged a plain crossbow for an equally decorative one. Sister Maela rolled her eyes.

The party continued south to the Tresendar crypts. Three stone sarcophagi stood amid oak-tree carvings, with six skeletons in rusty mail propped against the walls. Zenvon was visibly unsettled, despite their stillness. Maela's first examination revealed nothing, but Veyra suspected animated skeletons and warned that they might attack. She could not explain why they had not already moved. Pip offered to go first, while Zenvon tried not to appear frightened. A successful History check identified the tomb as the burial place of the Tresendar family elders, briefly reassuring him.

When Zenvon stepped toward the northeast door, the skeletons animated. Sister Maela identified them as undead, initiative was rolled, and the session ended before combat began. Zenvon immediately wondered whether the party could run back through the door. The abrupt awakening made this the player's favorite cliffhanger of the sessions so far. Early in the next session, the GM ruled that the skeletons should not have attacked characters wearing Redbrand cloaks. The correction governed their behavior afterward; it did not erase the ensuing battle or its consequences from the campaign record.

## Locations
### Tresendar Manor Basement
A subterranean complex beneath the manor ruins, featuring natural caverns and finished stone rooms used as a Redbrand hideout. The Nothic lurks in a crevasse within the cavern and is not chained.

The northern cavern has finished stone walls and a flagstone floor, with barrels, empty crates, packing straw, hammers, pry bars, and nails. A secret door here leads toward the armory. Iarno's escape passage reconnects with the cavern and the party's original entry route.

### Redbrand Common Room
A large room furnished with worn tables, chairs, wall benches, and tapped ale kegs, decorated with brown and red draperies. Four scarlet-cloaked ruffians were gambling around a table heaped with coins and trinkets when the party attacked.

A nearby southern room held bugbears and at least one audible goblin. After the fight, the party closed the common-room door, bluffed the investigating bugbear, and barricaded the bugbears' door with barrels. The Nothic later came here to eat the bodies.

### Wizard's Workshop
A laboratory filled with bubbling alchemical devices, distillation coils, alembics, retorts, and bookshelves crowded with strange tomes and parchment. Iarno was attempting to brew a potion of invisibility, but his notes showed that he had not succeeded.

The room contained Urmon's journal and three valuable reagent bottles. Veyra judged most other contents to have little value.

### Iarno's Quarters
A chamber draped in scarlet cloth containing a small writing desk with a matching chair, a comfortable bed, and a wooden chest at its foot. An open secret door to the north provided Iarno's escape route.

His desk held orders for alchemical supplies and the spider-sealed letter. The open chest contained gold and gemstones.

### Redbrand Armory
A chamber lined with spears, swords, crossbows, and bolts, with a dozen dirty red cloaks hanging by the entrance. Zenvon picked the locked eastern door to reach it after finding a secret passage from the cavern.

The stockpile prompted Pip's conclusion that someone was building an army. It included decorated weapons, but there was too much equipment to carry away at once.

### Tresendar Crypts
A desolate burial chamber containing three large stone sarcophagi, false columns carved as spreading oak trees, and six skeletons in rusty mail. Zenvon identified it as the resting place of the Tresendar family elders.

A southeast double door was covered in tarnished plates. Movement toward the northeast door triggered the skeletons at the session's end; the following session corrected that response because the party wore Redbrand cloaks, while retaining the played battle and its consequences.

## NPCs
### Ssarnak
A telepathic, one-eyed creature with long fangs and claws that lurks beneath Tresendar Manor. It extracts secrets from nearby minds. Everyone could hear its speech, but Sister Maela alone understood its Undercommon.

It bargained for one victim, ate Rondar, and later accepted the four dead Redbrands as another meal. Iarno's shouted complaint revealed that he paid it to protect the entrance and supplied it with bodies. The creature showed little interest in further conversation once it began feeding.

### Rondar
A Redbrand prisoner sacrificed to the Nothic. He knew of someone who had disappeared in the lower cavern but could offer no way past the creature. He did not recognize the unusual elongated-skull goblin.

Rondar refused to accept his fate quietly: he ran, screamed for help, survived Pip's attempted shove, and protested Maela's claim that killing him was impersonal. Zenvon wounded him, and Maela delivered the fatal mace blow.

### Pip Thistlewick
A brave companion who contributes tactical advice, warning about enemies on both sides and suggesting a barrel barricade. He criticized Zenvon's coldness toward Rondar, nevertheless tried to stop the prisoner escaping, and later charged the Redbrand gamblers.

Pip's blackstone shard remains a personal mystery; he wants to recover it from the peddler bound for Phandalin. During the pursuit he wounded Iarno with a javelin. He recognized the armory's military implications, chose an ornate crossbow, and offered to lead when Zenvon hesitated in the crypt.

### Sister Maela Dawnforge
A stern companion who translates Undercommon and takes harsh action against criminals. She killed Rondar after acknowledging and dismissing his protest, and used her mace in the common-room fight.

She helped Zenvon listen at a door, found Urmon's Dwarvish journal, and attempted Sacred Flame against Iarno. Her initial examination of the crypt revealed nothing; she identified the skeletons as undead when they animated. She also rolled her eyes at Zenvon and Pip selecting weapons for their decoration.

### Veyra
An impulsive magic-user who supports the party with Magic Missile and Fire Bolt. A bandit wounded her while attempting to escape, and she killed the final two common-room enemies with a split Magic Missile casting.

Zenvon only partially persuaded her to preserve enemies for questioning, but she later asked whether he wanted Iarno killed before attacking. Fascinated by the workshop, she identified the unsuccessful invisibility potion project and valuable reagents. In the crypt she suspected animated skeletons but could not explain their initial stillness.

### Redbrand Ruffian
Four scarlet-cloaked human warriors found gambling in the common room. One called for bugbear assistance; a bandit attempting to get past Veyra wounded her. All four died, leaving no prisoner to interrogate.

Their coins and cloaks became party loot, and their bodies became the Nothic's second meal. A nearby bugbear investigated the disturbance but accepted Zenvon's impersonation and withdrew.

### Iarno "Glasstaff" Albrek
A wizard overseeing the Redbrands and working for The Spider. Recognized from Sildar's description, he fled through a secret door and survived hits from Veyra's Fire Bolt and Pip's javelin.

He escaped using movement and teleportation, berating the Nothic for eating instead of guarding the entrance. His abandoned papers exposed supply purchases and orders from The Spider; his laboratory notes documented unsuccessful invisibility potion experiments.

### The Spider
A mysterious figure associated with the goblins and a broader network of bandit gangs. A spider-sealed letter ordered Iarno to capture the party if possible, kill them if necessary, and send their dwarven maps.

The letter gave the party evidence of Iarno's allegiance and a lead worth bringing to Sildar.

### Skeletons
Six undead guardians in rusty mail, initially motionless against the crypt walls. Veyra suspected their nature before they stirred, while Zenvon's movement toward the northeast door triggered initiative.

No attacks were resolved before the session ended. Early in the following session, the GM ruled that the Redbrand cloaks should have prevented their activation, but retained the ensuing battle and its consequences as recorded play.
