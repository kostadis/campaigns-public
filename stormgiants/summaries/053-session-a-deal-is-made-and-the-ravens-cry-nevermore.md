# Chapter 53 A deal is made, and the Ravens cry Nevermore.

Date: 21 to 22 Eleasis 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
Brassik watches the Dragon’s Breath, the airship of the cult whose commander is Delsephine, approach Raven Rock. The place is a geographical anomaly: whatever the time of year, it is always covered in snow, and nothing in his reading explains why. He has a paid gig as a scholar, which is way better than being a deckhand, so he pulls out his map of the area and tells the 3HPP of his research. He coughs, takes a swig of some brew, and describes the spirit mound of the Black Raven and Gray Wolf tribes on the icy slopes of the Spine of the World: the 100-foot-tall stone carved in the likeness of a perching raven, the carcasses the Black Ravens leave at its feet, the giant ravens that gather at dusk to feast, the four 50-foot-tall menhirs the Uthgardt shamans use to track the seasons and the stars, and the Gray Wolves' altar mound in a wolf-shaped depression southwest of Raven Rock, a rectangular slab of stone with the phases of the moon carved into its sides.

Orsik, irritated at all the digging at Morgur’s Mound, declares, "To the altar." Brassik asks, "Yes, but what about Raven Rock?" and Orsik cuts him off: "The altar is where it is. It’s always at the altar. We aren’t looking anywhere else." Brassik splutters, but remembers Orsik's blade and temper and falls silent.

Unla Key flies to the altar with the wings of a pegasus, a 30’ wingspan on a 3’ tall, 1’ wide halfling that never ceases to amuse Delsephine. Orsik and Vardis climb down the rope ladder and begin the long trudge to the altar, while Thistle flies around scouting with Thuul. Brassik had spent the evening chatting with Thistle, and discovered that the Ranger's relationship with his drake was more of a partnership; Thistle described it as anarcho-socialist and said they take turns being in charge. "Fascinating," Brassik mutters.

While the cleric, the fighter, and the mage dig, Thistle approaches some giant ravens and asks, "Have you seen any Uthgardt Barbarians?" The darkest of the ravens answers with a question of its own: "Who are the barbarians, the people who respect their ancestors or the group tearing up a religious site to steal an artifact?" Thistle tries to explain himself and realizes he has firmly planted his wingtip into his mouth. The murder of giant ravens goes "Craw! Craw! Craw!" Another raven asks whether he means the people who respect the Raven Rock, and a third says, "Who knows? It could be today, it could be tomorrow, it could be five from now". Thistle asks them not to tell anyone who took the artifact. The first raven answers, "Nevermore!" Thistle is alarmed; he does not know what that means and cannot remember what Uncle Joon said.

He flies to Vardis and Orsik and shouts, "When will you guys be done?" A wet, snow-covered, steaming Orsik shouts, "We found it!" Thistle warns, "Then we need to get out of here ASAP. I think we may have incoming." Vardis and Orsik hoist the thigh bone on their shoulders and march back. Thistle and Unla Key talk to Delsephine, who agrees to bring the Dragon’s Breath closer to the rest of the group; she fears the Ravens may not view her as an innocent bystander. She points her airship towards Great Worm Cavern.

On the 2nd day, flying over the world's spine toward the Great Worm Cavern, Brassik ponders how to make his last presentation memorable. The conversation turns to the contract between Brassik and Delsephine, which Klauth the Red Dragon has in his hoard. Klauth, Orsik learns, is "very supportive of your plans": dragons view the Giants as an obstacle to their eventual domination over the North. Orsik pokes Thistle about working with Dragons, and the fairy answers, "I am not opposed to all dragons. I am opposed to the Cult of the Dragon."

Brassik interjects that they were not members of Severin’s faction that misread Sammaster’s text on Dracoliches. In his telling, Tiamat or another Devil manipulated Severin into creating a summoning ritual for Tiamat, while Brassik's side believed in the original Dracolich vision of a new undead race of dragons that would transcend the petty differences of the different groups. Thistle, annoyed, calls it "academic nonsense", and Brassik, offended, insists it was about a contrasting view of the natural order. Unla Key asks, "Why is it Dracolich and not Lich?" Brassik's linguistic answer leaves Unla Key looking at him, bored, and Brassik, realizing he is losing his audience, pulls out his final map.

He describes the spirit mound of the Great Worm tribe inside a spacious cavern at the northeast end of the Valley of Khedrun: the ice-hung pillared entrance, walls too slippery to be climbed without gear or magic. Delsephine interrupts — "We do have such equipment for sale." — and Orsik mutters, "We’ll take two sets." The captain pockets the 40 gold, and crampons and other equipment materialize from under the hold. Brassik goes on to the slippery ice floor, the 70-foot-high ledges with their totem poles of chiseled ice carved like winged snakes, the caves of the Great Worm tribe warmed by steam vents, and the narrow crypts of the honored dead. He pauses to wonder whether he should keep helping with this assault on ancient artifacts and tribal norms, then remembers what he was paid for and continues. At the back of the cavern rises a triangular promontory, 120 feet above the floor at its peak, topped by an altar of frost-covered stone carved as a coiled serpent with great wings, representing Elrem, the Great Worm, to whom the tribal warriors make sacrifices in honor of Uthgar.

Orsik asks about the gong. Brassik offers two possibilities. The first is that it is the artifact itself: a circular disk 13 feet in diameter, once the shield of a frost giant champion, broken in combat and missing a large piece, weighing 250 pounds, made of red dragon scales bolted to a beaten copper frame. Given that all other artifacts were under the altars, he gives that theory little credence; the second theory "says that the gong is a gong." Orsik nods in agreement — "To the altar." — then tells his friend they should get the gong anyway: "We have a bong and a gong, which would be a great addition. And we could give it to Harshnag if it’s a shield." Brassik takes his notes below decks.

Orsik then approaches Delsephine about hiring Brassik into their show. Her eyes blazing behind her dark mask, she tells him, "He has a contract. But there is a payout clause." Klauth, she explains, is a necromancer, and the contract is for two lives, a living and an undead one; she values the living part at 300 gold and arrives at "1300 gold pieces". She is happy to negotiate for Klauth. Orsik probes what happens if Brassik dies or his body is desecrated ("we can use the parts in our necromantic constructs"), and learns that buying the living part releases Brassik from his obligations to Klauth, leaving him free to do whatever he wants. Orsik wants to meet this dragon; Delsephine will not say where he is, but promises that he will talk to and reward them once they have dealt with the giants.

Unla Key restructures the deal: rather than a straight-up cash deal, Delsephine will get residuals in the play they are running. She agrees, on the condition of "an upfront payment of 500 gold" with the residuals "capped at 6000." Unla Key shouts, "Done." He then tells Orsik that she does not know he signed a deal for the current play, and that their deal will not burden the sequel with her residuals. "It’s called show business." Thistle wonders, "Do we want to piss off a Red Dragon?" Orsik laughs it off: they will tell Bob to wind down the play so they can start a new one — "We have two gongs, and we’ll call it the Gong Show."

Later that day they arrive at the Great Worm Cavern. Members of the Great Worm tribe are getting ready to defend their totems; Unla Key and Vardis exchange a glance and incinerate them all with fireballs. The defenders are dead, the altar is theirs — and then the ground trembles, and a creature bursts from a snowbank, "a careening, blue blur of death," a beast like a mingling of worms, centipedes, and dragons, its dozens of legs hurtling it forward, wing-like membranes unfurling along its sides. With a screech it rises up on its many legs, mandibles clicking in ravenous anticipation.
