# Chapter 29  - Circles within Circles

Date: 5 to 23 Kythorn (the barge trip is not narrated) 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens mid-confrontation in Silverymoon. A fiendish half-human, half-demon spawn smiles at Unla Key and tries to charm the halfling, but fails as the diviner shifts the fate in his favor, and the creatures hiss their frustration. Orsik proposes a scheme to the mage: "We are going to exploit this town’s racism against undesirable races and run to the tavern we left Thistle and Vardis in and scream – ‘ungodly races’ and have the mob deal with them." Unla Key is delighted — "that is an overly complicated plan. I love it." — and the two embellish it until it involves a lightning bolt into the tavern blamed on the "Cambions," a pinned robbery, a lynch mob and murders. The Cambion, pleased to see the dwarven fighter still there, tries to charm him too, and fails.

Orsik and Unla Key run, with Orsik reminding the halfling not to hide because they want to be followed. Unla Key points out the flaw: "did you notice that the creatures can fly?" The Cambion keeps pace with the would-be thieves of House Margaster and strikes Unla Key with his spells. Badly wounded, having lost 2/3 of his hit points, Unla Key concludes, "This plan is not that good anymore." Orsik decides to run into a house for help, and the two bicker over whether a commoner would be any use against demons. Orsik barges into an unlocked house — the houses in Silverymoon are not locked, because only good races live there — and finds a sharply dressed halfling drinking fine wine in a candlelit, well-appointed drawing room. Orsik shouts, "DEMONS ARE CHASING US!" and the gentleman drops his glass. Unla Key shouts "Yes!" and is struck down by the other infernal creature.

The first half-demon follows Orsik inside and demands their master's treasure, offering that the master will forget this if it is returned. Orsik protests his innocence and appeals to the halfling. The Cambion that killed Unla Key shouts, "Found it." The one in the house offers terms: leave, say nothing, and leave the city by tomorrow, and his friend need not die. Orsik is too stunned to answer and only stares; the creature says "Very well" and nods to his friend, who kills the halfling — Unla Key, whom it had just described as almost dead (the chapter earlier says he was struck dead). Recalling that Unla Key had said the world would end if he died, Orsik looks at the sky — "Huh, I guess the world isn’t coming to an end." — then carries the body to the Dancing Goat. The tavern keeper demands to know what happened, and the reply comes back, "Impossible, such creatures do not exist in Silverymoon." Orsik glares and goes upstairs.

In the morning Orsik is stopped from carrying the corpse out. The tavern keeper, Nyalah Uuseff, in formal silk and gold, has asked the guard to investigate "these tales of ungoodly races being present!" The Silverwatch arrive to take Orsik to the city courts, and Orsik asks that his dead friend be raised to testify. The Captain, stunned at the idea of a murder by a demonic power, agrees and asks which temple Unla Key would prefer. Thistle finds Unla Key "unclear"; Orsik whispers "Cyric?", but Thistle recalls Uncle Joon saying that Silverymoon specifically banned Cyric. They settle on Mielikki, since Unla Key is a halfling, and at Her grove the high priest takes the body into the inner sanctum and returns the halfling "no worse for wear and lighter one magical ring." She tells the Captain the tale of the demon is true. Unla Key, asked about the world ending if he died, insists he knew he would be revived, and that "getting killed and having you raise me was a much better way."

The party is summoned to the Star Court. Thistle guesses that Lord Margaster is the judge, and Orsik and Vardis exclaim, "THOSE MARGASTERS?" In the court they face a judge and a beautiful, imperious woman in fine robes, flanked by two gorgeous men; the halfling whose house Orsik barged into sits as far from her as possible. The Judge asks Vinby Hearthgood for his account, and Vinby names the dwarf as the person who smashed into his house, to Unla Key's outrage. The Judge addresses Lady Margaster as Xamlyn, who wishes to reach an arrangement with the Dwarf and his friends. Orsik refuses to deal "with a literal devil," while Vardis warns that the Margasters are rich and powerful. When the court proposes magic spells and calls for the court magister, Xamlyn refuses the intrusion, recasts the affair as "a drunken party went wrong," and leaves with her men.

The Judge then turns on Orsik: he knows him as the one who did "that THING," cites Lady Margaster's sizeable investments, the city's need for the investments of the City of Waterdeep, the disaster of the War of the Dragons and the collapsed real estate market, and orders him gone by sunset. Unla Key asks whether they can buy some properties; Orsik wants nothing to do with the town. The Judge praises Thistle as "a credit to your kind," and Thistle flitters out declaring, "I’m done."

The group leaves through the Sundabar gate and heads south on Rauvin Road to Everlund. Thistle passes on Uncle Joon's view that Silverymoon betrayed the Silver Marches and their dwarf allies; Orsik first disclaims his ancestors' blood feuds, then, told "It was five years ago," puts Silverymoon on his shit list. They reach Everlund before sunset after an uneventful journey, passing between the Nether Mountains — orc tribes, Netherese legend, and blue dragons chased out of the northern Anauroch by a dragon known as the Doom of the Desert — and the Silverwood, once part of the High Forest and now scarred by loggers along the Evermoor Way. Everlund sits on the Rauvin River, walled, with five gates whose avenues run like spokes to the Bell Market.

At the gate a younger human guard asks whether the dwarf is Orsik; Orsik deceives him, and an older dwarven guard insists "Orsik was a dumbass" while this Dwarf is knowledgeable and thoughtful. The dwarven guard explains that Everlund's Council of Elders voted three to two to leave the Lords’ Alliance, condemning it for failing to aid Sundabar during the War of the Silver Marches, while Silverymoon "sat on its hands and did nothing," and says he wants to see Silverymoon "ground and burned into ashes." Thistle pulls Orsik away, reminding him that their mission is to go to Danivarr’s tavern, find a half-orc, and give him a platinum badge. Orsik says he is thinking of signing up.

On the main thoroughfare an older woman is warning attentive young men that the Kraken will return to destroy the city with its allies. She invites Orsik to join her group against the ancient evil for five gold pieces and a certificate declaring him "a fighter of the light!" The party is broke, having spent their last gold piece and badger pelt raising Unla Key, so she offers to waive the fee if they recover the star of light, an artifact of purely symbolic value stolen by kobolds and held in a cave a few miles out of town. She ignores Unla Key's spelling question and gives her name as "Miery – not Mary," after the goddess Mielikki her mother worshipped.

At the tavern, an old, prominent former noble's mansion now favoured by adventurers, the half-orc manager Dral Thelev greets them. Orsik presents the platinum badge, and Dral concludes that "Something must be amiss with my friend Darathra Shendrel." He pours his finest Silverymoon wine, which Orsik nearly spits out; Dral explains he enjoys drinking it precisely because the citizens of the good city would be appalled. He describes Silverymoon as living on the reflected glory of having one of the Silver Sisters as its founder, with the younger sister now the open Lord of Waterdeep and head of the Lords’ Alliance. At "Bottom’s up!" the heroes are teleported away.

They arrive in Moongleam Tower, greeted by a Turami man whom Vardis recognises as a Harper: Krowen Valharrow. He explains that Dral teleported them because a secret agent showing up at the tower's door would be a red flag to the Zhentarim and Lords’ Alliance, and that "Each faction is busy monitoring every other faction, and no one is paying attention to the giants." Vardis tells him the Ordning of the Giants has been sundered, cause unclear. Krowen offers help if they will find out and fix it, shows them a teleportation circle and hands them a map of circles for instantaneous travel through the North, which shocks Orsik since the Zhentarim have nothing like it. He adds gifts from Darathra for anyone who would take the quest — a coffer, "a birthday present for exactly such a moment" — and inside they find several spell scrolls. The chapter breaks off on a single word: "So?"
