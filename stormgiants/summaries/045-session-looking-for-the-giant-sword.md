# Chapter 45 - Looking for the Giant Sword

Date: 2 to 3 Eleasis 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
Thistle hated spiders — something about giant spiders confusing fairies and sprites with flies creeped him out — but the idea of having the halfling charge up ahead filled him with even more dread. So he fluttered ahead with his wing muffs through the dark tunnels until they opened into a large cavern covered in spider webs and holding three obviously giant cocoons. His verdict was a single "Yuch." and, staring at the mass of webbing, one word came to his head: "larder."

Orsik lumbered right after him and used his magic to burn through the webs to reach the cocoons, which he was sure must hold treasure. The first was the body of a goat that had been bled dry; the dwarf slashed through its belly hoping to find something and found nothing, muttering in disappointment, "But they eat everything!" The second cocoon held a dead dwarf. When Vardis shouted "Any treasure?" the answer came back "17 copper pieces, a deck of cards, and a set of dice." — to which the response was "Meh." From above, Thistle spotted that the third cocoon held a person who did not look dead, flew down, and handed her some berries.

Orsik introduced himself. The woman was a human clearly attired in the garb of a member of an Uthgardt tribe, and Orsik worked out that she belonged to the same tribe as the chieftain the party had killed — a detail he decided was not worth sharing. She gave her name as "Noori," and Orsik noticed her staring intently and with great hatred at Unla Key. She said she needed to get out and find her tribe; when she nodded that she had come to the temple with them, Orsik told her, "We found them trying to get in, and they were all dead." He smiled internally when she did not see through the deception. The group agreed to take her out of the warrens and back to the main temple, where they found Harshnag the Grim asleep on a bed, covered in rags, snoring loudly. Orsik warned Noori, "Be quiet; this giant can be very particular about people in his temple." Noori smiled at him and ran off.

The party went back through the crack in the wall, past the larder, following the right-hand rule. The tunnels led to an opening to the outside, a gap in the mountainside more than 400 feet above the valley they had come up the day before, with a steep and treacherous descent. Turning back, Orsik realised the caves were not part of the original construction: "These caves were formed when the mountain moved and cracked. Creatures have used these caverns as their homes." When Vardis asked whether he knew this sort of stuff, Orsik answered, "It’s a dwarf thing." Vardis was surprised: the Orsik he had known as a friend in Waterdeep was a gruff, dangerous, fun-loving tavern-goer with no interest in caves. He put it down to "More magic ring stuff,"

Vardis led on carefully through several intersections to another chamber, but before he could get his bearings Unla Key rushed in. His light revealed a single column supporting a domed ceiling and curtains of web stretching from the walls to a spider. Orsik struck twice with his green blade magic; the spider bit him, but his natural dwarven resistance blocked the poison. Vardis killed the creature with his bow, only for a pile of spider eggs to hatch and swarm. Vardis feared "Shit, the mage is going to be eaten alive," but the mage struck with a thundering clap and Orsik finished the creature. On the dead spider Vardis found a frozen potion of climbing and an old axe with a spectacularly beautiful sapphire.

Back in the room where Harshnag the Grim slept, Orsik poked the giant awake. Harshnag mumbled, "Haven’t slept in a proper giant bed in ages." The ensuing exchange turned into mockery: asked where he had been living, Harshnag said "Finding a giant-sized bed in the land of the little people is hard." Orsik asked "you’re homeless?", and the infuriated giant declared, "My roof is the stars, my walls are the trees, and my bed is the ground." Vardis doubled down, suggesting he could have built a house or slept in a hammock. "I don’t like hammocks." Harshnag tossed the pelt aside and headed north down the main hallway, peering into empty bedrooms and muttering, "Nice beds."

As Orsik caught up, an ominous thump sounded: every bedroom entrance had been sealed with magical walls of stone, and a massive 40’ diameter sphere of solid granite materialised and rolled south down the hallway. Harshnag smashed at a stone wall without breaking through, Thistle flew above the ball, and the rest of the 3HPP and the giant were hurt by it. The ball came back, slower; Orsik had everyone squeeze against the edges of the walls, and as it passed he saw what was moving it — the stone giant statue from the hallway’s south entrance, pushing it. The statue ignored the 3HPP, the ball vanished into the northern wall ("Magic."), the stone walls disappeared, and the stone giant walked back to its pedestal and became immobile. Harshnag, clobbered twice, proposed sleeping it off in one of the rooms, prompting Orsik's "Wasn’t he sleeping like 10 minutes ago?" and the question "Is he old?" Harshnag insisted, "I am not old by giant years. And a long time is long by your reckoning, not mine."

On the 6th day, Harshnag — who, the chapter says, really was old — woke from a very deep sleep in a giant-sized bed and room: "I haven’t slept this well in ages!" He leapt up shouting, "Let us find this giant axe!" The group went south past the immobile stone giant to stairs north of the doors to the temple of Annam, and at one of the little people's insistence climbed them. At the top a life-size statue of a female cloud giant in an angry mask, arms posed as if casting a spell, guarded another long hallway. Harshnag watched the group inspect it, expecting it to come to life; they decided there was nothing to worry about. The fairy stopped him from heading north: "How about I fly and avoid stepping on any pressure plates."

Thistle found rooms with two giant-sized beds, like the floor below, but here the northern end of the hallway had collapsed. In one room he found a giant bag containing a bong, a gong, and a waterskin, and laughed imagining a giant meditating, banging on a gong while he smokes from the bong. About to leave, he found a long, slender shard of ice roughly the size of a dagger, a rune symbol glowing within it. The chapter ends there.

## Locations
### The warrens
Natural caves, formed when the mountain moved and cracked, that creatures have used as homes. They include the web-filled cavern Thistle calls the "larder," an opening to the outside more than 400 feet above the valley, and the domed spider chamber.

### The main temple hallway
A hallway lined with bedrooms built for giants, with a stone giant statue on a pedestal at its south entrance. A trap seals the bedrooms with magical walls of stone and rolls a granite sphere down the hall. Harshnag sleeps in one of its rooms.

### The upper hallway
Reached by stairs north of the doors to the temple of Annam. A statue of a female cloud giant stands at its entrance; its rooms have two giant-sized beds each, and its northern end has collapsed.
