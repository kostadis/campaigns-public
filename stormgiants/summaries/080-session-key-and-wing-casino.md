# Chapter 80 -  Key and Wing Casino

Date: 29 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
Lord Khaspere Drylund was dead, and with his death came the darkest secret of the entire War of the Giants: King Hekaton was alive, and Orsik knew precisely where he was. All that stood between Orsik and the knowledge's use was getting out of the sewers, which meant getting out of the circular chamber, which meant getting through four Kuo-toa. The chapter's own verdict: "Easy-peasy."

A Kuo-toa in the water tossed a net at Orsik, but it fell harmlessly to the side. In the room at the end of the walkway, Thuul rushed out the door past the Kuo-toa trying to hold it shut, shrugged off their missed strike, and returned the favor with a blast of his breath weapon. Thistle spotted two Kuo-toa further down the ramp, cast a lightning arrow, and hit them both. Vardis, the cleric, rushed through and used his mace of terror; a field of powerful energy filled the room, and of the four Kuo-toa only one was unaffected. The affected three jumped into the water and submerged themselves as far from Vardis as possible.

The room had a second danger. At the north and south of the chamber were two valves, each with a Merrow beside it. The Merrow turned them simultaneously, and water rushed in from two pipes to the east; the room began to fill. The one remaining Kuo-toa Whip tried to net Thistle, who deftly avoided him. Unla Key used his telekinesis to open one of the drainage valves, slowing the flow. Orsik stared at the water and remembered his ring of swimming — " I never thought I would need this thing." — jumped in, swam south, reduced the Merrow there to 5 HP, and closed the valve with his superior strength.

Then the mysterious Oosith finally revealed himself: an aboleth. His first action was to dominate Unla Key. His attack on Thistle failed when Vardis used his powers as a light cleric to blind the creature. The three frightened Kuo-toa fled through the opened drainage hole. Thistle struck the Merrow to the north; it did not die, but the damage broke the aboleth's domination, and the creature, seeing how things stood, decided to flee. Vardis opened another drainage valve and the water level began to drop — whereupon the only Kuo-toa whip still in combat closed the valve Unla Key had opened.

The aboleth commanded Unla Key: "Destroy them!" Unla Key weighed his options. The chapter notes this was the perfect time for a spell he had not prepared. He thought of a fireball and discarded it. "I know," he said, pulling out his rock, the wand of wonder — and he became invisible. The aboleth shrieked in everyone's mind, "What the hell did you just do?!" Orsik and Vardis muttered, "Yeah, that's Unla Key for you." Orsik swam from the southern valve to the last Kuo-toa and killed him, and the aboleth shrieked, "The Kraken will have its revenge!"

With the fight over, Orsik stared at Lord Drylund's dead body and asked, "Hey, Vardis, can we raise him?" Vardis answered, "I am a light cleric, not a necromancer." Orsik groaned, "You can cast raise dead?" — "Oh, right." Vardis stepped close to the body and announced, "Like all Politicians, that's impossible. His brain is gone." Thistle observed that True Resurrection would work but would cost 50,000 gold pieces, and that was that. Thistle searched the body and found a ring with keys, 120 gold pieces, and three platinum pieces. Orsik muttered, "I want my Champagne du le Stomp, back." When Thistle wondered what to do with the body, Orsik shouted, "Take the gold, leave the body." Thuul picked the body up in his jaws and dropped it in the room; Thistle covered it and locked the door as best he could. A few minutes later the 3HPP emerge, covered in sewage, into the sunshine.

Outside, the party debated what came next. Thistle raised Lady Atalia: "There's a power vacuum, and someone needs to fill it." Orsik pointed out, "She wanted us to kill the Aboleth, and the Aboleth is very much not dead." Vardis, confused that his friend was so unwilling to run a trivial scam, sighed: "We'll lie?" Thistle then asked, "What about Pow?" Orsik liked it — "The COO. That sounds like an excellent idea" — and, delighted that his newfound intelligence let him entertain other ideas, asked, "What would we offer him?" Vardis and Thistle answered in unison: "Not to die." Orsik, realising his role was to help the team improve their ideas, objected that a threat would buy no loyalty once they left: "We are not going to stay here forever." Thistle suggested offering more; Orsik worried Pow could simply demand everything. Then Thistle joked, "What if we put the Casino in the Castle?" Orsik smiled, "Now that is a good idea." Shows were added ("We gotta have shows!"), and Orsik warmed to it: "A flying castle going around the world with live theater is a really good idea." Thistle red-teamed the proposal — would customers stay aboard indefinitely? — and Vardis answered, "It's like a cruise ship," adding that they could even let guests visit a place before the party went and pillaged it. Thistle remained unsure of the business model; Orsik pooh-poohed him: "Who could bankrupt a casino?" Thistle and Vardis stared at Unla Key. Thistle insisted that, once they had their plan, they talk to Lady Atalia first.

They found Lady Atalia at another fashionable cafe. Her smile at seeing the 3HPP evaporated when she learned the aboleth was not dead: "You let that thing escape? Damn you." She marched out, furious. When the party tried to follow, a small group of female rogues stopped them — "Lady Atalia is under our protection." The party claimed they had done just that, getting rid of "A sorcerer named Oosith," and the rogues, eyeing the very well-armed 3HPP, shrugged and said they would just follow. Outside her house, Lady Atalia snarled at Thistle in a very plebeian dock accent, "You were supposed to kill him. I don't want anything to do with you. We're done." Thistle shot back, "You are not going to become the new Waterbaron with an attitude like that." She warned that "The Kraken doesn't like it when anyone disrupts its plans," and that Drylund was going to come back. Orsik muttered, "I don't think he's coming back. But you knew that?" Lady Atalia sighed, "I would if I had drunk my potions. It's amazing how easy it is for people to read if they think you can read their minds." And the door was closed.

One of the Hands then turned to Orsik and offered to take "an equitable share of the Grand Dame, as well as an operating fee and a security fee." Asked how much, the Hand answered, "95% of the turnover and an annual security fee." The 3HPP nodded politely and headed for the docks, their stench clearing the crowds before them. On the way, Thistle cautioned again, "A flying casino will have a repeat business problem." Vardis brushed him off: "It's like a cruise ship."

At the Grand Dame they met Pow. Orsik explained the plan, and Pow, speaking with the accent of a citizen of Shou, was intrigued: "There is a tremendous opportunity here that I had not considered." He saw the flying ship landing in a city, opening up for gambling, and moving on — and noted, "The nature of our flying ship makes it possible for us to avoid any taxation and protection fees." Thistle added, "And there are kitchens and no monsters," suggesting the ice caves could be a winter wonderland. Unla Key set his price: "I am only going along with this if I get naming rights. I want this place to be called *Key and Wing Casino*." Pow shook hands with the halfling.

Thistle then flew off to Lord Drylund's room. He found no incriminating paperwork, no notes, and apparently no correspondence at all — which surprised him, since any organization needs some way to communicate. He did find some treasure, including an ornately decorated weapon with an octopus pommel. Before heading down, he suddenly remembered the crazy woman in Everlund who had warned about the Kraken — a member of a group called "Fighters of the Light," which the 3HPP had helped and, as a result, had become members of.
