# Chapter 35 - The Play

Date: 13 to 14 Flamerule 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary

Orsik's plan to run a play for profit is turning into a potential disaster. Standing across the road from The Talking Troll, he looks at the theater and mutters, "Looks nice." Thistle, annoyed, groans that the actor apparently bought the property, and Orsik is aghast: "Bought? Why did he buy the property? Couldn’t he have rented it? We are so going to need to take over this production."

Urgala, who had brought them the bad news, asks Orsik, "Let me get this straight: you made a deal with an out-of-work actor to put on a play and trusted him?" Orsik taps his Headband of Intellect and admits, "This headband isn’t working as well as I would like." For a moment he wonders whether the problem is that he has been listening to people with worse ideas. Urgala lays out the stakes: the play exists, Kaelen delivered it, and the buzz and the fame of the party's exploits have made the opening the event of the season. If the play earns a second night, they make the money they need; if not, "the Zhentarim and Hand of Yartar will get their pound of flesh." Her verdict is blunt: "The problem is the dialogue and story are a mess, the actors can’t act, and the set is a disaster." When Orsik asks whether they can delay the opening, she laughs heartily and says, "No." The party splits the work: Unla Key takes the script, Thistle the direction, Orsik the acting, and Vardis goes to collect the CGI.

Unla Key marches up to Kaelen, who greets him with "What an excellent script! I am so pleased!" The diviner shakes his head and says in an authoritative voice, "I have seen the future, and the future says this is going to be a disaster." Kaelen, whose confidence is poor on a good day, whimpers, "It’s that bad?" and rushes off with the script. His rewrite uses an archaic form of the common tongue that nobody would understand; Unla Key tells him, "It’s very authentic, and nobody will understand a word the actors are saying." Unla Key proposes a compromise of just enough archaic words — "Thou" and "Thee" instead of "You" — and Kaelen shouts, "BRILLIANT!!!" The cleaned-up script reveals the deeper rot: the protagonist's motivations are wrong and the play makes no sense. Unable to rewrite it all, Unla Key hits on a trick: "How about a play within a play about a bad play?" He builds an awful outer play around the real one, and Kaelen is ecstatic that this will "protect the existing investment." The ending remains terrible, and Kaelen is touchy about it, as if it matters more to him than the entire play. Unla Key manipulates fate to brainstorm a way through, and it works: Kaelen is pleased, the play has coherence, yet the story itself "seems oddly elusive."

Thistle meets the director, Everard Barney — an unimposing, heavy-set man in a multi-colored shirt and a purple scarf over his head, barely hiding a buzz cut and his enormous ears — who declares himself "completely committed to Kaelen's vision." Thistle answers, "Me too." Everard then sobs that Kaelen creeps him out, that the main actors won't listen, and that if this fails he will have to return to Waterdeep and be an accountant like the rest of his bookkeeper family. Thistle asks whether he has talked to his actors about what they want ("No?"), then studies the set and reads the lead actor, Amy: fluffy and joyful on the surface, status-driven underneath, and always answering "I don’t know, and I am not sure," as if hiding some mysterious past. Thistle advises that "For this lead actor, you must pander to their sense of being a star," and the two of them rework a soliloquy so the lead no longer sits on a pile of dung but stands on a hill above it — "Right, so they don’t make an association that I think Amy is shit." Thistle suggests making the drunken supporting actors' mistakes part of the play; Everard declines and resolves to scream at them louder in the morning. Thistle, who has dealt with Uncle Joon's alcohol binges for years, recommends buckets of water instead, and Everard says he will tell his own uncle about the water trick. As the day winds down Everard asks for ideas on the finale. Thistle stares at the incomprehensible staging, feels something is wrong, and then concludes the ending works great: "Change nothing."

Orsik decides the actors lack energy and physicality. Told by Thistle about Amy's needs, he considers being nice but mutters, "Terror is always the better option." He casts a spell to disguise himself as a human acting coach and introduces himself: "The name is Geoff Mamet." Amy questions whether Kaelen signed off and who is paying; Geoff-Orsik growls, "You are not here to ask questions," and Amy deflates. His shout of "GROW UP AND STOP ACTING LIKE A WEAKLING!" doesn't have the desired effect, and when Amy complains about the confusing lines and over-the-top gestures of the ending, he roars, "ENOUGH! You aren’t the director. You aren’t the screenwriter. Just deliver the lines, put some energy into it, and you’ll be fine." She meekly assents. Watching the rest of the rehearsal, he congratulates himself — "What a brilliant idea I had!" — then walks out, dispels the illusion, and walks back in, where Amy thanks "Mr. Orsik" for paying for Geoff Mamet, who "made all the difference."

Vardis goes to Urgala’s Inn to ask about the magical device. Urgala tells him that "Chancellor Geoffrey’s Invitation is what you need," a device Chancellor Geoffrey used to create elaborate invites with sound and animated visuals, and that it "won’t cost you anything." It lies south of Yartar in the Dessarin Hills, held by a tribe of Kobolds who pilfered it from one of Geoffrey's messengers without knowing what it is. Asked how she knows, she smiles: "I wasn’t always an innkeeper."

At the entrance of the Kobold Warrens, Vardis uses a scroll of disguise to become a kobold and follows dozens of footprints into a small three-foot cave and a warren of tunnels lit by giant fire beetles. Unable to speak the language, he shrugs at another kobold, which runs deeper into the caves; he follows past a chamber of dogs into an enormous chamber with a 20-foot-high ceiling, kobold-sized furniture, and a one-foot-high altar before a 15-foot-high monolith of rusting iron. The altar shows "the orc god Gruumsh strangling Tiamat," and he sees that Gruumsh is a new addition. Telling himself to "Focus on the mission," he presses into a cave strung with tripwires, reasoning that turning back would mean sacrificing their entire fortune — and he fails. A bell rings and kobold guards find him tangled; he plays a confused, hopelessly lost kobold and pulls it off, and a guard who will have to spend all day cleaning up the mess frees him and pushes him deeper.

He stumbles into what seems to be an open valley under a starry sky with the moon visible, then realizes it is an illusion projected by a ball with tiny, mirrored shards across its surface. "Gotcha." A large ogre sits beside it. Vardis walks up as the ogre shouts and waves his club, bows, and grabs the ball. When the ogre swings, Vardis ditches his disguise and uses his warding flare; the ogre, blinded, misses, and Vardis runs. At the tripwire cavern, the kobold guard who freed him disables the entire mechanism, and Vardis dashes past. Taking a wrong turn into a chamber of kobolds banging on the floor, with swarms of centipedes and a pack of kobolds closing in, he casts "Fireball." The blast incinerates his enemies and stops the on-rushing kobolds, and Vardis scrambles out carrying his mirror ball.

The following evening the play is a smashing success. Vardis, Orsik, Unla Key, and Thistle choose not to sit in the theater, spending the night in the Talking Troll ready to run or fight. They learn that a new character appeared at the end of the play: a demon-looking creature that introduced itself as Bob Saget and, using the effects of the CGI, cracked jokes that made everyone laugh. Thistle looks at the original play more carefully — "Damn." — and shouts, "This was a summoning ritual." The original ending was so confusing and bizarre because the theatergoers were meant as sacrifices to tempt the demon through the gate. "Well, we improved the play, so I don’t know?"

At the theater they find a demon in purple — purple top hat, purple formal jacket with tassels that seem almost alive, white gloves, two giant eyes with purple irises, jagged teeth, and purple skin stretched over a skull. The crowd should be terrified; the crowd is ecstatic. The demon introduces himself to Unla Key: "I am Bob Saget, my pronouns are he, him, and I am the tormentor of the kings who killed their jesters." He thanks them for arranging his escape from the Abyss. When Orsik asks, "So do we fight?" the demon answers, "Oh no, Orsik. I love this new job. I get to practice my material on a new audience. The Abyss is so - boring." Urgala tells Vardis, "WE HAVE A HIT! Everyone wants to come tomorrow to see it again." The 3HPP is delighted: they have their hit, a new star, and the coin will flow. Kaelen, however, is confused — he had planned the play as revenge on a theater-going public that never admired his creative genius, intending a demon to lay waste to the town and consume the theatergoers in hellfire. Now they admire him. He pauses and shouts, "I AM GOING TO BE FAMOUS!"
