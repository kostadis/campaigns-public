# Chapter 13 - Back to Parnast - 03-04-Tarsakh

Date: 3 to 20 Tarsakh 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens with a note addressed to the SEER from an unnamed agent who complains about how annoying it is to keep track of the party: "All the agents in the area are tasked to track this group of annoying individuals." The writer anticipates the SEER's reply — that this is the most promising group seen in these parts, that Parnast must not fall to the Zhentarim, and that "there is a tumult brewing in the North." The writer's own part is to ensure Parnast is part of the Lords’ Alliance and does not fall to the Giants, since its strategic location as "the last town before the Black Road is too important." Then comes the terrible news: "Orsik is a Zhentarim." The writer wonders whether any noble anywhere would have wanted him on their side, and closes: "The rest are free agents."

Meanwhile, just past the Marsh of Chelimber, the adventurers — carrying a full bookcase — debate the way back. Orsik asks Wallace for his counsel, which flusters him. Wallace lays out three ways to Parnast: the Graypeak Mountains, the Forgotten Forest, or Ambush Gap. Someone sums up the choices as "Scale the tall mountains covered in snow, dare the dangerous forest, or go through a place known as Ambush Gap." Everyone stares at Wallace, and he shouts, "Ambush Gap."

The journey is mostly uneventful until evening, when bugbears and goblins appear, well-fed, armed and ready to fight. The lead bugbear declares, "This is Bad Fruul’s territory, and you must pay a fee to continue unmolested." His second in command quibbles over whether Bad Fruul said "unmolested." Orsik bluffs that they have 10 gold and offers five; the bluff is weak, but Unla manipulates the fates and the gang leader is about to agree — until the goblins, outraged, scream at the bugbears and win the argument. The bugbears now want some of the party's fancy armor. Thistle protests, "But we had a deal," and Unla Key adds, "let’s be honest, you know how little gold we had." The second in command asks to be thrown a bone, "like a sword." Unla Key whispers to Orsik to try to convince them the marked cards are worth a lot, but Orsik's smooth tongue fails him and the bugbears grow angry. In the end Unla Key hands over the short sword he used on the Magmin and Orsik hands over the deck of cards. When the bugbear demands "two swords! One for the deal and another for tricking us!", Thistle offers the goblins goodberries, and the feeling of fullness leaves them much less interested in the ambush. The Chief accepts: "A sword, a deck of cards makes for a worthy haul. And you have paid appropriate tribute to Bad Fruul!" Asked where Bad Fruul is, and complimented by Thistle on how well the pass is guarded, the bugbear admits, "Look, we don’t actually meet Bad Fruul. Our Chief meets some other chief who then meets Bad Fruul."

Ambush Gap itself is a stretch of badlands at the foothill of the Graypeak Mountains, north of the Forgotten Forest. From above, the ground looks tiled; between the tiles run winding 5-10’ trails, said to have been formed by the rain event that created the Marsh of Chelimber, with tall 20’ stone walls on either side. On one of the tiles waits Grik, the Goblin chief, who tells his gang that Bad Fruul has ordered them to ambush anyone coming through the pass and has promised that anyone who comes up it "will be weakened by releasing poison!" Chief Grik hears the adventurers coming, "they are coughing and wheezing!" and announces, "This pass is under the control of Bad Fruul!" A fey indignantly shouts, "we already paid to pass through here!" Grik thinks "ugh, a fairy," and demands that they hand over all their equipment and treasure and leave naked, or be stripped and left to be eaten — "thus spake Bad Fruul!" The fairy remarks to the dwarf that this is "definitely not the deal we got from the other goblins." Thistle turns to Orsik: "I’m gonna get me some goblins."

The fight that follows is fought across the trails and tile-tops. Three goblins on a tile target Wallace; one hits. Wallace charges up the sheer cliffs and kills a goblin. Thistle's drake charges up to Chief Grik's tile and snaps the air, and Thistle's arrow misses everything. Wolves hidden further down a trail charge, missing the cleric but penetrating Orsik's armor. Vardis climbs onto a tile and uses burning hands, killing two of Bad Fruul's gang and badly wounding a third, who flees. Unla Key compares the position of the sun to a piece of the updated prophecy he was given, concludes that the end of the world isn't happening yet, and unleashes binding ice, killing two wolves and binding one wolf, the Chief and his henchman — but also hitting Thistle's drake. Orsik kills the frozen wolf and charges up the walls after the Chief. The two goblins on Wallace's tile flee. Grik interposes the last goblin beside him between Thistle's arrow and the drake's acid. Vardis kills a goblin on another tile with his bow. Chief Grik wisely avoids Unla Key's spells, only to be killed by Orsik. On the Chief's body the party finds a gold stylus and a note of orders from Bad Fruul: "Defend the pass with your lives! We will seize Parnast!"

On the 4th day of the 1st tenday of Tarsakh Thistle spots stones in a circle and advances quietly, telling Orsik, Vardis and Wallace to hang back, and sighs when Unla Key follows. The halfling explains, "If I don’t go to danger, the world might end. And I am feeling lucky today." It is a good thing, too: the halfling wizard finds an inscription in Fey that Thistle missed. Thistle understands that this is a gathering place for the Fey, where they would drink, eat and revel, and a portal between the Feywild and this place — though its magic has disappeared. He glares, "this is how Adimond Kroskas brought so many troops into the Feywild," and recalls that "Uncle Joon used to spin tales of how the North was a playground for the Fey." He is pleased to see signs of his home plane and sad to see "the decayed remnant of a gentler, happier age." The two-day journey on to Parnast is uneventful.

On the 6th day of the 1st tenday of Tarsakh Orsik heads to the Golden Tankard with the books in tow, but before he can get a drink, Hsing, the blind golden pseudo-dragon, appears. Orsik is furious. Hsing can feel the books' magical power. When Orsik suggests discussing them with the Wizard of the Crag, Hsing frantically insists the wizard is dead, wonders aloud, "did he become a Lich? Are we dealing with a Lich? I have to notify the SEER immediately," blinks out and back in, and returns with the correction: "Very well, I didn’t understand that you meant that Unla Key was the new Wizard of the Crag." At his whistle a covered wagon rolls up and two members of the Lords’ Alliance start loading the books. Orsik glares at them — the kind of men who kicked him out of every guard job he ever had, "Men dressed in too much armor that had not seen a blade in anger." When one says, "this is Lords’ Alliance official business," Orsik is about to start a fight, but Wallace steps in front of him: Parnast needs protection, his dad has secured help from the Lords’ Alliance, and although Wallace doesn't want to marry Elspeth, this is his hometown. The dwarf stands down.

Thistle asks the two men about the armor the party found that clearly belonged to the Lords’ Alliance; they stare at it dismissively and offer nothing. Pressed on whether it is an officer's armor, Wallace again heads Orsik off: "They are all rich bastards who wear blingy armor." Orsik laughs, "oh, you are all equal!" Wallace then grabs Thistle and hisses at him to stop waving the armor around, since it might belong to "some second cousin’s sister’s husband of these Lordies," or his dad might demand it as an offering. Orsik is pleased that Wallace is coming out of his shell, but can't resist trolling him: "what if Elspeth finds out?" Wallace takes the bait: "Elspeth? Shit. Why did we come here?" Hsing hands over 150 gold pieces. Orsik shouts, "I deserve more," and Hsing sneers that Orsik is not the kind to convince him to give out more than the SEER commands, then teleports away. Orsik's ax swings through the air: "I intentionally missed!"

Over the next two weeks the party settles into Parnast. The local militia is so pleased with Orsik's training that they ask him to join and train them further, offering free room, board, and two beers daily. It is the first time any guard has asked him to join, and he almost cries when they announce they want to be known as "Orsik’s Guards." While he trains them, a man in a dark cloak appears, saying Zuul Clovis told him Orsik wanted to join and had "the kind of moral flexibility that we look for." Orsik demands proof; the agent flashes his badge, and Orsik notes that "these can be faked, but let’s go on." The agent hands him a letter from "Your Family" to "Kin": they have lost contact with one of their relatives in the Forgotten Forest who was in service to Thornacious, a Royal Fairy Prince, and fear the Prince discovered his true purpose or that he was captured by local goblinoids. The agent explains, "Oblivillish is our agent in Prince Thornacious’ court. Find him." A day later Orsik shares this with the others. Thistle is troubled: the Prince is in exile, and Thistle's good friend Blasius, the Satyr, is part of his court. Reflecting on Fey courts, where princes and lordlings spy on each other to gain favor with Titania, he sighs that Prince Thornacious might even take a Zhentarim spy as a sign of prestige.

Unla Key buys a new sword from Calder the Halfling, whose shop is unaffiliated with Lionshield Coster; Calder mutters that "the Lionshield Coster are not welcome in Parnast." Unla Key spends the next week gambling at the Golden Tankard, losing half his initial stake yet curiously pleased, until a traveler accuses him of cheating. "But I lost!" Unla protests. "You should have lost everything!" Unla wonders whether the man is a merchant or a Zhentarim; Ragnar intervenes and the traveler leaves, staring at Unla Key as if to commit his face to memory. Thistle, meanwhile, challenges travelers to archery contests, wins more than he loses in the first week, gains a fan club of local children who call it "bowery," and in the second week wins against all comers. The children now accost any traveler with a bow: "I know someone who can beat you in a bowery contest!"

The final section, headed "Hsing Returns 10th day of the 2nd tenday of Tarsakh," finds a quiet day. Thistle is outside making trick shots for the children, Unla Key reflects on signs and portents of doom inside the Golden Tankard, and Orsik comes in exhausted from training. Ragnar calls them all to a back room — "You have a back room?" grunts Orsik — where Azam waits with Hsing. Orsik grabs his ax, but Wallace stops him again and explains the SEER: the Lords’ Alliance has two groups, the harmless Lordies and the SEER's people, "the spies, assassins, and cut-throats who know all and do all." "Make an enemy of the SEER; even the Zhentarim won’t protect you." He reminds Orsik that the SEER teleported a pseudo dragon into the middle of the Marsh of Chelimber, and asks him to do it for him and for his guards, since the alliance matters to his dad, who has been trying to get people to stop hating him "for the whole Cult of the Dragon." Orsik agrees but refuses to talk to Hsing, and goes outside with Wallace, asking Vardis, "and you, old friend?" When the door slams, Thistle turns to Hsing: "you gotta up your game. These quests aren’t worth the effort given the reward you have offered." The chapter ends there.
