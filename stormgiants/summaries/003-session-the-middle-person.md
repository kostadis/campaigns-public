# Chapter 03 - The middle person

Date: 1 Ches (evening) to 3 Ches 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary

The chapter opens under a section headed with Fiora Bendaega's name, in the middle of a job offer. "So, are you willing to take the job?" The fairy called Thistle stares at her and asks, "would this have anything to do with the Cult of the Dragon?" The answer comes back: "Would it help if I said yes?" Thistle grabs the note, and while he reads it, the middle person explains that she's but a middle person looking for adventurers for honest tradespeople working the black road. The middle person's attempt at empathy misfires: calling the Cult of the Dragon "a terrible tragedy" that was "terrible for business" only makes the fairy glare and answer, "not exactly helping me. I want revenge."

The middle person lays out the trade: "I connect people who need people with people who need jobs." The dwarf, cocking his head, decides the job looks like "a pretty sweet gig" and proposes, "I could take your job, and you can take the letter." The shocked middle person protests, "that is not how it works!" As the dwarf approaches threateningly, Fiora signals a guard, who advances growling, "are these guys giving you problems?" Vardis mumbles, "just having a conversation." Fiora whispers that "Diego is so fond of me" and calls him over to meet these fine folks. Fiora knows they will say yes, and when Orsik asks, "how long do they have?" the deal is closed: the merchant will not be there for three weeks, the journey takes two, so "You have a month, I would say." Fiora notices that Vardis remains dubious, pointing out, "you do notice that we poked the ghost?" When Orsik sees Diego reaching for his sword and greets him with "heya, Diego," Fiora winks to Diego, who backs down, and the group is told they can find the speaker at Nonnie's place: "I don't think you are ever going to be welcome here again."

On the 2nd day of the first tenday of Ches, Thistle squeaks, "Time to go to unload some of this stuff." The group visits the Lionshield Coster franchise in the town of Llorkh. The chapter describes the Lionshield Coster trading house as having storefronts everywhere and specialising in small towns and villages, and lists the rumours about it: a front for the Harpers, a Zhentarim operation, a cult front, perhaps Mammon's demonic power, or simply a trading house committed to making money, which "only the foolishly naïve believe." The franchise owner is an immaculately dressed man with a perfectly combed head of hair, a perfectly cut beard and freakishly white teeth, who announces in a gruff husky voice, "my name is Leo, and I am the proud owner of this franchise. I want you to know that I don't do credit for first-time buyers." Vardis asks whether buying something means they are no longer first-time buyers; Leo glares, and Vardis walks out, saying, "Diego wants to talk to me."

Thistle asks Leo for a scroll of dispel magic, priced at 300 gold. Thistle points out that the tube "has scuff marks, and the scroll is creased?" Leo is aghast: he has been nominated for the "Lionshield Coster best presentation award!" and fears the committee would remove him from consideration if it found out. Declaring "this is disgraceful," he sells it for 250 gold. Thistle also buys a new bow and new arrows before rejoining the group.

The group then finds an old, abandoned temple to Bane, built by the Zhentarim, who have since abandoned Llorkh. Its two shrines are to kinder gods, Chauntea, The Great Mother or Grain Goddess, and Tempus the Foehammer, the Lord of Battles. The chapter's italic passages describe Bane as representing ambition and control, Chauntea's faith as one of nurturing and growth, and Tempus's code of warfare, called Tempus's Honor. No regular priests attend the shrines, and Vardis sighs in relief: "won't be banned here."

Orsik brings up the Tower of Green Flame. Thistle and Unla Key stare at him as he explains that it is "rumored to be a residence of a mighty wizard that was destroyed during the Spellplague," that locals avoid it for fear of its arcane traps and wards, and that local children dare each other to go and touch it. Thistle's response is "not at night?" and Vardis and Orsik nod.

Orsik then goes to talk to Old Keller, despite Vardis warning, "I don't think he likes us." Orsik insists, "we dwarves stick together." At the stonemason's hut, Keller shouts, "Don't take a step closer." Keller accuses the group of "that thing in the dwarf's place of work, disgracing all dwarves in the process" - "The thing in the keep!" Orsik asks how he can redeem himself and is told, "Stop doing that thing! And when you are less likely to be goblin food, come back to me, and I can tell you what you can do to redeem yourself in my eyes."

At Nonnie's Place, a small inn for the few visitors to Llorkh who don't guest with the Iron Circle, the short, ruddy-cheeked Nonnie, proud of her vegetarian-only menu and insistent on being called "Aunt Nonnie," beckons the group to eat. Vardis mumbles, "nice to be treated like a human being." A woman at one of the tables says Nonnie "treats everyone like a human being. Even refugees like me." She tells them she comes from Greenest, a town to the west destroyed by the Cult of the Dragon, that she fled east to get away from the dragons, and that, hearing rumours of giants nearby, she is now going back west to see if her town is safe and to rebuild. Nonnie serves four cheese pies, returning 20 minutes later with pies "cooked to perfection, tasty beyond belief, and quite filling," and offers rooms that are "not the fanciest." As they head to bed, she thinks, "what a nice group of people."

The next scene is told from inside the wizard tower. An unnamed entity watches the group approach and decides, "I suppose the usual wards will do." Half the group keeps advancing: "the dwarf and the elf are still approaching." The elf touches the handle, and the entity is pleased that the dwarf and elf are hurt but not dead, remarking to no one in particular, "They are still alive; that should be a good enough warning for now." As the group walks away, it realises with sorrow, "the door can now be opened."

The group then makes the two-day hike to the third treasure trove that Veradaa Storr had pointed to, interrupted only by a druid collecting herbs. Her notes lead them along a narrow path halfway up a mountain in the northern Graypeaks, with a cliff on one side and sheer mountain on the other. "No way in," shouts Orsik; "Must be a way in," shouts Vardis. Thistle and Vardis find a pair of secret doors hidden in the rocks. Through the southern one, Thistle flits ahead down an unlit natural tunnel, warns Vardis to "be careful of the pit trap," finds another trap down the other branch, and rants at the dungeon makers: "Really, they have no imagination. The same pit trap, everywhere." He works out that the group can safely cross the weight-triggered trap as long as he flies across, then leads with a "Right-hand rule."

The tunnels end in a large ice-covered chamber where Thistle spots two large figures: "Frost giants!" Each dead giant clutches a bloody great ax and appears to have died from slashing wounds made by large bladed weapons. Vardis notices a diamond on a three-foot-tall pedestal, gleaming with an inner light that is "definitely supernatural," and shouts, "Unla Key use the scroll we bought." The diamond rises from the pedestal and beams of dazzling green light bathe everything in a sickly green glow. At Vardis's urgent "NOW, Unla Key," a magical hand streaks from Unla Key and slaps the diamond, to no effect. Unla Key then sees three masses of translucent green energy, pulsing in rhythm with the diamond and connected to it by energy, which move towards the group lashing out with barb-tipped tentacles. He shouts that "every time they lash out, the synchronized pulsing of the creatures and the diamond grows brighter!"

Vardis shouts, "destroy the diamond." Thistle objects: "that's a pretty expensive diamond; how about not." The two bring one creature to near death, but when the killing blow lands, the gem glows. Unla Key concludes, "we have to destroy the diamond." To Thistle's "would a blanket work?" he answers "No!" and spreads his magic missiles between the diamond and the creatures, barely damaging the diamond. The creatures strike back until only Orsik and Unla Key are alive, but that is enough: the diamond is destroyed, and the creatures with it. As the last creature dissipates, the diamond dust swirls into a green-tinted spirit that whispers, "Your companions want to kill you and take your possessions! Kill them before they kill you!" The group shrugs, picks up the treasure the giants left behind, and collects the diamond dust.
