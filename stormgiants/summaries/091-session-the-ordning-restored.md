# Chapter 91 The Ordning Restored

Date: about 13 Uktar 1492 DR; epilogue about six months later, about Mirtul 1493 (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-15; recorded 2025-04-02)

## Summary
The dust of battle had barely settled over the scorched sands of the Anauroch Desert when King Hekaton surveyed the fallen bodies of the rebellious giant lords and smiled. At Iymrith’s lair, at the northern end of the desert, he declared the giant civil war over and announced a new era of peace between giants and the humanoid peoples of Faerûn. He reaffirmed the traditional hierarchy of the Ordning to the remaining fire, cloud, and frost giants. Not every giant faction had participated: the hill and stone giants had not appeared, and the party was reminded that defeating the assembled leaders had not meant killing every giant enemy.

Hekaton thanked the adventurers for their alliance and welcomed future contact through the magical conch they already possessed. He also acknowledged unfinished business involving the Seer, the Lords’ Alliance, and the free peoples of the North. He intended to speak with the Seer eventually, although his reluctance suggested that he was in no hurry to negotiate with her. Finally, he gifted the party the dragon hoard beneath their feet. The party immediately appreciated the political convenience of giving away someone else’s treasure: accepting it made them indebted to the storm giant king.

Before departing, Hekaton exchanged a heavy look with Mirran. Her past betrayal would require a long and awkward discussion on the journey home. Serissa was excited to return, but lingered to suggest to Orsik that perhaps something could have worked out between them, before observing that she was too tall for him. She left the possibility open if he changed his mind; Orsik said goodbye. The giant factions dispersed, leaving the party alone at the lair.

Laela arrived delighted to find them alive. As Thistle tried to explain the intervening adventures—including a flying castle and a casino—Orsik enthusiastically credited him with saving everyone. He described Thistle as the brains of the operation, praised his elaborate planning, and later insisted that Thistle had produced their theatrical show himself. Laela was thoroughly impressed.

Her own story was uglier. Another dragon had burned an entire caravan while she was searching for Thistle, and Iymrith had become convinced that Laela was responsible and could transform from a fairy into a dragon. That mistaken conclusion led to torture, experiments, and demands that she demonstrate the transformation. Laela described having to practice patient breathing exercises. Thistle connected the experiments to what Iymrith had done to the party previously and mentioned the dragon’s claim that Titania was somehow involved.

Laela also revealed that Uncle Joon had found her, claimed to know where Thistle was, and worked to bring him to the lair for a spectacular theft. He needed Thistle’s help to steal something extraordinarily valuable that important people were seeking; he presented it as the score of several lifetimes. Thistle dismissed it as another get-rich-quick scheme. The party confirmed that Joon had been destroyed in a pit containing fire, acid, and poisonous gas. Only True Resurrection could bring him back, and nobody considered that a worthwhile use of the spell.

Thistle initially suggested questioning Thornacious about Titania’s involvement, but Laela proposed a vacation lasting a few months instead. Thornacious and Titania could wait. Looking at the defeated giants, she was confident that Thistle could handle anyone foolish enough to threaten them. The conversation dissolved into mutual enthusiasm over wing muffs, prompting Orsik to conclude that the two were made for each other.

Orsik’s retirement began with a letter from Wallace inviting him to visit Parnast. Orsik realized that Wallace had known him before the Headband of Intellect changed him. He decided that the headband had done its job. Now a hero with more treasure than he needed, he would discard it permanently, placing it in a bag of holding and tearing the bag apart to send it into the Astral Plane. He wanted to remove even the temptation to wear it again.

Without the headband, Orsik’s intelligence returned to six, ending his life as an artificer and elaborate tactician. He planned to live near Wallace as a farmer or perhaps serve on the city guard. Vardis welcomed the return of his old table-smashing, beer-drinking companion and accepted that he would once again be the more intellectually capable member of the pair. Orsik admitted that his cleverer self had become unpleasant. He invited Vardis along and left the flying castle to anyone still interested in reclaiming it.

In Parnast, the Seer arrived to praise their service. After trying unsuccessfully to explain matters to Orsik, she addressed Vardis: the giant crisis had been averted, the North had been served, and Parnast would always be a privileged member of the Lords’ Alliance. She promised to address Orsik’s outstanding troubles and ensure he was remembered as a hero of the realms rather than a villain. Unla Key, however, had disrupted some of her other plans; that conversation belonged with the wizard.

The Harpers likewise treated their decision to support the four adventurers as a masterstroke. Krowen Valharrow, their 18th-level mage contact, claimed the success, while past betrayals were handled through the convenient fiction that the headband-wearing traitor was dead. A Harper agent near Parnast was assigned to check periodically that Orsik and Vardis were not doing anything disastrous. Zhentarim agents also visited, expecting the clever, manipulative Orsik they remembered. Vardis explained that the Headband of Intellect had been lost, and the agents departed disappointed. Silverymoon recognized fairies among its goodly races, and the party also learned that their preferred candidate had won an election in a city whose name was not recalled.

The North nevertheless suffered. Over roughly six months, a barbarian horde attacked Everlund and the surrounding countryside. Silverymoon debated the matter and ultimately sent aid. Villages and farmland burned, soldiers and other people died, forests were destroyed, and some settlements the party had known ceased to exist. The raids eventually subsided: the horde was pillaging rather than establishing a government or conquering territory. The DM left open whether a new leader or a larger war might emerge from the violence.

Lord Zymorven died defending his hall, killing several attackers before falling, and the building burned to the ground. His son was not confirmed dead; the DM explicitly distinguished the father’s last stand from the son’s fate. The party’s victory had brought peace, but its consequences had also created destruction and further adventures elsewhere.

Bob had been killed in the battle with Iymrith and returned to the Abyss with an unexpected reward: credibility among demons. The party recalled that he had absorbed approximately 260 hit points of punishment, could not be subjected to Iymrith’s lightning breath, and drew her fury away from them. His intervention had humiliated the dragon and proved essential to the victory. Bob remained conflicted: as a demon he should hate the party, but they had done him a good turn. He resolved the uncomfortable possibility of owing them a debt by deciding that he owed them nothing.

Months after securing the artifact from Iymrith’s lair, Unla Key discovered that Knazreponnium Ketternoppapux—KP—had taken it using Time Stop. A Staff of Power, a polite explanatory note, a recruitment card, and an interplanar trade proposal remained. KP admired Unla Key’s nineteenth-dimensional chess: abandoning his allies at critical moments, entering the pit, and following the red dragon had brought him to the valuable prize. Unla Key had not actually caught the red dragon, but KP approved of the maneuvering nevertheless.

Research revealed that KP was a legendary figure in interplanar economics, a powerful wizard living in Sigil whose particular obsession was obtaining data about interplanar trade. Valint and Balk’s storage and transportation technology had supported commercial enterprises such as Lionshield Coster. But a merchant had discovered that combining interplanar storage devices could turn them into weapons and had begun selling them for that purpose. Powerful entities were deposited in the Astral Plane, and mind flayers recognized a way to attack enemies without the treaty complications of invading their worlds.

Threatened by the mind flayers and Githyanki, Valint and Balk agreed to take its manufacturing artifact offline while KP modified it. The component from Iymrith’s hoard was needed for that work. His safer devices would resist being nested inside one another and would report what was transported and between which locations, supplying the research data he wanted. The party also learned that KP had written the relevant regulations.

KP had failed to negotiate the component’s release from Iymrith and could not agree on a price with the Seer. He then dealt with the red dragon, which was reluctant to confront the legendary Iymrith. Orsik’s determination to fight the dragon made the opportunity possible, with an arrangement intended to keep Hekaton from interfering while the dragons fought. The red dragon’s anger reflected both that interference and the considerable money attached to the artifact.

Unla Key accepted the Staff of Power as a reasonable exchange. He also discovered that the Kraken Society had captured the flying castle, previously converted into a flying casino, before becoming distracted by a dandelion. His claimed knowledge of how the world would end remained unarticulated: he knew that he knew, but could not explain what he knew.

The campaign ended with peace restored to the Ordning and numerous unresolved threads: the Kraken Society, the castle, mind flayers, Red Wizards, Orsik and Vardis’s missing map, a possible wasting disease, and KP’s work in Sigil. The players discussed future adventures, favoring a smaller region and recurring characters after a campaign that had begun in February 2022. They agreed to take a month away and reconvene on May 6. The next campaign would use the 2024 rules, with no new book purchases required through the group’s shared access. Orsik’s story was finished; whatever came next would begin with new possibilities.

## Locations
### Iymrith's Lair
Located at the northern end of the Anauroch Desert, this site served as the final battleground where the giant lords were defeated, and where the party reunited with Laela. A vast dragon hoard lies beneath it.

- Hekaton announces the restoration of peace here before the surviving giant factions depart.
- The hoard includes the component needed for KP’s modifications to Valint and Balk’s manufacturing artifact.
- The deadly pit associated with Uncle Joon’s destruction contained fire, acid, and poisonous gas.

### Anauroch Desert
The vast, arid region in the North where the party confronted the dragon Iymrith and the giant leaders in the campaign’s climactic battle.

- The party explicitly places the lair at the desert’s northern end while reviewing its location on the Faerûn map.

### Parnast
A town where Orsik’s friend Wallace lives. Orsik intends to retire here to a life of farming or city guard duty. The Seer visits the party here and grants the town privileged status within the Lords’ Alliance.

- Wallace’s invitation supplies the immediate reason for Orsik’s return.
- Vardis is invited to accompany Orsik.
- Harper oversight and visits from disappointed Zhentarim agents follow the retirement.
- Its distance from the barbarian destruction initially limits what the retired adventurers hear firsthand.

### Everlund
A city in the North that suffered a brutal attack by a barbarian horde, resulting in burned farmland, destroyed villages, and significant loss of life before Silverymoon intervened.

- Silverymoon’s decision to help follows debate.
- The wider raiding period lasts roughly six months before conditions settle.

### Silverymoon
A city that recently added fairies to its list of recognized goodly races and sent military aid to Everlund during the barbarian invasion.

- The party treats fairy recognition as a meaningful improvement and recalls that cambions are already recognized there.

### Zymorven Hall
The ancestral home of Lord Zymorven, which was burned to the ground during the barbarian raids. Lord Zymorven died here defending it.

- He fought to the last and killed several attackers.
- The hall’s destruction does not establish that his son died; the DM explicitly leaves that distinction intact.

### The Abyss
The demonic home plane to which Bob has returned after the battle with Iymrith, now enjoying a fearsome reputation for having faced the legendary dragon.

- The DM corrects a suggestion that Bob might be encountered while searching the Astral Plane for Orsik’s discarded headband.
- His improved standing among demons complicates his anger toward the party.

### Sigil
The City of Doors, where the legendary wizard and researcher Knazreponnium Ketternoppapux currently resides and conducts his interplanar trade research. Mentioned as a possible destination for future adventures.

- The confrontation over Valint and Balk’s dangerous technology led to a meeting and negotiated settlement here.
- Unla Key would need to visit KP in Sigil to investigate his work further.
- KP’s recruitment card offers a potential route into that future adventure.

### The Astral Plane
A realm where powerful entities were accidentally dropped due to the misuse of interplanar storage devices, triggering the conflict between Valint and Balk, the Mind Flayers, and the Githyanki.

- Once the devices’ destructive interaction was understood, a merchant deliberately marketed them as weapons.
- Orsik sends his Headband of Intellect here by destroying the bag of holding containing it.
- Spelljammer is discussed as a possible way to explore the Astral Plane in a future campaign.

- He knew Orsik before the Headband of Intellect, a fact central to Orsik’s decision to remove it.
- During the campaign discussion, Wade singles Wallace out as a character he remembers and loved.

### The Seer
A powerful and dangerous figure who manages the interests of the Lords’ Alliance. She visits the party in Parnast, praises their service, grants Parnast privileged status, rehabilitates Orsik’s reputation, and notes that Unla Key’s actions disturbed some of her plans. Hekaton warns she is dangerous to make deals with.

- She initially addresses Orsik before shifting the explanation to Vardis.
- She promises to take care of Orsik’s remaining troubles.
- KP attempted to negotiate with her for the artifact but could not agree on a price. Her immediate concern was the Ordning crisis.
- Her objection to Unla Key’s conduct remains an unresolved matter for the wizard.

### Krowen Valharrow
An 18th-level mage and Harper contact who considers the party’s mission a smashing success, overlooking past betrayals through the convenient political fiction that the headband-wearing traitor was dead.

- He views backing the four adventurers as a vindicated strategy.
- Harper approval still includes periodic surveillance of Orsik and Vardis near Parnast.

### Bob
A demon who proved vital in the battle against Iymrith by absorbing massive amounts of damage and drawing the dragon’s fury, preventing her from focusing on the party. He has returned to the Abyss with significant street credibility, though he remains conflicted about his relationship with the party.

- The DM estimates his contribution included absorbing approximately 260 hit points of damage.
- Iymrith could not use her lightning breath on him.
- His humiliation of Iymrith has given him a reputation beyond his bad dad jokes.
- He avoids acknowledging a debt to the party by deciding none exists.

### Lord Zymorven
A noble who died a hero’s death defending his hall against the barbarian horde, killing several attackers before falling. His hall was burned to the ground.

- He fought to the last.
- His son’s death is not established; the DM specifically distinguishes the son from the father.

### Knazreponnium Ketternoppapux
A legendary wizard and researcher obsessed with interplanar economics and trade metrics, residing in Sigil. He used Time Stop to swap the party’s artifact for a Staff of Power, having arranged the red dragon’s involvement to secure the component for his research into safe interplanar storage devices.

- Commonly called KP, he regards the exchange as a reasonable trade and leaves courteous correspondence.
- He offers further employment through a recruitment card.
- His research interest is understanding commerce: the new devices would disclose their contents and transport routes.
- He wrote the regulations his modified devices are meant to satisfy.
- Failed negotiations with Iymrith and the Seer preceded his arrangement with the red dragon.
- He admires Unla Key’s choices as nineteenth-dimensional chess.

### Unla Key
A wizard in the party who secured the artifact from Iymrith’s lair through what Knazreponnium called “19th dimensional chess,” only to find it swapped for a Staff of Power. He is left with Knazreponnium’s interplanar trade proposal and the knowledge that the flying castle has been captured by the Kraken Society.

- Several months after securing the artifact, he discovers that it has been exchanged.
- His curiosity leads him to research KP’s reputation and residence in Sigil.
- He did not catch the red dragon despite following it.
- His attention ultimately wanders to a dandelion.
- He claims to know how the world ends but cannot explain the knowledge.

## Items
### The Conch
A magical item the party already possessed, allowing travel to Maelstrom; Hekaton welcomes future visits.

- Hekaton presents continued access to him as part of his thanks and offers assistance if the party needs anything.

### Iymrith's Hoard
A massive collection of treasure located beneath Iymrith’s lair in the Anauroch Desert, gifted to the party by King Hekaton as a reward for their heroism—described as more wealth than they could ever possibly spend.

- The party jokes about Hekaton gaining their gratitude by giving away another creature’s possessions.
- Orsik cites his wealth as a reason he can retire rather than pursue further quests.

### Headband of Intellect
A magical item that granted Orsik immense intelligence, transforming him from a simple fighter into an artificer and tactical mastermind. Orsik chose to discard it by placing it in a bag of holding and sending it to the Astral Plane, returning to his original self with an intelligence of six.

- Orsik destroys the bag to prevent future temptation, rejecting suggestions to sell the headband or leave it with Vardis.
- Wallace’s acquaintance with his earlier self helps motivate the decision.
- The headband’s loss also explains the Zhentarim’s disappointment and supports the Harpers’ account of the former traitor’s disappearance.
- During the rules discussion, the group acknowledges that using augmented ability scores to qualify for additional classes had been a permissive ruling made for enjoyment.

### Wing Muffs
Specialized items that Laela and Thistle enthusiastically discuss at their reunion; ownership is not clear from the exchange.

- Their discussion includes the difficulty of obtaining them locally.
- The shared enthusiasm reinforces the party’s impression that Laela and Thistle suit one another.

### Staff of Power
A powerful magical staff left by Knazreponnium Ketternoppapux as a reasonable trade in exchange for the artifact he took from Unla Key using Time Stop.

- The DM identifies the replacement explicitly after explaining KP’s research.
- Unla Key is described as considering the exchange reasonable.

### Interplanar Storage Device (ISD)
Magical containers—classical examples being bags of holding and portable holes—produced by the merchant firm Valint and Balk. Their misuse as weapons by combining them caused a crisis with the Mind Flayers and Githyanki. Knazreponnium Ketternoppapux’s research aims to create safe, regulated versions with built-in safeguards and trade-tracking metrics.

- Safe devices resist being placed inside one another.
- Existing storage and transportation technology supported businesses such as Lionshield Coster.
- Mind flayers saw the weaponized versions as a means of attacking enemies without invading planets in violation of treaties.
- The proposed devices would report their contents, origin, and destination, supplying KP with research data.
- The table jokes about self-manifesting devices that export their own cargo manifests.

### Knazreponnium Ketternoppapux's Proposal
A document found by Unla Key alongside the Staff of Power, detailing Knazreponnium’s plan for interplanar trade management and the introduction of safer, regulated interplanar storage devices for the merchant firm Valint and Balk.

- Its intended result is renewed trade without threatening the interplanar balance of power.
- KP’s accompanying correspondence includes a card for adventurers interested in further work.

### The Artifact
A rare magical component found in Iymrith’s lair that KP needed to modify Valint and Balk’s separate manufacturing artifact for safer interplanar storage devices. Knazreponnium Ketternoppapux orchestrated an elaborate series of events to obtain it, ultimately swapping it for a Staff of Power while Unla Key was unaware.

- KP had known of its presence in the lair for some time.
- Direct negotiation with Iymrith failed, and negotiations with the Seer broke down over price.
- Its precise identity and further details remain material for another adventure.
- The manufacturing artifact was taken offline under the negotiated settlement pending KP’s modifications.

## Spells
### Death Ward
A protective ward mentioned as being active on Orsik during the final battle, ensuring he would not easily fall in combat.

- Before the epilogue begins, Wade answers the prospect of Orsik’s demise with, “No, he's got Death Ward on him.” Then: “He'll never die.”

### True Resurrection
Mentioned as the only spell powerful enough to potentially bring Uncle Joon back to life, given the total destruction of his remains in the pit of fire, acid, and poisonous gas—though the party considers it definitively not worth casting.

- Orsik emphasizes that there is no trace of Joon left.
- The party treats his death as settled and jokes that they would simply kill him again if necessary.

### Time Stop
A high-level spell used by Knazreponnium Ketternoppapux to freeze time and seamlessly swap the party’s artifact for a Staff of Power without being detected, leaving only a polite explanatory note behind.

- Several months after Unla Key secures the artifact, he discovers the exchange.
- The replacement and documents are discovered where he had stored the original.
- The accompanying materials include the interplanar trade proposal and a card offering future work.

### Goodberry
A simple healing spell mentioned humorously—one of the party had a goodberry with Orsik’s name on it, intended not as a revive but as a snack to keep him alive through the campaign’s final stretch.

- Thistle says, “I had a goodberry with Orsik's name on it.”
- Vardis’s reassurance extends beyond surviving the battle: “I want to see you live out your happy days on a farm.”
