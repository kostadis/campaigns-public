# Chapter 79 - Hekaton's location revealed at last

Date: 28 to 29 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
Aboard the ship, Orsik looks at the presumptive Waterbaron, Lord Drylund, and smiles: "Getting information from him will be easy." He shows Lord Drylund the golden goose coin that Queen Serissa had given the 3HPP in the Maelstrom, a coin found on the body of one of the assailants who had murdered her mother, Queen Neri — a murder that would lead to the unraveling of the Ordning and the disappearance of King Hekaton. Lord Drylund acts unimpressed: "Yes, that's one of ours. I know it's worth ten gold pieces, other than that …" Thistle is unimpressed with Orsik's line of questioning and muses, "Why is one coin different from another?" But Orsik was trying to throw Lord Drylund off his game, and he succeeds; Drylund volunteers, "On this ship, the coin is worth ten gold; off, it's worthless. But you knew that. Why do you ask about it?"

Orsik explains where the coin was found and why they are asking. Lord Drylund walks to the aquarium, picks up his Octopus, and pets it. Thistle cannot figure out his game, but Unla Key does, and signals that Lord Drylund is lying and is worried. Orsik speaks reassuringly — "Why are you worried? We're all friends." — and Thistle flies up to Drylund to echo, "We are all friends." Drylund mutters that he is not nervous, and that "stories of murder and death of Queens and Kings of Storm Giants worry me. Imagine if they thought I was responsible." Even Thistle can tell he is lying. Orsik presses with the verbal blade, offering support in the upcoming election and noting that they are supposed to meet Drylund's associates later. Drylund splutters, "I will have nothing because I know nothing. We have many customers, and one must have left with a coin in their pocket." When asked whether he minds them asking around, he answers, "Ask!" As they leave, Orsik asks where they meet tomorrow, and is told, "Ah yes, near the Waterbaron's palace in the central market." Thistle whispers, "Isn't that where they tried to kill us last time?" Orsik only smiles: "Very well."

The night flies by. Unla Key wanted to gamble, but the free booze got in the way. Leaving the boat in the early dawn hours at the Yartar Docks, the party notices Lady Atalia seemingly waiting for them. When they try to follow, she moves away discretely — but not so discretely that she cannot be followed — and sits down at a tavern, where the 3HPP join her table. She whispers to the dwarf, "Mr. Orsik, please sit down," recommends a drink that is "perfect for getting rid of the effects of alcohol," and, staring at Unla Key, says, "He will need five." When Orsik answers her question about what information he wants with a smiling "You know," she tells him she knows he wishes to learn whether "your fake identity as your father is working out." Infuriated at the invasion of privacy, Orsik gruffly tells the group, "Someone else has to do the talking."

Thistle takes over: "We want to know the connection between the Grand Dame and King Hekaton." Lady Atalia laughs that she completely misread them; she thought they were looking for Lord Artan's kidnappers — "A young lord of Yartar who was kidnapped." Orsik interrupts that this is "a very parochial concern," that they know about Lord Drylund's kidnappings and assassinations and do not care, and that they are interested in the connection between the Grand Dame and the disappearance of King Hekaton. Thistle notices she looks anxious and, probing deeper, sees that she, like Drylund, is trying hard to deflect away from King Hekaton. She offers, "I can't help, but I know someone who could. An evil spell caster named Oosith."

Vardis objects to her framing: "Evil is very binary. And it's pretty judgmental of you. I mean, what if there is some underlying childhood trauma?" Lady Atalia, taken aback to hear this from a cleric of Corellon, unburdens herself: Oosith has been running the operation to kidnap lordlings, and she has been blackmailed into helping; Lord Drylund figured her out and "insisted that I help. I had no choice." Vardis replies, "Yes, it's amazing how when our lives are on the line, we have no choice." Orsik adds, "It's like killing a Vampire by making a deal with Lich," and when that draws a protest, insists, "It's just an example. She doesn't know what we are talking about, do you?" Sensing there is more than she wants to know, Lady Atalia says she has no idea. Asked about Oosith, she says that he is "in the sewers to avoid being caught by the Hand or the Shields of Yartar. Fish People protect him." Thistle mutters, "Kuo-toa." Reaching out to Vardis, she says she knows where he is, that killing him would be a great favor, and that "I am not an evil person. I was blackmailed. And I do so desperately want to go back to the light!" Vardis groans inwardly at the "light" and "dark" motif of morality, is tempted to deliver a full-throated sermon on it, and decides not to. Instead he tells Orsik, "I think we learned everything we needed to. We have another meeting to go to." Lady Atalia stares — "You won't help?" — and the 3HPP walk away.

Unla Key, now sober and rested enough to memorize his spells, is pleased with the tavern's drink and determined to learn the recipe. In the market, Lord Drylund appears and introduces the merchants: Tapouach, "who sells the best apples," Tapuz, whose oranges are impressive for their sweetness, and Mitbach, "where anyone buys goods for their kitchen." Unla Key lifts Tapouach's purse while Drylund talks, finds only 23 coppers — "This guy is so poor. That was a waste of my time." — and steals an apple as well, eating it as Drylund speaks. Tapouach notices, but realizing Unla Key is a friend of the Lord, he smiles.

Orsik asks openly about meeting "a sorcerer named Oosith perhaps, who specializes in kidnappings?" Lord Drylund, who speaks with the characteristic h's and gargled r's of the Calishites, is shocked: "We do not discuss this here. We use code. Oosith is my campaign manager in charge of special persuasion operations." When Orsik adds that Oosith is in the sewers, Drylund angrily demands how he knows; Vardis answers before Orsik can, "Because we are equally connected." Drylund calls it "an unacceptable security risk." Puzzled by the phrase "Campaign Manager?", Orsik gets a laughing explanation: "It's essential to appeal to what the voters value. They value their children, and I have them. If they wish to receive them, they will vote for me." Orsik tells him he needs a better campaign manager, and Drylund agrees to take them to Oosith.

He leads them deep into the sewers of Yartar — tunnels roughly 8 feet high, with a trench of flowing sewage in the middle and a pathway on either side — until they open into a large area where a Kuo-toa appears. Thistle, who notes that "Spend enough time with the Cult of the Dragon, and you will learn undercommon," translates: the Kuo-toa is unhappy because only the emissary is supposed to come there, Drylund insists the party are friends, and the Kuo-toa objects that they are not members of the society. Thistle interjects, "We are friends and have come to help." The Kuo-toa answers, "This is a private society business," and tells Drylund, "You should have never brought them here." When Drylund says they wanted to meet the boss, the Kuo-toa agrees: "The boss, yes, I think they should." Drylund tries to excuse himself for "operational security reasons and plausible deniability," and the Kuo-toa laughs, "The boss doesn't want to see your ugly face!" Thistle needles Drylund about being spoken to that way; Orsik tries a deep, scary voice to intimidate him, slips into a squeak — and Drylund caves anyway: "I insist we go." Thistle exclaims, "We are here to ensure that we clear up any misunderstandings between you and Mr Oosith."

Three more Kuo-toa emerge from the darkness and march Drylund and the 3HPP down to a much larger circular room, 15 feet high and filled with water about 15 feet deep, with a walkway east to a small room at the far end and an edge walkway running north and south to a pipe and a valve. As they step onto the ground, a voice speaks in their heads: "I apologize for the unpleasant local. Perhaps you could come to my office?" "Finally, a friendly voice," acknowledges the ranger; Orsik is pleased — "At last, someone treating us with the respect and dignity we deserve!" — and Thistle piles on. The voice invites them to talk about Drylund's election in the office at the end of the walkway, adding, "And Mr Key, there is alcohol." Thistle laughs when Orsik says, "A siren song."

Orsik thinks, "I only trust Vardis. And I don't trust you," grabs Drylund, and shoves him forward. The voice says gently, "There is no need for hostages, Mr. Orsik." Orsik thinks, "And that's why I have one." Drylund is shoved first into a room that looks like the offices of a very important person. Then the trap is sprung: the Kuo-toa slam the door shut and try to lock it, and Unla Key casts telekinesis to shove it open. Drylund pleads, "You have to save me. They'll kill me." Orsik demands to know what he knows about Hekaton, and confirms that this is his only price. Terrified, Drylund whispers: "I'm a member of the Kraken Society. We've been keeping Hekaton prisoner on the Morkoth. It's a special ship that can't be detected. The Morkoth sails around the northern islands of the Trackless Sea." Asked how to find it, he answers, "You have a flying castle; fly there and use your eyes. Now, can you please…" — and then his eyes bulge in terror and roll back, blood flows from his nose, and he dies in Orsik's hands. The mysterious voice calls out, "Well, that was annoying. Finding a new Waterbaron will be tricky. And we will have to kill you." The creature tries to charm Orsik and fails.

Thistle, declaring "There's no point in letting this go to waste," quickly moves the treasure into Orsik's bags of holding. Two items catch his eye: a pendant with a capital R wrapped in robes that belonged to nobles — Vardis mutters, "Another fetch quest." — and a peculiar device that causes scattered golden goose coins to glow. Thistle picks one up and notices a number, and Unla Key burps, "Well, that's how casinos prevent forgeries."
