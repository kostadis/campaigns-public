# Chapter 71 - Exploring the Lower Courtyard

Date: 26 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens as the 3HPP leave the lair of Glazhael the Undying. The dracolich has emphatically refused to share her plan to defeat Sandesyl, calling it a secret, so the party must rely on the word of an undying dracolich. On the way out Orsik turns to the creature and observes, "Dragons can’t just say things simply." Vardis agrees: "Always gotta be churching it up." As they go, the narration recalls the words of the Lords’ Alliance: "No alliance is surer than one where narrow interests align."

Outside the Dragon’s lair, the 3HPP decide that killing any potential undead allies of Sandesyl is a good plan, and the lower courtyard of Blagothkus’ castle is a good enough place to start. From within the glacier they follow a corridor to an ice-sculpted stairwell that opens onto the lower courtyard. Walls of solid ice enclose it, and it is partially overhung by another courtyard, where Blagothkus stands watching. Sweeping arches of ice hold the courtyard aloft at a height of over 100 feet, and doors of sculpted ice with iron hinges lead to outbuildings and to the main keep on the far side from the gate towers.

Orsik notices a lot of skeletal remains and orders, "Let’s get rid of them. Toss them over." Vardis, a cleric of Corellon worried about the rights of the dead, asks, "Do we want skeletal remains to be falling onto the ground?" and wonders whether the Dwarven gods will seek vengeance. The answer comes back, "Better there than as animation material for Sandesyl." Confronted with that brutal logic, Vardis remembers that burning corpses is a Dwarven tradition and suggests, "Let’s burn them on the way down?" Orsik empties a bag of holding of its valuables and fills it with corpses while Thistle stares at the sight. Orsik carries the bag to a covered gateway that leads to an open portcullis and a drawbridge, and dumps the remains overboard. Unla Key stops drinking and shouts, "Target practice!" As the dead dwarves fall, Unla Key and Vardis burn them with spells, and the day is noted in the Evermoors as the day the midday sky lit up with fire.

From the upper courtyard Blagothkus smiles and shouts, "I have been looking for someone to do this for almost a year! THANK YOU!!!!" Orsik acknowledges him and asks, "We were told there were undead here? We only found dead?" The cloud giant, trying hard not to sound condescending, replies, "Undead like the dark. They are probably not out in the sunlight." someone answers, "That makes sense!" while Vardis tries hard not to laugh.

Unla Key leads the group from the gateway to the doorway on the port side of the castle. It is a barracks that has already been burned, slashed and looted by earlier adventurers, with soot still on the walls, and whatever treasure it held was taken a while ago. "None for us?" asks Orsik. "None," replies a down Unla Key. But Thistle, whose eyes of True Seeing see all, finds scattered coins among the linen and the cracks of the room, and then remarks, "Huh. That’s interesting." He explains, "Not all of the corpses were dwarves; some were dragon wings." — "High-ranking members of the cult with the ability to fly." The castle, it seems, belonged to the cult and then to someone else.

The group then heads to the starboard door and the stables, where the stench is overwhelming and decaying yet very animate Wyverns wait. Thistle shouts, "These are undead." Unla Key asks, "Can I use dominate?" and Vardis reminds him that the undead cannot be dominated. Unla Key announces, "Fireball, 6th level, here we come!" and Orsik rolls his eyes: "3rd, please. We need that sunbeam for later." After a sullen "FINE!" the crimson bead streaks out and explodes; one Wyvern takes the full blast while the other avoids some harm.

Vardis lays out a plan to Orsik: "Okay, here’s the plan. I will use the divine power of Corellon to turn them; whichever one  doesn’t get turned, you attack." His holy symbol flashes with radiant light, and both creatures flee, one to the bow side of the stable and the other to the stern side. Orsik dashes stern-ward, sighing, "Out of range again." Thistle’s arrow strikes the sternside Wyvern, but the creatures are immune to his lightning damage. Unla Key then pulls out his magical rock, the artifact of wonder; the world shimmers and a pile of hay disappears. "I believe the hay is in the ethereal plane?" comes the question, and Thistle nods. "Two more shots!" follows, and the cleric, the dwarf, and the fairy sigh.

Vardis hurls a lance of pure light that sets one Wyvern glowing, and the glow helps Orsik aim his glowing green blade so that the creature is soon truly dead. Thistle repositions but one of his arrows misses against the stables' cover. Unla Key raises the stone again and asks, "Wand of Wonder?" Vardis shouts, "Yes, we save spells for the real battle!" while Orsik shouts, "No, magic missile! End the battle!" Unla Key casts the missiles, and the line that follows is "You made an excellent point, Orsik." (the chapter does not name its speaker). Vardis follows with a sacred flame that leaves the creature just ever so charred. The Wyvern misses Orsik and tries to flee sternward; Thistle strikes it with two arrows and Thuul burns it with a burst of acid, but it still lives. Orsik again dissuades Unla Key from the artifact, and Unla Key instead casts a spell that ends in the deafening peal of a funerary bell. "They are undead! That’s necrotic damage!" screams Orsik — but the wyvern dies, to Orsik and Vardis’ surprise. "They are resistant, not immune. I know what I am doing," comes the smug reply. "No," mutters Orsik. "Debatable," grouses Vardis.

The search afterwards turns up a copper figurine of the goddess Yondalla, which Thistle finds and which Unla Key says lets its bearer cast a ritual spell in half the time. Orsik finds 120 gold pieces in saddle bags along with a bag of carefully wrapped sweets and a cork. Vardis suspects the cork is special — "With our luck, this cork will be necessary to save the realms," — and casts detect magic, while Thistle examines it with his magical eye. It is a cork, nothing more. Orsik observes, "We have a bottle of champagne de le stompe. If we need a cork, we can use that one."

Heading into the open lower courtyard, Unla Key mutters, "That’s interesting." He explains, "The key element of being a lich is the phylactery. As long as that is not destroyed, you can come back." Asked whether these are liches, the answer is, "Yes. Sandesyl is conducting broader experiments on the undead to create alternatives to Dracoliches. They will come back." So the 3HPP again collect the remains and throw them overboard, with Unla Key and Vardis burning them as they fall. To "Where did you learn that?" the answer is "Magic School", and when that is mocked, Unla Key, pissed off, names the Nirany School of the Arcane, describing its mentoring master wizards and its freedom to choose classes. Orsik rolls his eyes. Meanwhile Thistle checks the remaining gatehouse rooms and finds them empty and free of corpses.

From the center of the lower courtyard, the group heads stern-ward to a room on the port side: an abandoned kitchen. Unla Key’s brash entrance provokes the undead. A chill spreads, rime creeps across the surfaces, and weightless, formless specters glide out of the shadows toward the 3HPP; two of them look very different. Orsik notices a misty raven and, remembering childhood stories of dangerous spectral ravens, warns, "The raven appears to be the real problem." He strikes the raven before the others can dissuade him but does not kill it. Two specters drain life force from Orsik with a wave of negative energy, and Vardis thwarts one creature with a warning flare. The raven flies incorporeally to the far end of the room and caws, charming Orsik and Unla Key, leaving them stunned and prone. The chapter ends as one of the specters attacks the stunned Unla Key, critically wounding the mage.
