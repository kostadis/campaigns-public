# Chapter 38- Harshnag the Grim of the Legendary Force Grey

Date: about 17 to 18 Flamerule 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens inside the head of Harshnag the Grim, a Frost Giant travelling with the party, who sits and stews over how little respect he is getting. Orsik and Vardis keep calling him Frosty, and he complains that being called Frosty is disrespectful, especially when he is offering them help to get to "the secret lost temple of the Giant god." He is no kinder about the halfling Unla Key, whom he judges to have "such a narcissistic, self-absorbed personality" and "a complete disregard for personal safety." He mutters that Krowen was insistent that they were his agents, and grumbles "Little people"!

Harshnag then addresses them formally: the Harper Agent Krowen Valharrow has said that they accepted being his agents for hire to learn about the events surrounding the end of the Ordning. When Vardis is confused about what the Ordning is, Harshnag explains it as "hierarchical order that the Giant races fit into" — a caste system created by Annam, the God of all giants, with storm giants at the top, cloud giants next, fire and frost giants as peers, and hill giants at the bottom. Vardis is delighted that a rigid caste system has ended — "That’s great! Freedom!" — and Unla Key is pleased at the prospect of "chaos!" The Frost Giant glares: "It is the Ordning that has kept the peace for so many centuries." Without it, he warns, the Giants would attack and destroy each other and everyone in between.

Remembering what Krowen said, Harshnag reminds them that they were given access to the teleportation circles and that there was a quid pro quo that they would help; he watches them slump. He explains that King Hekaton of the Storm Giants has disappeared, and that without him the powerful Giant leaders have started to wage war. It is time "to consult with a higher authority." He knows the location of a mountain temple, the Eye of the All-Father, built by his ancient ancestors under the Spine of the World, which according to giant legend contains a divine oracle, and he will lead them there so they can learn how best to end the giants’ threat to the North.

The second section, told from Unla Key's side, records that Harshnag the Grim agreed to a 100 gold piece share of the 5,000 gold piece reward for the capture of the brigand Worvil Forkbeard, also known as the Weevil. Unla Key was very disappointed to hear that Frosty didn’t know how to make a castle fly, and asked whether that meant Cloud Giants are more powerful. Frosty's reaction was awkward: he brought his ax next to Unla Key, and the blade was taller than the magic user. Unla Key was not phased — he had faced death, and believed that if he died the world would die with him, so the world would keep him alive. When Unla Key asked whether there are any good cloud giants, Frosty broke out laughing and mocked "short-sighted, short-lived species who think biology determines morality," insisting "To be sentient is to have the ability to make choices." Thistle piped up "Silverymoon" as an example of a place that would believe that, which Harshnag accepted as "One of those places full of the bozos." He clarified that he had really been laughing at the idea of a cloud giant helping them get a flying tower. Orsik, seeing an opportunity, asked whether there was anyone he might know, and Harshnag named Count Thullen, the younger brother of Countess Sansuri: "Thullen is a good person. His sister, not so much."

After two days of travel the edges of Longsaddle came into view. Harshnag described the town as "This sleepy little frontier village and member of the Lords’ Alliance." Orsik asked his take on the Lords’ Alliance; Harshnag spat on the ground and called it a federation of rich, powerful people who rule with an iron fist and "want to tax us," and Orsik nodded. Harshnag described Longsaddle straddling the Long Road, its rustic inn the Gilded Horseshoe and its fest hall the Gambling Golem, where card games and a local marbles game known as scattershields are popular. Beyond the town Unla Key saw horses and herds of cattle on sprawling ranches. Harshnag seemed to sense what Unla Key was thinking about such a defenseless town, and warned that monsters or brigands sometimes mistake Longsaddle for easy pickings, unaware that the Harpells, a family of powerful human wizards, live nearby at Ivy Mansion, west of the town, and that magical wards throughout warn them when trouble’s afoot. Unla Key's response was "Gambling!" Harshnag begged the group to bring him a pot of the famous soup from the Gilded Horseshoe. Unla Key headed straight for the Gambling Golem and placed a small wager, influencing it with his ability to see the future and adjust the fates. After a long night, the group continued to their final destination, Xantharl’s Keep.

At Xantharl’s Keep the point of view shifts to its lord, Narbeck Horn, who is annoyed that Orgazol "has once again accepted a bribe" from an adventurer who insists on talking to him. A member of the party tells him they are pursuing a dangerous criminal called the Weevil who is in his town; Narbeck answers each point with "So?" and then warns them not to arrest the wrong person, because "The Ax of Mirabar shows no mercy to those who arrest the wrong man." Told that the Weevil is a dwarf, he apologises, says his job is to set policy rather than handle details, and refers them to his lieutenant. Narbeck smiles evilly at the lieutenant, who realises these bounty hunters may not be the only people arrested if this goes sideways.

A horn blows and Narbeck is shocked into action: "we are under attack." Another officer whispers to him and he smiles — "A foolish group of goblins, hobgoblins, and ogres are attacking! A Frost Giant leads them." The attack comes from the west. The fairy volunteers some help to Narbeck just as another guard shouts, "Another Frost Giant is attacking the first one!"

Vardis has no interest in guard duty — "If I wanted to be a city guard, I would have kept the job at Parnast." Looking for a way out, he notices a group of bugbears coming in from the east and realises the attack is a diversion: the Falling Orc, where Larg, aka Weevil, is hiding, is in the east, and the bugbears are probably looking to secure the reward. Vardis runs; Orsik, seeing the money about to be lost, follows, and Thistle and Unla Key join the pursuit. The battle with the Bugbears is explosive: Unla Key and Vardis let loose fireballs that wound poor weevil, and Orsik and Thistle finish the thieves with good old-fashioned violence.

In the final section, Weevil shouts "Thank god you saved me!" and claims he was about to be captured by these evil creatures. The fairy walks up to him, shows him the wanted poster picked up in Longsaddle, and mutters, "I don’t think so."

## Locations
### Eye of the All-Father
A mountain temple built by Harshnag's ancient ancestors under the Spine of the World. According to giant legend it contains a divine oracle; Harshnag promises to lead the party there.

### Longsaddle
A frontier village and member of the Lords’ Alliance that straddles the Long Road, with rows of homes and businesses on either side of the trade route and ranches of horses and cattle beyond. It was founded by the Harpells, and magical wards warn them when trouble’s afoot. The group spends a long night here.

### Gilded Horseshoe
A rustic inn in Longsaddle, famous for a soup Harshnag begs the group to bring him.

### Gambling Golem
A friendly fest hall in Longsaddle where card games and scattershields are popular. Unla Key heads straight for it and places a small wager.

### Ivy Mansion
The Harpells' grand house, west of Longsaddle at the end of a long, winding path.

### Xantharl’s Keep
The group's final destination in this chapter, ruled by Narbeck Horn. It is attacked from the west by goblins, hobgoblins and ogres led by a Frost Giant, while bugbears come in from the east.

### Falling Orc
The place in the east of the town where Larg, aka Weevil, is hiding, and where the party fights the bugbears.

### Parnast
Mentioned only by Vardis, who once had a city guard job there and did not want another.

## NPCs
