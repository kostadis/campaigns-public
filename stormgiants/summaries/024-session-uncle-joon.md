# Chapter 24 - Uncle Joon

Date: about 12 to 29 Mirtul 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens aboard the cloud giant's castle as the last of the cultists fly off. Vardis mangles their host's name — "Zephyr – OS!" — and the cloud giant laughs and bellows back, "The name is Zephyros. Not Zephyr Oh-Es!" When Vardis laughs a little, the giant responds, "I can hear that!" Zephyros asks whether they "Still think it is the right decision?" and the party holds firm: Vardis shrugs, "World is fine the way it is, warts and all," Unla intones, "Yes, we definitely think," and Thistle adds, "We're not fans of cultists."

The journey to Triboar continues, and Zephyros cannot resist pointing out landmarks. Over the High Forest he describes it as "vast and mysterious, larger than most kingdoms, encompassing mountains," a place where he has heard "the brown bears are larger than sheds and the stags have antlers as wide as a wagon." A day later he points out the Star Mounts to the northeast, which they cannot approach "because of the powerful winds that swirl through and around the mountains." As the days pass, the group realizes their rations are their only food and worries that Thistle's good berries will be their only sustenance; Zephyros takes pity on them and sends down some of his food. Reaching the edge of the forest, he names the Iceshield lands and the Dessarin Hills beyond, remembering fondly "spending time with the dwarves that mined those lands," though "that was so long ago, I am not sure they are still there."

Thistle, peering over the edge of the cloud that makes up the castle, spots a silver dragon approaching with several dwarves hanging off it: "Guys, we've got incoming." The dragon lands before Thistle, Vardis, Orsik, and Unla Key. Thistle notes that the dwarves are shield dwarves, that the dragon appears uninjured and wears a harness the dwarves used to hang onto, and concludes that they must be working together. On landing, the dwarves quaff potions and become gaseous.

The conversation goes badly. Thistle asks, "Are you here to talk to Zephyros?" The startled dragon brings its head close and says, "You're not a giant," and Vardis can't resist: "Far from it." The dragon tells Thistle, "You're not very tall for a fairy," and, when Thistle protests he is "quite normal-sized," replies, "I didn't say you were short. I said you weren't very tall." When Thistle says "We struck a deal with him!" the dragon concludes they are enemies — "We will fight you in combat, defeat you, and feast on your bodies. It was nice knowing you!" A shocked Thistle quickly insists, "We're just travelers," and points toward the inside of the castle. Orsik interrupts to ask, "Why are you enemies? He seemed nice. Is there something we should know?" The paused dragon asks whether they are allies of the giant. Thistle says Zephyros offered them a ride and that he is looking for his Uncle Joon; Orsik says he has a map with what he is looking for and muses that they are their own allies, "Not a lot of allying going on here." The halfling mage blurts, "I don't even like these guys." The dragon, about to breathe fire on the group, pauses and instead asks these innocent bystanders to step aside "while the strike team and I, Clarion, destroy the orb."

Sounds of combat come from Zephyros' room — swords clanking, furniture breaking, spells being cast. Orsik is suddenly interested in the orb, and Unla Key asks, "Is the orb that makes it fly? I have a tower, see. Can I get an orb in my tower?" Clarion explains a strict mission "to destroy the orb and prevent this giant from bombing towns and villages across the north," and adds, "I have not been sent to help halflings fly their castles." Asked what happens when the orb is destroyed, the dragon says, "Oh, the castle stays motionless for several months while the cloud giant assembles a new one." Unla Key looks horrified at the idea of no alcohol for months while eating good berries. Clarion, in the tone of a parent whose child has burned their hand, says, "This is what happens when you ally yourselves with evil cloud giants." An annoyed Vardis objects — "But we're just passengers. You keep calling us allies" — and asks whether anyone has actually seen any rocks. The dragon says they have seen "distinct evidence of cloud giants dropping rocks on settlements," and made "a reasonable inference." Vardis looks at Orsik, who recognizes this as the reasoning of governments that send in the army to look like they are doing something. The dwarf asks, "Do you have proof that it is this giant? You're making a lot of assumptions here," the elf agrees it "Could be a different giant," and the fairy says, "I haven't seen any rocks."

With the dragon still talking to Thistle, the chapter gives the line "Well, the Lords' Alliance." without naming its speaker, and Orsik says, "Oh, we know that group." Thistle says they have been working for "the SEER" and did a few missions for her. Asked whether this is "official SEER business," he answers, "Yes?" — and the dragon says, "My commander will want to talk to you." Thistle assumed the person with wings was in charge; the dragon smiles: "a common misconception." A female shield dwarf removes her helm: "I am Diana, and I am in charge of this mission." When Thistle nods that they work for the SEER, Diana tells the dragon, "It would appear we interfered with the wrong castle." Vardis asks, "Would you say your princess is in another castle?" and Diana, after deciding it is "human humor," grumbles that "no self-respecting dwarf would want a princess in a tower." Orsik walks her through the evidence — rock droppings, but "none from this castle," "No, no threats," — until his question about "long-winded speeches about how Cloud Giants will rule the world" draws the counter-question, "Are you racist towards Cloud Giants?" Diana, in shock, explains, "Cloud Giants are both good and evil, so I am double-checking that Zephyros is one of the good guys," and that she is "just trying to do my job." She calls the mission a bust.

As things settle, Thistle asks about "the Cult of the Dragon and Adimond Kroskas." Diana says, "Well, we did fight them to a standstill." The dragon has never heard of "a person named Adimond Kroskas," but was not heavily involved with the efforts against the cult. Orsik shows the map again, and the dragon looks at it carefully: "I am sure I have seen something like the Northwest. No, I saw it to the northeast." Unla Key tells her about the evil cultists who had come earlier to get Zephyros to be their ally, and she laughs, "Should have led with that," and flies off.

The castle continues over the Dessarin Hills and the Dessarin River and lands near Triboar. Orsik shouts that Triboar is "a dump" and "LIKE PARNAST 2.0!" — just as Vardis points out a local militia member. The guard, a member of the Twelve, does not take kindly to the description and proudly describes his town: "a bustling mercantile town on the intersection of two important highways, The Long Road and the Evermoor Way," with half its population on ranches and farmsteads outside town and "many entertainment options." Unla Key perks up. The guard directs them to the Market Square and the Tower of the Lord Protector, and names the current Lord Protector, Darathra Shendrel — "She's from Waterdeep!" Vardis and Orsik, both Waterdhavians, just smile; the guard calls her "fair and clearheaded and widely regarded as good."

Thistle asks about his Uncle Joon. The guard reports that he "started drinking at the Everwyvern House but has now been reduced to drinking at the Talking Troll," growing "progressively sadder." Thistle says, "I'll need a small bucket" — "To dump water over his head." The guard laughs and gives directions: past the market, next to The Cart and Coin, across from Ransor's Open Road. As they go, Orsik and Unla Key note the Triboar Arms, Wainwright's Wagons, and The Happy Horse Ranch, and caravans in large fields. Thistle politely borrows a bucket from Syreen Widdens, owner of The Cart and Coin, and marches into the Talking Troll — a dim, smelly, low-beamed dive of battered furniture and drunks, its burned roof unrepaired, birds nesting in its holes.

The tavern keeper greets them: "Welcome to the Talking Troll; what can I, Kaelen Sarssir, get you?" His flair prompts Vardis to ask whether he is an actor — "Why yes!" Kaelen points to a drunk fairy sprawled on a table, speckled with bird droppings. Thistle dumps the water on his uncle, who shouts, "I didn't do it," then pivots to having Thistle buy him a drink: "Kaelen, this is my nephew. Did I ever tell you what a wonderful boy he is? He's going to buy me a drink!" Kaelen asks whether the nephew will pay the whole tab. Uncle Joon offers Orsik a 50/50 split that would "double the size," confusing Orsik ("how does this help?"), and Uncle Joon blushes; then comes the line, unattributed in the chapter, "Don't worry, I used to be dumb like you. I understand." Kaelen names "30 Gold. 15 is what he owes, but taxes, late charges, and that I must listen to his moaning all day," explaining he is "a method actor, getting material for my off-High Road production." Uncle Joon sneers that he "couldn't act your way out of a wet paper bag."

Thistle stares at his uncle and says, "Adimond Kroskas." Uncle Joon starts, pauses, then laughs — "You never could intimidate anyone, my boy" — and tells Kaelen his credit "just went to 30 Gold." Orsik, with the same glare that stared down Bad Fruul, says, "We have come a long way to ask you this question, and you will answer it." Uncle Joon crumples into a small ball and whispers, "It's worse than what you think, boy. Adimond was the alchemist who figured out how to make drakes from dragon eggs." Adimond's next plan was "to combine faeries and dragon eggs to produce a more powerful and deadly drake," and Uncle Joon was trying to make a deal with him — "To get your girlfriend back, my boy. Why else? You're family, and he had captured her." Thistle knows he is lying but keeps that to himself.

Meanwhile Orsik gets a drink from Kaelen. The Talking Troll's wine cellar is extraordinary, including a scarce sparkling wine, Champagne du le Stomp, of unknown provenance; Kaelen says he was told "it comes from a hidden dimension where a Vampire rules," and that the elf who sold it called it the last of its kind. Uncle Joon shouts that Kaelen "found the cellar full" and it is "just some random bottle from some random vintner." Then Uncle Joon tells Thistle that "the ancient dragons don't have the same relationship that other people have towards their gods," that when Tiamat showed up not every one of them was pleased, and that Adimond "was working for some of those dragons." Adimond's plans "were too twisted for even the Cult of the Dragon," so he found other sponsors who allied with the ancient dragons: "I have a name – the Kraken Society." Asked where they are, Uncle Joon says, "If I knew, I wouldn't tell you," and warns that "Ancient Dragons are the most powerful creatures in the north." Asked, "What about Laela?" he answers, "Last I checked, Adimond had her." Thistle wants to scream at him.

Before Thistle can consider his next act of vengeance, two brothers walk in: Jacoby and Persson Hysstryn, upset that nobody is looking for their brother, Mirak Hysstryn. Orsik tries to minimize his presence. The brothers offer "a magical bow we would be willing to trade for information about our brother," and Jacoby imitates the Lord Protector, Darathra, to the clientele's amusement: Mirak "ran away with a lady friend," and if they bother "that poor lady" again, she will have the Twelve throw them in jail. Persson insists, "My brother is in this town, and she is not looking for him because she thinks I'm crazy." Orsik, in his city guard tone, suggests they have had too much to drink; Jacoby snarls, "We haven't even started drinking. We're sober." Thistle ignores Orsik and offers to help. Mirak has been missing for 10 years; his wife claims he ran off with one of his lady friends, and Persson adds the enduring rumor that the lady friend was Urgala Meltimer, an innkeeper in Triboar — "She's always denied that," Jacoby mutters. Persson says they paid a drunken priest to ask his god, and that "Helm says that Mirak is still at home and never left." Orsik, now paying close attention, tells Thistle he thinks "it's a plane of shadow over the house," and declares, "I am smart." Unla Key whispers that they could lie; Jacoby, seeing the gleam in Thistle's eye, mutters, "I want proof."

Thistle starts with the last clue. The cleric is not forthcoming until Thistle pays his tab. He introduces himself: "I'm Silvarren Loomshank, Loomy to my friends," banished from Neverwinter to the Allfaiths Shrine in Red Larch, and unable to leave because of his debts to this tavern — which Thistle has just paid. Before leaving, Loomy adds what he did not tell the brothers: "Helm said something about being… about not being buried in the ground, but above the ground." Thistle tells Orsik, "We have two clues. Let's go chat with Urgala." Asked whether they are taking the uncle, Vardis answers, "I speak for all of us, no."

At the Northshield House — stately, clean, and quiet — a large, powerfully built woman with graying hair greets them: "Fellow adventurers!" Urgala adventured mostly in the Dessarin Hills, where "There is always a tomb to be plundered." Asked about regrets, she tells a long story about a mine, of which Orsik gathers only that a demon was involved, ending: "And I had to abandon the mine when my wife plunged into the Underdark" — "Over ten years ago." Orsik asks whether she worked for the Lords' Alliance; she calls them "Those pompous fools," saying "The North is about freedom," and that she believes "in small local governments." She gives Thistle a raw map with notes on landmarks to the mine, which he carefully copies, and explains that the Dessarin Hills "are rich enough to make you rich if you are poor and just poor enough not to be worth owning," so wildcat miners abandon their mines to monsters from the Underdark and others. When Thistle steers toward Mirak, she says, "You've been talking to Jacoby," and admits, "Yes, Mirak and I were involved for a while," after her wife disappeared, but denies any part in his disappearance: "Mirak had no other woman than me." With an angry glint she suggests his wife, Tolmara, found out — then stops, "I shouldn't accuse anyone of something I can't prove," and asks them to keep it quiet. Thistle sympathizes; Vardis tells Orsik, "Guilty. One dead lover is an accident. Losing two lovers is carelessness or a pattern." Urgala tells them Mirak's wife's house is the Six Windows Inn.

The Six Windows Inn is a low-rent, two-story, chilly and decrepit building, and Thistle notices there are no windows into the attic. Confident in his stealth, he flies toward a partly ajar window — and on a "1 in 400" chance event breaks the window, trips on a beam, and falls out, hanging by a rope. Vardis asks, "What is he doing?" Unla Key suggests, "Maybe it's a distraction?" and Orsik agrees: "Yes, it is. Let's go in." Tolmara screams at Thistle — "Thief! You were trying to sneak into my house to avoid paying rent! I will call the Lord Protector! I have rights." — and rushes outside waving a rolling pin, letting the other three slip in. On the stairs, residents come out to investigate; Unla Key offers, "I could cast sleep?" and Orsik growls, "A bit of overkill?" before using his best Chief of Local Militia tone: "Nothing to see here, folks." The residents shrug. Orsik shoves open the attic door and discovers bones.

Outside, Tolmara throws potatoes at the dangling Thistle — "I never trusted a fairy." Thistle claims, "It was a bumblebee, a giant Bumblebee," and the story strangely resonates with her: "My poor boy! A giant bumblebee! Let me get you a salve." She rushes back inside. In the attic, Unla Key concludes, "This fits Mirak's description. But we'll have to take the bones to speak with the dead; can't find any evidence of what killed him." As fingers touch the bones, the skeleton animates, and Orsik strikes it down with one blow. Tolmara arrives at the attic door, which Vardis has barred, screaming that she will call the police. Orsik opens it: "I think we will be calling the Twelve." Her renters are happy to see her go — one mutters, "Good riddance on that peeping tom." Leaving, Orsik shouts, "Good work on that distraction, Thistle!"

With Tolmara in tow, the party heads to the Tower, where a large crowd — the two brothers among them — is screaming and arguing. The Lord Protector screams, "Silence!" to brief effect, then orders, "You four, and you two, and you, inside. Now." In her office she asks, "Who is in charge?" and Orsik answers, "Thistle Underwood." When she tries to stand over Thistle, he flies up to her eye level. Told they were investigating a murder, Darathra is furious with Jacoby and Persson for "your wild goose chase," until Thistle cuts her off: "We found a murdered body." The brothers explode at Darathra over a decade of being ignored, claiming noble families would have been treated differently. Darathra sighs that they will check it tomorrow "when we can get a cleric to cast speak with the dead," and dismisses everyone except Tolmara: "Not you." In the marketplace, Tolmara's failure to leave the building draws cheers and groans from vendors; Unla Key observes, "They were betting on who was going to spend the night in the tower!"

Orsik then visits the local Lionshield Coster outpost, a famously well-stocked store whose competitors claim it relies on an interdimensional trading firm, and which the Zhentarim have tried for years to figure out. He finds it in shambles. Owners Alaestra and Narth are distraught, and Alaestra blurts out, "I'll give you a 10% discount if you figure out who stole our treasure." Orsik agrees, keen to re-establish his intellect after Thistle's detective work. The vault was breached; the thief left behind "a leather harness and a masterly crafted grappling hook." They were reluctant to consult the harness makers because some think the coster "predatory." Then Alaestra blurts, "They stole the key." Over Narth's attempt to shut her up, she explains that the key is how they get more goods: "We write a list and then put the key on the list. And then the goods arrive, and some of the money is taken. The company doesn't like it when the key is lost." Orsik realizes he has leverage. Narth offers 20%; Orsik stares impassively; "And 50 Gold?"; Orsik stares impassively; "I can't go higher." Orsik: "done."

At Othovir's Harness Shop, Othovir admits he did not make the harness but judges it made in Waterdeep, "a decade old," and fit for "a person 150 pounds." Unla Key thinks, "I know a tavern owner who fits that bill." Othovir sends them to Harriet about the hook. Harriet, who runs the smithy Uldinath's Arms across the road from Foehammer's Forge, says, "Not mine. Only Ghelryn could have made it," teasingly calling him "a good man but a lousy smith." Ghelryn is too old to have been the cat burglar. Orsik impresses him — "We killed a hill giant!" — and Ghelryn, eager to know whether they crushed orc skulls, eventually yields to Orsik's appeal to Dwarven pride: he sold the grapple to Darz Helgar, caretaker of a cottage on the Northern Campgrounds.

At the Northern Campgrounds — a fenced field for wagons and oxen, with tent space and a stone well — Orsik shows Darz the harness and grapple. Darz sits down on his steps, defeated, and confesses: "Yeah. It was me. I stole the gold and the bags." He says Kaelen — "the guy who runs the shite tavern" — knew about his past and threatened to tell the Lord Protector unless he stole it. Darz "did ten hard years in Waterdeep's dungeon," is tired of running, and asks only, "Just promise me you get that bastard; get him good!" Orsik's eyes lighten: "You have contacts with the Thieves Guild? Give me whatever you have, and I promise you nobody has to find out." Asked about Kaelen, Orsik answers, "Oh, we will deal with him."
