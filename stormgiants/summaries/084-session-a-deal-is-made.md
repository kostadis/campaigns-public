# Chapter 84 - A deal is made

Date: about 8 Uktar 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens mid-battle in a sandy courtyard of ruined walls, where the 3HPP fights two blue dragon—purple worm hybrids. Orsik feels his blood boil: he is alive, and soon, he believes, he will be a hero, dying while fighting these abominations. He savagely hurts the creature, and it bleeds and howls. Then its tail catches him, and he protests, "Wait, that’s unfair. I am not near his tail." The hybrid's tail turns out to be far more flexible, and much longer, than Orsik had guessed; its sting misses.

Thuul rushes the second dragon, coming from the south, while Thistle spreads his arrows between the two and thinks, "Those arrows of dragon slaying would have been useful." Lightning strikes cross the courtyard from opposite sides, hitting Unla Key, Thistle, and Vardis. When Unla Key tries a counterspell, Orsik shouts, "Lair actions. They are not spells." The hybrid strikes Orsik again, this time without missing, and he uses the gift Thornacious gave him so long ago to avoid being poisoned. Vardis summons a djinni and sends it against the dragon Orsik is fighting. Bloodied, Orsik mutters, "Today is good to die!" Meanwhile the other hybrid kills Thuul with its stinger. The Djinni deals thunderous damage to the hybrid, but the creature lives.

The first dragon uses its frightening aura before it breathes. Only Orsik is affected — "I can’t be afraid. I am Orsik." — and the fear the magic finds in his mind is that somehow he won't die and won't be the hero. The lightning breath stretches to a point 90 feet away. Orsik thinks, "This is it. I die now, and I am a hero," and when a voice tells him to try and live, he answers in his head, "No, this is it. I am going to be a hero!" Unla Key is also hit by the lightning and struck down. Vardis, at death's door from the other dragon, is struck down by it as the cleric tries to heal his friend. Thistle, flying, fires two arrows; the 3HPP is down to him alone, and he is not powerful enough to kill two adult dragons. "Maybe I can call the SEER now?" He tastes failure, blames his own impatience for rushing in without gathering allies, and is briefly tempted to blame the circlet of intelligence. He resolves not to go down without a fight.

Before anyone can act, a portal opens and a gargantuan blue dragon steps through. Thistle recognizes her from the Maelstrom: it is Iymrith. He aims at her in one final, pointless gesture of rebellion — and she starts clapping. Cultists jump off her back and rush to heal Orsik, Unla Key, and Vardis. Never has a dwarf been so angry to be alive: "I WAS GOING TO BE A HERO! I WAS GOING TO DIE AND BE A HERO, AND YOU SAVED ME! I WAS THREE SECONDS AWAY FROM BEING A HERO!!!!!" The cultist who healed him walks away as quickly as he can. Iymrith paces on her hind legs, wrecking the remaining walls and shifting the sand to uncover old floors and cover others; beside her the two hybrids are nothing.

Ignoring Orsik, Iymrith greets Thistle — "My dear boy, Thistle, it’s so good to see you." She explains that for centuries she has been working on a new hybrid dragon; these hybrids are deadly but mindless, kept in the desert to keep out unwanted guests, and she wanted "something that had the innocence of a fairy and the destructive power of draconic kind." Thistle says, "I want her back." Iymrith tells Orsik he is everything she was promised he would be; asked by a dubious Thistle whether that promise came from the SEER, Iymrith denies working with her, calling the SEER the real power of the Lords’ Alliance, committed to imposing an order, and declaring, "Orsik is right to distrust her. She is evil."

Iymrith lays out her proposal: every so often she calls a meeting of the five clans, the heads of the chromatic dragons, to show her latest creations, and "It would be awesome if you four fought Laela at this festival." She does not want Laela killed: "I want you to prove she is a Fey Dragon hybrid and as lethal as I claim she is." Asked whether he can leave with Laela afterward, she insists, "I am a scientist. Of course, you can leave. I am not a slave keeper. I am an artist." Then, with almost casual indifference: "Or I could kill you all right now." Thistle shouts, "This meets my goals."

Hearing that a dragon might still kill him, Orsik thinks, "I CAN STILL BE A HERO!" and sets out to stop the fairy, needling Thistle that his girlfriend is "some kind of freak fairy-dragon hybrid." Thistle insists, "My goal is to rescue her from Iymrith," but begins to waver: "I have to see her." Iymrith explains the experiment: a creature that can exist in the Feywild and the prime material plane at once, swapping its body through an interplanar link, its shapeshift intrinsic rather than magical and, as a fairy, proof against magical detection. Laela is still very much a fairy, with a squeaky voice and complaints about her wingtips needing wing-muffs. Orsik calls her an abomination; Thistle objects that they haven't seen her. Vardis, sitting in the sand, shouts to Orsik, "It’s been a long dry spell. He’ll take anything." Orsik refuses to help: "Helping a dragon is not heroic." Thistle allows, "I am a good person, and although this meets my goals, it is questionable."

Iymrith claims she infiltrated the dragons to get investors; when accused of starting a draconic civil war, she answers, "I needed investors, and when you need investors, you do what you must do." Thistle sympathises: "We had investors. Some of them weren’t good people. I can relate." Pressed by Orsik, she names them by degrees: the giant lords she has been playing off, who thought she was a giant but now — "thanks to you" — know she is a dragon; a delegation of Red Wizards; a representative of the Unseelie Court, at which Thistle cocks his bow; and the Seelie Court. Iymrith is genuinely surprised that Thistle thought she was experimenting on Queen Titania's subjects without the queen's knowledge, and laughs until a tear falls: "There’s a reason you were kicked out of the Seelie Court." Thistle puts away his arrow: "It checks out."

Iymrith says a success will satisfy the contracts and let her leave. Orsik observes that her being in breach of contract is far better for them; she counters that she will still escape, they will be dead, the dragons will keep fighting, and Laela will be imprisoned and used — or else they rescue the girl, make Thistle happy, and "stick it to the Seelie and Unseelie courts." Orsik's only objection remains "Not heroic." "You’re right. You want to be dead." / "I would have been a hero! All you had to do was delay arrival for three seconds." Frustrated, Iymrith asks, "Can someone please tell him to be quiet?" The cleric who healed Orsik worries that Iymrith may be unhappy with their battlefield performance.

Orsik calls Thistle aside, and the two agree to say yes and use the time to rescue Laela. Thistle demands proof of life, and Iymrith hands him a sending stone, tiny in her claws. Laela's voice comes through, scolding him — "You’ll get yourself killed. And where the hell have you been? I’ve been looking all over for you." Vardis notes that her looking for Thistle is not consistent with Iymrith's version of the facts. Laela says she is "In a cage in Iymrith’s lair," and when Vardis cuts in, she is outraged: "Why is there an elf? Are you working with elves now? I thought we said we don’t like elves!" Thistle whimpers, "I needed helpers," and the voice is cut off.

Iymrith then concedes: Laela is not working for her. She found her after Laela torched a caravan in dragon form and captured her, but now Laela refuses to switch into her dragon form, practising a powerful fey meditation, "fey-fullness." Orsik laughs, "I get it. The secret is that she’s always angry!" Glum, Iymrith admits, "I admit it. I can’t get her to turn into a dragon." Her peers think she has lost the mojo. Orsik notes, "For an all-powerful dragon, you sure have a self-confidence issue." Iymrith abruptly takes on the posture of a terrifying ancient blue dragon: she is sure of her power, but wants to be known as "an artiste, a creator" — "I won’t just be the Dragon that got lucky with drakes." Orsik turns to Vardis: "Lots of parallels with Sandesyl’s experiments."

The event will happen "At the coliseum." Iymrith draws a map of stunning detail on the ground: Laela emerges from the hole in the ground, the party fights a few rounds, then leaves; Laela need not appear as a dragon, only turn into one. Unla Key, who has been drinking, shouts, "This seems like an incredibly bad idea," then, "I’m in." Orsik proposes talking with Laela beforehand so she won't get nervous; Vardis adds, "And have some performance anxiety." Iymrith, exhausted, plops on her belly, rises, and looses a bolt of lightning that overwhelms the sun, screaming, "I SHOULD NEVER HAVE DONE THIS EXPERIMENT!!!!" — and pleads to Thistle, "How was I supposed to know that combining Dragons and Fairies would create this much drama?"

Thistle's "Perhaps a play?" gives Orsik his plan: they will come a few days early, with a script and dialogue, for a top performance. Thistle asks for two skin-tight unitards. Orsik flatters the confused dragon into agreeing she had thought of all this already; she agrees, "Of course I had, but keep repeating the details so I can make sure you missed nothing." Vardis recalls, "The last time we rehearsed that little, we got Bob." Orsik insists they need days in advance and focus, since "There might be other things in your lair," and Thistle adds, "We believe in method acting." Iymrith, desperate, says excitedly, "YES!" Thistle admits to Orsik, "I don’t have the headband of intellect," then shouts, "We’re good." The cleric who healed Orsik is relieved.

It is a three-day hike to Iymrith's lair. Teleporting is impossible because the coliseum has been magically protected from Scrying and Teleport, and only those who worship her greatness may fly on her, but the abominations can carry them. Studying her, Thistle sees that Iymrith is different from other dragons — she has tools she appears to be using and equipment she seems to be wearing — yet even she is in a jam from one too many deals. Asked why she doesn't just eat the Red Wizards, she answers, "Because they are the minions! I made a deal with Szass Tam," and pleads, "I needed investors." When Orsik asks why she didn't self-fund from her hoard, Iymrith looks angry, horrified, and confused, and roars, "SPEND. MY. HOARD!"
