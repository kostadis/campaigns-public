# Chapter 65 Orsik experiences personal growth and the fellowship against Bob dissolves.

Date: night of 23 Eleint to 25 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The battle with Bob is over, and Bob is gone. It "wasn’t that epic of a battle." Bob was ready with his zombie army, and despite their attempt at caution the 3HPP and their allies were noticed. Orsik, who as an artificer could make bags of holding, had prepared two of them. While Bob taunted the party, Unla Key and Thuul approached the creature; Unla Key tossed one bag of holding towards the demon, and as it stood at the demon's feet Thuul rushed forward and stuffed the second bag into the first. Bob, the Red Wizard, and Thuul were caught in the blast. Lord Zymorven was momentarily outraged when he realised that several citizens of Silverymoon who were still alive had been caught up in it too. With Bob gone, the zombie hordes were easy pickings for Vardis' power over the undead, a few fireballs, and the sword and bow of Zymorven, Darathra, and Urgala. The Drow fighters who had been protecting the Red Mage fled before they could be caught.

Vardis finds the whole outcome very funny. The theater is full of dead zombies dressed as members of the 3HPP, decayed bodies made up to look like Vardis, Orsik, Unla Key, and Thistle. When Urgala asks where all the theatergoers are, the party admits they do not know. Lord Zymorven, trying to keep calm, asks, "Does that mean we killed 200 of Silverymoon’s citizens?" Orsik promises they will go find them, and Vardis is keen to, because he still wants to know what the play was about.

Darathra is furious: "We sent him to the Astral plane. We were supposed to kill him. He’ll be back." Orsik protests that he had "a foolproof idea." Darathra pleads with the theater's roof and shouts, "Do you not understand how the multiverse works?" She tells Orsik he sent "300 innocent people to a pocket dimension" and that "We have no way to bring them back." When Orsik answers that they will get them back, she screams that "THE ONE PERSON WHO KNOWS WHERE THEY ARE IS LOST IN THE ASTRAL PLANE!!!" Urgala, recovered from the shock of the battle, asks, "Is this my fault, too?" Darathra fears the zombies dressed as the 3HPP will be blamed on them: "THIS IS A DISASTER!" Vardis tells her patiently to calm down.

Staring at the carrion, Orsik proposes burning everything: "It was a tragic fire." Urgala sees "some promise in that idea." Vardis doubts it, but Orsik taps his headband: "My headband is tingling. This will work." Vardis mutters that the headband is malfunctioning.

Lord Zymorven then makes a long speech praising Orsik's ruthlessness while making it clear that "Orsik and Orsik alone made that decision" and that he himself was not a party to it. Vardis realises Zymorven is setting out who will bear responsibility. Zymorven declares the debts between their families cleared. Orsik studies "the windbag" and mutters, "Good Chat." While Zymorven keeps droning on, Urgala mutters, "There is a reason I dumped his ass," and she and Vardis trade jokes about the "experimental years," with Urgala smirking when Orsik says they are "just friends" and Vardis calls them "Legacy friends."

Zymorven insists there is no evidence Bob was a demon. Orsik notes that if Bob comes back, "that might be proof," and when Orsik suggests Zymorven is not proud of what they accomplished, the old fighter grows paler and says he is "very proud" of Orsik's "valiant leadership." Orsik accepts the "we": Zymorven is supposed to help with the aristocrats and spread the news that they saved the town. Zymorven still frets that over 200 people have disappeared. Orsik counts about 50 zombie bodies and lays out his plan: burn the place, the bodies cannot be identified, "They died in the fire." Zymorven shrieks at the idea of admitting they used fire spells, then offers his own version: Bob, "who was a demon, used fire spells in the theater," and although they defeated Bob they could not save anyone else. Vardis adds, "Don’t forget we saved the town." Orsik is happy to get their stories straight.

Darathra, asked to agree, says she was never there and never saw anything. Orsik remarks, "Sounds Harper-like," then covers his mouth: "Did I say that out loud?" Darathra glares, sheathes her sword, and marches out, telling Orsik, "I am out of here. We are done. Don’t ever come looking for me." She will still say hi, but there will be no more favours or special requests, and at the door she shouts, "And no more plays."

Unla Key sets the fire: a mote of fire to the balcony and a bright, shiny star streaking into the middle of the theater, but the fire is not robust enough to sustain a burn. Thistle piles up the rotting food and asks Orsik to smash the benches; Orsik asks whether that is "Because I am a dwarf?" and Thistle answers, "No, because you are strong." Hearing the talk of race, Lord Zymorven quietly leaves. Urgala bursts out laughing, says she has not had this much fun in years, and offers the party her help: "If you need anything from me, I’m here for you."

Unla Key then proposes an alternative story: blame a "demonic cult of the ungodly races," because the people of Silverymoon will believe it. Orsik, troubled, refuses: they already have a story with Lord Zymorven and cannot have two. Thistle lowers his bow; he had been planning to pincushion the mage if Unla Key did not get with the program. Vardis smooths it over as "a normal part of the brainstorming process." Thistle places the crushed beams on the compost pile and lights a bonfire that will do the trick.

Orsik wants to stay and be praised, but Urgala urges the party to run: "There are spells that could reveal the truth." Orsik, flustered that he did not think of it, has a profound insight: "My job is to identify the most brilliant idea." Urgala leaves before she loses her game face. On the way out Orsik reassures Unla Key, "You set up the Astral gate. Without you, we’re fighting Bob." Unla Key replies that he was only thinking of the chaos his story could have caused: "It’s not that I am evil, but it’s one hell of a way to burn this evil place to the ground."

At the eastern city gate, with the theater blazing behind them, a guard is delighted to see the real-life heroes of the play until he sees the flames; he apologises and runs off, muttering about someone he cares about who attended the play. To avoid their friendly vampire and her spawn, the 3HPP board their airship in the morning.

On the 5th day of the 3rd tenday of Eleint, in the control tower, Orsik tells Esclarotta, "This castle isn’t safe." Esclarotta disputes that the party owns the castle: "I am the flight command system. I am integrated with the whole castle." Orsik muses about getting rid of the dracolich and the vampire to have the whole castle, and about working the control crystals himself, which earns a grave "What?" from Esclarotta. She asks, "why can’t we be friends?" and complains that the party gives her orders and never asks how her day was. When Orsik grumbles that she has them traipsing around Faerûn instead of saving the world from giants, she asks why they do not: "You have the conch. Why don’t you find out what’s up with King Hekaton?"

Esclarotta explains that she and Blagothkus had a long chat, that she had misplaced her rage at dying onto him, and that they are happy together and willing to share the castle. Seeing Thistle willing to leave the woman he loved for a higher cause made her appreciate that "Blagothkus killing our son wasn’t his fault"; the gods, and "in particular, Annam," were manipulating him. When Vardis tries small talk with "We saved Silverymoon," she praises them: "That is what heroes are." Orsik, in awe, realises, "We did save them!"

Orsik decides to treat Esclarotta as part of the team. They discuss the vampire: Esclarotta says it must be destroyed but that "the greater good matters more." Blagothkus speaks for the first time, observing that the ends-and-means question is "precisely how we ended up in this emotional conflict between me and my wife." Orsik wonders about setting the vampire and the dracolich against good dragons; Unla Key suggests that, as the vampire is the last member of "the Cult of the Dragon," they could fetch her a cult artifact, such as "an evil tome how to create a Dracolich from a dragon." Vardis notes they would be trading one problem for another, which is "our MO." Orsik tells Unla Key to blow the conch, and the world shimmers.

Into the Maelstrom: the 3HPP arrive atop a glowing glyph in an alcove in the northeast corner of a rough-hewn chamber, roughly circular with a 100-foot diameter and ceilings over fifty feet high, slick with moisture and lit by bioluminescent lichen. A pipe organ and a powerful female voice sound from a spiral staircase. Vardis suggests talking to "Queen Serissa because she might know where Hekaton is." Orsik studies the giant masonry, grudgingly impressed but finding "Shoddy work," and concludes the place was hard to find because it is deep underwater. Vardis discovers that the pool is the physical entrance. He wants to know whether the conch will take them back "to the Sigil"; Orsik, applying his new insight, says, "Yes, that’s a great idea, Vardis." The conch is silent: its wielder can blow it only once per day. Vardis urges caution at the geyser to the south, and it is a good thing, for it erupts with scalding water. Following Thistle's "Right-hand rule?" they head north, past a statue of a male storm giant blowing a pink and blue conch shell, giant-sized guest rooms, and a store room of giant supplies, where Orsik finds no particularly good vintage among the wine barrels.
