# Chapter 61 - Into Ironslag

Date: 16 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The fight with the Yakfolk continues, and they prove harder to kill than many of the enemies the 3HPP have faced: there are many of them, they are hardy, and they hold hostages, which makes a Fireball "dicey." Worse, the Yakfolk have priests who can summon an elemental. An earthen monster seizes Thistle in its fist, tosses him against the ground "like a rag doll," and slams "the puny fairy" again.

Unla Key computes the space needed to catch the most enemies, shouts "Four," and the room explodes with a fireball. One of the Yakfolk dies screaming in the blast. Another fires arrows at the halfling and lands a glancing blow; Unla Key smiles and says, "I am – after all – Unla Key… For you." Thistle squirms free of the Earth Elemental and with his longbow kills one Yakfolk priest with an arrow to the throat, wounding the other. Thuul slips past the elemental to reach the surviving priest; the earthen creature punches him as he goes, with "a slight crunching sound," but the drake survives and scalds the priestess with his acid. She screams and dies.

Unla Key, who has recently learned Cloud Kill, plots its placement. He sees two options, one that catches the elemental and one that does not but catches an extra Yakfolk, and reasons it out aloud, first muttering "What did my teachers say?" and then answering himself, "Right, immune to poison." A poisonous cloud streams from his fingers and engulfs three of the Yakfolk; two thuds follow, and choking. Outside the chief's tent, Orsik fights the chief and kills him. Thistle tries to kill the Earth Elemental "who showed him so little respect" and is slammed against the ground again. The cleric of Corellon, seeing Thistle brutalised, uses his mace of terror; the elemental is frightened and burrows deep into the ground. The last Yakfolk in the cloud dies with a thud, and the tent falls quiet.

One of the captive elves speaks: "I am Princess Halani Meliamne, and this is Jevin and Vandar. We are from the Moonwood. The Yakfolk captured us. If you bring us to our homes, there is a great reward." Unla Key turns to Orsik: "We made allies!" Thistle, wary of making more enemies, questions the princess and learns the Yakfolk had performed the ritual of possession on several of her guards. Unla Key, seeing the profit, offers the party's flying castle, telling her to climb the rope to the top level and avoid anywhere without bright sunlight, and Thistle warns that "There are some vile creatures in our sky castle that we have not yet had a chance to get rid of." Asked about giants, she tells them of a doorway to the north and an elevator down into the Giant's home, and adds that the Yakfolk used their ritual to take over some dwarves from Citadel Adbar "and kill the dwarven king."

Thistle asks how the Moon Elves feel about Silverymoon. The Princess is bitter that Silverymoon betrayed the Silver Marches in their hour of need, and Thistle mutters, "I know!" She says the era of Silverymoon's dominance has ended: "Our people can no longer support or rely on the support of such fair weather allies. A new organization will protect the people of this world." When the fairy asks, "You wish to establish, A new world order?", she realises she has said too much and pleads that she has been in prison for some time. Staring at the dead Yakfolk who tormented her, she says angrily, "I would not be surprised if the agents of the Silverymoon put the Yakfolk up to their plots." Vardis says convincingly, "This would not surprise us at all."

After a short rest the group heads for the door down into the Fire Giant's domain: 20 foot tall, riveted iron plates, handles 9 feet above the floor. Thistle flies to the handle and the rest push it open. Beyond is a portcullis, which Vardis lifts; Unla Key mocks him, "You should become a fighter." Vardis reflects that clerics of Corellon Larethian serve a god with trust issues, ever since his first consort betrayed him, and that for someone like Vardis, "already on the outs," a further show of disloyalty may provoke his god.

Past the portcullis lies a cold, dark, vast chamber with a 50' ceiling and an 80' diameter, 500' deep shaft. A 60' diameter oak-and-iron platform hangs from an iron rig by chains and constantly rises and falls, powered by a water wheel behind an opening in the southeast wall. Hidden behind boxes, Thistle times it: 15 stops each way, 30 seconds each, 15 minutes end to end. The group looks for another way and finds a staircase in an adjacent room. Thistle recalls what Uncle Joon told them: Ironslag sits at the base of a 500 foot cliff, its entrance "a pair of 50’ tall adamantine doors" that are "airtight valves sealed by magic." Orsik asks, "so this is the only way in?" — "Probably?"

The descent is tedious. Orsik's mining experience guides them through a maze of rough-hewn tunnels, and he thinks, "crappy construction, poor workmanship." Some 500 feet down, Unla Key hears the dialect of rock gnomes, and a hissing voice shouts, "don’t fool around! Keep working!" Thistle wonders why the giants are not doing the work; he and Vardis misremember what little they know of giants ("They suck," mutters Vardis; "they are lazy!" adds Thistle).

Orsik spots two fire salamanders guarding dwarves and gnomes and proposes to sneak up and attack. Orsik and Unla Key move silently and Thistle hides behind Orsik, but Vardis "could wake up the dead." Orsik growls, "why did we bring you here?", casts Expeditious Retreat, catches a Salamander and strikes it with his sword, and is scorched by its heat. Thistle remembers that Uncle Joon said salamanders are really good smiths. Unla Key steps forward and banishes the Fire Salamander furthest from the group; it doesn't stand a chance against his "manipulation of portents and fates."
