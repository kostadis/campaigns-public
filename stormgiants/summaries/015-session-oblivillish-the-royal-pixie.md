# Chapter 15 - Oblivillish the Royal Pixie

Date: about 21 to 23 Tarsakh 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens inside the head of Oblivillish the Royal Pixie, who sees in Thistle a chance to escape his exile: *"There may be a way for me to get out of this exile if I can just convince this Fairy."* Thistle's plan is to deal with the ettin and then take Oblivillish back to Thornacious, but Oblivillish warns that "Prince Thornacious may not be so happy to have me back; after all, there was that misunderstanding about the stag." When Vardis asks who killed the stag, Oblivillish is annoyed and admits it: "I did! But I didn't know it was his stag." Thistle points out that the Prince "was quite explicit when we met him that all stags belong to him," and Oblivillish, who has never groveled because "I am noble. People grovel to me," concedes that this "may explain my current exiled status." He then orders Thistle to teach him to grovel, and when Thistle rolls his eyes, he kneels awkwardly and asks, "Is this better?" Thistle sighs, "yes."

Asked about an ettin named Gralm, Oblivillish says he worked for him until the party killed "those goblins," which probably ended his employment. Thistle's eyes twinkle with an idea: Oblivillish will present himself as "the last member of your group that was wiped out by a group of Bad Fruul's goons," saved by the party, who have come to offer their sword to his boss. Oblivillish likes the plan and quickly agrees, then spends a while swallowing his pride and learning to grovel while Wallace, Orsik, and Vardis try hard not to laugh.

A few hours later a nearby tree glows with an iridescent blue flame and the outline of a door lights up. Oblivillish complains that "The Forgotten Forest is trying to help us, but why does it always do this?" and bets "a fistful of fire ants that door leads to a puzzle room." A friendly, toothy maw forms in the swirling flames, yips, and vanishes. Unla Key declares, "I'm going in," and everyone shrugs and follows.

The passage leaves Unla Key nauseous in a cold, fifteen-foot square room of blue marble with a plush velvet floor, full of disembodied giggles. A small dog with a vibrantly-colored mane appears and speaks into Thistle's head: "My name is Pandi, and I want to play a game! Can we play a game? CAN WE?" Pandi demands to know what they want. Thistle guesses a grail and then a hallway, drawing the complaint that "they don't teach fairies anything these days in fairy school," and Vardis tries "keep walking through an exit." Unla Key, who has been watching the wrong guesses, whispers like a showman, "a passage?" Pandi leaps around the room in joy: "Well done, Unla Key! But now begins the rrreal fun." Pandi then produces a rainbow of shapes from an invisible pocket and asks for "rrrarrrtwork for the rrralls." Vardis and Thistle eagerly begin assembling the Tangram puzzle and Unla Key joins in; within minutes the finished picture scintillates and attaches itself to the wall. Pandi trades "the gift of passage" for it, and with another jolt Unla Key is outside among hills, with smoke and drums rising from an encampment just over the nearest crest.

From the hill crest the group sees Gralm's army: bonfires, tents, huts and ramshackle buildings, a sea of humanoids, and countless guards. Vardis doubts Oblivillish can carry off the deception; Thistle answers, "He's a spy. He should be able to deceive some guards." Oblivillish snorts, "I am a great spy," puts on his goblin attire, and talks the group through a guarded entrance. Goblins and hobgoblins glare at them as they pass, and Unla Key notices a hill giant under some construction but decides he must be mistaken. The guards bring them to a center hut over 30 feet tall, its floor made entirely of skulls, where the ettin Gralm sits on a ragged wooden throne. Orsik notes that Gralm is "a wee bit short for an ettin at only nine feet" but the four piercing eyes subdue his urge to be snide.

Gralm's two heads disagree from the start. The head with fancifully braided blonde hair says, "We knew you were coming," while the bald head shouts, "No. Less talk. More eat! Hungry! YOU LOUD! MUSIC EVERYWHERE! QUIET!" Prodded by Thistle, Oblivillish tells his story — "Sire, these adventurers saved me from the agents of Bad Fruul" — and Thistle offers an alliance, producing the SEER's contract for Gralm to sign against their common enemy. The bald head answers, "No more talking. Eat Fairy!" and Vardis replies, "that is not a proposal we're willing to make." Unla Key spots that the balding head is suffering from sleep deprivation caused by the Fey, and the blonde head confirms that "Since the Fey decided to expand into the Forgotten Forest, their music has kept him up all night." Unla Key takes out two pairs of magmin shells with powerful insulation properties and makes two pairs of ettin-sized earmuffs, explaining, "I like sound and am very particular to hearing the sounds I want, not the sounds other people want me to hear." The bald head puts them on and falls asleep; the blonde head tries the other pair, sighs, and removes them.

Gralm declares the party "enemies of my enemy and friends of mine," but demands "feats of strength and drinking," starting with a contest against Grindle, a massive ogre almost 13 feet tall who eyes the tiny Fairy and asks, "toy for me?" Thistle bristles — "no touching" — and Gralm orders Grindle off. Unla Key, who recalls that he manipulated the fates twice today, tries to postpone the contest, but Gralm explains that the evening's food is already eaten, "This is the entertainment," and the camp might otherwise view slaughtering the guests "as a reasonable alternative entertainment."

The contests alternate with Gralm's drinks. First comes Pixie Sangria, "a sickly sweet garnet concoction of my own creation," which all six contestants hold down. In the boar dead lift, Orsik boasts, "I've lifted fatter officers over my head," and fails; Thistle casts speak with animals so the boar will hold still but cannot lift it; Vardis and Wallace also fail. Unla Key casts reduce on the boar and picks up the tiny animal, and Gralm shouts, "The halfling wins!" Next comes The Everyday Ettin, a dark ale with hints of orange; Gralm watches for an effect, sees none, and sighs. For the brick wall that must be knocked down "with your feet," Unla Key volunteers amid laughter, has Orsik scream on his signal to distract the hut, and destroys the wall with a thunder wave. Grindle pouts and screams, "unfair! Unfair!" After The Ettin Grinder — crème liqueur mixed with sour juice — Vardis mutters, "I have had hazing rituals for city guards that involved less drinking," Thistle starts to hiccup uncontrollably, and Gralm looks relieved. In the boar dog eating contest Unla Key wins again by consuming 24 boar dogs. The last drink, the Harpy's Dream, affects no one, and Gralm smiles.

Gralm accepts the arrangement — "We know that Bad Fruul's power wanes as ours waxes. The time to strike is now." — on condition that the party bring him the heads of the forest creatures plaguing him. When Thistle asks which, the answer is "Why the fey!" Thistle objects that the fey are not his enemy, and Gralm offers a choice: "either bring me four heads for my collection or convince the fey to stop playing music near my tent!" Oblivillish whispers that "Prince Thornacious is a fool," expanding his sphere of control into Gralm's area, and that without a solution Gralm will not join the alliance against Bad Fruul and "the hill giant will kill the Fey and this two-headed creature." The group heads back into the Forgotten Forest to find the Fey.

The view shifts to Blasius, the Satyr, drinking from a hip flask beside a small pond with a giant eagle wearing an ornamental leaf necklace. A Sprite shouts, "that blasted Satyr Blasius is why I was banished from Thornacious's service! We should attack – now!!!" Blasius recognizes Thistle and Oblivillish. Thistle flings himself at Oblivillish to grab him but lands alone on the forest floor; Orsik's tackle is deflected into the dirt by Oblivillish's training in "the secret art of the Spra-Magaw." Blasius thinks, *"I'm going to die. Better dead than a chief of staff."* The killing blow never comes: he opens his eyes to see a sleeping Sprite and a smug Unla Key standing behind him.

After a swig from his flask, Blasius asks what they are doing there, and on seeing the sleeping Oblivillish slurs, "Ox, no! Thees is oblivilliss. No guonder he was so unchappy." He confesses that Prince Thornacious "wants to build a great empire" and even destroyed the Fey circle, while Blasius just wants to go home. Thistle keeps interrupting to pitch a grander forest for the Prince — "The High Forest with the Star Mounts at its center" — which Blasius privately dismisses: *"this Fairy is so ignorant of the court customs."* Blasius offers Unla Key a drink, asking, "Tell me this isn't the best alcohol you ever had?" Unla Key answers, "It depends on how much of it you got!" and Blasius hugs him: "for you, Mr. Key, the alcohol will always flow!" Then he weeps out the truth: "It turns out I was drunk when I told Prince Thornacious that Oblivillish killed a stag." Since the exile, Thornacious has made Blasius do "his work" — "meetings, discussions, action items, more meetings, decisions, recommendations, and follow-ups" — with no drinking allowed. If the party kills the Unseelie camped nearby, he can blame the dead stag on them and get Oblivillish restored. Thistle is shocked: "Unseelie here!"

Learning that the Unseelie brought warped instruments and play warped music, Thistle sees "A solution to all of our problems!" and Vardis agrees that dead Unseelies would solve several problems at once. Blasius leads them to a large clearing where dryads and seedy halfling men and women play cards over a large pile of coins, surrounded by large stuffed animals — lions, tigers, and bears — rotted with age. The ranger notices a broken harp in pieces beside one of the dryads. Orsik counts the coin and readies to attack, but Thistle, who knows the Fey cannot be trusted, wants to be sure these really are Unseelie. The card players curse each other — "May the false Queen Titania fart on you." Blasius insists, "See, I told you! The Dryads are Unseelie," and Oblivillish commands Thistle "as a royal Fey" to kill them, while he and Blasius hug and call each other dear friend and brother. When Thistle argues that dryads are not Unseelie, Oblivillish explains that "All that is required is to swear fealty to the Gloaming Court!", an organization sworn to the Queen of Air and Darkness and enemy of the Seelies. Thistle reluctantly accepts — "I guess they are playing cards, and cards are evil" — and is corrected to say "Queen Titania." Orsik, increasingly annoyed with Thistle's reluctance, shouts, "Let's do it!" When Blasius turns to Unla Key — "And you giant fighter?" — Unla Key replies, "Just keep the booze flowing," and Blasius hands him another flask.
