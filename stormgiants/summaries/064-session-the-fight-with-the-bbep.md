# Chapter 64 - The Fight with the BBEP

Date: 16 to 23 Eleint of 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
As the castle flies towards Silverymoon, Thistle remembers that Laela is near Ironslag and may be a prisoner of the Giant. Looking down at the Evermoors below the clouds, he mutters a private apology to her: he has to save Silverymoon, and "You’d never forgive me if I let the city burn to save you."

On the castle, Orsik tells the 3HPP, "We need some allies who would be discreet." The party runs through the list of people who owe them or have helped them: Darathra, "who owes for her election"; Lord Zymorven, who owes them for saving his son; Urgala, for figuring out what happened to the love of her life; Tamalin Zoar and her brother, High Captain Horix Zoar; the Hand of Yartar; the Zhentarim, who, Orsik observes, "like it when we do things for them, not for us"; and the rich folks in Mirabar and Xantharl’s Keep. Orsik considers fetching guards from Parnast or Silverymoon, then sighs, remembering Wallace, and says, "Let the folks in Parnast be in peace." He perks up with the idea of going to the Harpers and making the case that he is trying to redeem his family name from the damage done by "my son, Orsik, with a K" — but when the others stare at him, he laughs that he does not think Krowen Valharrow would go for that. He turns to Esclarotta and shouts, "To Zymorven Hall!"

On the 9th day of the 2nd tenday, the castle settles a few miles from Zymorven Hall. Lord Zymorven is not surprised; his scrying magic tells him whenever anyone approaches his homestead. He stands in full armour, muttering "I am the guard of the road!" and wishing he had his giant killer sword. He recognises Vardis, Unla Key, "and that charming fairy," but asks where the dwarf is. There is a dwarf among them who looks a little older. The dwarf pulls out Lord Zymorven’s giant slayer and introduces himself: "I am Orsik’s father, Orsik, and I have come for help." Zymorven says he owes the son a debt for saving his own son. The "father" explains that his son produced a play starring a famous actor who created a persona, Bob Saget, that was a demon, and that a real demon, or doppelganger with magical powers, killed and replaced the real Bob and now plans to destroy Silverymoon with a zombie plague. Zymorven wants to tell the leaders of the city; the dwarf answers that Bob would then release his horde, so they are assembling a strike team to attack quietly, and "The watch will know if we fail and face a weakened evil." Zymorven exclaims, "How noble of you! So different from your son!" Orsik, putting on "a masterful performance of dwarf acting", says, "Yes." As they march to the castle he warns Zymorven to stay only in the tower and avoid the night and the shadows, because "We have a vampire problem."

On the 1st day of the 3rd tenday, Urgala Meltimer greets Lord Zymorven with a smile that darkens when she notices Orsik’s father and the rest of the 3HPP. Once Orsik explains, she says, "I suppose we could have one last adventure." Someone then remarks, "enigmatically", that "Saving Silverymoon is a thing of ours."

Darathra Shendrel, the Lord Protector, is not happy to see the 3HPP. She had assured Krowen Valharrow that these were the contractors he was looking for, and it had blown up in her face; she remembers his tone as he said, "They betrayed the circles." She examines the dwarf, convinced he is Orsik, though the narration states that "the dwarf isn’t Orsik." He tells her he is here to redeem his family name. She lists the son's offences — she lent him her credibility and magic artifacts to help the heroes of the Ordning, and "He unleashed hell up and down the coast." The father seems particularly excited: "He did that? Tell me more!" Vardis snickers, "Some father you turned out to be," and Unla Key shrugs, "Kids." Darathra says the son betrayed the Harper’s circles and several agents were killed, and stares at the elder Orsik: "I am not sure if the apple falls far from the tree." She then realises he is just a father who wants to fix his son's mistakes, and concedes that the son did save the town, and that the information the 3HPP provided against Cindell prevented the town from changing its allegiance to the Lords’ Alliance. She tells Vardis she is pleased to see he has abandoned "that boat anchor of a miscreant", since as a Corellon cleric his association harmed those who believed in his god — and adds, "There was something off about his intelligence."

Vardis considers speaking freely but remembers "how much funnier it will be when she finds out she helped Orsik," and simply asks her to join them. She agrees: "I owe you that much for saving the town from Cindell. But after this, all of our debts are cleared." Entering the castle, Urgala asks why they are confined to one part of it and why there are zombies; Unla Key snickers, "It is an interactive experience." Orsik mutters, "There’s a vampire." Zymorven is appalled that they brought it next to his house; Vardis mutters that they were a mile away and it was sunlight. Zymorven insists the castle must be positioned away from Silverymoon. Then they hear a roar. "A dragon," explains Orsik; "A DRAGON!" shrieks Zymorven; "Technically speaking, it’s a Dracolich," explains Vardis. Unla Key starts to raise the plan where they "unleash…", and Orsik cuts him off before Zymorven can hear: "We agreed that was a bad plan." Unla Key is not dissuaded: "but was it?" Asked if there is anyone else, Orsik says he is not begging the Harpers, doubts the Zoars will help, and says the Hand of Yartar "isn’t going to mix well with this group."

On the 3rd day of the 3rd tenday, Unla Key — who has spent years studying the fates — is sure that Bob is not the cause of the world's end, and so he is bored; he knows the world doesn't end, "Unless he decides otherwise." He also knows that if they don't stop Bob tonight, Silverymoon will suffer a zombie apocalypse. He weighs the question of Kaelen, a playwright whose partly thwarted summoning ritual brought forth Bob Saget instead of a fearsome force of destruction. Unla Key's friends saw Bob as a pet to exploit for profit; Unla Key knew Bob was evil incarnate, but, as he would say, "Money?" He does not understand Orsik's reluctance to unleash the Dracolich and the vampire on Silverymoon — "The Destruction and Chaos would be – beautiful." — and muses that the Dracolich's chaos would cover an attack on Bob, the theater's destruction could be blamed on the Cult of the Dragon, and Laeral Silverhand, as First Lord of Waterdeep who created the coalition that defeated Severin and Sandesyl, "is a perfect patsy." He takes a swig of his drink and forgets.

Back in the present, in Silverymoon, he watches Thistle examining Kaelen’s house and offers, "I could fireball the place?" Thistle will have none of it. Unla Key remembers the argument at the castle: Lord Zymorven shouted, "Kill the young man? I think not!" and wanted Kaelen interrogated; Orsik said they had no time; and the argument would have gone on for hours had Urgala not reminded everyone that Kaelen's original plan was the mass murder of the entire town of Triboar, which the 3HPP thwarted, and that as far as she is concerned they should have killed him on the spot. Thistle reports that Kaelen is sitting in his house tapping his fingers, the place covered in dust, the door unopened in a while — "Something is very wrong with him." Unla Key tries "Fireball?" again; Thistle refuses, since they don't need to attract attention, don't know whether Kaelen is connected to Bob, and "The demon doesn’t know we are about to attack him." Unla Key sighs.

Vardis stands watch for Thistle. A guard on patrol recognises him as a member of the 3HPP "and a goodly race", smiles, chats for a few minutes, tells him to be careful, and leaves. Orsik appears from the shadows: "Fucking racists." Vardis: "Hey, I benefit from Elven privilege." Thistle floats into view: "I think we leave Kaelen where he is." Vardis agrees that killing Kaelen would tell Bob and lose them the advantage of surprise. Lord Zymorven asks whether they will tell the Lord of Silverymoon before they attack; someone asks whether they want to put them in a state of panic. Orsik whines, "but how are we going to get the credit if we don’t tell them?" and Vardis teases him with a mischievous grin. Unla Key points out they are going to make a big mess, they'll know it was the party, and Lord Aerasumé "won’t be too happy" to hear he let a demon summon a horde of zombies in his theater next to the royal palace. Orsik, in a flat, bored, annoyed tone, says, "Good Orsik wouldn’t do it just for the accolades. He would do it because it’s the right thing to do." Vardis wonders whether Orsik is finally seeing the wisdom of the "let it burn" strategy. Lord Zymorven tells Orsik, "My dear boy, I understand having to cover up your son’s failings," adding, "We have that in common." Orsik mutters, "Yes, we do have that in common." Zymorven promises to ensure Silverymoon hears of their "courageous, heroic sacrifice", and Orsik, through what Vardis imagines are teeth grinding adamantine steel, says, "That’s so generous of you."

Darathra waits in the darkness, thinking that she owes the 3HPP many things and should tell the Harpers, but "I made compromises and made choices. Not all of them wise." Thistle explains that Bob Saget has created a portal at the entrance of the Theater; theatergoers enter it to see a play while, in the real theater, he creates zombies "with the help of a Red Wizard in league with the Drow." The theatergoers, dressed in their finest, pass through doors that slam shut behind them — but they are not inside: "the portal is inside the theater." Unla Key casts dispel magic to remove the wards on the entrance — "Alarm spells." Inside, Darathra sees the horror Thistle described: a large rectangular building with a stage at one end, benches at the orchestra level, and a balcony reached from the back, with patrons watching an empty stage splattered with blood and the odd body part. The patrons all wear one of three repeating costumes — they are dressed like the 3HPP. The half-orc behind the concession stands has a broken neck, its head lolling unnaturally; another, in an Orsik costume, is a Tabaxi whose neck is partially severed. "They are all zombies." Thistle whispers, "That is what we said."
