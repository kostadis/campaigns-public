# Chapter 69 Confronting Adimond and a lot of exposition

Date: 26 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
Thistle stands at a door and listens. Unla Key tries to push past him to open it, but Thistle blocks him; the fairy hears nothing but wind and water and carefully opens the door. Inside is an armory holding weapons and armor sized for storm giants. Thistle searches it quickly and finds four quarterstaffs carved from sunken ship masts, a large barrel holding six tridents, an open crate of ten coral-encrusted rocks, and a storm giant-sized mannequin wearing a golden suit of scale mail. On the walls hang three enormous shields made from dragon turtle shells and six massive greatswords with coral-inlaid hilts and whalebone blades lined with shark teeth. The shields and weapons are not precious, especially given their weight. Orsik and Thistle debate stealing the golden armor: Thistle argues about the sagacity of stealing from the Queen, while Orsik takes the position that they won't notice. They finally agree that, although it is a profitable heist, they should conserve resources and avoid accumulating enemies until after they deal with Adimond Kroskas.

Thistle takes the door to the east, where three descending sets of stairs lead to submerged areas southeast, north, and northeast of the armory. Orsik turns left into a room full of submerged caves; four fully submerged caves hold sharks, and a passage heading northeast opens into the vortex area. When Thistle calls the sharks fish, Orsik corrects him — "These are mammals." — and leans on his headband of intellect to insist on it. Thistle, a bit of a naturalist, is aghast and angry, but cooler heads prevail and they do not come to blows.

While the argument runs, nobody watches Unla Key, who pokes and prods at some giant clams. Thistle notices Unla Key struggling to open one and offers his sword; inside is a grapefruit-sized pearl worth 500 gold. As Unla Key reaches for it, Thistle slams the clam shut: "After we deal with Adimond." Orsik, seeing how protective Thistle is of the Queen's treasure, opens the other clam behind his back, finds a crystal ball, and stuffs it into his Bag of Holding — the chapter notes that he will later act surprised to have found it. Unla Key then puts things together: "These pools are all connected to the Vortex. If we go through the Vortex, we can pop up in the throne room." Thistle turns to the 3HPP, and they shout, "It’s your moment, man, let’s do this."

They swim through the Vortex and up through the pool, surfacing in the middle of the throne room in the middle of an argument. The Imperator and Queen Serissa are stunned, and Iymrith — "or Adimond Kroskas" or whatever name she is using now — glares. Orsik charges and strikes the storm-giant-shaped Iymrith twice, his sword striking deep both times. The storm giants in the room are even more shocked when she turns into a blue dragon and buffets everyone with her wings. Vardis reaches for bat guano and sulfur, points at an epicenter that will only affect the dragon, and basks in the warmth of the exploding blast. Iymrith sends out wave after wave of dragon fear; all but Orsik and Thistle succumb. The dragon heads to the pool, shoves Thistle aside, and dives in, her last words: "This is not the end of this."

The Imperator and Queen Serissa recover and start demanding answers, and Mirran and Nym charge in. The Queen raises her voice with regal bearing and demands silence: "Could someone please explain to us what is going on?" The party answers all at once — Thistle screams "He’s getting away!", Orsik shouts "He’s afraid of us!", and Vardis calmly asks "You had a dragon here, did you know that?" — and she waves for silence again. Thistle flies to her and explains that the blue dragon is one of the leaders of the Cult of the Dragon and that they were tracking him down. Serissa turns on her sisters: "Did you know this creature was a dragon? Did you knowingly ally yourselves with a dragon?" More storm giants arrive; Mirran and Nym shake their heads and mutter apologetics. When Thistle again shouts that the dragon is getting away, the Queen dismisses it: "That is of no concern to us. What is of concern is who the dragon is and who you are."

An unattributed reply — "Enemy of the dragon!" — follows; the Imperator squints at Thistle, Thistle shouts that he is its nemesis, and the Imperator — over 30 feet tall — examines the 3-foot fairy and tries hard not to laugh. Serissa turns to Orsik ("Brave knight?"), who sighs and names the dragon: "The Dragon’s name is Adimond Kroskas. We’ve been tracking Adimond. He’s a blue dragon in disguise who has infiltrated your court. And we are here to save you." Mirran breaks the silence by claiming the party were trying to assassinate the Queen, and the Imperator cuts her off and has the guards take the sisters to their quarters; the Queen's sister protests but moves meekly away. Orsik then plays a game, asking Serissa whether her sisters told her about the date they were going to go on; the Queen's eyes bulge, she defers it — "We can discuss it later." — and then realizes he could be helpful to her. Thistle offers to help find her missing father, which the Imperator dismisses as "Nonsense."

Serissa turns on the Imperator, reminding him that he was the one who suggested they take Adimond Kroskas' input and support, and twisting the knife when he stammers that he didn't know: "Isn’t it your job to know." He pleads that the dragon confused them all and that his magics were powerful; the party each insist that they were not confused. Serissa calls for silence again: "And this is why we must ally with the little people." Orsik is annoyed, citing his time in Silverymoon; the Queen gets the innuendo, calls them bigots, says her comment is about relative size, and asks whether the small folk do not refer to her people as giants. Orsik concedes ("Touche."), and they settle on "non-giant". Serissa explains the correct pronunciation of the giants' own term for giants, but the 3HPP realize it requires giant-sized lungs, show little enthusiasm, and the Queen moves on.

Serissa explains that with Adimond gone, their attempt to create a weapon against the other giants to retain power is no longer available — a stopgap until they found her father. She disagreed with the Imperator but had no choice: Adimond Kroskas claimed his fairy/dragon hybridization experiments would create a new breed of dragons to use as a weapon against the other giants. Asked by Thistle whether he experimented here, she says no, and that the experiments no longer matter beside finding her father. Thistle raises his girlfriend: "My girlfriend is Laela, and she was captured along with me and Thuul." He explains that he and Thuul escaped as the cult disintegrated, and that Adimond got away with a bunch of people, including her. The Queen wonders whether Adimond was using them in experiments, and says that only her father knows where Iymrith lives. Orsik needles Thistle about dating "a fairy-dragon hybrid super-mutant"; Thistle insists "There is no evidence of that."

The Imperator then supplies historical context. Adimond Kroskas — or Iymrith, or the Doom of the Desert — has been conducting hybridization experiments "since - forever" is rumored to have created Drakes, and pursued the fairy-dragon experiments as part of a deal with Tiamat during the war of the dragons; when Tiamat's plans were thwarted, Adimond approached the storm giants, who did not know he was a dragon. Vardis coughs loudly. In a condescending, professorial tone the Imperator explains that before the shattering of the Ordning, King Hekaton was arguably the most powerful of all Storm Giants, presiding from this citadel over a court with representatives of every race of giant; Orsik checks whether the Hill Giant guards object, finds they do not, and mutters "Amazing." The account goes on: Hekaton came to believe that the age of the giants was past, with Annam, the All-Father, not answering prayers; several months ago the ordning was shattered, and the king bullied the weaker giants into submission to hold his court together. Orsik laughs at the arrogance and contempt of it. Queen Neri was fond of the small folk and urged Hekaton to respect them, until the day she failed to return from one of her trips to the sword coast; her corpse was found on a small island, clearly killed by small folk, and the king threatened vengeance on the Coastal territories. Princess Serissa, who shared her mother's affection for small folk, persuaded him to uncover the truth first. Years earlier Hekaton had judged his two eldest daughters, Mirran and Nym, unfit to rule — Mirran "tempestuous and prone to emotional outbursts," Nym "cold and unloving" — named Serissa heir apparent, and foolishly made this known to the elder sisters years before his disappearance.

Orsik laughs, and when asked why, says "There is more to ruling than subjugating other giant races." Queen Serissa applauds him. She asks what to call each of them — "Dwarf?" for Orsik; Vardis confirms being an elf; she asks the fairy whether he is a fairy — and Unla Key shouts that he is going to put on his horn because he identifies as a dragon. She laughs and says this is why they must make common cause with the Dwarves, Elves, Fairies, and Halflings. More somberly, she explains that her sisters were furious when she took the throne after her father disappeared, and might have lived with it, but Adimond showed up offering magical weapons and Mirran and Nym allied with him. Thistle declares that allying with a dragon makes them unfit to lead.

Serissa lays out three problems, the first being to find her father, and produces a token found on the bodies of the small people found dead with her mother. Unla Key recognises it in his thoughts as a Golden Goose chit used on the Grand Dame, worth ten gold, from a boat in Yartar run by Lord Drylund, who was trying to become the new Waterbaron — and he sees a way to make some coin. He twists the fate to make Queen Serissa more gullible and declares, "I have an idea, but I would need some money for the research." Asked how much, he says "50,000." The Imperator laughs ("Preposterous."). The Queen sighs that Adimond's demands drained even their treasury, then offers "the hideous golden armor"; over the Imperator's protest she gives it to Unla Key for his research, asks that it stay between them that it was melted down, and winks at the 3HPP. Unla Key realizes she knows, and she warns: "I am still the queen of the storm giants. If you were to take this money and not produce results, there would be consequences." The chapter ends mid-reply: "I wouldn’t dream of it."
