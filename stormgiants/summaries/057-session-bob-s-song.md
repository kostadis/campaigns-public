# Chapter 57 - Bob's Song

Date: 28 Eleasis 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
Standing on the deck of the Dragon’s Breath as it prepares to land on the Cloud Giant Castle, Orsik turns reflective: "We seem to have collected a lot of enemies." Vardis nods, but Unla Key surprises everyone by reminding them that they have also collected a lot of allies. He lists them: the new mayor of Triboar, Lord Zymorven, Tamalin Zoar, founder of Calling Horns, and her brother Horix Zoar, High Captain of Everlund. He adds the SEER, and when Orsik grumbles "We tried to kill her," Unla Key answers, "Unsuccessfully, and so we should add her to our friends list." He goes on to Wallace and his dad, Queen Titania, Prince Thornacious, and Uncle Joon, at whose mention Thistle blanches. Unla Key finally floats "the possibility of this fairy dragon hybrid that happens to be Thistle’s girlfriend," and Thistle is tempted to try to kill him. Orsik scowls, "Okay, then maybe I was too down on myself."

Vardis brings the conversation back to business: "The question is what to do about Silverymoon." The group toys with convincing the Uthgardt Tribes that they were agents of Silverymoon and rejects it; they consider implicating the Harpers and dismiss that too; Orsik ponders a Zhentarim angle and discards it. Delsephine shouts, "So are you going?" Orsik grabs the ladder and starts climbing, Thistle flies down, Unla Key flies down on his 12-foot wings, and Vardis follows down the rope ladder to the upper courtyard of Blagothkus’ castle. As he climbs, Vardis remembers the eerie song that Bob Saget sang, a long parody addressed to a "king" made to kill his jester and condemned to tell bad jokes forever, with a chorus beginning "Bohemian rhapsody is nothing compared".

Thistle, flying down on Thuul’s back, sees what look like dead members of the Cult of the Dragon and thinks "Good riddance," until he realises they are not dead. "Zombies!" he shouts. The 3HPP realise they can land out of reach of the slow-moving undead near the iceberg top that protrudes through the upper courtyard, and Thistle suggests, "Let’s go to the tower on the port side of the castle." Delsephine moves the Dragon’s Breath to drop Orsik and Vardis nearby; Unla Key follows Thistle, who notes that the mage wanted to cast fireball. The tower is ancient and crumbling, its windows sealed with ice, a balcony of sculpted ice 75 feet above the base, and its door smashed open and then boarded shut. Inside, Thistle finds exsanguinated corpses of orcs, and Orsik, pointing at a spot on a neck, shouts, "Vampires."

While the others ponder their next move, Unla Key has already made his: he flies to the upstairs balcony and enters. Thistle follows and peeks in, and sees a half-elven moon elf vampire smiling at Unla Key "like a cat smiling at a mouse." She screams, "You can’t be alive. You’re an abomination." The stunned fairy points at himself and begins, "Me? Look, I just escaped the Cult of the Dragon …" She cuts him off: what Severin did was not the Cult of the Dragon but "a twisted, devilish plot by Zariel to eliminate Tiamat from her plane of Avernus. She was an annoying office mate." When Vardis arrives and asks, "a friendly vampire?", she replies, "I am no such thing. I am Sandesyl Morgia.

Sandesyl tells Thistle, "Severin hired Adimond to create a fey hybrid dragon." She does not know where he is, and says of Severin, "But I believe in making clean breaks. They decided to go their way, and I decided my way." Thistle's attempts to draw more out of her meet ignorance and indifference; she wants to talk about the experiments. "Severin was successful, but it almost drove that fairy mad. She would transform between the two creatures. It was more a form of lycanthropy than a fusing of the two essences." She thinks the fairy looked like Thistle; he blushes and describes the love of his life, and she confirms, "Yes, it was her. Can you turn into a dragon?" Thistle tries and fails. She then explains that the castle is owned by the cloud giant Blagothkus, that she does not care who owns it "as long as there is a fresh supply of bodies to exsanguinate," and that she is happy to be the castellan.

Orsik is intrigued: "So as long as we provide enough bodies, you will leave us alone?" Thistle asks, "Have you heard of Silverymoon?", and Orsik is surprised at the fairy's dark turn before remembering that the fey can be very dangerous. Sandesyl calls it "a city full of pompous yits. Tasty pompous yits." and says she wants a steady supply of sentient humanoids to exsanguinate, adding that she uses her magic to silence the screams outside the tower. "How considerate," Orsik mutters. Asked about the master of the tower, she says Blagothkus is still there and "has been moping. It’s been weeks," and jokes about dining on him.

Orsik remembers the story of Blagothkus: Eigeron and his father came to the Eye of Annam seeking wisdom; the oracle told Blagothkus he could never impress the gods enough to earn their favor and urged Eigeron to step out from beneath his father’s "dark shadow"; in the fight that followed Blagothkus slew Eigeron and retired to his castle to mourn. Eigeron asked the heroes to avenge his death by killing his father. Orsik shrugs and asks how she became a vampire and how many spawn she has. "That would be telling," she says, and follows it with a threat of vengeance should they ever think her "an old helpless vampire obsessed with her necromantic research." Orsik nods in approval at the threat.

Vardis asks about the Ogres; Sandesyl explains they became part of her necromantic experiments once they were no longer viable as a food supply. She refuses Orsik's request to have the zombies perform in his next play. Rambling about her long service to Blagothkus, she says he once tried to ally himself with the Cult of the Dragon, which led to the First Faction capturing the castle; it crashed in the north and the First Faction captured it again; Blagothkus went to the Spine of the World to talk to his god "and when he came back, his son was dead." The First Faction got rid of her before leaving the north, so "I don’t know how Blagothkus got his castle back." As the 3HPP leave, she hands Orsik a book titled "Dusk," saying, "This is why I wanted to become a vampire." Orsik sighs, recognising the young adult fantasy series about vampires and girls, but thinks better of poking fun at a creature that goes from 1 to 50 in anger in an instant.

The group then heads to the tower in the stern of the castle, the chambers of Blagothkus. It is in shambles, and inside are a pair of ogre zombies wearing distinctive plumed helmets, clearly not under the vampire’s control. The zombies do not want to be ignored, and after a moment they are all killed.
