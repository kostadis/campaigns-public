# Chapter 33  - Rescuing Harthal

Date: 5 to 8 Flamerule 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
After arriving in Yartar, the 3HPP met their contact in the Hand of Yartar, Eltio, at the Wink and Kiss. Eltio was surprised to see them alive and even more impressed that they carried the Letter of Mark from Tamalin Zoar, so she gave them what they needed: "The boy is in prison, probably about to be executed. As for the sword, Brenner, a thug of a captain of the guard, has it." She knew precisely where to find Brenner — in the central market of Yartar, just below the Waterbaron's residence, a tangle of interconnected alleys that spread out "like a bowl of spaghetti," where each intersection had a store or a stall and everything sold in Yartar eventually found its way.

It was pretty late in the evening when Brenner finally showed, going from store to store demanding his cut of their profits for the protection he offered. The giant slayer sword hung at his side, and he carried the smug satisfaction of a man with some power who loved to wield it. Around him were several city guards who looked bored, frustrated, and annoyed. Orsik at first wanted Unla Key and Vardis to use fireball on the tightly packed group, which shocked Eltio, no shrinking violet when it came to violence: "Are you mad? Create a 20' fireball just outside the Waterbaron's residence? We'll be hunted down and killed." She paused, stared at Orsik, and said, "you're Orsik, the guy who does the thing." The angry dwarf muttered, "fine, no fireball." It was unnecessary anyway. All but one of the guards died.

Eltio turned to the heroes at once: with Brenner's death about to be announced, the authorities would tie it to the prisoner's friends and kill him, so the boy had to be freed that night. Thistle muttered, "this would be a good time for that convenient entrance into the prison." The Hand of Yartar corrected him — "well, not so much an official backdoor, more of an unofficial secret passageway through an ancient temple of Bane." The 3HPP were not convinced. Orsik insisted on fighting through the Waterbaron's house, "front door or bust," until Eltio explained the problem of assaulting a center of government and living long enough to escape. Even Orsik came round to the secret door.

The route began in the sewers of Yartar, along a passageway Eltio had heard of from her contacts — "I'm sure this will get you to where you want." Orsik and Vardis were unsure but went ahead. As advertised, the passage led to the abandoned temple of Bane, a large chamber with a pool of water in the middle. Thistle, Vardis, and Orsik hugged the eastern wall and then the southern wall to keep their distance from the pool. Unla Key rushed to take a dip, but noticed the water weird in time and backed away. Thistle, meanwhile, spotted three markings — one on the south wall and one on the closest pillars to the wall — forming a triangle. "Stop, it's a trap." Asked how to get around it, the answer was simple: "The other way." The party walked around the room to the secret door Eltio had promised, quietly opened it, and found a room full of very bored and tired guards.

"Can we use the fireballs now?" A few moments later the guards were dead, charred, and blasted. The last of them shouted as he died, "you have no idea what you have done." Picking over the bodies, Orsik and Thistle found a lot of silver coins, and Thistle muttered about a tradition in which the families of the condemned paid a silver bribe to see their loved ones one last time before execution. North of the guardroom was a room of chests holding the possessions of the condemned. The containers were trapped, irritatingly but not fatally, and the haul was thin: an unused great sword, a spell book with no spells but doodles, a note about a condemned man whose family took all of his possessions, a potion of healing, and some coins and cheap gems. The other chest was magically trapped; Unla Key dispelled its magic, and inside were more silver pieces and a glass decanter of rich red fluid, a potion of greater healing. Thistle scouted west down a hallway past a visiting room where families had met the condemned — nothing of value there "but the tears of sorrow and despair" — and found that the hallway led to the Waterbaron's castle, so he turned back.

At Orsik's urging they tried the eastern doorway. Beyond it lay a richly appointed room, out of place in a prison: bookshelves, several cadaverous creatures standing around moaning, a table with a skeleton, and a desk behind which sat a pale-faced woman. The sounds of desperate people and chains came from beyond the room's other doors. The woman asked Thistle, "what can I do for you?" and he asked to see Harthal Zymorven. She consulted her book, said, "I suppose I can have you meet him," and sent one of the moaning creatures — clearly zombies — to fetch him. When Thistle offered to go along, she refused: "No, I can't have you contaminate the experimental work that we are conducting on the prisoners. Every bit of air, food, water, and sentiment is tightly controlled."

When Harthal appeared he shouted, "Father, you came for me." Thistle was about to protest when he noticed that Orsik looked precisely like Lord Zymorven. The woman violently held the boy back as he ran towards his father: "He can't leave. We need him for our experiments. Experiments that have been sanctioned by the highest authorities in Yartar. Although if you were to provide us with fresh bodies, I could let him go." Orsik-Zymorven smiled and returned with several of the guards' bodies. This time the woman's fangs were unmistakable: "yes, these guards will do nicely." Thistle noted she did not call them her guards, and she explained, "the Waterbaron is under the mistaken impression that these guards can stop me from traveling among the citizens of his city." The vampire laughed. As Harthal prepared to leave, she whispered a warning that he must leave the city that very night, or she would have to kill him, and asked whether Lord Zymorven could find a way out of the city. Orsik-Zymorven remembered the portals and said, "yes."

An hour or so later, approaching the Harper hangout, Harthal recognised Thistle — "wait, you're Thistle Wendrod" — and after a scream of frustration cried, "your girlfriend is why I ended up here." Thistle was stunned. Laela had wanted to marry this guy? He could not decide whether to despair or to be delighted she was alive — but Harthal had called her Thistle's girlfriend, and there was hope. Harthal explained that Laela was the rogue who convinced him to steal the sword and go adventuring; he had only wanted some experiences like dear old dad. After he killed someone who badly deserved it, Laela abandoned him to go find the absolute love of her life. She had come to Yartar to join the Hand of Yartar, thinking they could help her find Thistle, but they were "too local of an operation." "And I really felt used." "You were," muttered Orsik-Zymorven. Thistle, as condescendingly as possible, told the boy that this was why he should stay in his castle and be Lord Zymorven, and Harthal, staring at his supposed father, said, "dad, I am so sorry! I just want to go straight home and become the next Lord and never follow some crazy Fairy that wants to use me to find the love of her life!"

At the Harper headquarters Thistle set up the exit from the disguise, announcing that Lord Zymorven had urgent business and would stay behind while his ally Orsik waited at the other end of the portal; Orsik-Zymorven, befuddled, agreed. Thistle noticed that the hallucinatory terrain shaped like a garden, with its "misappropriated fairy culture," was gone, replaced by a more conventional modern Waterdhavian look of straight lines and austere surfaces. A man appeared: "my name is Gauche the Young, and you must be the group known as the Three Halfpints and a Pint." Gauche is of average human size and heavy build, with amber eyes, a broad nose, and gray hair that shows he is not that young. His predecessor had been killed, he said, and the 3HPP were the agents Krowen Valharrow had hired. He laid out the history: after the end of the age of sundering the gods could no longer enter the world on their own, until Severin, of the Cult of the Dragon, found a tome with the aid of Tiamat containing a ritual that would let her back in. The Harpers were weak then and most of their field agents dead, so Ezra piloted a program of hiring adventurers with Harper funds. Gauche granted that "The success of the First Faction and Ezra can not be denied," but disagreed with the policy: "You are not Harpers. You are mercenaries." Only the success of Ezra's program and his promotion obliged Gauche to let them use the teleportation circles — "But I don't like it. Not one bit."

Asked what happened to the other guy, Gauche said, "Oh, he was killed by a lightning bolt." "A mighty one." The room Kolbaz was in had been destroyed "as if something substantial materialized in that room, destroyed everything in it, and then disappeared," near the teleportation circle, as if whatever it was had come through it. Whether anyone else had come through the circle was "Harper's business and no business of yours." When Thistle offered condolences, Gauche replied, "Yes, his death was a tragedy. But this is a plum position, and I have been looking forward to it, so it's – how sad and congratulations, I suppose."

Thistle examined the area around the circle and saw that something massive had materialized and dematerialized there, smashing walls, roof, and ground; the marks on the stone made it evident this was not the work of a giant. The papers left on the desk showed that research into Adimond Kroskas was the proximate cause of Kolbaz's death. One fascinating sheet said Adimond Kroskas created the ritual the cult of Tiamat used to first create Drakes — "That sounds about right," thought the fairy — and another revealed that Adimond Kroskas is an alias for someone else, not who he says he is. Thistle told Gauche he suspected Adimond Kroskas had killed Kolbaz, but Gauche was remarkably disinterested: "I tell you, sirs, Harpers will solve this mystery with the appropriate urgency and priority." Pressed by Orsik-Zymorven, he agreed to look into his predecessor's death but called Adimond Kroskas "a distraction." Measuring the marks, he mused that the killer was "demonic in nature or a wizard." Orsik-Zymorven mocked him — "or a wizard disguised as a demon?" — and Gauche, missing the bait, answered, "yes, I must look into wizards who masquerade as demons." He would pursue every possible angle except who Adimond Kroskas is or where he went.

The group emerged from the teleportation circle in Everlund into the Moongleam Tower, before Nespril Menk, a tall, coltish, gangly, green-eyed apprentice of Krowen Valharrow who speaks with the sophistication of a Chondathan. "The 3HPP have arrived," he said. Orsik, still disguised, was about to object when Thistle greeted him as Orsik and said Lord Zymorven would meet them in his hall; remembering that Lord Zymorven never left his hall, Orsik quickly transformed back before Harthal could see the deception. Vardis nearly laughed, thinking of his friend's new genius: "The sudden increase in intellect is like a suit of armor that you just bought and aren't quite used to using."

"You are Krowen's latest project," Nespril sighed, and offered them the secret exit through the sewers that Krowen, "doing the big picture stuff," had neglected to mention, so that the Zhentarim would not spot them. Orsik could not shut up, probing how the Harpers avoided Zhentarim spies and whether the "special random system" was written in a book. Nespril warned that Krowen Valharrow would be very upset if the book went missing, and when Orsik pitched independent contractors, Nespril exploded: "I am not a believer in Krowen's support for the Ezra model. Hiring anybody to do a job and saving the world if the check clears. I believe in the old ways where being a member of this group meant you believed." Thistle steered Orsik off by reminding him they still needed to collect the money from Lord Zymorven.

Orsik announced, "Okay, first we go to Danivarr's House and talk to Dral Thelev and give him Tamalin's note." In the well-appointed tavern Orsik and Dral caught up about their mutual friend Tamalin, whom Orsik praised: "she really helped me see how I can better monetize my play. Brilliant business lady." Dral explained that the Zoars are exceptionally well connected — High Captain Horix Zoar, captain of the army, is Tamalin's brother — and that the High Captain has never forgiven High Marshal Methrammar Aerasumé for abandoning his allies, so Zoar broke the compact and dissolved the alliance. Even so, the High Captain could get them into Silverymoon as diplomats of Everlund, "making it impossible for the local police to arrest you." The heroes then headed north on Rauvin Road, and on their second day, north of Silverymoon, they were attacked by some Crag Cats.

Meanwhile, at Zhentarim HQ in Yartar, two unnamed voices praised Orsik: "Orsik's a great agent. He was determined to find the Sword of Giant Slaying without revealing us." The other agreed that they could have simply told him where the sword was and helped him get it, "But he's a stand-up guy who wants to do things independently."
