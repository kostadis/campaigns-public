# Chapter 26 - We are in showbiz!

Date: about 29 to 30 Mirtul 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens with Orsik staring at Darz Helgar, and "the ex-con knows he will have to spill the beans." Darz explains how a thieves guild in Waterdeep works: a secretive group with a strict hierarchy, bosses giving orders, and informants inside the city guard. His own guild "was the Coffin Nail, and it was a well-oiled machine run by a ruthless leader who commanded a legion of skilled thieves and killers." But that was ten years ago, when he got caught; since then he has lost contact, and he is not even sure the guild still exists, because "We had a lot of enemies, and a guild doesn’t survive long."

Orsik is dissatisfied and asks, “where could I find the trace of this guild?” Darz considers the dwarf and answers, “I would go to Skullport.” Darz then makes a hand signal marking him as a member of the Zhentarim, and Orsik reciprocates. Darz warns that "The Black Network in Waterdeep is a bit of a mess", split into two factions, one of them said to be led by Manshoon. He tells Orsik that Manshoon was killed by Fzoul but cloned himself, that the story goes all the clones were killed, but that "I know Manshoon is alive and well." Orsik, whose intelligence was recently augmented, recalls an old story about a spell caster who stashed copies of himself around as a form of immortality, and realizes it was about cloning, and perhaps about Manshoon.

Orsik reminds Thistle that they promised Darz to keep quiet about his role in the crime and to deal with Kaelen Sarssir. “I’m here for the ride,” says the fairy. Orsik, Vardis, Thistle, and Unla head to the Talking Troll to learn more about the thespian's plans; Kaelen dreams of an "off-high-road production in Waterdeep."

On the way, Thistle walks through the situation: Darz was blackmailed by Kaelen into stealing from the Lionshield Coster, Darz wants the party to get revenge on Kaelen, and Darz does not care if they rat him out. Once again Thistle fills a bucket with water before reaching the inn. As he enters, Kaelen shouts that "he’s already in debt. Drank the money you gave him; now he owes even more." Thistle sighs and dumps the bucket on his uncle Joon.

Meanwhile Vardis and Orsik talk to the drunken priest of Helm, who asks, “Did you find the dead body?” When Vardis nods, the priest mumbles, “Helm spoke truly to me.” Orsik explains that the mayor holds the body while they wait for a cleric who can cast speak with the dead, and asks whether the priest is one. “I don’t like talking to the dead,” the cleric replies, then starts to cry about his old life in Neverwinter, a theological argument with the high priest, and his banishment to the Allfaiths Shrine in Red Larch: "I was a victim of temple politics!"

Kaelen asks whether Thistle is there to cover his uncle's debt; Thistle refuses, suggesting that "spending time in a debtor’s prison might be a good thing for him." The bartender then asks whether they will drink, or whether they might review his script and do a few scenes with him. Thistle is about to object when he realizes Orsik has a plan. The suddenly genius-level Orsik answers with a malevolent smile, “That sounds like a fantastic idea. But do you have a back room where we could act out?” Kaelen leads them down to the cellar, and the fairy is convinced "this will not end well for the thespian blackmailer."

The cellar is dark, dusty, and cramped, lit by a few flickering candles, its bottles stacked haphazardly and caked in dust and cobwebs. Orsik asks, “how did you afford this?” Kaelen explains that the former owner, "Ha-Ha Pinkerton, a short man with bright, cheery cheeks, always smiling", was desperate, sold it cheap, and then disappeared. Kaelen describes the scene they are to act: a rogue and his victim, the owner of the cellar, who is in reality a vampire. Thistle, after his unfortunate acting performance earlier that day, defers to Unla Key, "who performs brilliantly as the vampire."

As Kaelen is about to propose another scene, Orsik interrupts: “we’re looking for a special key, a key we are told you might know about, a key that belongs to the Lionshield Coster.” Kaelen turns pale, and Orsik immediately knows his denials are lies. Kaelen confesses that the keys are worth a lot of money to the right buyer, and that he wanted to sell them to turn the bar into a theater. Ever since he saw "The Inn of Shadows off Highroad", he has wanted to be on that stage.

Thistle suggests taking the key, or keeping a dive-bar owner in their pocket, or selling the key for "over 5000 gold to the right buyer." Orsik thinks it through: the Lionshield Coster would hire bounty hunters for this — "I mean, I would take that job." He tells Kaelen, “I want the key and a bottle of Champagne du le Stomp.” Kaelen protests, “That’s my prized bottle.” Orsik replies, “That’s the price of harassing my friend.” He then proposes that they become investors in Kaelen's dream, an arrangement that is really "more of a loan-sharking arrangement", though Kaelen is too pleased to notice the malevolence in Orsik's eyes.

Kaelen dreams aloud of fixing the roof and hiring "a special effects crew of magic users, not ALM, mind you, but still", and names a cost of "at least 5000 gold!" Thistle whistles, “are you trying to con us? That’s outrageous.” Orsik offers 500 as an initial investment, and a disappointed Kaelen offers to put them on "the leadership circle plaque", since "The founder’s circle is reserved for investments over 2000 gold." Vardis is about to object, but Orsik presses for a return. Kaelen says it will take about a month to fix the roof and put on a show, with money from tickets and from the cellar. When Orsik asks about their cut, Kaelen turns ice cold, and Orsik realizes how this actor could have blackmailed a criminal. Orsik tells him the party will keep quiet about his role in the lost key, and proposes "a 70/30 split." Kaelen answers “Of the profits.” The chapter closes the exchange with Orsik, who has worked with actors, knowing "that no production ever has a profit".

With the deal done, the companions go to Lion’s Share. The owners, Alaestra and Narth, are delighted to see their key, and Alaestra looks relieved. They do not budge when Orsik tries to renegotiate, and Orsik senses that their employers are scarier than he is. With their 20% discount, the companions buy healing potions and new weapons; Thistle splurges on boots of elvenkind that he must wait for until tomorrow, because the shop needs the keys to get new inventory.

As the sun sets they reach Urgala Meltimer’s Northshield House Inn. Urgala is curiously well-informed about what happened in the Sheriff’s office, because "rooms are not as private as people think in a small town." She cries for a moment — “I suppose that affair was never going to end well.” — then offers a free night with breakfast for the peace they brought her. Thistle pushes his luck by asking for her eternal gratitude, and Urgala flashes the temperament that made her a deadly warrior: “You’re a sweet talker fairy. But I was an adventurer for a long time, and I have dealt with your kind before. One night, a free dinner and breakfast, and say yes before I change my mind.”
