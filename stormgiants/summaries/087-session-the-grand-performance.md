# Chapter 87 The Grand Performance

Date: about 12–13 Uktar 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-15; recorded 2025-02-26)

## Summary
The party arrived at Iymrith's vast and imposing lair with a few giant allies and four war mages available for the coming confrontation. Their rescue scheme was repeatedly described as “one dimensional chess”: bring Bob down to Laela, exchange the disguised demon for the prisoner, let Bob monopolize the audience’s attention, and escape during the spectacle.

Iymrith greeted them with a reminder that many powerful guests would attend the following day. Failure meant someone would be burned to a crisp. When Orsik volunteered, she devised a punishment tailored to his ambitions: he would never die from a dragon, even if she had to protect him herself. Her appetite for cruelty also surfaced in conversation about a former bully, whom she had eaten and still fondly remembered eating.

She led the party past Yuan-ti devotees worshiping a stone idol carved in her likeness and into the lower levels, where Laela was trapped in a magical contraption. Orsik distracted Iymrith with questions about her worshipers and claims of method acting. When Iymrith demanded a script and a triumphant monologue, he became her director. His decisive maneuver was an artistic ultimatum: Laela could not rehearse properly while confined, and he would abandon the production if his vision were compromised. A natural 20 on Deception persuaded Iymrith to release her for rehearsal.

With Orsik and Vardis taking Iymrith upstairs, Thistle checked that nobody was watching and explained the substitution to Laela. He offered her his cloak to help her sneak away. Laela’s doubts deepened when she noticed his dragon eyes and learned what Iymrith had done to him. She briefly demanded the dragon’s death, but Thistle emphasized surviving first.

Laela also supplied the scale of the danger: the five heads of the chromatic dragon families, giant representatives, the Red Wizards of Thay, Prince Thornacious, and other guests would attend. They intended to inspect her before the show for evidence of magical trickery. The substitution therefore had to happen after those inspections. Iymrith had threatened to kill everyone else and keep Laela alive for a thousand years of torture if the promised transformation failed. Against that alternative, even the party’s escape plan seemed worth attempting.

Meanwhile, Orsik rehearsed Iymrith’s monologue. Her initial delivery was dreadful, but she enthusiastically accepted instructions to lower her voice and speak faster. She praised Orsik as a genius while repeatedly reminding him that she would regret having to kill him. He used the same insistence on artistic freedom to obtain access to her guests, hoping introductions would make them easier to recruit against her later.

Orsik flattered the ancient red dragon Flume with an exclusive preview. Vardis encouraged the expectations of Claugiyliamatar, an ancient green dragon who feared illusions or a disguised dragon being passed off as a fairy. She had invested heavily and wanted a usable weapon in return. Unla Key’s attempt to needle the ancient black dragon Obsidianth about his rivals instead produced boasts about trade dominance and an offended departure.

The party then approached Duke Zalto and his four fire giant attendants. Their presentation of items from his private quarters, supposedly preserved for him by Iymrith, initially strengthened his affection for her. They recovered by suggesting that her discretion actually concealed embarrassment and gossip. Zalto began wondering whether the small whip he had received was an insult. His guards restrained him as the party hastily withdrew.

Mind flayers arrived as another investment group. Iymrith refused to explain their involvement beyond calling them investors and appeared nervous around them. Questions about possible connections to the Kraken Society went unanswered.

That night, Uncle Joon appeared and admitted he had engineered the party’s approach to Iymrith so they could distract or kill her while he stole an item from the hoard. He had debts and obligations to someone of great importance, though he retreated from his initial suggestion that Titania was involved. He had been waiting for weeks and complained that the party had arrived during the enormous gathering. They overpowered him, gagged him, and tied him up in the lair, arranging for him to hold the prize he had wanted.

The next day, the allied magic users entered under the cover of being performers. Iymrith opened with lightning, the other dragons answered with breath weapons, the giants pounded their fists, and the mind flayers hovered silently. Before her speech, Iymrith forbade anyone from eating her creations, singling out Flume because a previous exhibition had ended in a bloody mess.

Her monologue celebrated impossible ambition and culminated in the introduction of her perfect assassin, the fairy dragon. The inspections had already taken place. Bob flew up disguised as Laela while Thistle returned below for the real prisoner. Instead of transforming, Bob told bad jokes. Iymrith’s irritation became a five-second ultimatum. Bob approached, revealed himself, and claimed the entire experiment had been his elaborate prank at her expense. The party laughed, booed, and encouraged the crowd’s ridicule.

The allied storm giants arrived amid the confusion. Their king’s presence initially offered the other giant factions an irresistible target, while the chromatic dragons focused on recovering their investments from Iymrith’s legendary hoard. The Red Wizards withdrew to pursue other plans; the mind flayers stepped back to watch, with nobody eager to attack them.

Orsik used Unla Key’s portent of 19 and his own Persuasion bonus to produce a 26, calling for Iymrith’s defeat and promising gold afterward. The dragons remained intent on treasure, but the speech redirected the allied storm giants from their rival giants toward Iymrith. Seven storm giants, the mages, Bob, and the party now offered a plausible force against her.

The session ended with the battlefield’s dangers established rather than the final fight resolved. Dragons and giants were converging around the hoard entrance, the cloud giants were waiting to see what happened, and stray breath weapons or falling rocks threatened anyone crossing the melee. Thistle was still trying to get Laela clear. Orsik had embraced the possibility that defeating Iymrith might earn the party favors even from the ruthless factions looting her treasure.

## Scenes
### Arrival at Iymrith's Lair
#### The party arrives at Iymrith's castle to fulfill their bargain and prepare for a grand performance.
- The party arrives at Iymrith's castle and is greeted by the blue dragon, who emphasizes the importance of the upcoming event.

- Thistle’s initial plan calls for Bob to accompany them invisibly to Laela’s cage, exchange costumes with her, and emerge for the apparent transformation.

- The dragon threatens the party, stating that if the performance fails, someone will be burned to a crisp — and that Orsik specifically will not be allowed to die.
- Iymrith’s tailored threat is explicit: “In fact, in fact, I will make sure you never die from a dragon, even if I have to protect you from dragons myself.”
- Iymrith worries that the party arrived only the day before despite requesting several days of rehearsal. Unla Key answers: “This show's gonna be improv.”
- Iymrith leads the party through her lair, passing a group of Yuan-ti worshiping a statue in her image.
- Orsik challenges the logic of worshiping an idol when Iymrith herself is present. When she tries to end the conversation, he insists there is time and invokes method acting.
- The party is taken to a lower level where Laela is held in a magical contraption she cannot escape.
- Laela is delighted to see Thistle. The reunion gives way to Iymrith impatiently tapping her claws and asking about the performance.
- Iymrith demands a monologue for the performance, expressing a desire to show her investors she has achieved the impossible.
- She explains: “I want to show all of those who've doubted me and all of my investors that I have achieved the impossible.”
- Orsik is appointed as the 'director' to help Iymrith practice her monologue while Thistle works with Laela.
- Orsik successfully convinces Iymrith that Laela needs to be out of her cage for the artistic vision of the rehearsal to be realized.
- His threat to abandon the production includes: “My artistic vision is being compromised.” A natural 20 on Deception makes the bluff succeed.
- Costumes become another artistic necessity: Orsik insists this is a dress rehearsal and cannot proceed without them.

### The Fire Giant Confrontation

- They recall their earlier infiltration of the fire giants’ lair, including the Duke and Duchess’s private bondage equipment. The DM confirms that the giants never directly met the party during that intrusion.

- Joon invokes debts and obligations, first mentioning Titania and then retreating from that identification: “Maybe it wasn't Titania.”

- Joon’s protests that the betrayal was not personal and that he could still help fail to change their minds.

## Spells
### Polymorph
Used to disguise Bob the demon as Laela the fairy dragon, enabling the party's infiltration and the switcheroo at the heart of their plan to humiliate Iymrith.

- During the act, Unla Key considers an additional transformation into a dragon to prolong the distraction.
- The table debates the idea, but no additional casting is confirmed before Bob reveals himself.

### Detect Magic
Anticipated to be used by the Red Wizards of Thay to inspect Laela's magical nature before the show began, which factored into the party's timing for the swap.

- Laela describes the inspection as involving detect magic and similar checks.
- The DM confirms the inspections are complete before Bob appears onstage.

### Prestidigitation
Suggested by a party member as a way to add minor magical flair to Bob's performance to make it even more ridiculous during the switcheroo.

- Orsik raises the suggestion while Bob is failing to transform.
- No casting is confirmed.

### Wall of Force
Proposed by the party as a strategic means to seal the entrance to Iymrith's hoard and trap the looting dragons inside during the chaos.

- Thistle proposes sealing the opening after the dragons enter.
- The plan remains hypothetical at session end.

### Mold Earth
Considered by multiple party members as a means to collapse the entrance to the hoard and trap the greedy dragons and giants underground.

- Orsik raises the possibility of collapsing the opening, and more than one party member indicates access to the spell.
- The discussion does not result in a casting or a collapsed entrance.
