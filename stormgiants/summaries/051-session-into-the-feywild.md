# Chapter 51 - Into the Feywild

Date: about 10 Eleasis 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens inside Orsik's head. Somewhere deep in his brain, a part of him is contemplating the nature of intelligence: "Intelligence was the process of inventing new ideas from observation. Wisdom is measuring the validity of those ideas against the world's reality." Orsik has suddenly become supremely intelligent, and he wonders whether that intelligence is overwhelming his wisdom, whether he is like a child who can see things without contextualizing them correctly, and whether he will become "a force for good? For evil? Or for Orsik?" He even questions whether his desire to age is wisdom or foolishness. But that is a thought for another time: right now Orsik is under a large tree, attacked by ghosts, and needs to focus.

"It's gone," shouts Thistle. Orsik rests a moment, then his warrior training kicks in, and he spots a human-sized shape barely distinguishable from the background and shouts "Ghost!" Vardis unleashes a raw blast of coruscating divine energy, but the ghost slips away into two arrows from Thistle's bow. Orsik burns it with a fire bolt, and still it is not dead. Vardis shouts, "FIREBALL!" Unla Key prepares the Bat Guano, then has a change of heart and strikes with a mind sliver, shattering whatever mind was there. "Two more to go!"

Mid-fight, Orsik's inner voice reminds him that if they had paid the guide on the ship 50 gold, they might have avoided all of this. Orsik answers it out loud — "We didn't get rich by writing a lot of checks!" — and the inner voice retorts, "You got rich by spending money irresponsibly!" Vardis, seeing his friend troubled, asks whether this is about the 50 gold; "Yes," grumbles Orsik, and Vardis replies, "Well, it wasn't real estate." Vardis then runs out from under the giant tree, shoots at something in the sky, and calls "Missed!" Thistle's bow strikes true, and he mutters, "Amateurs." Annoyed, Orsik throws a hand ax at the ghost to show Thistle who the boss is, and barely scratches it.

The ghost approaches the 3HPP, and the pleasant face of the dead Uthgardt chieftain is replaced by the horror of the undead, scaring everyone and unnaturally aging Thistle. Orsik's complaint: "damn it! Why didn't I get older." Vardis' arrow hits the creature, and as it dissipates Orsik bangs his sword against his shield: "Okay, one more to go, guys!" The final ghost materializes next to Unla Key. A spell from Vardis has no effect ("Bummer," mutters Orsik). The ghost attempts to possess Unla Key, but the diviner twists the fabric of time and space and the spirit cannot overwhelm the mage's mental defenses. Orsik advances on the ghost, which shows the damage of Thistle's two arrows — and there the chapter's account of the fight breaks off. A few hours later, covered in dirt from digging and with the skull of some Giant that radiates necromancy in their bag of holding, the 3HPP approach their airship.

The narrative shifts to Thistle. The Fairy is worried: Titania, the Goddess of the Fey and ruler of the Fey court, had punished someone for insulting her, and she wasn't usually the forgiving type — "Forgiving? Hah! Vengeful, spiteful, cruel, and heartless." The sky sparkles and a sprite with a high-pitched squeaky voice, wearing pink pants and an orange camisole, appears and speaks in common on behalf of the Seelie Court. It announces that the party has interfered in the ongoing punishment Queen Titania imposed on Helia and Halisa; she "commends your subterfuge and subtlety in freeing the two Dryads and is pleased that it was to advance a personal and selfish goal." Orsik-Thornacious appears, smiling and waving. In fey, the Sprite tells Thistle that it is not fooled by the trick the Dryads fell for, that Titania was pleased with the use of trickery, and that she can use Thornacious' involvement as another reason to keep him out of the Feywild. Returning to common, it delivers the sentence: "A Fomorian of the Unseelie Court has taken refuge in the upper lands. You are to investigate and deal with the problem."

Orsik-Thornacious whispers, "We can kill that thing?" Thistle, turning pale white, says, "If this is all we have to do, we got off light." The sprite squeaks that others argued for more drastic punishments, and that "the Queen appreciated how you dealt with your Uncle Joon." Thistle sees Orsik's intelligence and wisdom at war: his intelligence says he could kill the Sprite unnoticed and deny it, and he is about to when Thistle intervenes: "Do you really think that the Seelie Court won't know who killed their emissary?" The Sprite opens a portal — "In you go!" — and when challenged that the target was supposed to be near this forest, answers, "Yes, it is near this forest in the Fey." Orsik-Thornacious is about to strike; Thistle insists "This is normal, totally normal!" though his voice betrays his worry, and somehow persuades him through. The ranger squirms at Orsik-Thornacious' muttering about how "It's not like anything bad happens when you go through a portal."

In the Feywild the colors finally make sense to Thistle: bursts of oranges, yellows, purples and greens bloom into kaleidoscopic patterns that slow and settle into leaves, flowers, sky, trees and grass. "Home," he thinks.

An elven hunter appears and announces himself as Drauchanar, or Chan or the Knife, a hunter of rare game like the Fomorian. As Thistle accepts the offer, he realizes Orsik still looks like Thornacious, and Drauchanar objects that if the Prince comes, he will not be permitted to keep the prize. Thistle suggests, in common, that the Prince disguise himself as "something that nobody would suspect, perhaps a dwarf?" Drauchanar squeals in a sing-songy voice that this way "the Prince can guide us in the hunt, the Knife can keep the head, and all the forms of society and culture can be preserved!" — then "Follow me! A-hunting Fomorians we shall go!" Thistle follows reluctantly, unable to shake the feeling that something is watching him.

At sunset Drauchanar insists on singing despite Thistle's plea that it is "not necessary." The old elvish song, about the love of Corellon and Araushnee before the sundering, enthralls Vardis, penetrating even the nearly impervious shell of cynicism he has built up over the years. Later Orsik wakes everyone with "Where is he?" and accuses: "Your friend led us into the forest and abandoned us." Thistle scans the area and explains that the Knife was ambushed and dragged off by some kind of giant-like creature and a four-legged beast. Orsik wants to abandon the quest, but Thistle — empowered by the strange magic of the Feywild and his belief that pissing off Titania will not be a good thing — convinces him to continue.

The trail leads to a cave opening 20 feet in diameter in a wall rising out of the forest, with a cyclops standing guard. "Just when I finally need Harshnag," mutters Orsik. Thistle points to a waterfall, and Orsik is sure: "There is always treasure behind a waterfall." The 3HPP end up wet in a tunnel behind it. "I told you!" gloats Orsik — but there is no treasure, and he tries hard to disbelieve the walls.

A goblin in studded armor pokes their head out and asks, "What do you want?" Told the 3HPP want to kill a Fomorian, they squeal in delight and return with a much larger goblin with a nasal, squeaky voice. Orsik growls, "It's pronounced Treasure Chest," leaving the goblin confused, but Thistle assures them it's a Fomorian. The larger goblin introduces itself — "My name is Tlega." — and is touched by Thistle's flawless pronunciation. Tlega explains that the typical Bully/Bullied relationship with the Fomorian Maergun has fallen apart because Maergun has become abusive, not just a bully: "It's time for him to go." Orsik is suspicious, but the Fairy says, "I looked into their eyes, and I saw their soul. They are not lying." Orsik proposes getting the goblins to help, and Tlega urges all the Goblins to assemble to attack.

Then Mirk, another Goblin boss, appears, unhappy with Tlega's decision, arguing that the Bullied/Bully relationship is the core of Goblin society and that the line is just an imaginary line. The argument deflates Tlega and the other goblins. Before Orsik can try intimidation, Unla Key says, "I got this spell, Jim's Glowing Coin." Orsik approves — "Yes, that's awesome. That will work great." — and Mirk and Tlega, distracted, quickly agree that dispatching Maergun is the right next thing to do to advance the needs of the tribe.

## NPCs
### Orsik
Newly and supremely intelligent, and uneasy about whether his intelligence outruns his wisdom. Fights the ghosts with fire bolt, a hand ax and his sword and shield; jealous that the ghost aged Thistle and not him. Spends the latter half of the chapter disguised as Thornacious ("Orsik-Thornacious"), is tempted to murder the Sprite and to strike over the portal, wants to quit the hunt when Drauchanar vanishes, and is convinced there is treasure behind every waterfall.

### Thistle
The Fairy and ranger. Lands two arrows on the first ghost and later mutters "Amateurs." Is unnaturally aged by the ghost. Fears Titania, talks Orsik out of killing the Seelie Court's emissary, coaxes him through the portal, and feels at home in the Feywild. Reads the trail of the ambush, keeps the quest going, and vouches for the goblins' honesty.

Casts a blast of divine energy and shouts "FIREBALL!"; his arrow dissipates the second ghost, but another of his spells has no effect. Teases Orsik over the 50 gold. Is enthralled by Drauchanar's elvish song despite his shell of cynicism.

The diviner. Switches from the Bat Guano to a mind sliver to destroy the first ghost, and resists the final ghost's attempt at possession. Breaks the goblin deadlock with "Jim's Glowing Coin."
