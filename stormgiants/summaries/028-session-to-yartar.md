# Chapter 28 - To Yartar

Date: 2 to 5 Kythorn 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary

The chapter opens on the 2nd day of the 1st tenday of Kythorn, from Vardis's point of view, in the Talking Troll. It was "particularly active that morning," though not because of the patrons. Uncle Joon was still there, and Vardis wondered, "Did that guy ever leave," then laughed as Thistle dumped another bucket of water on him. The drunk priest was also still there, moaning about his new assignment. Vardis considered helping him, then remembered, "nope, that's not what I do. People need to take accountability for their own lives." Scraping bird droppings from his shirt, he muttered, "and the birds, the birds are still here." A parade of locals had come to offer the party information about where they might get help, money, or both.

As the party debated "Where to next?", Vardis endured it only because there was ale. His meditation was interrupted when Ghelryn Foehammer, the smithy, coughed and presented a sealed letter: "I have a letter of marque for you. At great personal danger, you killed a fire giant." The Dwarf allowed that it would probably have been safer to let the Giants be and defeat their allies, but called the party's refusal to back down from the hated enemy "a tribute to you and your bravery," and told them to "Take this to King Morinn or Queen Tithmel at the Citadel Felbarr. They will reward you for your heroism." Vardis took a swig of ale: "more choices."

Vardis stared at Unla, who was interested in the map. The next line, "If we could figure out how to make my tower a flying tower, that would simplify these travels," is not attributed in the chapter. Vardis thought "And you, more powerful," but only muttered, "choices." He reflected that the newly intelligent Orsik had to add more choices to the mix, just as the Dwarf said, "we do that too." Vardis told his friend, "you had straightforward plots in your head; now you have very complex ones." Looking at the map, Vardis added, "and don't forget Urgala's dungeon or cave complex or whatever it was in the Dessarin."

Orsik then laid out a route: "Let's go east to Yartar, then we can take a boat north on the Surbrin River and hit Zymorven Hall. We can go to Silverymoon and then deal with the Harpers." The others stared. He explained that "we minimize travel time, hit most of the quests, and are closest to the reward from the dwarves," and, confused by the staring, said "It's just the Traveling Orc Hoard Problem."

As they started to leave, Thistle reminded Orsik to ask their new partner when the play would be ready. Kaelen was non-committal until the Dwarf threatened to bruise the playwright's skin, and then agreed there should be a production in 45 days: "So let's say 5th day of the third tenday of Flamerule. We can use the Summer Solstice to advertise to all Waterdhavians who are coming to escape the crowds." He asked whether they could get Cindell to come for dinner, since "it would help ticket sales among the Waterdhavians!" Orsik snarled. "I guess that is a no."

The group then began their long walk to Yartar. It was a walk because Orsik could not convince Narth to hand over some horses. The journey was uneventful except for a raiding party of black-haired, blue-eyed Uthgardt who tried to ambush them, which "ended poorly for the Uthgardt." Someone in this section (the line is unattributed) remarks, "I almost had convinced them to run away," but these raiders hated Magic Users, so when Unla Key tried to intimidate them, it enraged them. Looking over the corpses, the narrator could see why they were so desperate: they had nothing of value. Their totem was an elk. "What a waste of time." Even Thistle's encyclopedic knowledge of the area was of no use.

On the 4th day of the 1st tenday of Kythorn, from Thistle's point of view, the party reached Yartar. Thistle remarked that Uncle Joon had visited the place, and described it as standing "At the intersection of the River Surbrin, the Dessarin River, and the Evermoor Way," a "fortified crossroads way point for caravans and merchants," renowned for river barge construction. Orsik interrupted — "I knew it! We can take a river." — and Thistle glared, waited for Orsik to realise he had rudely interrupted, realised that would not happen, and continued: more merchants were choosing to stay and set up at the Fishyard, and, according to his Uncle's tales, "there has also been a steady rise in crime and shadowy business dealings." Orsik smiled: "my kind of town."

"And first, we have to get through the YSA." Orsik told the guard, "We're just here to put up some posters for our play and hand out some playbills," and the guard reminded him that "this is a law-abiding town." Then the guard recognised Orsik — "Hey, you're that guy who did that thing." Thistle gripped his money tighter. Orsik smiled and said, "I'm not him." The guard agreed: "No, you're not. Orsik was a short dumb dwarf, and you are anything but. He would have pulled his sword and thrown himself out of Yartar."

Thistle handed over a playbill — "No harm, no foul! And enjoy our play!" — and when the guard asked whether it was "an expressionist, naturalist play, or is it a more classical production," Thistle, realising Kaelen had not yet written it, improvised: "it's an original production about the struggles of Uthgardt tribespeople facing the onslaught of civilization that is taking their ancestral lands." Civilization would be a river boat from Yartar, "the fastest, most unsinkable boat," destroyed "as a result of the hubris and callousness of the captain," with "a romance angle between one of the officers and an Uthgardt tribal leader." Vardis smiled: "it's spectacular. It's both well crafted and has a timely message." The impressed guard recommended *The Three Rivers Festhall*, "at the crook of where the River Surbrin and Dessarin River meet," and then, after Thistle's reply about sailors, the *Fishyard*, an open-air market with goods from Calimshan, "home remedy potions, luxurious fabrics, and more unique magical components." When the guard mentioned that Yartar was home to Lionshield Costers, Orsik — whom Thistle really wanted to stop — asked, "We have one of those in Triboar; they get around?" The guard's face darkened: their "use of pocket dimensions avoids the use of traditional trade routes," which hurt local trade and "local guards who need some money." Thistle concurred but did not tip him, and said to Vardis and Unla Key, "to the Fishyard."

In the market, from Unla Key's point of view, there were Calishite perfumes in shimmering crystal; home remedy potions brewed by Talni Barcoba, a red-haired self-proclaimed Wise Woman, "quite beautiful, despite a large scar"; and luxurious fabrics sold by Imber Chen, "a lavender-haired gnome whose clothes match his violet eyes." Orsik asked, "paper?" Unla Key whispered of a merchant named "Baash'a Kweelani" who sold magical goods for discerning magic users, bald with "bright flaming red skin." Then Unla Key noticed the food — fresh catches from the three rivers, and honeyed treats for his sweet tooth — and thought this a place where "the most fantastical dreams could be made real." "Not, too, end of the world, real," he thought.

Thistle mumbled that they needed someone who could tell them the goings on of Silverymoon, and the magic user shouted, "To Imber!" Imber, who has a clear Calishite accent, was startled to see a fairy: "why I haven't seen a fairy in months!" To Thistle's "Not my uncle?" he replied, "I did not know that fairies have uncles." Orsik rolled his eyes — "casual racism rolling off the bat. Lovely." — and Imber was offended, explaining he had read that they were "born through asexual reproduction in egg creches," and then accusing Orsik of "heteronormative assumptions about binary reproduction." Orsik seized the moment: "oddly enough, we are doing a play precisely about that." "A progressive play!" The date given was "the 5th day of the 3rd tenday of Flamerule," and when Imber asked the name, Vardis shouted, "Unnecessary," adding that "it is progressive, and it is at the Talking Troll." Unla Key admired the chutzpah of the two. Imber noted "It's a two days journey," and Thistle promised "a major transdimensional event."

Back to trade, Imber said he had plied the rivers for many years, among them to Silverymoon. Prompted by Unla Key's nudge, Thistle asked for news. Imber described Silverymoon as "a spectacularly beautiful city and long a powerful and influential member of the Lords' Alliance," said "Our Waterbaron has allied itself with them to stay in power," and called the Lords' Alliance "the only power trying to bring order into the anarchist chaos that the Harpers promote and the Zhentarim exploit through their taxation." Unla Key smiled a little at Orsik clenching his teeth. "And they all have uncles," said Orsik. Imber explained the city is populated primarily by "the goodly races. Humans, Dwarves, Gnomes, Elves, Halflings, and Half-Elves." "Half-orcs, too?" asked Vardis. "No, I said the goodly races." Thistle remembered his Uncle's account that Silverymoon likes to have the "right" kind of people within its walls. Orsik: "it's a cultural fit. I get it. So only humanoids." "No, I said goodly races." "Yeah, I am not going to use that phrase." Imber glared. Unla Key reflected on how progressives can be "so curiously blind to their faults" — a property he has exploited many times — and now understood the issue the Margasters had with Othovir.

From Orsik's point of view, Imber declined to take them to Silverymoon, but when Thistle asked "do you know a guy," he did: "Friends come and go. It's the enemies that accumulate." This was "Cap'n Squint", "a small halfling in a tricorne hat" who "barks orders at men twice his size and stomps the planks as if he were ten feet tall," formerly on Imber's boat and now owner of his own barge, likely leaving tomorrow. Tonight Squint would gamble on the Grand Dame, "the most famous and luxurious boat owned by Lord Khaspere Drylund," who, Imber said, "wants to be the new Waterbaron when the old one dies."

Imber then stopped Orsik from leaving without considering his wares. Asked "Paper?", he said no, but offered "this fine white cloth that you could easily paint a big banner on announcing your play," and an introduction to a member of the Hand of Yartar who could get them a stall. Thistle suggested asking someone who already had a stall, and Imber, delighted, offered his own. Unla Key asked, "do you work on commission." "I expect a flat fee." Orsik proposed a coupon code scheme giving Imber "a 10% share of those tickets and those tickets alone." Imber refused: "No. I want half a gold piece to have your banner up for the next 43 days." Orsik fretted about the "conversion funnel" and that "There is just not enough data for us to value this spend." Thistle recalled a magical dust in the Fey that could track anyone who touched something, but admitted, "but I don't have any of that here." The Dwarf shook Imber's hand: "deal."

Thistle used this to learn what play the folks of Yartar would like: many actors and action exploring the challenges of the mercantile class, and they intensely disliked dictatorial and hereditary governments. Orsik and Vardis advertised the play across the markets. Unla Key tried to set up a pump-n-dump of the play's tickets, and Thistle followed him. The two were surrounded by members of the Hand of Yartar, "A woman's only thieves guild" — three thugs and a powerful magic user, who explained it "wasn't personal, just business." Unla Key was not part of the contract, but running his scams without their cut was unacceptable, and they asked him to stick around while they killed Thistle. Thistle fled and disappeared; the magic user and two of the three thugs could not find him. Unla Key negotiated a deal with one of the thugs, giving him a cut of the VIP tickets at a much lower price to resell.

Thistle arrived in the morning just as Captain Squint was about to leave with the group, north on the River Surbrin.

The chapter closes with a section of Rumors and Stories of Yartar: the city's dictatorial ruler is the Waterbaron, currently Nestra Ruthiol, who has aligned herself with the Lords' Alliance to keep her power; the Waterbaron serves for life, and rivals work to undermine the position; the Hand of Yartar and other factions are growing; business among competing merchants "is often loss and won by rumors, sabotage, or other underhanded means"; and the Shields of Yartar, "a coalition of 150 mounted guards," keep the peace and reside in Shield Tower, "the massive keep to the west of Yartar."
