# Chapter 17 - Big Trouble in Little Parnast

Date: 1 Mirtul 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
Orsik returns to Parnast counting the money from the reward Hsing gave him, and the town looks worse than he remembers. He thinks, "this town looks shabbier than the last time we were here," and asks Vardis, "did this place look this dumpy when we left?" Unla Key yawns that "after a vision quest, everything looks uglier." Looking more carefully, Orsik sees make-shift shelters where strangers live and animals in hastily set-up pickets. "Feels gloomy," he thinks.

A member of the militia, called Orsik's guards, executes a miserable salute and pours out the news: "Commander Orsik, you know that army we were pretending didn't exist? Well, it's a bit more real these days." One farm has been sacked and people have fled into town. Orsik's answer is "We attack at once." The guard steers him instead toward Ragnar, who has been putting together local defenses in Orsik's absence — "Not really that successful, but he's trying." When the guard notes Orsik has been gone 20 days, Orsik replies "A little bit less," and, hating being judged, puts menace into his voice; the guard backs off with "No judgment, boss." Thistle asks whether any ettin showed up, and the guard turns pale. Orsik lightens the mood by shouting "off to Ragnar the Red," which sets off a running argument with the guard over Ragnar Redtooth's brown hair and his name, ending with the guard laughing, "Gods, we missed you, Orsik." Orsik pats him on the shoulder: "good job, soldier."

At the Golden Tankard, Ragnar Redtooth — brown hair, a large belly, prominent muttonchops — is bidding three men goodnight: two militiamen Orsik recognizes and a short half-elf clad in worn furs. Ragnar welcomes the party, calls to Elspeth for food, and she brings cheese, fresh bread, oil, a pitcher of ale and mugs. Ragnar explains that he has met "resistance and ill fortune at every turn" and suspects foul play. Meanwhile Orsik notices Wallace has slipped outside to talk to Elspeth, and smiles.

Ragnar names Gundalin Cartwright and offers 50 gold each for information. Orsik asks, "So you want him dead," and Ragnar answers "no information." Ragnar complains that Cartwright or one of his cronies questions his decisions every day, yet he needs them: "They are the most skilled craftsmen, and without their help, we won't be able to defend the town." Looking carefully at Thistle, he says the townsfolk "still blame me for the whole Cult of the Dragon nonsense," insisting he was "the man working from the inside." Thistle can't resist — "I wonder why." — and then mutters, "I'll deal with you later." Ragnar wants proof if Gundalin is behind the sabotage, and if not, wants Orsik to reason with him. Orsik is starting to nod off, so Ragnar turns to Vardis.

From Ragnar's point of view, Orsik is a good soldier, but Vardis is whom he needs now: "I need someone who has brains and judgment, not a strong sword arm." He warns that if the villagers don't work together, "They will all flee into the hills and be slaughtered." Vardis privately thinks there might be some coin, and that Orsik seems attached to the town's safety. Ragnar reports that the young half-elf Tove brought military intelligence: monsters are on the move, the forces constantly moving and destroying what they find, and Tove could not find their main camp. "I'm telling you an attack by Bad Fruul's army is imminent."

Asked "what sabotage?", Ragnar lays out three incidents. Tools disappear from the tool shed in the center of town, "A few every day, just to be annoying and slow us down." The day after the southern watchtower was completed, it collapsed, killing a militiaman named Donnal; one man thinks he saw a light near the tower's base just before, and the men blamed materials scavenged from Old Parnast that had been cursed by the Cult of the Dragon — though Ragnar asserts there is no more activity of that Cult here. And that morning, the cart that hauls materials from Old Parnast broke its second axle in a tenday; "The wheels and axle had been replaced by Gundalin." Ragnar is relieved when Vardis says, "we'll see what we can do."

The group brainstorms: Unla Key wants to check the shed for anything good, Orsik wants to stake out the shed and follow any thief, Thistle wants to check the watchtower and stake out Gundalin, and the suggestion is made to talk to the villagers. Outside, Orsik shouts at Wallace to get going; Wallace kisses Elspeth and promises to talk later. When Orsik explains what Ragnar thinks of Wallace's father, Wallace rolls his eyes: "Right, my father is doing his best to betray the town so that Orcs will take over? Really?"

Azam runs up: "I forgot to tell Mr. Underwood where his uncle is." Uncle Joon is in the town of Triboar, "about 400 miles away." The good news is he did not join the Cult of the Dragon; the bad news is that he joined some secretive organization that even the SEER doesn't know about. Knowing that Thistle, Orsik and Vardis might leave, Azam grows desperate and delivers the SEER's message: "the protection of Parnast from Bad Fruul is of paramount importance to the Lords' Alliance." Thistle, Orsik and Vardis realize the town would be destroyed if they left. An earlier Orsik might have gone, but this Orsik is loyal to the guards he trained. He asks whether Joon is going anywhere, and Azam assures him that Joon has set up shop and the Lords' Alliance would keep track of him if he left.

Screaming in the center of town cuts the conversation short. Three afflicted horses: one on the ground trembling violently, one dead, and a third staggering as Eryn tries to steady it. Chandra Stol, the local cleric, arrives and just stares. Thistle declares the horses "inferior beasts of burden to Drakes" and wants to move on. Wallace steadies the trembling horse, announces "My Gods! The animals must have been poisoned," and then, dramatically, "and that one is dead!" Wald and Eryn, the owners, roll their eyes, and Eryn, a childhood chum of Wallace, mutters, "the knowledge one accumulates as an adventurer!" Unla Key, using his standard investigative stance of one eye opened very big and one closed, finds "some evergreen branches in the horse's mouth and hay!" Thistle examines the horses again, concludes "yep, inferior beasts of burden. Nothing to see here." and pats Thuul on the head.

Chandra finally squeaks out her recognition of "the adventurers who brought me my temple and saved the village from those raiders!" She cannot cast a spell, but says Silas' wife, who passed away, was a herbalist and may have something that could help. Vardis sneers, "well, I have a spell," and casts lesser restoration on the horse near death. Thistle, surprised to see the dying horse stand, casts '*speaks with animals*' on it. The healed horse explains that they ate the hay, felt gross, ate more hay, noticed a funny taste, ate more hay, and got sick; the sick one moans, "why shouldn't we eat more hay? It's food. Food is always good." — and then projectile vomits at Thistle, who barely avoids getting drenched. Vardis shouts, "let's go find this herbalist."

Around them the villagers are in massive debate: kill the horse because it's the plague; it was poisoned as retribution for Wald's actions during the last Greengrass festival; tear up Silas' garden and feed it to the horse. When Thistle asks loudly whether anyone knows anything about the evergreen, the argument erupts again, and tension rises between the refugees and the residents of Parnast. Chandra whispers, "please figure this one out. We can't have a riot." Orsik can't resist: "I agree with those who think that it's a right of every law-abiding citizen of Parnast to carry poison." Another round of shouting follows over poison, antitoxins and proper stowage. Vardis and Orsik pocket the evergreen, and Thistle says, "let's go check out this herbalist."

Silas Carras greets them as the Defenders of Parnast and offers his "*wake-up sauce*" for the halfling, explaining it is the thing people drink in the morning after drinking too much. Unla Key instead asks for "some antitoxin for this venomous evergreen." Silas explains, for the n-th time, that his dead wife was the herbalist, not him, and that the Defenders are welcome to look in the backyard. His wife liked puzzles, and her garden layout hid herbs in nested enigmas from folks who didn't want to pay. When Thistle announces he will use his survival and nature skills, Silas mutters, "you'll need to think through this puzzle," expecting it to take a very long time — and is shocked when Unla Key hands Thistle the antidote. Silas mentions that some of his wife's bushes were trampled recently. Thistle finds a trail and chooses to follow it alone, while Unla Key stands up and shouts, "the evergreen is Yew, and this is the antidote."

Following the trail, Thistle overhears the farmer's joy that their horses are healed, and the poison-rights debate Orsik started still going. The footsteps lead right to Gundalin Cartwright's house — to the front door.
