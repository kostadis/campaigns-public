# Chapter 01 - There are no magic traps in that cave.

Date: on or before 29 Alturiak 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens with an excerpt from the Great Sage Konstantinious the 12th, framing the world after the War of the Cult of the Dragon. Tiamat had been defeated at the Well of Dragons, a victory for the people of the North won only because the threat of extinction forced the factions into unity. That unity did not last. The divisions that kept the Harpers, Zhentarim, Lords' Alliance, Order of the Gauntlet, and Emerald Enclave apart "reared their ugly heads", and the common ground "evaporated as quickly as the portal summoning the Queen of Dragons." Towns across the North were rebuilding, many having been destroyed or plundered to build the hoard meant to entice Tiamat, and villagers waited to see how much of the recovered treasure would come home. The evil dragons were defeated, the armies of the North too battered for war, the Cult destroyed and its project of the dracolich over: "In short, the realms were at peace."

Rumors persisted, though, that Cult leaders had hidden treasure away rather than handing it to Tiamat — a plan B, or plain avarice. The sage judged most such maps to be cons, ways to fleece a foolish adventurer or to trick the naïve into clearing out an inconvenient goblin tribe: "Oh, those goblins were allied with the Dragon and have treasure!" There was always treasure, but whether it was the Cult's, "Who knows."

Four strangers converge on the same prize. Orsik and Vardis had run out of garrisons, armies, city guards and factions willing to hire them; the word at Secomber, Zelbross, Loudwater, Llorkh, and Parnast was that "No one wanted to hire them." Parnast was "literally, the end of the road." When they heard that a treasure hunter, Veradaa Storr, had a map of Cult gold, they saw work guarding her treasure — and a deeper motive, since the two of them had a treasure map of their own and hoped she might reveal a clue about it. Thistle Wendrod, a fairy, was searching for someone — "he had to find her" — driven by conviction, by the stories his uncle had told him, and by a burning desire for vengeance on the Cult that had imprisoned him for a long time. A guy he knew, who happened to be a Harper, had told him Veradaa was the real deal. Unla Key, who liked to think that "Luck is what other people call my intuition," heard the rumor of Cult gold, trusted his instincts when others dismissed it, and set out to offer his services.

All four arrived in a clearing along the Black Road near Parnast to find Veradaa dead. Unla Key wondered, "Would they all come to blows?", and was relieved when the other three introduced themselves as having come to work for her as an assistant — privately concluding, "They were all obviously trying to scam the poor woman." Together they went through her belongings. Vardis reported, "our travels were uneventful, but there were rumors of Giant activity – rune-covered boulders, huge footprints, and missing livestock." Unla Key dismissed it: "Giants this far south is unheard of."

Thistle shouted "Found it!" and presented a set of notes describing five caches hidden by the Cult of the Dragon in the Graypeak range: a first cache in an abandoned cave at the base of the southernmost mountain, thought to be the smallest and "not thought to be protected by any traps or magic", in unsound tunnels where goblins are known to live; a second buried beneath a boulder pyramid on a flat field of volcanic stone between the two highest mountains of the middle section; a third behind a secret entrance in a green-sided mountain, hidden by a cultist who was "an expert in magical curses"; a fourth in an abandoned dwarven ore-treating outpost overrun "by something terrible and immortal"; and a fifth in an abandoned shrine once revered by giants atop the tallest summit, a dragon perch from which a bell can sometimes be heard. Debate broke out at once. Thistle noticed the dwarf and the elf talking in hushed tones about a map, Unla Key seemed lost in his head, and Thistle could not recall any story of his uncle's that applied. He reflected, "The group is still storming," "not yet norming or performing." Eventually consensus emerged, and Thistle happily headed for the first cache, since everyone agreed it must be the safest because there were no magical traps.

The chapter then shifts to the defenders. Sergeant Gronk, a goblin, considered his mission a disaster: "We came with two giants, one died in a cave-in, and the other is a tiny little creature, and to top it all off, half our soldiers are little toys." He ordered his goblins to hold the cave "until whatever magical curses we triggered end," and when he heard the group arguing at the entrance, he smiled and set his troops in partial cover. His chosen cavern had plenty of cover and a tunnel to the south for retreat; a giant lay buried in a cave-in in the east tunnel, the source of the dust that tickled his nose. In Gronk's telling, the battle went his way at first — "two of the invaders are dead", the pompous dwarf slaughtered, the flying creature put down — until a magic-user cast thunderwave and killed two of his goblins. His last thought: "Damn it," "will that giant ever recover?"

Unla Key did not die: "I am knocked down, but I get up again." As he watched the fairy flitting away down the southern hallway, he heard scurrying and high-pitched squeaking and found not rats but a swarm of goblin-sized mice with tiny scimitars and shields, which seemed comical until he saw how many there were. The swarm overwhelmed him and the lights went out. As the swarming goblins were killed, they grew back to normal size, until the area in front of the southern cave was almost impassable with bodies — a clue to the group that the curse on these creatures was not permanent, and that it might be wise to press on.

Orsik and Vardis, the "two anti-heroes", followed the fairy at a safe distance under their motto, "never be too close to the front or the rear." Over Thistle's head they saw a lantern-lit cavern where a small creature in ratty furs, holding a club, stood grumbling before a stone chest as tall as his eyes. A yappy growl gave the party away, and Orsik and Vardis recognised the creature as a tiny giant and tried hard not to laugh. It screamed, "Slayermighty the Giant will tear you apart and feed you to Blooddrinker! I'll be ruling all of Giant-kind soon!" It could not lay a hand on the group. When Slayermighty died, Orsik turned to Vardis: "Didn't even get a chance to swing its club!" The fearsome bear Blooddrinker rolled onto his belly expecting to be rubbed.

While Vardis and Orsik opened the chest, Thistle played with the tiny bear, and when it began to convulse, his quick thinking saved it. Blooddrinker, the size of a cat and "adorably ferocious", bonded to him at once. Then Orsik groaned that another giant had come first and left a note, signed by Clan True-Nimbus of the Clouds, mocking the "Stupid runt-giants of the hills" as "too slow and dim-witted to challenge us" and claiming "The treasure is ours." Thistle was disappointed, but it was not a complete loss: Slayermighty had carried two vials of alchemist fire, a potion of healing, and a necklace of 20 copper charms.
