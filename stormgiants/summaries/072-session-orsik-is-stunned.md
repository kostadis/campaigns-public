# Chapter 72 - Orsik is stunned

Date: 26 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens mid-fight, with Orsik stunned by a spectral Raven. Stunning, he muses, has been a rare occurrence for him "ever since he became super-intelligent": being stunned happens when you can't anticipate the other person's actions, and he now realizes it can also happen when the stupidity of someone's actions stuns you. In this case it is neither, he reminds himself; the spectral Raven has simply stunned him, and all he can do is ask himself, "Where did it go wrong?"

Frozen in place, Orsik reflects on his life. His decision to become a fighter, he thinks, was less a decision than a natural outcome of his physical traits: he was strong, obstinate, and not particularly clever. Now that he is intelligent, he sees the limits of the profession — "His job is to go into the breach, smash, kill, and be targeted while other people do the clever stuff." He mocks it in his head: "Woot! I killed the creature with my sword by standing next to it! Woot! I am so cool." He remembers the joy of being the most challenging, brutal, and dangerous monster in any room, and he now understands the phrase "Drag them down to your level and beat them with experience."

While Orsik reflects, Thistle flits about and kills a specter with his arrows. Unla Key, also stunned, is unconcerned even as a figure detaches itself from the shadows and glides toward him — "weightless, formless, undead" — and saps his life force as it flows through him. From the corner of his eye Unla Key sees that Orsik is also being targeted by this variation of the specter, and watches it flow through more of the 3HPP.

Then the Raven emits a soul-chilling cry that induces despair. Thistle, ordinarily resistant to such evil, is not this time: the cry reminds him of his lost love and of the despair he has felt over the years looking for her. Terrified and with trembling hands, he still manages to strike down the Raven with his bow. The despair lifts, and Thistle redoubles his efforts to find his lost love. Orsik notices the flash of a fireball, and the searing flames jolt his consciousness free. He stands ready to do what fighters do — "Except they are all dead." The rest of the group stares at him smugly, as if to say he is not really needed. "Orsik knows better."

With the undead gone, the 3HPP looks around. "This was the kitchen," remarks Unla Key. Thistle notices a note; Vardis asks whether it is a recipe. It is a rant: Sandesyl was accused of betrayal and treachery. When the doubt is raised that vampires can create specters, Unla Key inserts casually, "A specter is created when the soul of a creature is ripped from it while they still live." Someone answers, "That sounds like what Sandesyl would do." Thistle turns pale as he realizes Sandesyl is not a dilettante vampire obsessed with Romance Novels — "She's a disciple of Sammaster." He explains that Sammaster was chosen by Mystra and originally figured out how to make Dracoliches, and that Uncle Joon told him some members of the Cult of the Dragon pursued research into other magical processes for creating undead. Unla Key mutters, "Not part of the end game, so not interesting." Asked what end-game, he answers, "THE END GAME."

Vardis finds a magical gem and hands it to the wizard. "A gem of elemental summoning," says Unla Key as he pockets it. When Vardis asks whether they are going to take Sandesyl down, Unla Key announces, "Not now": although she isn't part of the end game prophecy, his intuition about bad plans is "screaming bright red – bad idea." Orsik is pleased — he had been stunned that Vardis asked Unla Key at all, "the kind of stunning thing that happens when you hear something stupid."

Unla Key then rushes from the Kitchen into the unlit barracks, and Orsik is stunned again: "From the unlit Kitchen to the Unlit Barracks…" Vardis observes, "Look, we have money, and we can always get a raise dead spell," then thinks, "Unless you happen to be a cleric banned from his temples. But that's what money is for." Unla Key returns sheepishly, realizing he had already explored the barracks earlier, and adds, "And it's not like we must go to the 'good' clerics." Orsik asks, "But will they raise or animate you?" and Thistle replies, "Great question," "I guess you find out after the fact?"

North of the barracks lies a series of rooms. One holds dead stone giants who were obviously the cooks for the giants. Another holds rotten food, but Orsik finds a bottle of Blackstaff wine and cannot resist sharing his recently acquired fine wine expertise: it is a wine from Waterdeep with "a big, bright, and unbalanced flavor," said to capture the spirit of the City of Splendors and exported to the Western Heartlands, the lands along the River Chionthar, and Baldur's Gate "as of the late 15th century, Dale Reckoning."

Across the hall from the pantry is a room containing half of the dragon's remains, wearing the distinctive purple garb of a Wyrmspeaker, the inner circle of the Cult of the Dragon — clearly the Black Dragon Wyrmspeaker. Thistle remembers stories: "She's Rezmir." Rumor had it she was in charge of this castle and responsible for moving the hoard that was part of the ritual, and that she was killed by the heroes of the War of the Dragons — though others speculated she ran afoul of internal politics. Orsik finds a note that bolsters the internal-dispute theory. Its writer protests being audited — "The idea that I need to be audited beggars' belief" — calls the auditors who infiltrated the Black Dragon Hatchery spies, insists "The five heads of Tiamat do not attack each other and do not distrust each other. They are one and five at once," and demands that any future audit "must involve all of us or be considered a trick by our enemies." Orsik scours the room for treasure but finds nothing.

"So now what?" asks Vardis. The answer reasons that the glacier holds a lot of ice outside the caves the Dracolich uses, so "Sandesyl must have carved her lair into the ice," which would explain how she could take control of the castle and show up wherever she wants. Turning to Unla Key, the speaker agrees the mage is right — they need to rest up; Sandesyl will not let them raid her lair and retreat, they will have to get rid of the Ogre Zombies, "But once we do that, then she's really going to be pissed off."

The group returns to the castle's control tower. The crystals and runes spin into the shape of a female cloud giant's head whose rune-formed mouth speaks, the sound coming from everywhere. "I've been working on my avatar. Not quite what I want," declares Esclarotta. She asks whether they removed the undead infestation and, before they can answer, remarks that the undead were the products of Sandesyl's necromantic experiments. Blagothkus jumps in: "I just want to make it clear that this isn't my fault." Esclarotta sighs that they had discussed precisely this — taking accountability for his actions and decisions.

Esclarotta then tells the story. After the War of the Dragons her husband became obsessed with the role of giants; she was crashed into a glacier in Miklos, and her "worthless husband" could not get the help of the Frost Giant Jarl to free her. The heroes of the War of the Dragons freed her. For a while she was under the control of the Frost Giants, who used her to defeat the dragons at the final battle "in the Well of Dragons above the summoned temple of Tiamat." They thought Sandesyl had fled the castle; she had not, and once the cult was destroyed she transformed the new occupants into undead — "You met the Jarl, I believe. He was a skeletal giant you destroyed?" Sandesyl could not control the castle or Esclarotta, and so agreed to find her husband, stuck on the Miklos glacier. Instead of getting over his "ludicrous megalomania," Blagothkus visited the Oracle, where the All-Father said he was a failure and that their son was the leader the giants needed — and all their son had to do was kill his Father. "Our son failed," she says, and in her grief they ended up stranded where the party found them; she refused to move, because her castle was infested with undead and her son was dead. The runes reform into a floating fist pointing at the Cloud Giant: "and my husband was a fool."

Thistle is tempted to ask why Esclarotta is obsessed with destroying the Cloud Giant Countess, and the answer comes anyway: "And to top it all off, Countess Sansuri was making a play for the Ordning!" — a "classless poseur of a magic-wielding cloud giant" as QUEEN of all giants. "THIS IS ALL HIS FAULT!" Unla Key observes, "He made a deal with the devil," and is corrected: "No, a Vampire. Tiamat was the Devil. Severin was the one who made the deal with the Devil." Thistle feels awkward ("It's not like you want a devil running a play"); Orsik adds that folks sometimes make deals with devils without realizing it — "They don't understand every clause in the contract" — and Thistle continues, "They audition for the part, and you don't know." "People have to be forgiven for these kinds of things," says Orsik soothingly.

The runes return to the control panel. Esclarotta mutters, "Forgiveness is hard. But you are right." Then the fist and finger return: "Are you going to do anything other than sit on your fat ass?" Blagothkus stares: "Stand?" As the couple settles into another standoff, Orsik intervenes and explains to Esclarotta that he has learned that sometimes direct action is necessary to deal with the mistakes of the past, and that just watching and praying is not enough. She agrees, and informs the 3HPP that Blagothkus will help them defeat Sandesyl.
