# Chapter 34 3HPP Productions

Date: 8 to 13 Flamerule 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
On the Rauvin Road to Zymorven Hall north of Silverymoon, the 3HPP escort Harthal Zymorven home. Four crag cats, which take the group for "a father with his three children", crash through the underbrush and surround them. Harthal hides behind Vardis. One cat pounces at Thuul and lands inches short; the guard Drake hisses. Another white cat bites Thistle, damaging his armor – "nothing that can’t be fixed." Vardis scorches the cats with his rays and then summons the light of Corellon to blind one, which swings its paws uselessly. Unla Key seems distracted, looking at the stars, until Harthal screams at him; the wizard answers "I am." Orsik and Unla Key then kill the remaining cats with well-timed blows, and Harthal stares at Unla Key in awe. When Thistle mutters "I think Uncle Joon said these are worth something," Orsik objects that they are hours from a thousand gold pieces and asks why anyone would carve up an overgrown cat. The crag cat ends up in their bags anyway. Thistle announces that he will fly ahead, and Orsik covers for him by saying Harthal is doing some scouting, which relieves Harthal after the last incident.

At Zymorven Hall, Lord Zymorven is surprised to see Thistle Wendrod and saddened that he is alone. Thistle, annoyed at the interruptions, explains that they had to spring the son from prison, that he was on death row for murder, that an ally was disguised as Lord Zymorven to convince him to come, and that it was not really a breakout but a bargain with a vampire who was the jailer. Lord Zymorven takes it well: "Well, all is well that ends well. If my son comes, I will give you your reward." He supposes he must avoid visiting Yartar for a while; Thistle advises only avoiding the vampire, and he laughs. A few hours later Harthal and the 3HPP arrive. Lord Zymorven is delighted, praises "a good old fashioned prison break" with a wink, and hands Thistle the reward. Seeing Orsik carrying the sword, he says, "may that sword bring you better luck than it brought my son."

A few days later, on the Rauvin Road north of Everlund and south of Silverymoon, the 3HPP meet a group of orcs. Orsik tells Vardis they don't need to talk to them, but Thistle approaches. A matronly-looking orc calls him a fairy, and another orc asks whether he knows a good place for a homestead, since the giants forced them to pick up and leave. When Thistle repeats "a homestead?", the orc asks whether he subscribes to "that Silverymoon nonsense", and Thistle blushes. The orcs ask to let their children see him; the young orcs are delighted and Thistle performs flying tricks. A teenage-looking orc challenges him to shoot a tossed rock. Thistle misses, his fingers slip and the arrows fall to the ground, and the teenager shouts "I AM THE CHAMPION!" The matron winks, thinking he failed on purpose, but Thistle is flabbergasted. The chapter notes that the young orc later became well known among the local tribes and Thistle became known as "the fairy that couldn’t fire." The matron gives Thistle jam; he blurts "orc jam?" and she is offended: "They really have you confused about Orcs."

A few days after that, on the road to Yartar on the Evermoor Way, the 3HPP see another band of Uthgardt barbarians. They are almost spotted, but Unla Key tricks the barbarians and so overcomes the loud noises made by Vardis and Orsik, who were both trying hard not to be noticed.

On the 3rd day of the 2nd Tenday of Flamerule the group reaches Triboar, which is overrun with people; the inns are overflowing and rich folks from the outlying ranches stroll about with bodyguards. Orsik thinks of finding old friends, decides that might be a bad idea, and disguises himself magically. At the Talking Troll, where their business partner Kaelen is supposed to have been working on their play, Thistle judges the production a coming disaster: the sets are barely painted and oddly shaped, and the actors struggle with staying awake and sober. Hearing one line, he says even Prince Thornacious would demand exile, "at the very least." A note bearing a hand stencil and the message "Meet me outside." is dropped into Unla Key’s hands. Thistle asks whether this is the Hand of Yartar that tried to assassinate him; Orsik says "they were framed," and Thistle grumbles "they would say that."

Before they can leave, Urgala approaches. Thistle explains to Orsik who she is, and she invites them all to her Tavern; Orsik, confused, thinks she wants to see his sword. At the Northshield House, repaired after the damage the magmin did, she tells them over tea that Kaelen has borrowed 5000 gold pieces, saying the party were good for it. If he cannot repay, the Hand of Yartar, the Zhentarim, and some local criminals might not forgive him, and once they deal with Kaelen they will come for the party. Orsik proposes that they charm everyone and make the play a success; Urgala laughs that they would need "an entire month of sold-out performances." When they admit they do surrender, she says she likes to think her good friend Lord Zymorven did not make a mistake in giving his sword to them. She says the play can be fixed with new writing, plot, acting and directing, and that a magical item, Chancellor Geoffrey’s Invitation, can create a background set with animations and music that seem almost real, lasting longer than an illusion spell and needing no mage.
