# Chapter 83 - Choices are made.

Date: about 1 to 8 Uktar 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
King Hekaton rushes after Unla Key, who has entered the teleportation chamber behind the throne room. The halfling wizard can't wait to return to his cloud castle and go after the ancient blue dragon, thinking, "There is treasure to be had and excitement to be found." The rest of the 3HPP arrive a few moments later with Imperator Serissa, who asks them, "I can’t convince you to take care of the problem that affects both our people?"

Orsik realizes he could do the heroic thing: save the Giant King, score the Giant Princess, and perhaps someday become the Giant King himself. But he wonders whether that would make him a hero, and answers simply, "No." His personal honor does not permit it. He must kill Iymrith or die in the attempt, "because heroes kill dragons and save girlfriends."

The Imperator sighs and attempts to teleport the heroes back to their cloud castle. Nothing happens. Hekaton thinks, then mutters, "Unfortunately, something is blocking the teleport spell," adding, "I fear someone has cast forbiddance." Unla Key is unconvinced and demands to take control of the teleportation chamber. For a moment he ponders whether he should teleport into Waterdeep's treasury chambers instead, but decides against it. He reports, "Yup, most of the castle is blocked. Someone or something has made it impossible for us to get to the castle except for some parts of it." Thistle protests, "That's impossible. They would need a very powerful cleric and to have the cleric on hand. We haven’t been that far away for that long."

Hekaton asks Thistle whether the Cult of the Kraken knew about their castle, warning that it "has access to mysterious powers and magic." Unla Key privately recalls how they brought their castle to Yartar and attacked a high-ranking member of the Kraken Society, who escaped; every member of that society probably knew about the castle. He wonders, "But how could they have sent a message from so far away?" and remembers how Lord Drylund died, as if something had triggered it, which leads him to suspect the members of the Kraken are interlinked in some kind of hive mind. He decides the King doesn't need to know this, since the decision to show the castle to the world wasn't his idea, and the King might think it's his fault for not stopping Orsik, whose idea it was. He turns to the King and says, "No." Serissa observes that the party "were kept on a ship we could not find, despite the best of divination magic I could pay for," and that "The Kraken Society knows how to hide things." Orsik declares, "We will rest and head out in the morning."

In the morning, King Hekaton teleports them just outside the great doors of Ironslag. Orsik is not tempted to find his lost treasure, "because that’s the obvious thing to do," nor to save the King's kingdom, "because that’s the thing anyone would do." From Ironslag the 3HPP head through the Silver Marches to Fork Road, bound for the town of Ascore at its end. The chapter recounts that Ascore was once a port city of the dwarven nation of Delzoun on the northern shores of the Narrow Sea during the Netherese Empire, but by the 14th-century DR was "a lonely, windswept ruin on the edge of the Anauroch, near the Frozen Sea, reputed to be haunted by ancient evil." Thistle has explained that the desert Anauroch is the side effect of the actions of the mad king who brought down the Kingdom of Netheril. Vardis finds it interesting that the 3HPP's story began near Anauroch in Parnast and will now end, one way or another, in Anauroch. From Ascore, Iymrith's lair is close by.

On the road they meet merchants from Citadel Adbar who pass on rumors that the Uthgardt Barbarians are raiding and that the Giants are on the prowl; the 3HPP express confusion and doubt. When the merchants say they are going to Silverymoon, Thistle is horrified: "That’s a very bad place. They have cults to Asmodeus! They had a horde of Zombies that we saved them from! There is a demon running, or was running a play! Don’t go there." The caravan driver turns to Orsik and asks, "He’s not a goodly race, eh?" The guard beside the driver looks the young, brash dwarf over and asks, "Are you Orsik?" Orsik answers, "No it was my son."

A few days later, as the party prepares to sleep, the SEER appears with her gold pseudo dragon, Hsing, wanting to make a deal. Orsik growls, "How did you find us?" She explains that Hekaton, a friend of the Lords’ Alliance, was willing to share the information she needed, and when Orsik says it is impossible for her to show up in exactly the right place, she sighs, "It’s called Divination." Hekaton, she says, trusts them and sees them as "heroic," and would consider a permanent alliance between the King of the Giants and the Lords’ Alliance if they dealt with his problem. Orsik mutters, "Not interested." She offers to make him a hero instead of "the annoying prat"; he laughs, "A mass hypnosis?" She replies that she will claim it was all an elaborate ruse she set up to get him close to King Hekaton: "You will have statues and monuments all across Faerûn."

She then turns to the others. To Unla Key she offers his cloud castle back, "without Esclarotta." To Vardis, "Cleric of Corellon," she promises that his friend will "become a hero and live," and that she could "solve your Corellon problem and get you into the temples again," perhaps even engineer an apology. To Thistle she offers "arrows of dragon slaying and a powerful magical bow" if he turns from Iymrith to deal with the Giants first. They do not say yes. The SEER is confused: her information was impeccable and she had exploited every weak point her agents reported. Unla Key mutters, "See, the castle is mine already. So you aren’t giving me anything I don’t already have." She catches herself before screaming and offers a tax-free license for his casino and a permit for all cities of the Lords’ Alliance. Unla Key shrugs, "No." Thistle, pained, agrees, "No." Vardis looks at Orsik and says, "No."

Furious, the SEER tells them that for the first time in 40,000 years there is an opportunity for peace between the Giants and the rest of the kingdoms of the North, and that Hekaton will only agree if "you four" handle his problems, believing it a sign from the All-father. She tried to offer him anyone else, but he insisted on them. Orsik answers, "See, killing a dragon is heroic." She retorts, "Anyone can kill a dragon. Not anyone can save the realms." He insists, "No, killing the dragon and saving his girl is heroic." She threatens, "If you do this, I will guarantee that your name is cursed for a thousand generations." Orsik screams back that having her create a fake story about his life while he does her organization's bidding "doesn’t make me a hero. It makes me a fake."

Orsik tries to strike her, but she is in a sanctuary and he can't. Vardis casts a fireball, and she counters it. Unla Key has heard enough: he envisions a diamond box, a jeweled jail only he can see, pinches ruby dust in his pocket and turns it like a key, and she is trapped in a forcecage. She smiles. A few moments later she escapes, leaving a note: "Orsik, I won’t bad mouth you. I disagree with your choices. But if you defeat Iymrith, at least consider the possibility that you could do the realms a favor." Orsik realizes she must have cast a time stop and teleported herself out after writing it. After a moment of silence, Thistle asks, "Does anyone want a goodberry for dinner?"

A few days later the 3HPP finally reach Ascore. Uncle Joon's account describes a millennia-old road running into a great door set in a hill, flanked by two massive stone statues of crouching griffons, opening into a black passage descending into the earth. The city holds two famous monuments: the stone ships, once mighty vessels of trade and war that plied the Narrow Sea and now lie half-buried in the sand, and the red pyramids, standing in a circle in the very center of Ascore, which Uncle Joon told Thistle to avoid. The group navigates around the eroded city to the toppled stones of an ancient ruin, "the stone bleached bone-white after eons." As they walk through it, the ground trembles and two blue dragons emerge from the sand, one in front and one behind. Orsik prepares to fight, and Thistle notices something strange: the dragon's bottom half resembles a purple worm.
