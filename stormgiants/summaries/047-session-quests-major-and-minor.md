# Chapter 47 - Quests Major and Minor

Date: 3 (evening) to about 6 Eleasis 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens with Vardis groaning as his friend asks the Oracle "what's your favorite book" and the Oracle answers with the "collected sayings of Annam" — and the last torch winks out. "Well that's it," mutters the arrogant dwarf, and he marches towards the exit of the room.

That evening, resting in one of the rooms of the faithful, the elven cleric asks his friend, "what next?" Orsik pulls two little notebooks from his pockets, one marked "main quests" and the other titled "side quests," skims them, and tells Yazmeen, "we should probably bring you back to civilization." "That would be nice," mutters the freezing gnome. Vardis points out that they can ask more questions, six per day. Thistle overhears and shouts, "I want to ask where Adimond Kroskas is." The next morning the Oracle answers: "Maelstrom."

Thistle presses on: "And how do I get to this Maelstrom?" The answer: "King Hekaton's conch will take you there. The path you choose here will lead you to it." Thistle pieces together what he has learned — the Storm King's court is in chaos, a conch of teleportation will take him to the Maelstrom, which may be the name of the court, and his enemy Adimond Kroskas is there. Orsik, of course, asks "what act can I perform that will most impress the Zhentarim?" After a moment of silence, Annam answers that he should "End this Giant War thus restoring the trade routes and secure preferential treatment from the Storm Giant King for Zhentarim trade merchants." Unla Key declines to ask anything, whispering smugly, "I know how the world ends, this Oracle has nothing to teach me." Thistle, remembering a game of two truths in a lie he won against the royal lawyers of the fey court, tries a trick question about which burial ground holds the artifact that will lead them to Duke Zalto. Deep laughter echoes in the chamber — "I have been doing this for a very long time." — and a torch winks out. Asked "what do the evil giants want?", the Oracle answers: "Power, glory, and the favor of their gods, so that when the ordning is remade, they are elevated to the top." Then he asks, "what actually happened to Urgala's wife?" She "fell to her death in a well that lead to the Underdark." "Anticlimactic," he thinks.

At the entrance of the Eye of the All-Father, Orsik finds a ladder hanging before him and, above, a strange vehicle: a small ship with sleigh runners held aloft by a giant red balloon. Thistle flies straight up; Orsik grumbles and climbs, ready for combat, and finds a crew clad in black leather with black leather masks. Their captain introduces herself as Delsephine and names her crew — Nyzroth, Brassik, Laz, Oriskus, Perella, Tralt, and Zalthia. They come on behalf of Klauth, "the Great Dragon of the North," who offers the vessel and their services as a gift so the party can cross his dominion, urges them to "Slay their leaders," and bids them come to his hidden vale for a reward if they quell the giant threat.

Orsik calls a "huddle." He reminds the 3HPP that Giants and Dragons are rival ethno-religious groups who hate each other, and that Klauth, whose kind was badly wounded at the Well of Dragons, does not want the Lords' Alliance sniffing into the Spine of the World. "The enemy of the enemy," he says, and they agree. They accept, and ask to be taken to Everlund. Delsephine is unsure how that helps the mission; Orsik tells her it is "really complicated" and that, for reasons of operational security, it stays within the 3HPP. She stares at him and orders her crew to set sail.

Aloft, Orsik notices the fairy growing uncomfortable. Thistle asks Delsephine about the Cult of the Dragon. She glares, seems ready to hurt him, then explains that "Severin was a fool" who poured the cult's resources into the plot to bring Tiamat into the world, abandoning the cult's divine purpose of ending the war between Tiamat and Bahamut through the Dracolich. She says Tiamat was played for a fool by Zariel, and laughs, "and of course she failed." She was not a member during those events: she left when Dracolich research was put on hold, and seized the opportunity Klauth offered. Orsik, an open-minded kind of guy, is taken aback by her barely hidden deep sexual attraction to the great wyrm. When Thistle asks about his nemesis, she spits, "Damn him. Severin trusted that creature to provide him powerful magics. And he or was it a she? Did not." Orsik is intrigued that Kroskas was important enough to work with the leader of the Cult of the Dragon. As they fly off, Orsik remarks to Vardis, "well here we are, and no frosty." "He bailed on us." Thistle tries to explain that he felt he needed to stay and guard the eye, then shrugs: "yeah. I mean he rarely entered the fight." "Like pulling teeth."

After several days of airborne travel, Orsik is back in Danivarr's House talking to Dral Thelev, who asks whether they have solved the mystery of the Ordning and brought peace to the world. "We're working on that." Vardis adds, "it's an ongoing project." A momentary look of concern crosses Dral's face — "I am sure Krowen Valharrow chose you for a reason." Thistle says they are looking for Captain Zoar, and Dral sends one of the boys in the tavern to fetch him. The Captain arrives with three guards, irritated at being summoned, and sizes them up: "Smelly adventurers?"

Things are about to go sideways when Thistle hands over the letter of Mark from Tamalin Zoar, Captain Zoar's sister and the mayor of Calling Horns. The Captain recounts her career from cutthroat and brigand, through a gang of women thieves in Triboar, to building a town and becoming mayor; "I am very proud of her. If she thinks you are worthy of a letter of introduction, then you are." "Of course we are," says the smooth Vardis. Thistle asks for passage to Silverymoon and a note naming them as the Captain's agents there, and — to Orsik's groan — insists it reference "a criminal organization operating in Silverymoon that is trafficking demons."

To Orsik's surprise, Captain Zoar is excited: "Dral get me a drink. I have a story to tell." He rails that Silverymoon abandoned Sundabar when the orc hordes attacked, which is why they left "the Lords' Alliance which is not an alliance," rambles through the battles around Sundabar, and concludes, "I am not surprised they are trafficking demons." He sends a guard to fetch a letter, orders food — "those little fishies that you make with the special sauce" — and asks the group's name. "3HPP," mutters Thistle. "You are the three half-pints and a pint?" Orsik insists it doesn't stand for anything. Zoar has heard of the play with Bob Saget, a smash production he has not yet seen but looks forward to when it comes to town in a few weeks; each performance has a different ending, and "Bob Saget puts on a terrifying performance as a demon." Orsik, calculating coin, urges him to see it several times, then probes for deaths and murders, admitting the original plot had some murders that they stopped. Zoar, planning to take his children, is told it is "Totally safe." Delighted, he decides to keep the group's real name on the letter to make Silverymoon "eat some humble pie!"

Thistle asks, "have you considered running for mayor?" and Orsik, with the smile of a wolf, adds, "you think about it and let us know how we can help you." The Captain says he is quite pleased as High Captain and supports the current speaker, Vatrice Stormwright, who is up for re-election in a few years — though being seen to defeat the Giant threat "would greatly increase my prestige." With High Captain Horix Zoar fed, a little inebriated and happy, Orsik turns to the group: "let's go get ourselves an artifact."

Orsik reflects on what he knows of the Nether Mountains: dark and brooding to others, to him a repository of riches like granite, and said to hold magical items of the ancient Netheril. The Fighters of the Light might have lost such an artifact, though why Kobolds would have it confuses him. From merchants he learns the mountains are full of orc tribes, and that blue dragons in the eastern peaks were chased out of northern Anauroch by a dragon known as the Doom of the Desert. "I'll have to ask Delsephine if she knows anything about that," he mutters.

Thistle finds the trail and leads them to the Kobold cave. Vardis notes that Kobold warrens tend to look the same and watches Unla Key and Thistle walk in with no effort; he and Orsik squeeze through the small entrance. The tunnels are barely tall enough for Vardis, 5-6 feet and sometimes 4-5 feet high, lit dimly by fire-light beetles. "No matter, that's what our light sources are for."
