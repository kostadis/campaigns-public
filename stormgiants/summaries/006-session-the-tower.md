# Chapter 06 - The tower

Date: 9 to 12 Ches 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
In Llorkh, Orsik wakes curiously refreshed — "time to see what we can do here" — while Vardis groans, "seriously, can't we just sleep more." The two join Thistle and Unla in Nonnie's rustic main dining hall. Between bites, Thistle declares, "I would like to buy better armor," and Orsik mutters that they could sell their treasure at "the local Lionshield Coster." That settles it.

The shop belongs to Leo, who is, as always, spotless: immaculately dressed, facial hair cut to perfection, skin smooth as marble. The party assume magic is involved, but as Leo would say, "this is all 100% natural, Leo." Unla Key stares — "to spend so much time on one's appearance." Orsik asks whether Leo has a magnifying glass that could fit an object he shows him; Leo can have one "in a few days," at extra cost, since it is not part of the standard inventory. When Unla Key snorts "dd bay?", Leo is aghast: "we don't work with THAT firm, sir!" He names a fee of 5 gold to cover shipping. Thistle anxiously asks for studded leather armor, and the dwarf proposes they simply sell everything to Leo, who agrees at a 10% discount.

With the group flush with cash, Leo notices the dirt the heroes dragged in — his doormat is spotless and his floor is not — and runs off for cleaning implements, muttering, "what if an inspector came in?" Once the store is clean again and he has registered how much coin the party carries, he talks up his business: he keeps "a minimal inventory in a small town," cannot match the range of the Aurora Realms Stores, "famous for their magical inventory and boutiques of rare and exotic items," and tries "to offer lower price goods for the common sentient." Unla Key reminds the group about rations, which Leo supplies. When Thistle asks about magic, Leo eyes their clothes and their coin-counting and judges a very rare item to be "out of your price range," offering instead "a potion of healing and a potion of climbing and a scroll with detect magic." Unla Key shrugs.

The same day, the tower itself watches. "Hmm, they have come again." It notes that Vardis has used blessing to help his friends deflect the permanent antipathy field surrounding it, and assumes "the mage and cleric will not continue without their warriors." The cleric shouts, "Alright, Unla Key, Let's go check this place out. This is our shot," and adds, "this is what you get for swearing off that thing you used to do." The tower sighs, "oh, that thing." Vardis tries to grapple the dwarf and haul him to the tower; the dwarf escapes and curses Vardis. Unla Key tries to grapple Thistle and fails, and Thistle is more subdued in his reaction. The tower sighs, "I suppose I will have to see what can be done," and when the two open the door it says, "ah, this will do nicely."

Vardis tells Unla Key, "I'm game, let's go do this," and Unla Key agrees. Beyond the door is not a tower entrance but "a stairwell into a dungeon," and at the bottom a plaque reads "Acererak's Construction Company." Vardis shouts, "It's a generative dungeon!" — one that "generates every time you enter" — which leaves the halfling thoroughly confused. Sent ahead with "lead on!", Unla Key heads south at a T intersection and south again at the next, following the passage to an archway and a large hexagonal room with a rope ascending to a balcony. He spots a corroded key in the southwest corner and picks it up while Vardis casts a ritual of detect magic; no magic is detected.

A cranky bugbear arrives. Vardis does the talking, pleading ignorance about the tower. The bugbear explains: "The rules are you have to find a way to get out." He smells their rations and threatens, "Or I could eat the two of you," then eats their food. Asked how one gets out, he lays out the three schools of thought: one group seeks a secret passage; another believes there is no escape and that you eventually die of hunger and turn into a zombie — Unla Key goes pale but is relieved to be with a cleric; and a third believes that dying gets you kicked out as a reminder "to not mess around with a wizard's tower." The bugbear has decided to "tough it out and find the secret door." Prodded by a punch from Vardis, Unla Key invites him to pair up, and the bugbear accepts: "my mom always used to say that two walking meat bags is better than rations." He draws a map and describes the dungeon's other inhabitants — a tribe of goblins who just try to stay alive, a few ogre zombies that can be avoided by moving fast, and himself. Asked whether anyone has escaped, he smiles: "those goblins I killed escaped."

Vardis leads the group west into a large room with a good wooden door to the north, candles and spirals of white stone scattered on the floor, and a wall of tapestries depicting vile acts. ("Nope, not what Orsik did," Vardis thinks.) Behind the tapestry he finds "the secret door!" and smugly asks the bugbear how long he has been looking for it — "A really long time." Opening it triggers an ice dart that bounces off his armor. A passage leads to another door; Vardis, deciding the bugbear is "the new Orsik," has him open it. It is a mimic. The bugbear slashes at it, but his morning star sticks fast and the mimic strikes him. Vardis rubs his silver circle and casts a guiding bolt — "a green glowing dart" — that critically wounds the creature, and boasts, "First the door, and now the bolt, I am on fire!" The fight then sours: Unla Key's cantrip is ineffective, the grappled bugbear misses with a javelin, and Vardis's last spell does little better. Then Unla Key smiles — "sleep." The mimic falls asleep, the bugbear smashes it with his morning star, and Vardis kills it.

In the next room lies a pile of treasure with a note: "congratulations for making it this far!" Beyond the northern door is a staircase. As Vardis says "let's get out of here," a voice enters his head: "if you use the key, you forfeit the gold." Vardis reads it as the tower's thing trying to keep them there, "tempting us with gold." The bugbear refuses to leave — "more treasure," he growls — and heads back into the dungeon. Vardis and Unla Key are teleported back to Llorkh beside Orsik and Thistle; it is late afternoon.

On the way to Aunt Nonnie's they meet Diego, "the paramour of Fiora," who wants to know if they entered the tower. Unla Key manipulates fates and convinces him both that they escaped the building and that it is essential that Diego and the Guard investigate the tower. After Diego leaves, Vardis explains the secret of the entrance. Aunt Nonnie asks whether they plan to go back; Vardis says it was a one-time thing, and she is disappointed. Orsik snickers and shouts, "are you talking smack about us?" — Aunt Nonnie takes offence and raises her prices, and Orsik apologizes under Vardis's glare.

On the 1st day of the 2nd tenday of Ches, on the road to Anauroch, the heroes come upon a group of down-on-their-luck cultists of the Dragon — dirty, robes tattered, trying to be inconspicuous. Thistle exacts a modest revenge by killing several and keeps one, starving and desperate, for questions. All the arrogance of the Cult of the Dragon is gone. Thistle learns that his nemesis Adimond Kroskas abandoned the Cult just before the defeat at the Well of Dragons, apparently to work with a Giant — surprising, since the Giants and Dragons loathe each other, and Adimond had always struck Thistle as a true believer. The cultist does not know where Thistle's drake is, only that if it is anywhere it is in the desert. As for Thistle's beloved, the news is terrible: the enslaved people either died from mistreatment or were eaten alive by a Dragon as a sacrifice to bring Tiamat into the world. When the cultist tries to reassure him that being eaten alive would be best, because "there can be no greater honor!", Thistle disagrees and kills him.

On the 2nd day of the 2nd tenday of Ches a group of kobolds attempts an ambush and is defeated, and the journey to the Oasis of Vuerthyl in Anauroch to meet Azam the caravaneer continues.

## NPCs
### Leo
Llorkh's shopkeeper, spotless and immaculately groomed, insisting "this is all 100% natural, Leo." Prim and fussy — he cleans his floor mid-sale for fear "an inspector came in" — and disdainful of "THAT firm." He buys the party's treasure at a 10% discount, will order a magnifying glass for 5 gold, and sells rations and minor magic.

### Aunt Nonnie
Proprietor of the place the party stays. Disappointed when Vardis says the tower was a one-time thing, she takes offence at Orsik's outburst and raises her prices.

The tower's own voice. It watches the party arrive, notes the blessing against its antipathy field, and decides "this will do nicely" when Vardis and Unla Key open the door. A voice — read by Vardis as "the thing that runs this tower" — warns, "if you use the key, you forfeit the gold."

A cranky bugbear trapped in the dungeon who eats the party's rations, explains the theories of escape, and allies with them.
