# Chapter 25 - The Murder and the Theft

Date: about 29 to 30 Mirtul 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter is told as two investigations running side by side. In the first, Jacoby and Persson Hysstryn convinced Thistle to dig into the mysterious death of their brother Mirak. The trail led first to an affair: Mirak had been involved with an ex-adventurer, Urgala Meltimer, who owns the Northshield House Inn. From there the group learned, from a drunk priest of Helm who was passed out in the Talking Troll at the time, that Mirak's body was in the Six Windows Inn.

The recovery itself turned on Thistle. While he staged what the chapter calls "Thistle's brilliant distraction at the Six Windows Inn," the rest of the group sneaked in and uncovered, in the attic, the remains of Mirak. With those remains as evidence, they convinced the Lord Protector to at least put Tolmara in prison until a cleric could be found who could cast speak with the dead. The Lord Protector Darathra, noting that the heroes had caused quite a commotion, demanded that they stay in town until the next day.

Urgala Meltimer also opened up to them. She told them of the death of her first wife in the Underdark, and of the many places to scrounge for treasure in the Dessarin Hills — a place with enough to mine as a wildcat miner but not enough to be worthy of controlling, and home to bandits and uncivilized types. She gave them a map of one area she thought was of extreme interest.

The second investigation began with a shopping trip. Orsik and Vardis went to the Lionshield Coster Outpost, Lion's Share, and met its owners, Alaestra and Narth, who were in disarray because their vault had been breached. The vault had held special keys used to get inventory through the Lionshield Coster's interplanar supply chain. The chapter sets this in a wider frame: the chain leverages interplanar trade routes, an approach the famous gnomish sage KP first analyzed for its impact on trade across the entirety of existence. KP's research is controversial because, although the multi-planar optimum for economic efficiency can be reached, the equations ignore the impact on local social cohesion — and Orsik and Vardis found themselves thrust into exactly the kind of social disruption that planarization's advocates dismissed. The store had undercut local merchants, and the locals looked at Lion's Share with caution and disgust. Alaestra was relieved to find outsiders who could help.

The only clues were a leather harness and a masterfully made grappling hook. The master harness maker Othovir admitted he had not made the harness, was sure it had been made in Waterdeep, and judged it would fit a thin person of about 150 pounds. Harriet Uldinath did not recognize the grappling hook and insisted it was not from her shop, though she admitted it was better than anything she could make and suggested it might be the work of her competitor, Ghelryn Foehammer. Ghelryn, a dwarf who believes in privacy but is convinced that privacy ends at the limits of burglary, revealed that he had sold it to Darz Helgar.

Darz Helgar, the campground overseer and an ex-criminal from Waterdeep, gave way as soon as he was presented with the harness: Kaelen, who runs the Talking Troll, had forced him into the job. Darz had been in Waterdeep over ten years earlier and had spent time in its dungeons. Orsik, very interested, offered to keep the secret if Darz would reveal anything about the thieves guild. Darz agreed: "It was ten years ago, but I will."
