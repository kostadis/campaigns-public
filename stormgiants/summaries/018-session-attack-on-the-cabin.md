# Chapter 18 - Attack on the Cabin

Date: 2 Mirtul 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The wyvern's morning howl wakes the village of Parnast, and the adventurers filter one by one into the Tavern of the Golden Tankard to plan their next moves. Wallace has a spring in his step and a smile; Orsik and Vardis are nursing heads sore from too much drink; Unla Key is taking his first drink to settle his nerves; and Thistle is fresh from eight hours of peaceful sleep. Ragnar shouts, "To the cabin?" and Thistle answers, "I suppose so!" The rest of the group looks at the overly cheery ranger and nods.

They follow the trail toward the cabin where the mother of Tove Brandimen was supposedly being held hostage. The evidence Tove had produced the night before supported his claim, but Vardis and Orsik had their doubts. Vardis had looked at the note supposedly from Kragota Threeteeth, the Mother of Bones, and whispered to Orsik, "Remember when we pulled the hostage scam?"

After a few hours of hungover or still-drunk silence, the party crests a hill and spots a cabin among massive boulders and old fir trees. "How pretty," Thistle remarks. "Except for the screaming." He makes out human voices using local slang, then blushes and claps his hands over his ears: "Oh my. Those are inappropriate things to be saying." Vardis and Orsik decide there has been enough screaming for one day and charge into the clearing. Thistle shrugs and follows, Wallace mumbles about scouting first but follows his mentor, and Unla Key, seeing everyone charge, charges too.

In the clearing Orsik sees a cage of human prisoners, a firepit to its east, and the Brandimen cabin behind both. He assumes that killing the prisoners would stop the screaming, then realises the screams are fear: a goblin is torturing them with a hot poker. Unla Key, following Orsik, is surprised to see Orsik surprised to see orcs and goblins. The wild charge has been noticed, and the goblins and orcs have taken cover to ambush the five adventurers.

A goblin in a dirty green jacket steps behind a shed east of the fire pit and shoots at Unla Key, who protests, "Why me?" Orsik mutters, "because you're in range and aren't wearing armor." Unla Key does not take cover, while the goblin does. An orc throws a javelin from the cabin over the prisoners and misses Orsik. An orc wearing a necklace of skulls beside the prisoners is pierced by an arrow from Vardis, and Wallace slashes him with his sword. Thistle and Thuul attack two goblins; one swings a scimitar uselessly at the ranger and his beast and then runs, and the other misses with an arrow. An orc steps out, throws a javelin at the robe-wearing wizard and misses, and Unla Key taunts him: "can't hit the broadside of a barn!" Believing the multitude of planes is safe only if he takes risks, Unla Key refuses to hide.

The green-jacketed goblin turns his bow on Orsik and hits only the dirt. "Charge!" shouts the orc with the necklace of skulls, rushing Orsik. The reason soon becomes apparent: Kragota is screaming orders at her troops. "Crush the Puny creatures!" she yells, displeased with them. "Do I have to do everything?" she shouts, hurling a javelin that slips from her fingers and sticks in the dirt.

Vardis fires scorching ray at the two goblins that fled Thistle and Thuul, badly wounding the one that had fired the arrow. Wallace slashes at the Ogre and misses. Unla Key casts Rime's Binding Ice, trapping the ogre but not the orc beside her. Thistle kills the scimitar goblin. The goblin with the bow finally hits Unla Key and shouts, "finally!" An orc advancing on Wallace and Orsik brings his axe down with a thud on ground nowhere near them, and Orsik kills him. The green-jacketed goblin steps out of cover muttering, "We must kill the wizard!" and hits again. Orsik taunts the enemy: "go ahead and try and kill us puny creatures! You can't even kill an unarmed wizard!" Kragota surprises everyone by casting a spiritual weapon in the form of a hammer and striking Unla Key, who stands his ground, convinced that the universe would end if he fled.

Vardis casts faerie fire but affects only one orc. Unla Key twists fate so that Wallace lands a critical hit on the Ogre, then points Wallace into position, advances, and casts Aganazzar's scorcher. The Ogre avoids most of the flames; an orc fighting Wallace does not, and a goblin behind the Ogre dies. Orsik's shout to "get a healing potion" is drowned out by the fire and the screams. Thistle flies up for a better angle on the Ogre and brings Kragota to 1 HP. She screams, "Fairies! We had a deal with Prince Thornacious!"

An orc beside the unarmored Unla Key misses him again and stares at his weapon in confusion while Unla Key stands smugly; Orsik kills that orc and advances on the Ogre. The goblin in the green jacket (written once as the "Green Jacket Goblin") slips out of the shadows, shoots Unla Key, and squeals as he falls. The Ogre attacks Wallace with her spiritual weapon and swings her hammer at the flanked Orsik. Vardis rushes to the fallen Unla Key and heals him. Wallace punches the Ogre, who dies. Back on his feet, Unla Key rushes an orc next to Orsik and casts another scorcher, killing the green-jacketed goblin and wounding the orc, then distracts the orc, and Orsik kills it.

Searching the bodies, the party finds scattered coins adding up to 50 gold and six sapphires worth 100 gold. They then turn to the prisoners, who at first talk over each other. A man tries to explain and a woman interrupts to say he is wrong. Another woman finally explains that they belonged to a commune that Kragota raided, sent by Bad Fruul to destroy it, and that several members died. Orsik and Vardis try to express sorrow. Thistle asks to see their hands to check for a missing finger and finds none. He asks whether they have seen a woman matching Tove's description of his mother, and they sadly shake their heads. One woman sees the finger and breaks down crying; her friends explain that it belonged to a member of the commune. Orsik asks whether they have seen a half-elf matching Tove's description. "Oh yes! They were thick as thieves with Kragota," one woman answers. Then comes the unattributed line "I told you we should have fed him to the Wyvern." When Orsik asks what they know about Bad Fruul, the answer is "Bad who?"

The cabin is a small rustic place with animal skins and antlers on the walls. "What an ordinary woodman's cabin," says Thistle. "What a mess," says Wallace, nearly vomiting from the rotting food and trash. Unla Key looks over a large, haphazard pile of tools in one corner. Thistle finds a trapdoor under the rug ("Classic," shouts Orsik) and climbs down a steep ladder into a small earthen room. It is not a root cellar. Long, multicolored ropes of braided human hair strung with small tinkling bells hang from the ceiling. Orsik and Vardis jump down, and all three see a crude altar painted with a black sunburst. On it are two black candles flanking an elven woman's skull and the shattered remains of a jade statuette, with chests and bags of treasure beside it. "Bane," Orsik and Vardis mutter. Thistle, whose Uncle Joon had explained everything to him, tells the others who Bane is: a ruthless god of tyranny who believes himself the rightful ruler of all the planes, demands absolute loyalty, and has followers everywhere, even among the Lords' Alliance and the Zhentarim.

The group heads back to Parnast. What weighs most on their minds is that Chandra Stol will be so upset at having to feed the Wyvern cooked meat instead of living prey.
