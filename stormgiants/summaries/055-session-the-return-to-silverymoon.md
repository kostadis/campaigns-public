# Chapter 55 - The return to Silverymoon

Date: 22 to 23 Eleasis 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
As Orsik passes the totem in the cave, a Coatl emerges and demands that the party destroy Wormblod, who it says has perverted the tribe, promising a boon in return. Thistle, remembering killing Wormblod in the Eye of the All-Father, answers at once: "We did it!" Vardis tries to stop him, thinking, "We should haggle for something more." But it is too late — they have all been blessed with a Charm of Restoration. Orsik sighs, "I guess that’s useful," and the creature's eyes bulge: "you are not appreciative of my boon?" The 3HPP nod like annoyed teenagers made to thank an annoying uncle and say, "Yeah, we appreciate it." The creature slithers its tongue in and out and mutters, "Usually, they appreciate me more!"

Further down in the cave the 3HPP find the Remorhaz’s lair and take its gold, gemstones and minor magical artifacts. Vardis notes that if they had not attacked with the fireballs, they might have got the gong without fighting the creatures. "But fireballs are fun," mutters Unla Key.

Back on the Dragon’s Breath, Brassik is delighted with his newfound freedom and the chance to work on background research and the play. The chapter notes that "Thistle never thinks to ask Brassik about his girlfriend." Brassik finds that odd; the narration ironically observes that "It’s not like he’s a well-informed Candlekeep scholar with deep knowledge about the occult and goings on of the area." Brassik is pleased and thinks "that information will cost them." He reserves particular scorn for "the buffoons, Orsik and Vardis, who refused to pay me a fair price, and now they will never know where their precious little treasure map goes."

The next day, as the sun rises over Silverymoon, the 3HPP and Brassik arrive at the gate. After debating which angle to use, they reject the letter from the Ambassador and instead say they are business partners with his Lordship. "Working with the non-goodly races?" spouts the guard. Orsik glares, and the guard sends for his captain, Sir Cedric Hollowcrest, who arrives moments later and apologises: "I am so sorry. These are the famous 3HPP! The redeemed Orsik!" Orsik gruffly announces, "I am Orsik Sr." The introductions continue — "The brokenhearted Thistle!" and "The honest Vardis!" — and the reason for the fame becomes clear: "Bob Saget’s play has made you heroes. His performance was divine!"

The original guard then recognises Unla Key and gushes, "YOU’RE UNLA KEY! Omg! OMG! I am so in love with you!" Unla Key asks him, "What's the most dangerous thing around us right now?" The answer is "You." The guard asks for an autograph made out to Borin Greycastle, and the answer is "One." The guards sigh, and someone tells them, "Cheer up; you can sell this in the open markets or collector for 20x!" The reply: "Sell this? Never."

Moving through the Mulgate toward the Moonbridge on Moonway, the party realise that Bob Saget has taken several liberties with their story and made them minor celebrities. Thistle, who has paid good money for it, takes the group to Wyrmworks, where Rebecca Deakin has been researching for him. She greets them with "Oh, you are alive!" and explains, "I figured anyone stupid enough to look into Adimond Kroskas would be dead by now." Unprompted, she declares that her research has reaffirmed her faith that Bob Saget is right: "Silverymoon must abandon magic. The Spellguard is a crypto-fascist tool of the mages and a force of control." She looks around furtively — "I spoke too much." — then continues anyway, railing against wizards who treat non-magic users as lesser people, against the out-of-towners at the Wizard school, and against experiments that turn into plagues. "Bob’s right. Just because they can cast spells doesn’t make them better." She prides herself on research done the old-fashioned way: "I open books. I do research. I use my brain." Seeing Unla Key, she hastily clarifies: "I am talking about the Silverymoon and the Spellguard!"

Turning to Thistle, Rebecca reports that Adimond Kroskas is a powerful spell caster who knows the spell that allows one to transform Drakes from Dragon eggs. Thistle objects — "This drake is not a spawn of a spell." — and Rebecca corrects herself: "Right. He’s a lesser drake." Pure Drakes, she explains, were created from a Dragon egg, and lesser drakes were their offspring. Thistle suggests to his increasingly annoyed Drake that it sit outside. Rebecca goes on: Adimond was hired by the Cult of Tiamat to work on a fay-dragon drake hybrid. A man who said he was Thistle's uncle, Joon, told her that Adimond went into the Feywild to get the First Flower of Spring as a critical component of his spell research, and that the fairies were important too — "unclear if it was as test subjects, components, or treats." The weirdest part, she says, is what Adimond Kroskas is: the Cult of the Dragon referred to him as a dragon, and some as a Dragon's pet. "I am betting on the Dragon."

Thistle shouts, "UNCLE JOON! I am going to kill him." Vardis remembers the death of Kolbaz, who last researched Adimond, and the odd damage in his chambers. "A Blue Dragon," he mumbles. When Thistle's rage subsides, he understands with horror that the stories of his girlfriend’s violent behavior may not be as innocent as he first thought. Orsik, more concerned with the play, cuts it short: "C’mon, you got your answers."

At the High Palace, a banner announces that the play is sold out for the next two months, and the 3HPP are mobbed. Unla Key shouts, "FINALLY, THE RECOGNITION!" and Orsik declares, "I am a hero!" Both sign autographs. A pair of guards then summon them to Lord Methrammar Aerasumé, who greets them warmly: the play has been a smashing success and "Profits will delight all of you." He describes the finale, in which Orsik uses all his battle mastery, Vardis summons his god’s powers, and Unla Key unleashes his spells, with effects so impressive that "There is a rumor that some CGI device is being used." Asked who portrays them, he says, "I don’t know. You should ask Bob." When Thistle wonders why he was not in the final combat, Lord Aerasumé explains, "We couldn’t find an actor small enough, so we used a puppet stuck on a stick. It’s an amazing likeness."

Orsik's idea of a new play is shot down: "People aren’t bored. The play has become a spiritual investigation." Orsik wonders whether the son of Alustriel Silverhand agrees that magic is dangerous, and his Lordship turns menacing: "There is danger in everything. There is danger in the sword. There is a danger in your questions." He refuses to debate magic's risks: "Magic has served Silverymoon for generations." His mother, Lady Alustriel Silverhand, once ruled the city, and "Those who say otherwise are traitors to Silverymoon."

Orsik then tries to use the play to escape paying Delsephine, citing business concerns with a red dragon and asking for 6,000 gold pieces. "My dear boys, you don’t understand how profitable this play has become." His Lordship hands them 12,000 gold pieces and says, "You can cover that 6,000 from your share." When Unla Key asks what percentage that is, he replies, "There are only two truths in theater, Unla. The first is that there is never a profit. The second is when the producer gives you some money, you take it and don’t ask questions." "I am never buying a flying castle," Unla Key whines. Blagothkus’ castle is Thistle's answer: he points out that they could always take it.

That evening the group meets a very different-looking Bob Saget, who is delighted to see them: "My muses!" He boasts that the theatre is full every night, that the audience's minds get twisted more against magic per their instructions, and that the play is "nothing more than a successful perversion of society that will achieve your, not my, stated aims in 30 days." He is cagey about who plays the heroes — "nobodies. They get their moment in the sun, and we replace them." — and explains that "The scene works best if it's amateurs rather than real adventurers," because the actors' terror is what makes the scene work. He refuses them seats, saying Kaelen wrote the play that summoned him and "Your presence would be a – distraction." Orsik smiles politely, resolves to get in anyway, and buys some invisibility potions.
