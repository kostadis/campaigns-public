# Chapter 05 - Thoughts and Prayers

Date: 6 to 8 Ches 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The group climbs toward the highest peak in the Graypeak mountain range under a warm sky of brilliant blue. The climb is tedious, and Orsik stares at the quiet sky and shouts, "there is nothing to excite the nerves!" Everyone stares at him. Then Vardis points into the distance: a griffon. Unla Key grouses that Orsik has brought it on himself: "you do realize that this is your fault? You could have been quiet, but you taunted fate!" Orsik first asks, "What does he have against us?" before realizing that it is not personal — they are a meal to the creature.

Orsik dumps his food in a pile and urges everyone else to do the same and hide. When the others ask "some or all," he reasons that four meat sacks must look pretty tasty and that a bigger meat sack is needed in front of them, so he shouts, "all." The griffon lands at the substantial pile, pecks at the jerky first and then the dried fruit, purrs contentedly, rubs its belly and flies off. Unla Key asks, "do we have to worry about flying poop? Is their poop like a bird's?" Orsik rolls his eyes, but not before Thistle says, "great question. My uncle never saw a griffon poop."

A few hours later the group makes the final push to the peak. The air grows thin and cold, and after passing through a layer of cloud they emerge into sunlight above an endless sea of white. Unla Key notices writing on the wall and casts comprehend languages: the writing forms mantras, most extolling the virtues of Knowledge, Magic, Intelligence, Meditation, and Understanding. He sighs, "worthless religious claptrap." At the top of the sloping path stand a large brass bell and a cave entrance.

Four giant owls swoop in to attack. The first strikes at Orsik and escapes his counterblow, and he shouts, "Ranged weapons." He hurls two hand-axes, which fall into the cloud cover — "useless!" Another owl strafes Thistle and misses. Vardis shouts, "Let's go inside," while someone insists, "I at least want to try using my bow!" and the arrow strikes an owl, a severe blow but not a mortal one. Then Vardis declares, "I have a plan," steps as close to the edge as he feels is safe, pulls out the symbol of Corellon Larethian — a silver circlet — and summons his god's power in a blast of light that burns everywhere. One owl falls to its death. Another swoops at Vardis, who dodges at the last second.

The Magic-User studies the owls' flight and realizes they fly predictably, probably as they were trained to do. He summons his divination skill to twist the fates in his favour, draws two birds closer than they should come, and casts sleep on them; Vardis and Orsik cheer as the two owls fall into the abyss. Thistle hits with his bow once more, while Vardis' new bow is too new and he misses. As Vardis, Thistle and Orsik huddle together, the last bird attacks Unla Key, 20 feet away, and almost deals a killing blow to the surprised halfling. Unla Key bellows, "I SURVIVED AN ATTACK! I AM INVINCIBLE!" The bird is skewered dead with a magic missile. Vardis asks, "I thought owls were friendly!" and Thistle answers that the owls flew in a particular pattern, "suggesting there is some kind of trainer." Unla Key, meanwhile, is popping good berries.

Fed up, Orsik charges into the open passageway and bounces off a magical force field. Vardis shouts, "the bell?" Unla Key reads a riddle etched into the bell: *What doubles when you share it, is unseen but gives power, and is useless unless practiced?* Thistle offers "Music!", Vardis shouts "Magic!" from a safe distance, and Unla Key suggests friendship, reasoning that "If you don't practice it, you have no friends." Orsik grouses, "why would giants have a riddle about friendship?" Unla Key remembers the mantras, and Thistle agrees that "Understanding" is the word. Thistle volunteers to do it — "well, I am a little bit tougher than you. I'll do it." — and warns Unla Key to stand further from the ledge. The bell is rung, the word said, and the force field collapses. Orsik runs in.

Thistle rushes in with Orsik. The entrance is big enough for even the largest of giants and opens into a massive cavern with 50' tall ceilings, collapsed in places and littered with debris and large rocks. Once Orsik nods that the place is safe, Thistle notices four huge copper braziers; he flies up to check them, but they are empty save some residue coal and incense. A long stone slab 15-feet high, topped with a tattered cloth, catches his attention, and its decorations reveal that the walls were once painted in similar patterns, now largely ruined. Orsik and Unla Key rush to the front while Thistle hangs back near the exit by one of the braziers. Unla Key says, "I take risks."

"And I was lucky!" Unla Key thinks, as all the braziers magically light up. In the south of the room, where Vardis and Thistle were hanging back, two creatures that look like living icicles appear, shrieking and gibbering in pain and frustration. The one from the southeastern brazier gets behind Vardis and breathes a blast of icy frost, catching Thistle too, though he hides behind some rocks. Vardis, his skin burned by the brazier's heat, says, "I need to get away from this," but stands his ground; his blow is dodged. Thistle repositions and misses. Unla Key thinks, "I ventured, and I gained. The world will not end today!" and casts toll the dead on one creature. Orsik finishes it off, and it explodes on everyone but Unla Key — "Lucky again," he thinks. The remaining creature casts fog cloud, blinding everyone, until Unla Key sees a flash of magic power streak through the cloud, hears a shriek, and finds a satisfied Vardis standing.

Orsik climbs and reports gold plates and dead cultists. Asked whether the plates are worth a lot, he answers, "Yes, Unla Key, but it would be bad if we angered a god. I think there's the same writing as before." Unla Key asks, "Would it be less sacrilegious if we melted the gold first?" Vardis shouts, "No!" Unla Key takes them anyway, while Thistle loots the cultists' bodies and shouts fairy curses.

As the group walks out, the booming voice of a storm giant greets them. Thistle whispers to Orsik, "I think he can kill us without breaking a sweat." The giant warns that "Annam, the all-father is in no mood to be trifled," and introduces himself: "I am Kraddock." He says he was told the shrine was desecrated several days ago and has come to see what state it was left in. Kraddock stares at the halfling trying to hide the large gold plates behind his back. Unla Key first claims, "we stopped those bandits from stealing the gold plates," and, as Kraddock's face grows sterner and his friends back away, tries again: "we're treasure hunters, and we got this map, and we didn't think it belonged to anyone." He weighs twisting the fates to make the giant fear him, concludes it would be a very brief battle, and instead says, "Kraddock, look, we just got here today." Kraddock offers him a way out — "I'm sure that you were just trying to ensure that the gold plates were not stolen." — and when the plates are confirmed as "these sacred gold plates," Unla Key smiles, "why didn't you say so!" and hands them back. Kraddock says, "I wasn't going to kill you. I was just going to invoke a curse of the All-Father," and carefully shines the plates.

Kraddock looks at the little people with sadness: the All-Father has made decisions he does not agree with but serves. He delivers bad tidings. The Dragon Queen Tiamat failed in her plots to conquer this world, "But in failing, she inspired the giants, and they are at war with you." He declares, "The age of dragons is over." When the small fairy asks about the cult, he explains, "The Cult of the Dragon was pursuing a spell that Tiamat told them would let her enter the world." He advises them to tell friends and loved ones "to steer clear of locations where giants live or even where giants were rumored to live," and when the dwarf asks where that is, he answers, "Great question, this part of the world that you little people live in." The party objects to his language — "We're big. You're little." draws "Not cool!" and "You should think about your language. It can cause harm." — and Thistle shouts, "you need to understand how your communication is being received instead of how you think it is intended." Kraddock concedes, "You are so right," and says King Hekaton has talked about a newer, inclusive way of talking about all people, and that "His great wife who disappeared had much to say." He worries that "the age of tolerance is over as well."

He describes the ancient kingdom of Ostoria, which stretched "from the Spine of the world to the great desert to the east, to the sword coast to the west, and then to the kingdom of Amn in the south." Asked, "The entire North?" he confirms that there was a giant kingdom here, and that over the centuries "the under 16 feet crowd" — a phrasing everyone nods is better — "Invaded from the south and drove the giants to the north and to isolated pockets of giant power." Vardis observes that the giants may now see this as a time to reclaim what is rightfully theirs and may end up terrorizing the entire sword coast, and asks what Kraddock will do. Kraddock replies, "I am just a shaman," and lists his chores: the drapes, the roof that needs patching, the braziers that "haven't had charcoal in years," the frescoes, polishing the "alters," and getting rid of the dead body. Vardis screams, "The complete destruction of the sword coast is at risk, and as long as your shrine is fine." Kraddock says, "I must pray to the All-Father to understand what he wants. I offer you my thoughts and prayers and will ask him to protect you from what must come." Someone replies, "So basically nothing." Kraddock answers, "Yes." Then he hands Vardis some potions as they leave — "perhaps more than just thoughts and prayers." When Unla Key, the thief, asks, "If you came with us, that would help even more," Kraddock answers, "There is such a thing as pushing your luck?" As they leave, he sighs, "perhaps the All-Father will learn that this war will bring no glory to anyone."

On the 7th day of the 1st tenday of Ches, after a long rest, the group heads back to Llorkh. The weather has turned cold and they have no food; they explain their circumstances and buy some game from someone the chapter refers to only as "his." Thistle guts the rabbit — Vardis and Orsik, it seems, spent too much time writing subversive poetry to learn that life skill — and it is one of the best rabbits the group has ever eaten. The next day snow falls; they try to walk through it but grow too cold and tired, so Thistle starts a fire and they wait it out. Vardis says, "let's double time to Llorkh."

Just as they can almost taste Nonnie's food, a giant centipede erupts. Orsik shouts, "Seriously, this single giant centipede pops out in front of us in this massive field." A few seconds later it is dead. At Nonnie's, Aunt Nonnie — as she likes to be called — is as friendly and welcoming as always. Orsik asks, "What's wrong with her Vardis?" and Vardis replies, "I don't know. She's probably affiliated with an evil cult." Over vegetarian entrees, Aunt Nonnie tells them, "There have been so many reports of kobolds coming around. And those guards seem unwilling to do very much but stay in their tower." Orsik mumbles, "I'd do that." She stares at him: "No, you wouldn't," "because of that THING you did."
