# Chapter 37 - On the Road Again

Date: about 15 to 16 Flamerule 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The 3HPP are on the road to Xantharl’s Keep. Thistle Wendrod flitters along, looking about for any information about his beloved, and thinks back over the peculiar conversation the group had with the owner of The Triboar Travelers.

It had started because Orsik wanted to meet the local Zhentarim agent. Orsik was now a well-known agent with quite a bit of renown, and he wished to cash in on his popularity. The agent was Urlam Stockspool, of fairly conventional Illuskan stock, middle-aged and missing an eye; he wore his allegiance on the eye patch, whose cloth bore a red-emblazoned symbol of the Zhentarim. Urlam was quite happy to see Orsik, gave him a kiss on each cheek, and agreed to go to the Talking Troll for drinks. He arrived there with quite a crew, and Thistle snickers at the memory of Orsik about to object to buying drinks for 10 people, then caving when he realised they were all members of the Zhentarim.

The conversation went nowhere until Thistle asked about Adimond Kroskas. Urlam answered, “Well, that is a name I haven’t heard in a very long time.” When Thistle fluttered closer and said they were trying to track him down, Urlam looked uncomfortable and explained that the black network is “now all about making money and not trying to bring about the Apocalypse to enable Bane to take over all of creation,” focused on “profitability, gross margins, and endless growth,” and that Adimond Kroskas used to work with that group. Thistle, confused, asked what the Cult of the Dragon would have to do with Bane. Urlam said Adimond Kroskas was not a member of the Cult but a free agent — “He was one messed-up dude. People paid him to do things.” — and that, by the rumours and speculation he had heard, “if you ever needed somebody to create a hybrid magical creature, he was your guy.” The people who knew him, Urlam said, are dead, and those who may know are very senior in the Zhentarim hierarchy; the Zhentarim were involved in the events surrounding the fall of the Cult of the Dragon. Pressed by a frustrated Thistle for somewhere to look, Urlam first called to Kaelen for more wine, and only after Kaelen returned with another bottle — which Orsik had to pay for — answered: “Silverymoon. They have schools of magic with the greatest libraries.”

Orsik's own memory of the evening picks up when Thistle asked about House Margaster. Orsik called them “Those devil-worshipping pieces of – bovine feces” and twice insisted they needed to be taken out. Urlam, swirling and sniffing his wine, said House Margaster had come asking for a discount, and that when he objected, cultists turned up and killed some of his guards — “No made men, but still.” He also told them that he bet Othovir had told them how to hurt House Margaster, but that Othovir has a relationship with the Margasters. Orsik was angry and very surprised when the normally taciturn, quiet cleric said, “He set us up.”

Orsik grew angrier still when Thistle asked Urlam whether he had any contraband he needed to dispose of. Urlam was furious; Orsik intervened, scolding Thistle for bringing that up in a nice conversation, and shouted to Kaelen for “Another round.” Thistle backpedalled, saying they had been thinking of using the contraband to disgrace House Margaster. Urlam said, “I just want them dead,” then added, “That’s not an official Zhentarim position.” He offered 200 gold for the job; Orsik said he would do it for free but that the sum was pretty low; Urlam proposed a “Zhentarim friends and family discount,” and Orsik agreed, since he was going to do it for free anyway.

As the 3HPP march on, Unla Key thinks about Hyuth, who is his kind of guy: self-centered, obsessed with his own importance, putting his needs above everyone else’s. “I like him,” Unla Key says — then catches himself: “I mean, I don’t – like – anyone….” The halfling reflects silently that if the world does not end he could retire like Hyuth, with a big house, terrorizing people, involved in manic plots to destabilize the local community.

Orsik and Vardis call a rest, and the group camps by a fire in a clearing off the side of the road. As Unla Key gets ready to sleep, a booming voice announces, “It is I, Harshnag, the Grim of Force Grey.” Unla Key readies his spells, while Orsik delivers a complete paragraph on who Harshnag is: a legendary good-aligned frost giant, a monster to some and a hero to others, remembered in Waterdeep as the largest member of Force Grey, “a notoriously destructive adventuring company,” believed to be hundreds of years old and wandering the wilderness “seeking to lay his evil giant brethren low.” Unla Key is impressed by this erudition and annoyed when Orsik notices and taps his headband; the magic-user’s identity is tied to being the brains, smarter than everyone else so that his evil plots go undiscovered.

Harshnag — a typical-looking frost giant except for his plate armor, the skull of a white dragon worn as a helm, and a formidable jagged axe — sits by the fire. “I have been looking for people like you. The end of the world is near.” Unla Key smiles, until Harshnag explains: “The ordning has been broken, the storm giants no longer have the power to keep the smaller giants in check, and King Hekaton is missing.” Unla Key sighs to himself, “Not the right end of the world.” Harshnag explains to a confused Orsik that the giants are kept in a strict hierarchical power structure that stops them meddling with the other races, and that when it is disrupted the giant kings wrestle for control and their squabbles hurt the other people of the North.

Harshnag says Krowen Valharrow told him the group had agreed to take on the quest to discover what was happening with the Giants, and he proposes they consult a higher authority: a mountain temple built by his ancient ancestors under the Spine of the World, the Eye of the All-Father, which according to giant legend contains a divine oracle. He offers to guide them there. Unla Key, annoyed, objects that they were going to claim an award for “a scumbag named Weevil who happens to be in Xantharl’s Keep.” Harshnag assures them they can claim that bounty, but expects his share, “a minor 100 gold.” The group decides that 100 gold is a small tax on their 5000 gold, one they can live with, and Orsik snickers, “Sure Frosty the Snow Giant!”
