# Chapter 09 - Ending a civil war

Date: about 24 Ches to 1 Tarsakh 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens mid-ambush. Wallace stares up at Bugbears on the cliffs, hurling javelins at the group below. There is no cover, and the only way to reach the attackers is to climb the twenty or so feet to the top of the cliff, unable to fight back while climbing. As blood spurts, Wallace's first words are "What the hell! Bugbears?" and he wonders how he got here.

In his own thoughts he recalls the road that led to this moment. Orsik had said they had to hike to find treasure and glory: some hobgoblins were going to investigate some magic-user's tower in the Marsh of Chelimber, and Wallace's dad thought it would be something Orsik and the gang would find valuable. Wallace is not sure his dad expected him to go along, but he wants to make his own mark, and he is proud that his dad "really understood how to survive. We are survivors."

As they set out, the golden pseudo-dragon that belonged to Azam showed up. Wallace thinks Vardis explained that the pseudo-dragon does not belong to Azam; rather, "Azam belongs to the pseudo-dragon." This ancient creature, Hsing, said he would like them to investigate the tower. Orsik explained that Hsing had been giving them jobs for a while, and since they already planned to visit the tower, saying yes was easy. Wallace notes with some scorn that for all Orsik's talk of becoming a "kick-ass Zhentarim," he did not negotiate for any money.

Wallace's dad was pleased. He figures that the defeat of the Cult of the Dragon and the chaos in the Zhentarim will encourage the Lords’ Alliance to expand and take control of the Black Road, and he wants them to claim this last outpost before Anauroch. Wallace suspects his dad will tell the Lords’ Alliance he had already sent them on this quest, or that the Lords’ Alliance told him to send them and he is pretending he heard of it on his own.

They hiked down to the gap between the Graypeak Mountains and the Forgotten Forest. Orsik never asked what Wallace knew of the area. The opening is called Ambush Gap, a prized piece of real estate for gangs: taking the Black Road through Parnast is costly, and Ambush Gap lets caravans avoid the fees. Wallace observes that the Zhentarim, ever since the War of the Dragons, have not been able to reassert their control over the Black Road, so many caravans try other routes, and this one is "so damn obvious." He resents that Orsik sees him as a young boy who knows nothing, when "You don’t survive in Parnast without knowing things, people, and what’s going on." The javelins land "more or less where I knew they would hit us."

The Bugbears were not very lucky that day and did not count on a flying ranger. Wallace is underwhelmed with Orsik, who throws his sword at the bugbears (the chapter writes "threw his sword"). The battle ends quickly, but the looting yields very little money. Wallace privately concludes that the bugbears "are working for someone." He decides to keep quiet, survive, and find Orsik's Zhentarim contact to offer the treasure's location.

The view shifts to Feetor, a mud mephit hiding among the clumps of trees with hanging vines in the marsh. He is weighing which side to join: "The half-ogres are powerful but have left. And the grabby water monster is powerful and will never leave." He has just avoided being killed in an ambush "by halfsies today." Hearing someone fighting the grabby water monster, he goes to look. From his vantage point he sees a water-filled channel splitting around a small muddy island strewn with a half-dozen dead hobgoblins, weapons drawn, sprawled halfway into the fetid water. He muses that "The Ogre-shippers seem confident that the intruders will rid them of the grabby water monster," and expects the creature to turn invisible and kill them all.

The haze limits ground visibility to 100 feet, so Feetor creeps closer. He sees a weird glow surrounding the monster, making it visible to its enemies. "They have the power to unhide the grabby water monster?" If the monster cannot hide, it can be defeated. "A new power! I must join this new power!" He steps out onto a vine-covered branch, clapping his flabby hands and flinging stinky droplets of mud, and settles on an angle: playing the dumb swamp creature. He greets the victors with "You killed the evil grabby water monster that has kept me prisoner, all these years." He tells them the monster "ain’t gonna stay dead," that it reforms out of the marsh after a couple of days, and that the mud-folk serve whoever is toughest: some reckon the grabby water monster, some Mama Manyknuckles, "but my vote is that’s you lot." Then he remembers his manners: "I’m Feetor." He strikes an awkward pose and smiles, "it works!"

Feetor at first worries he has thrown in with unintelligent folks, since the Dwarf asks him what killed the hobgoblins. But the Fairy asks the right question: "where is the lair of the grabby water monster?" Feetor does not know and suspects it has none: "Probably thinks the whole swamp is its lair." The monster is immortal and ancient, always reforming elsewhere in the marsh within a few days, and mostly ignores the mud mephits that "serve" it. Feetor also overhears the human whisper to the Dwarf, "there’s a reason we don’t announce that we are looting bodies before we loot them, Orsik."

From Orsik's side, picking through the bodies turns up a map: "Hey, here’s a map to the wizard’s tower." While Orsik and Vardis compare it with their lost map, the mephit coughs and pitches his plan: defeat Mama Manyknuckles, "and then you are the King of the swamp, and I am your loyal follower!" Orsik thinks, "more followers." Asked whether he can leave the swamp, Feetor nods that he is bound to it, then, unprompted, describes Mama Manyknuckles as "the particularly ugly leader of a band of half-ogres" who lives deeper in the marsh. He avoids her and her half-ogres, who mock him and call him fat and muddy: "I am appropriately sized." Seeing Orsik unconvinced, he explains there is "a big civil war between the mephits," some backing Mama Manyknuckles and some the grabby water monster; kill Mama Manyknuckles and there will be peace. When Vardis asks where the grabby water monster is, Feetor says it is gone for a few days, so they are now the most powerful creatures around. Vardis taps Orsik: "Three days as the King. Not so bad, eh?" Orsik smiles and shouts, "fall in."

Thistle, using the map and the mephit's information, quickly locates Mama Manyknuckles' hideout. Mid-march he stops with a "Tsk, tsk." and announces "The map was wrong," reading the true route from the water flow and the bugbears. Within three hours they reach a very large kidney-shaped island with two encampments, one to the North and one to the South. Feetor shouts, "there is where Mama Manyknuckles rules!"

Orsik tries to draw Feetor out on how to attack, but the mephit is evasive. When Orsik begins "You being an expert on mud mephits," Feetor bristles: "You tokenizing me? Not every mud mephit knows everything about every other mud mephit." Thistle barely restrains a laugh. Orsik clarifies that he wants the other Mud Mephits aligned with them, and Feetor seizes on the word: "Yes! A token of your power!" Before Thistle can intervene, Orsik and Vardis confer and Feetor receives a gold coin. The mephit vanishes into the swamp, which burbles and bubbles for a few minutes, and returns: "They agreed to wait and see who lives and who dies!"

The group decides to wait until sunset to attack. Thistle conjures back his drake, lost to the water weird, then flies up to scout. The southern camp holds four large, crudely built huts, one sturdier than the rest and cobbled together from rafts and flat-bottom swamp boats, plus a fire pit at the northwest side of the camp with a heap of logs and a pair of ogres around it. Two half-ogres spot him and curse, "damn bug!" Mistaking him for a swamp creature, they throw rocks and dirt; Thistle zigs and zags, does not engage, and they go back to what they were doing.

In the middle of the island, between the Razorvine, Thistle finds a sinkhole framed by a wide, roughly square stone foundation whose weathered, crenelated edges make it look less like a foundation and more like the top of a large keep or tower. "Found it," he thinks: the wizard tower. The sinkhole floor is ancient, well-laid flagstone under a thin sheen of swamp water, with a badly weathered statue in the northwest corner. Then something unexpected: "A large table with filthy furs and a bed?" and two wide ramps of logs and dirt leading down. "Someone lives here," he thinks.

The chapter closes with Unla Key, who believes they should have attacked during the day, having seen portents and signs that favoured daylight. As the group moves quietly toward the island at night, Unla Key hears burbling: "The mephits are all talking and taking bets on who will be the marsh’s new king." Feetor, who has figured out Unla Key, offers the Halfling a way into the action, but it requires "special mud." Unla Key is crestfallen until Feetor says, "I am happy to front you some."

## Locations
### Ambush Gap
The gap between the Graypeak Mountains and the Forgotten Forest. Per Wallace, a prized piece of real estate for gangs, because it lets caravans avoid the fees of the Black Road through Parnast. The ambush by the Bugbears happens here.

### Marsh of Chelimber
The marsh where some hobgoblins were going to investigate a magic-user's tower. It is home to the mud mephits, the grabby water monster, and Mama Manyknuckles' half-ogres; its haze limits ground visibility to 100 feet.

### The kidney-shaped island
A very large island deeper in the marsh with encampments to the North and the South, where Mama Manyknuckles rules. The southern camp has four crude huts and a fire pit.

### The wizard tower (sinkhole)
In the middle of the island, between the Razorvine: a sinkhole framed by a weathered, crenelated stone foundation that looks like the top of a large keep or tower. Its flagstone floor is covered with swamp water. It contains a weathered statue, a table with filthy furs, a bed, and two log-and-dirt ramps leading in. Someone lives there.

### Parnast
Wallace's home ground. "You don’t survive in Parnast without knowing things, people, and what’s going on."

### Black Road
The caravan route through Parnast. Wallace's dad hopes the Lords’ Alliance will take control of it.

## NPCs
### Wallace
A young local who knows Parnast travelling with Orsik's group. He is shrewd, knows the area, and resents being treated as a boy who knows nothing. He keeps his suspicions to himself and plans to find Orsik's Zhentarim contact to offer the treasure's location. His inner voice is sardonic: "And I figured Orsik wasn’t a dumbass."

### Wallace's dad
Unnamed. Pleased by the quest, he hopes the Lords’ Alliance will expand and take the Black Road. Wallace suspects him of claiming credit for sending them, or of being told by the Lords’ Alliance to do so.

### Hsing
The ancient golden pseudo-dragon associated with Azam, who asks the party to investigate the tower and has been giving them jobs for a while.

### Feetor
A mud mephit of the marsh, bound to the swamp. He is opportunistic, theatrical and quick with an angle, and he speaks in a folksy register ("coupl’a day", "Most’a we mud-folks"). He defects to the party after seeing them beat the grabby water monster, pitches the overthrow of Mama Manyknuckles, and brokers the other mephits' neutrality for a gold coin. He takes offence at being tokenized, then names himself "number one Mud Mephit." He also offers to front Unla Key some special mud for the betting.

### Orsik
The Dwarf of the group, who aspires to be "this kick-ass Zhentarim" in Wallace's estimation. He finds the map to the wizard's tower, accepts Feetor's offer with an eye to "more followers," and leads the march with "fall in." He announces looting a little too openly for the human's liking.

### Vardis
Explained Hsing's relationship to Azam, in Wallace's recollection. Compares maps with Orsik, asks where the grabby water monster is, and teases Orsik about "Three days as the King."

### Thistle
The group's flyer. He reads the water flow to correct the map, conjures back his drake after losing it to the water weird, and scouts the island by air, where the half-ogres take him for a bug. He finds the wizard tower and signs that someone lives there.

### Unla Key
The Halfling. Unla Key believes daylight was the better time to attack, based on portents and signs, and is drawn into the mephits' betting by Feetor.

### Mama Manyknuckles
Described by Feetor as "the particularly ugly leader of a band of half-ogres," who mock and attack him. She is a rival to the grabby water monster for the mud mephits' loyalty, and her camp is on the kidney-shaped island. She is not seen in this chapter.

### The grabby water monster
An immortal, ancient creature of the marsh that can turn invisible and reforms elsewhere in the marsh within a few days after being destroyed. The party defeats it; a weird glow makes it visible to its enemies during the fight.

### The half-ogres
Mama Manyknuckles' band. Two of them in the southern camp throw rocks and dirt at Thistle, cursing "damn bug!"

## Items
### The map to the wizard's tower
Found by Orsik on the dead hobgoblins and compared against the party's own lost map. Thistle judges it wrong: "Anyone who followed this map was liable to get lost for hours."

### The gold coin
Given to Feetor as "A token of your power," which he uses to secure the other mud mephits' agreement to wait and see.

### Special mud
What Feetor says a bettor needs to join the mephits' wagering; he offers to front Unla Key some.
