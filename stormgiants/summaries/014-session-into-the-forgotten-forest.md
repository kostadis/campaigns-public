# Chapter 14 - Into the Forgotten Forest

Date: 20 to 23 Tarsakh 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens in the back room of the Golden Tankard, just after the door slams behind Vardis. Azam stares at the remaining adventurers and thinks, "oh dear." When Thistle demands something more than the reward Azam's leader has provided, Azam is left speechless — "The audacity!" — until Hsing, the pseudo-dragon, raises his head from a sleepy position and says in his quiet old voice, "I have information on your missing uncle." Thistle says he would rather have information about his friend, but Hsing presses: "Yes, I know. But I think you want your uncle’s location first."

When Thistle asks how he can know they have the information, Azam is outraged, standing to shout, "you insult Hsing? You question the honor of the Lords’ Alliance? You question the all-powerful SEER?" Hsing silences him with a shout of "AZAM!" and reminds him that these are adventurers who cannot tell the difference between a lizard and him. Asked how he knows who Thistle's uncle is, Hsing answers that "The SEER spoke," and Azam adds that they can also offer gold. Thistle and Unla Key confer and agree to the quest.

Azam relays the SEER's message. The Ettin Gralm has a realm in the Lonely Moor with a force of Hobgoblins and Goblins under his control, a tenday journey through the Forgotten Forest. Azam's agents say Gralm has been hunting down Fairies for food, and word has come that Bad Fruul is trying to force him into the army Bad Fruul intends to use to destroy Parnast. Azam has two magical contracts: one in which Gralm agrees not to align with Bad Fruul, and the preferred one, an alliance of Gralm and his forces with Azam's side against Bad Fruul. To Thistle's "Ettins and contracts?" Azam explains, "Ettin law is poorly developed. So we rely on these to enforce magical penalties if the counter-party violates the terms." Unla Key looks unimpressed until Azam whispers something to him and then asks aloud, "Mr. Key, would this be enough?" Unla Key smiles. Hsing teleports away, and Azam settles in to wait for the party's return.

In the main hall, Orsik, Vardis, and Wallace are drinking. Thistle flits up, tells them everything, and announces, "He’s giving me some vital information about my family, so we gotta go kill this ettin." Unla Key corrects him — "get Gralm to sign a contract." Orsik is unmoved by the offer of 100 Gold for the whole group, even when Thistle describes Uncle Joon as an excellent source of intel and Unla Key claims he can turn the 100 Gold into 300; Vardis coughs, "or zero – we saw that." Wallace pulls Orsik aside: he needs to protect his village, because Bad Fruul is going to attack it, and with the Ettin it will be worse. Orsik, confused, proposes killing Bad Fruul instead — "I have nothing against the Ettin. I barely know him." — and Wallace has to explain that the aim is a contract, not a killing.

Azam coughs and admits he misread his notes: he had been told to offer a potion of healing and a potion of greater healing if, during the attack during the festival, none of them put personal glory over saving a villager of Parnast, and if they successfully defeated the attack. Wallace keeps working on Orsik, reminding him that although he doesn't love Elspeth she is a friend, and that his Dad is here. Orsik, wanting to kill something, suggests killing the Wyvern first; Wallace defends the Wyvern as "a cost-saving measure" that stands in for a prison, guards, and food, and acts as a deterrent. What finally turns Orsik is Wallace's question of whether Uncle Joon could help Orsik and Vardis with their map. Thistle promises that Joon knows everything about the North and is "going to find your family treasure!" Vardis seems more interested, Orsik notices, and he relents: "This is the one town where a city guard wants me to work for them. And it’s where I have found my first lackey." He nods, and the group heads off.

On the 1st day of the 3rd tenday of Tarsakh the journey to Gralm’s realm crosses the grasslands that abut the great desert. At midday they find a clearing over a quarter mile wide where nothing lives and the birds fly around it; at its center stands a 50’ rusty sword. Unla Key recognises it as a sign from one of the many prophecies of the end times and resolves to try to remove it. His companions refuse to join him. He enlarges himself, takes a slight electric shock, and pulls hard, but the blade will not budge. Then the ground stirs and a decayed hand pops out. "Mr. Key is no fool," he thinks, and runs for the perimeter; when he looks back, the land is undisturbed.

The next evening the group reaches the edge of the Forgotten Forest. They remember the rocks of the Fey Portal they had found and realise the portal may not be as inert as they thought, but an approaching electrical thunderstorm is the more pressing concern. As Mr. Key rushes into the forest he overhears Orsik saying, "don’t be the tallest thing in a field when the storm god Talos is throwing his bolts."

On the 3rd day of the 3rd tenday of Tarsakh the drizzle becomes a raging thunderstorm as they march deeper into the forest. Around midday the storm abruptly stops, the sun becomes blindingly hot, and the group finds itself surrounded by a gang of goblins led by a hobgoblin named Mohm. Thistle, Orsik and Thuul kill the hobgoblin; the goblins shoot their arrows, flee, and hide behind trees. Before the group can attack, the ground sprouts trees and vines that try to entangle everyone, and only Thistle cannot escape. Thuul rushes into the forest and disappears in a pile of fine dust. Orsik quips, "I guess this is why they called it the Forgotten Forest!" and Thistle, in horror, recognises "a Fey Crossing."

A voice from the forest calls, "Thistle, is that you?" The goblins mutter about traitors. Mr. Key kills two with a volley of magic missiles, and Orsik throws a handaxe to kill the last goblin. As the group stands at the forest edge, Thuul reappears and snaps at the space where the goblins were — the Fey Crossing had sent him six seconds into the future.

The voice asks whether this is "The Thistle Wendrod, related to the Uncle Joon," and Thistle's reply — "What did he do?" — confirms it: only a family member would ask that. Thistle realises this must be the sprite in Prince Thornacious’ court who is a spy for the Zhentarim, the one Orsik is looking for. The sprite introduces himself as Sir Oblivillish. He explains that he was a court member until he went hunting and killed a stag that the Prince felt was his — the group remembers how possessive Prince Thornacious was about his game. As punishment he was cursed: he starts invisible, becomes visible, and then becomes invisible again, and he demonstrates that his invisibility no longer works. He left the court, which he calls "a lowly court, what with the exile," insisting that Queen Titania sent the Prince into exile and made the whole court follow him. Orsik privately sizes him up as "my kind of backstabbing lackey," and when the sprite protests that he was sent into "double exile," Orsik begins to think about killing him. Wallace picks up on this and tells Orsik to reread the notice from the Zhentarim. The dwarf reads it carefully and shouts, "OH, YOU ARE THE SPY THAT I AM SUPPOSED TO PROTECT!"

An Ettin with a realm in the Lonely Moor and a force of Hobgoblins and Goblins. Azam's agents say he has been hunting Fairies for food, and Bad Fruul is pressing him to join his army. He does not appear in this chapter.
