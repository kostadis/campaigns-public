# Chapter 74 - The Vampire's Lair

Date: 27 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The Undead Ogres were still climbing the tower, slowed for a minute by the darkness Unla Key had cast. The chapter follows this with an unattributed line: "The wand of wonder accomplishing something other than amusing the wizard, surprising." Blagothkus urged the party to use the moment, since the zombies could not see them and Glazhael had left, to run to Sandesyl's tower. Orsik was not impressed: the whole purpose of the maneuver was to kill off the ogres where they were primarily defenseless. Blagothkus pressed on, explaining that the Zombie Ogres were not a reserve but "just a way to keep Sandesyl's enemies trapped so that the dragon can kill them from above." Orsik answered him: "Then run. I have a mission, and I intend to complete it."

Thistle felt the dragon fear keenly, reflecting that with his new dragon eyes he should be terrifying the dragon, "not the other way around." Then he noticed something odd: Glazhael had moved out of eye shot, "almost like she wants me to approach Sandesyl's tower." As Thistle flew to the tower, Unla Key flew on his giant Pegasus wings, Vardis ran, and even Orsik seemed to have abandoned the quest to kill all of the ogres.

The old argument about Bob resurfaced. "Last chance to summon Bob," intoned Orsik; "One enemy at a time," whispered Thistle. Vardis sighed that he had liked the idea "until I realized we were planning to give him a near-invulnerable emissary in our plane of existence." Vardis pleaded with his eyes for help, and surprisingly Unla Key gave it, explaining that Bob is currently trapped in the Astral Plane, where he does not have access to all his power. "So he's impotent." / "I wouldn't use that term, but yes." The matter was settled: "Let's leave Bob where he is and go kill a vampire."

The tower was empty. Blagothkus explained that Sandesyl had carved out her own lair in the ice caves below. Last time she had been in the tower, but last time the party "hadn't killed her wyvern-liches, gotten rid of her material for zombies, and started an attack on her Ogre Zombies. And most importantly, she invited you." Because Sandesyl is not trusting of her minions, Blagothkus predicted she would send several of her spawn to find the party's bodies, and might leave her lair once she believed them dead. Orsik was pleased: "An enemy worthy of his tactical brilliance." When Orsik wondered how they could sneak with the giant along, Blagothkus offered to stay and guard the entrance, noting that Esclarotta said he had to help them only if they felt he was a help. Vardis patted Blagothkus on his kneecap and said, "Oh, don't be like that!" and the fairy squealed, "Staying here would be a hindrance." Blagothkus sighed and squeezed himself into the tunnel below the tower, which opened into a large cavernous area.

"Right hand rule," shouted Thistle. Vardis remembered a saying of the old guard of the Cult of Corellon — that if you keep doing something the same way, he will ensure the other way is easy — which is why Vardis always did things the other way. Thistle followed the ice tunnel north into a large cavern holding a statue of an enormous red dracolich, skeletal, crackling with arcane energy, with burning embers in its empty eye sockets, blackened horns, tattered bat-like wings, and demonic runes etched into its bones. The cleric recognized it: "It's Inferno, the Hell-Bound Dracolich, a once powerful red dragon who made a forbidden pact with a demon lord in its quest for immortality. The legends say that this was the first of the Dracoliches." Unla Key noticed something more about the statues — "This is Sammaster, the original leader of the Cult of the Dragon holding the Tome of the Dragon - the definitive book on Dracoliches." It almost looked, he said, like Sammaster was learning the secrets of undeath from Inferno.

Thistle wanted to move on; Orsik wanted the treasure, in case they needed it to defeat Sandesyl. Thistle scanned with his new eyes and found that, other than some valuable gems, "There is nothing of value." It was too late: Unla Key was already flying up to the statue of Inferno and prying the gems loose from the demon's eyes. The four statues surrounding the dracolich animated and advanced on Unla Key. Thistle threw a tantrum — "I TOLD YOU THIS WOULD HAPPEN!" — and then told Blagothkus to "Go clean up his mess, the rest of us will move forward." Orsik did not see why Thistle was upset, since the only resource being spent was "a giant we didn't count upon." Thistle retorted: "A giant we could have used to grapple the vampire?"

Continuing to the right, Thistle found a large cavern to the northwest with two vampire spawns — the same ones he had met earlier in Sandesyl's lair — gossiping in their coffins. He turned south instead, violating the right-hand rule, along an ice-hewn passageway that opened into two caverns: to the east, orc zombies busy cleaning; to the west, the sound of a Frost giant wheezing. Sniffing, he concluded that Sandesyl's lair lay to the west, through either the two vampire spawns or the frost giant. "I thought we were going to fight a vampire, not an army of minions," he muttered to Orsik, who was curiously smiling. Blagothkus rejoined them, slightly worn down from defeating the armored horrors but still in fighting shape.

Blagothkus explained the frost giant. He had lost the castle when it crashed and landed on the Miklos Glacier; the Heroes of the War of the Dragon showed up and convinced the local frost giant jarl, Brunvild, to help them repair and steal the castle. Brunvild thought he was rid of Sandesyl when the castle left the glacier, but she was a stowaway. She let him try to destroy the Cult of the Dragon and then turned on him — "That's why there were so many Frost Giant skeletons." Since she could not run the castle without Blagothkus, she came to him; Brunvild is kept alive "so she has something to snack on." Blagothkus added that "Esclarotta has a different version of the facts." Orsik wondered about another ally; Blagothkus judged Brunvild too weak. Thistle thought: "A distraction?"

Thistle flew off through the shadows, past the orc zombies and the ghost that gives them particular instructions on keeping the caves clean, and flew right into the exhausted, emaciated Frost Giant's face. "I'm here to free you, Harshnag sent me." Brunvild asked if Harshnag was there, and whether Thistle was not a little small to be allied to a giant; Thistle answered that he is a fairy with a mighty bow, powerful magics, and Dragon Eyes. Told that Blagothkus was alive and had seen the error of his ways, Brunvild was bitter: "Help? He's why I am trapped here. He's why my men are dead. He's why the dwarves trusted me are dead." When he asked about the dwarves, Thistle remembered the cadavers he had tossed over the Evermoors and said, "They aren't here. Maybe they escaped?" The orcs, it emerged, had been turned into Sandesyl's undead cleaning service under a ghost so obsessed with the cave being clean that it has not let them move out of it for two years — "No overhead. Smart."

Brunvild said that while Sandesyl lives he cannot escape. Thistle laid out the plan: Brunvild escapes, draws some of her allies from her lair, and the party kills her. Thistle recognized the look of a prisoner convinced that one more escape attempt is better than "living like a blood bank," and Brunvild saw the same expression in Thistle — that convinced him. As he got up he remarked, "Flying creatures have had a weird impact on my life. First, there was a Kenku, and now you." Thistle was stunned: the flying Kenku who defeated the Cult of the Dragon was a story the prisoners told themselves, and Kenku don't fly. But guard drakes were moving toward the giant's nest, following Brunvild as he lumbered out through the southern tunnel, and there was no time for questions.

Thistle flew back to the group: "We need to attack now." The vampire spawn were not surprised, but the 3HPP were surprised when Orsik waited for Unla Key to cast haste. "Why hasn't he been using it?" Unla Key shrugged: "it would be too easy that way."
