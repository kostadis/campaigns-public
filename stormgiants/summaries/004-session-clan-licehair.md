# Chapter 04 - Clan Licehair

Date: 3 to 5 Ches 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens on the 3rd day of the 1st tenday of Ches with Thistle handing out breakfast. "Good berries!" he proclaims, promising good berries every morning for everyone, and Vardis, Unla, and Orsik take their share and thank the ranger. After two days of hiking through the mountains in an uncommonly warm spring, the group arrives at an old abandoned dwarven outpost. The narration casts the gentle weather as a reasonable recompense by the gods for the near destruction of the world that Tiamat and their negligence nearly triggered.

On the 5th day of the 1st tenday of Ches, Veradaa Storr's notes bring the group to a hallway leading inwards. Thistle sees that the dust and cobwebs have been disturbed by many footprints, "too many to tell what has gone through here." Orsik studies the centuries-old stonework and mutters, "my kinsmen built this." Vardis needles him that his kinsmen want nothing more to do with him than Vardis's own do with Vardis, and Orsik turns pink with embarrassment. Pressed, and stared at by everyone, he finally gives in: "Fine, it’s clan Licehair." The group erupts in laughter, Vardis loudest of all, slapping Orsik on the back. Orsik gruffly insists that "Clan Licehair is a proud clan that secured the massive gem hoard of a black dragon," adding that he is not a member because he doesn't have lice in his hair. Vardis laughs louder, then offers, "well, there may be a gem hiding there."

Around the corner the group finds a room holding four small creatures — "kobolds," Thistle whispers — behind a small barricade blocking the passage to the south. In the middle of the room stands a small stone building with no apparent entrances, though it has holes. A kobold spots them and shouts, "begone foul creatures!"

The chapter then shifts to the kobold Agra, hiding inside the stone building with another kobold, Gen. Their red scales are marked with burns and their clothes look stinky even by kobold standards. Agra mutters that "That damned fool will fight those heavily armed adventurers," clamps a hand over Gen's mouth when he is about to act, and shouts, "Let’s sit tight and wait." He hears Nug arguing with Thistle over whether the fairy is or is not a filthy creature, and hopes the adventurers will be lured into the trap. To his horror the trap fails: the hidden kobolds move so loudly "that they could wake up the undead." The stones they drop on the group make him smile — "Filthy interlopers!" — but then come the telltale sounds of a sleep spell and the dull thud of dead bodies hitting the ground. When Gen asks, "what will we ever do?", Agra answers, "surrender and pray for the best?", grabs something white, and steps outside shouting, "we surrender!"

The group only stares at him, and Agra notices a halfling chewing berries. Thistle, remembering that kobolds served as foot soldiers of the Cult of the Dragon, demands to know whether they worked for the Cult and praised Tiamat. Agra sees that the fairy bears the telltale marks of one tortured by the Cult of the Dragon, turns pale, and pleads, "look, it was an accident, right? I was under orders. I didn’t mean any of it!" The enraged fairy shouts, "Where is my girlfriend?" Agra says that with the defeat of the cult and so many dragons he simply does not know, and that "we have been serving giant masters now." Misreading one of Thistle's ridiculous poses as forgiveness, Agra offers the kobolds' services as boot lickers. An elf in the group asks if they want to be slaves; Agra, offended, says "no, employees. We are very fluid in our loyalties." Vardis sighs that they don't have enough money.

Facing pointed weapons, Agra trades information. With the dragons defeated, the kobold tribes have been reduced to working for giants; a fire giant hired his tribe after "our Red Dragon left," while others were hired by hill and cloud giants, who seem to be competing for something — odd, because they don't normally do that. He tells a tangled story: after the Red Dragon, his boss made a deal with a cloud giant; when the cloud giants arrived in their castle the kobolds ran for the hills; the fire giants then sent them here to find the treasure of the cultists, saying the area was too small for them. Gen had heard the place was cursed, so Agra's pair hid in the stone building. When the other kobolds — "dead buffoons," he corrects himself — entered the room to the south, creatures emerged and attacked, and exploded when they died. Their leader Nug had said there was treasure in the corner of the southern room, along with "a glowing magical fire symbol, and a river of lava." Thistle keeps coming back to his girlfriend; Orsik cuts in with "got it, cloud giants, girlfriend. Go on!"

Agra asks to be allowed to leave now that he and Thistle are "friends". The surviving halfling suggests, "kobolds make good shields?", leaving Agra paralysed with fear until Gen proposes offering some treasure. The dwarf takes it and shouts, "go and no looting." Vardis stops them to ask whether they might tell the Fire Giants about the group. Agra explains that the Fire Giants expect them back with treasure or not at all, on pain of "a, how shall we say, negative review of our performance that would result in our termination" — then picks up some dirt, squishes it in his hand, and swallows it.

"Let them go," Orsik shouts. Tired of being looked down upon, he has decided to stop doing "that thing he does," and announces, "I’m going in." When Vardis asks if he is okay, Orsik answers that he is done being the guy everyone hates, that it got to him that Old Keller didn't even want to talk to him — "It’s like I am not even good enough to be a member of Clan Licehair. LICEHAIR!" He pushes through the barricade into the smoky southern room, where large fires crackle and a brighter glow splits the room in half like a river. A large burst of fire erupts from a prominent symbol etched into the wall in the southeastern corner, and the stench of burned kobolds is rank. Orsik pauses, thinks, and says, "hey Thistle, why don’t you go on ahead."

Vardis smiles. Thistle finds that he burns within 10 feet of a fire, plots a careful route around the hot spots to the fire symbol, and realises that disabling it will be necessary if they are to take the silver. He cannot tell which god it represents, tries to remember what his uncle said, and begins damaging the symbol with arrows while Orsik, Vardis and Unla watch. The symbol belches fire and a magmin emerges; retreating to the corner, Thistle draws out the magmin hiding in the lava and is surrounded. Orsik, Vardis and Unla silently agree to help. Unla Key's thunderwave pushes the creatures away and they explode harmlessly, and Orsik kills the last with a thrown hand-ax — but Thistle takes a dying magmin's explosion in the face and has to brush off lava to avoid burning to death. Vardis heals him.

Thistle resumes shooting the symbol, then announces he needs to cast a spell, and that afterwards he must "figure out how to sever the connection to the god." Asked how, he replies, "I’ll figure it out." He doesn't in time: another magmin emerges from the lava. Ignoring it, Thistle asks himself, "C’mon, what did Uncle Joon say about these gods?" — and while dodging the creature's arms it finally clicks: "got it!" The symbol becomes inert. Orsik kills the Magmin, whose exploding entrails burn Thistle; Thistle shrugs. The smoke clears, and Orsik realises the room had been a forge before whatever evil artifact took it over. The group takes some silver and extracts several magmin shells prized by spell casters.

As they leave, Orsik muses about legends of heroes who stayed in one place killing hundreds of summoned monsters until they became powerful beyond measure. Vardis yawns: "that’s a legend." Most of the time, he says, the summoned creatures get annoyed and something really awful gets summoned that kills the hero. Orsik scratches his beard and sighs.
