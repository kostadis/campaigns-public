# Chapter 30 - Finally working for the Harpers

Date: 23 to 30 Kythorn 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
In his tower and inner sanctum, the Harper Krowen Valharrow studies the adventurers before him and wonders whether they are the tool he can use to unlock the secrets behind the fall of the Ordning. Ezra had discovered that using mercenaries derisked core Harper agents and provided plausible deniability, and since then the Harpers had completed missions that would once have been impossible. Krowen is an old-timer who believes in the old ways, but these are desperate times, and this group had been sent by Darathra herself. He asks, "So, are you in?" When they only stare, he privately worries that he is coming off desperate and wonders how Ezra pulled this off with the First Faction.

The dwarf answers, "we have been doing this for some time, so yes." When the dwarf then asks how they get out, Krowen, recalling reports that the dwarf is close to the Zhentarim, answers "The front door!" The elf points out the contradiction: they had been sent through a special teleport spell involving some alcohol to hide their identity, and now they are told to walk out the front door. Krowen privately concedes that "he saw through my ruse to burn Orsik," then puts on his best confused old mage look and says, "I can’t believe I forgot that!" Their next destination is "Yartar to find the sword." When Vardis asks what they must pay, Krowen answers "Nothing!" and the dwarf mumbles, "Only our loyalty," before insisting, "I always wanted to work for the Harpers."

The fairy then asks Krowen whether he has ever enchanted a flying tower. Krowen explains that the orb needed to turn a tower into a flying castle can only be obtained from the Cloud Giants, by persuasion or violence. When Vardis suggests they could handle a cloud giant, Krowen privately decides to dissuade them, warning that a cloud giant is "trained to fight like the greatest of fighters and armed to the teeth with magical weapons and defenses of all kinds." The look on the elf reassures him, and Krowen smiles as they teleport to his agent in Yartar.

Orsik shouts, "Time to get that sword!" and Thistle finds himself in a garden of statues of frolicking dryads and satyrs. He groans that it is all wrong — "That fairy would never be frolicking with Alciabes! She hated him!" — and the illusion falls apart. The real place is a 40-foot-square guardian patio enclosed by an 8-foot-high wall of ivy-covered stone, a garden in disrepair around the circle they arrived in. A masculine-looking person arrives and demands to know who they are. Orsik declares them "Agents of the Harpers," which draws the retort "Agents or Contractors." Learning they came from Everlund, the old man concludes they spoke to Krowen and rails against him: "Being a Harper meant something. It wasn’t just a job!" He then introduces himself: "I am Kolbaz and your host. Welcome to Yartar."

Orsik asks about Harthal. Thistle whispers that Harthal was dating a rogue and that the local rogues guild probably knows where he is, though Orsik can tell Thistle is uncomfortable meeting the Hand of Yartar after their attempted assassination. Kolbaz is dumbfounded that Thistle is an assassination target — "They don’t do that kind of wet work." — and Thistle replies that "the pointy end of their blades were pointed at my neck." At Orsik's prompting, Thistle asks Kolbaz how he feels about the Lords’ Alliance, and Kolbaz delivers a long denunciation of it as a league of wealthy lords who "do not care for the little people." Satisfied, Thistle asks whether he is allied with the Cult of the Dragon. Orsik recalls that the Harpers first identified that risk and rallied the factions in the War of the Dragon, and Kolbaz straightens: "I FOUGHT THE CULT AT THE WELL OF DRAGONS!"

Thistle asks whether he knows Adimond Kroskas; Kolbaz has never heard of him. Thistle explains that the Cult of the Dragon is trying to assassinate him, which Kolbaz finds preposterous because the cult was destroyed. Thistle insists the cult wants to clean up its mistakes, since a key ingredient for the ritual was the Spring’s First Blossom that Adimond collected. Kolbaz bristles — "I know what the Spring’s First Blossom is. I got a first-class education. I’ll have you note!" — then pauses, saying it was not used in the ritual: "I was involved in the research, which was not used in the ritual." Pressed twice, he answers "YES!"

To soothe Kolbaz's hurt pride, Orsik praises the illusion — "Only Thistle saw through it." — but Thistle begins listing its errors as "an inaccurate representation of the Seelie Court." Kolbaz suggests they meet for tea to improve it, and Thistle glares: "to make your magic fantasy land a bit more realistic as you treat my people as props to amuse?" Kolbaz has no links to the Hand, but tells them, "The Wink and Kiss is their hangout. Go to the tavern and ask a server." Vardis and Orsik are surprised there are so many rogues in town; Thistle cannot believe they did not notice. Thistle does not want to go to a bar full of people who want to assassinate him, but Vardis and Orsik promise to defend him, and Unla Key, still recovering from his ordeal, adds, "they will have to get through me to get to you, Thistle. All 6 of my hit points." Thistle realizes he has allies: "I love you guys."

The next day the group goes to the Wink and Kiss Tavern near the easternmost gate of Yartar, a maze of compact rooms and concealed passages, declared a safe haven for anyone who enters, where weapons are checked at the door. "Good thing we have spells," mutters Unla Key. Vardis notices an old painting of the former owners, Asklar and Beldora, and thinks he knew them. Orsik admits, "we are broke" — they sold everything to get him raised. Vardis muses about bribing someone with companionship, and Orsik agrees he has "that goth elf look." When a tiefling barhop arrives, Vardis proposes they "split an order of water," earning a glare. Thistle excuses himself to the restroom, and Vardis thinks, "coward."

The tiefling sizes them up and offers to introduce them to "a gal." She recognises them as the group that defeated the giant in Triboar and has been advertising the play. She gives her name — "Maia, short for Qumaia" — and tells them folks have been calling them the ‘three half-pints and a pint.’ Vardis laughs hard at Orsik's discomfort, and harder when Thistle announces "Thistle Wendrod and Companions!" and Orsik blusters, "so now it’s all about you!" Maia explains that her contact needs people to get some other people; Orsik insists they took down "Two" giants, and she corrects herself to "two fire giants." Unla Key wants to gamble, since he changes the fates, but Vardis warns that if he dies they cannot raise him.

Over the next five days "The three half-pints and a pint" work as bounty hunters, splitting into two groups. Unla Key, exploiting his ability to twist the fates, captures "Dashing" Dallon Flek, a handsome redhead; the others capture Tink Urbelle, a grubby gnome with curly hair. The Hand of Yartar member handing out the bounties, Elicia “Kal” Kallaphan, a severe half-elf, sees through their attempt at humor and pays them: "Here’s your payout, 200 Gold, 50 for me, and so 150 for you." It is a lot, but not enough.

That evening, back at the Wink and Kiss, Maia charges five gold for "the information you need to get the information you need," and points them to a lady dressed in white at the bar named Eltio, whom Vardis sizes up as a sullen, distant, and dangerous player. Unla Key, sober for two weeks after a lengthy drinking binge, mutters, "the longest." Eltio explains that information on Harthal will cost 500 gold, or they can get Tamalin Zoar to waive the fee. When they ask who that is, Eltio scolds Maia and sends her off with a warning, then explains that Tamalin is a legendary member of the Hand of Yartar who built her own town on the Evermoor Way on the way to Everlund. Vardis tries to win Eltio over with play tickets and a "GOLD sponsor" offer, which she declines, until Orsik promises: "look, we’ll get this letter as long as you promise to bring 50 friends to our play!" She answers, "deal."

Later, Kolbaz mutters, "Oh gods, no. That’s not possible," before a flash of lightning and he crumples to the ground. The feet of a small person sound on the floor beside him, and an ominous deep growl of a voice, impossibly loud for the speaker's body, says, "you meddling little fool." On the same date, the three half-pints and a Pint arrive at Calling Horns.
