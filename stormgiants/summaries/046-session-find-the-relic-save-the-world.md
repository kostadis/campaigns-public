# Chapter 46 - Find the relic, save the world

Date: 3 Eleasis 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
After securing the Shard of the Ise rune, the 3HPP head back into the Temple of Annam. Thistle opens by apologising to the group for not really being present while they explored the warrens and the rooms; they turn to him, confused, and he explains, "I was spending time in my dreams with my dearest." Harshnag groans, and Thistle chooses to ignore it. Now "fully here", Thistle declares that they must be far more systematic in their exploration, and points out two secret doors on the west wall, one to the North and another to the south.

Thistle flies over and squeezes through the northern door into a featureless room containing only a lever 12' high off the ground, which he cannot push. He calls for Harshnag, and the Frost Giant observes, "If you could melt this ice, it would be easier." Unla Key starts to explore esoteric options, including Fireball and burning hands, but Thistle sighs, pulls out a torch and lights it. Vardis praises him: "Bless our ranger." Harshnag takes the torch, lets the ice melt, and lifts the lever. A loud grinding sound follows, and Thistle flies off to find its cause: "Yes, this opens the Portcullis!"

As Thistle is about to enter the southern door, Orsik notices a crack in the temple's walls and is enraptured: "My goodness! Look at this crack! You can tell a lot from a break in a building. The stonemasonry is impressive!" Thistle waits while Orsik delivers a monologue on stonework, masonry, rock formations, cracks, and the use of magic, which he disapproves of because anyone can use magic and it dilutes the artisanal work; as far as he is concerned, magical stonework isn't stonework. He then concedes that a magical device can shape the stone, and concludes that using magic to make stonemasons more productive is fine, but using magic to shape stones is cheating and really about putting stonemasons out of a job. The southern room, like the northern one, holds a single lever, which Harshnag once again raises, and once again a portcullis opens. Thistle announces, "Well, it would appear we have to go through the southern doors."

Through the door Unla Key finds a vast, brightly lit room, 240 feet wide by 440 feet deep, with a 200-foot-wide alcove in the center of the east wall and six pillars, each 20 feet in diameter and 60 feet tall, supporting the vaulted ceiling. The room is warm and dry, its light and heat coming from a raging fire within a bowl-shaped basin in the center of the chamber. Unla Key marches toward it: "40,000 years this has been lit." Thistle thwarts Unla Key's attempt to conduct reckless experiments by rushing ahead and declaring the fire "boringly natural" — kept alive by natural gas escaping through holes in the basin, which sustains it indefinitely. Vardis nods. Around the fire pit stand six granite tables, matching benches, and two 9-foot-tall stone slabs stained with blood and grease; the southernmost slab holds neat stacks of beaten copper plates and goblets, green and black with age, while the dishes on the northernmost slab have been knocked over.

Then a centipede-like creature erupts out of the ground: a huge blue monstrosity with what appear to be hundreds of legs, winglike fans on its head, a mouth with thousands of sharp teeth, and a warmth emanating from its body. Harshnag bellows, "A remorhaz! I will take the Ax of Thrym while the rest of you hold off the creature. Then we will run away!" Orsik refuses — "No, we will fight!" — and just as he says it, the creature swallows Thuul whole.

Vardis shouts, "Frosty doesn't understand the power of Unla Key!" Thistle flies above a table to put space between himself and anything from beneath, and his arrows hit; Orsik mutters, "One arrow." Orsik advances and strikes the creature repeatedly with his blade, feeling the heat of its body burn him. A surge of psychic energy flows past him and hits the creature — Orsik recognises it as Raulothim's Psychic Lance — and the creature is stunned in place. A boulder lands, missing the creature, and Orsik mutters about help arriving "from afar". Harshnag then surprises Orsik by charging the monster. Vardis runs up to heal Orsik, who waves him off, so the cleric of the First of the Seldarine casts a sacred flame and summons a spiritual weapon in the shape of a long sword resembling Sahandrian. Thistle's bow fires again, one arrow hitting and one missing. Orsik attacks again, braving the flames, strikes twice, and shouts to Unla Key, "Again!" Unla Key answers, "I can't, but maybe this will work," and streams little bits of ice from his hand that trap the creature in binding ice. Unla Key smiles, and Orsik understands that the diviner has twisted the fates to get the desired outcome. Orsik cries, "Attack my friends, you, a horror of the ice!" and is delighted as the giant's massive attacks crack the immobile creature's carapace. Vardis finishes it with a swipe of his summoned sword.

Harshnag shouts, "I knew you could do it." Before Orsik can retort, Vardis responds, "Didn't you just say we couldn't?" Harshnag insists, "That was me encouraging you!" and Vardis replies, "Ret-con." Harshnag stares; Orsik, equally confused, remembers how Vardis went through his temporal phase, convinced that time wasn't linear and that facts could be retroactively changed to create a new reality, a process he called ret-conning.

With the creature dead and the Ax of Thrym in hand, the group heads back from the feast hall. Orsik proposes that the safe course is for Harshnag to go with the Ax of Thrym and hit the rune while the others wait; Harshnag nods. Orsik then stares aghast as Unla Key runs after the Frost Giant: "Are you insane?" Unla Key's answer: "To take no risks when you know that you will be there at the very end and if you aren't, the universe will end is personally liberating."

Beyond the thundering archway lies a hexagonal chamber, its walls rising 50 feet before tapering to a 90-foot-high apex. In each corner stands a life-size statue of a giant — hill, stone, frost, fire, cloud, and storm — holding up a heavy iron lantern whose cold, magical light illuminates a giant corpse lying beneath a shroud of frost in the middle of the room, a giant frost-covered Morningstar beside it. The giant wears a gold breastplate, and beneath the body is a circle of runes. A ghost in the form of a cloud giant rises from the ground and bellows, "Why have you come here!" Unla Key, who doesn't speak giant, just stares, until Harshnag whispers loudly in his ear, "Ask him how to use the oracle, ask him about the Ordning."

The ghost answers, "You are lucky." Unla Key agrees — "Yes, I am!" — leaving the ghost confused. It explains: "This is a true Oracle. You can ask six questions every day. Anyone who asks questions in the Oracle will receive a true answer." As the 3HPP start proposing questions about the treasure map's location and a missing girlfriend, the ghost speaks again with a quest: the divine oracle told him and his father that a great upheaval would upset the balance of power in the world; it told Blagothkus, his father, that he could never impress the gods enough to earn their favor, and urged the son to step out from beneath his father's shadow. Overcome with despair and envy, the father slew him in the fight that followed, and has since retired to his castle to mourn. Unla Key, delighted and worried, asks, "so what do you want?" The ghost replies, "For you to kill him!" Unla Key thinks, "One giant cloud castle," and asks where. "Parnast. I don't know if it is a real place." Orsik laughs, "We know about that place." Unla Key remembers the cloud giant's castle that blew past them so many months ago, almost killing them: "Payback and a castle."

Thistle marches into the circle and asks, "Where is Laela!" One of the lights burns out, and the Oracle answers, "Duke Zalto has her." Orsik jumps in and shows his old family heirloom: "What is this a map of?" Another light burns out: "Beyond the Silver Marches, near the Ice Spires in the iron tower."

Harshnag is aghast that they have spent questions on trivial matters rather than on how to stop the giants, and changes tack when he realises they might be comfortable with the war, warning that "a very powerful magic user" helping them might decide they are not deserving of his support. Thistle says he was "Just confirming the accuracy of the Oracle." Harshnag counts slowly to 10. Unla Key rushes in, and Harshnag is horrified but relieved when the halfling asks, "What must be done to stop the giants?" The Oracle answers, "Find a magic conch of the storm giant king, Hekaton. Use it to visit Hekaton's court. Root out the evil therein." Unla Key steps out and tells Harshnag, "Yep, checks out, this is an authentic oracle." Harshnag glares.

Vardis asks Harshnag, "Do you know anything about Hekaton?" Harshnag answers, "Yes, but what is this evil?" — and, standing in the circle, he has asked the Oracle, which blows out another light: "The storm giant court is in the chaos following the disappearance of King Hekaton." Harshnag quickly steps out; Vardis mutters, "Thanks." Vardis then shouts, "Where is the conch?" — "most direct question" — and receives the answer, "In the clutches of a giant lord." When Orsik proposes asking about Duke Zalto, the Oracle sighs, the last light goes out, and it shouts: "First, you must prove yourself!" Human warriors who came to these lands fought the giants, stole their relics and buried them, building altars to Uthgar, their god-king, atop them, surrounded by burial mounds. The party must retrieve one or more relics and bring them as tribute: "The more relics you deliver, the more paths you will have to choose from." Several evil giant lords stand ready to oppose them, but "You can ruin all of their plans by defeating just one of them. Which one that is remains to be seen."

Thistle asks, "What are we supposed to do now?" Harshnag sighs, "I know what needs to be done," walks outside the Eye of Annam, and sits on the main temple's floor: "I know the names and locations of all nine spirit mounds." He spends the next hour drawing a crude map of the spirit mounds relative to key landmarks and terrain features, but admits, "I do not know what relics are buried at each mound or which giant lord is associated with each relic." Thistle sums up: "We find a relic and come back, and the Oracle will tell us how to find a giant lord. But we don't know which relic is where." Harshnag confirms: "That is correct."
