# Chapter 58 - Enter Adimond. Exeunt Harshnag

Date: 28 Eleasis to 11 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
Blagothkus sat silently in the control room of his flying castle, and the situation was clear. A vampire, Sandesyl, had been treating the castle as her domain. She had been content to let Blagothkus travel the world pursuing whatever plots interested him, so long as he supplied a steady stream of victims. But when Blagothkus got into a funk and parked the castle in its current location north of Parnast, more food was needed, and Sandesyl reacted by eating through all of Blagothkus' minions. Over the intervening months that supply of victims had been slowly and brutally harvested. The 3HPP understood that the only living things left to eat were themselves, Blagothkus, and a pair of Ogres named Worug and Tak. Standing outside the tower, they could feel the vampire staring at them through the unseeing eyes of her zombie spawns.

Blagothkus now introduced a new character to this menagerie: his wife, who apparently controls the castle. Entering the tower, Thistle found a staircase of ice leading up to a chamber whose walls and ceiling sparkled with jewels — glowing moonstones, thumb-sized emeralds, silvery mithral wands, spheres covered in turquoise and gold, and dozens of copper levers and golden spheres set into the walls. The walls then seemed to vanish, giving a perfect aerial view in all directions, and more than a dozen glowing white runes drifted about the room like snowflakes. The cloud giant sat cross-legged, harried, tired, unshaven and disheveled. He pointed at the runes and screamed that she would not let the castle go anywhere, waved his hand through one of them — "See?" — and the voice of his wife, Esclarotta, boomed: "I am done with him."

When Vardis offered to drive, Esclarotta answered that she wanted Blagothkus dead. She laid out his "insane plan to help the Dragons bring Tiamat", in which the little people would be so weakened by the dragons' failure that the giants could rule; and she told how he had gone to the Eye of the All-Father and killed his son: "He couldn’t just let his son kill him." She sniveled that it was worse still because "we aren’t one of the cloud giant families making a play for rulership." Asked by Thistle who was, she named Countess Sansuri of Lyn Armaal — "a vain, glorious, greedy, and ugly cloud giantess" — whose family held magical artifacts when Ostoria fell, and she berated her husband: "Instead of going and playing your games with dragons, you should have seized the maps from the Countess!" Blagothkus stretched his hands toward the voice as if to say, "See what I am dealing with?"

The fairy asked whether she wanted help getting rid of him; she shrieked that she wanted him dead, and the floating icons arranged themselves into an arrow pointing at Blagothkus. The 3HPP learned that Esclarotta was furious at him for trapping her in this console, and Thistle sensed there was something more. When Thistle floated, "Let’s say we help you kill your husband here," Blagothkus roused himself for combat, and Vardis rushed to stand before him: "Hypothetically! It’s just a hypothetical." The problem, the party reasoned, was that if Blagothkus died the vampire would come in. Esclarotta grew petulant — "Maybe it would be better when he’s a vampire. He never had a soul anyways." — and Vardis whispered, "That was a mean thing to say…" Blagothkus, finally finding someone on his side in his endless arguments with his wife, sat down again.

The 3HPP debated what they wanted. Unla Key wanted the castle because he was tired of walking. The group learned that Esclarotta hated the vampire, though Sandesyl kept the castle clean and there were no rats on board. Realising the party would not kill Blagothkus for fear of the vampire, Esclarotta screamed at her husband for never dealing with Sandesyl when told to: "Bring Tiamat into the world, he’s got time for,  but dealing with a Vampire in his house? No. Not important for the big shot!"

The party settled on getting Esclarotta to fly the castle. Thistle filled her in on their adventures, making a particular point of the Fire Giants and Duke Zalto's ambitions and collection of artifacts. Blagothkus and Esclarotta laughed — "Fire Giants? How absurd." — and cracked jokes about Fire Giants that did not translate into common. Thistle was pleased — "laughter is the basis of any good relationship." — and asked for her help. Her terms: "You have to kill Countess Sansuri. I hate her. She is a cow!" Vardis laughed, "So we are doing body shaming now?", and Esclarotta corrected herself: "No. That was uncalled for. She’s an arrogant, selfish, horrible, absurd creation that should be dead." This turned into more screaming at Blagothkus over the lost play for rulership; he explained it was never in the cards, and she replied that if he had let Eigeron kill him, maybe she would be queen now. Sensing this could go on for hours, Thistle asked about the Fire Giants again, which set them both laughing. Esclarotta agreed to help but did not know where the giant lords were. When Thistle announced that the Oracle could tell them, the castle started to move: "Well, why didn’t you lead with that?"

As the castle took flight, there was a knock on the door. Thistle flew out to find a vampire spawn under an umbrella, dressed in the purple of the cult, asking where the castle would stop for a meal. Thistle shouted, "Silverymoon," and explained, "Because we have a vendetta against House Margaster." The stunned spawn said, "Well, that is a surprise," and left. At nightfall Sandesyl herself stood in the courtyard with her zombies and several spawns and asked who had the vendetta. Thistle declined her invitation to come outside — "How about not?" / "I can hear you just fine from inside here." — and she professed disappointment at his distrust. She explained she would not fight a war with the Margasters: "Asmodeus protects them. And I refuse to upset the archfiend’s plans. Only an insane person would do that." Unla Key shouted, "An insane plan? Cool!" Eventually Sandesyl suggested a village of humans along the way, Thistle countered with a village of orcs, and both then remembered the Uthgardt barbarians of Flint Rock. Sandesyl agreed and waited patiently for the castle to arrive there. Seeing the deal made, Delsephine told the 3HPP that Klauth wished them the best of luck in ending the war of the giants.

Sandesyl waited patiently for the castle to arrive at Flint Rock, and on the night of the 9th to morning of the 10th day of the 3rd tenday of Eleasis 1492, Sandesyl's vampires found a collection of Uthgardt nomads mourning their despoiled artifact, captured them, and brought them to the castle, which had settled some miles away. The vampires left convincing evidence, and a survivor to spread the tale, that the deed was done by monsters under the control of House Margaster in service of Silverymoon. For the next several days, until the castle reached the Eye of the All-Father, the screams of Sandesyl's victims echoed through the castle.

When the castle arrived, the 3HPP hurried past the domed entrance and the six pillars carved like giants, down to the main temple of Annam, where Harshnag waited. They told him they had a castle and were non-committal when he asked about Blagothkus. He picked up Thrym’s great axe, touched the rune on the portal, and walked into the Eye. There the party met Eigeron, who asked about his father. Learning that Blagothkus was not dead but that his castle was here, Eigeron went mad and tried to possess one of the heroes; Vardis invoked the power of Corellon and turned him. With little time, the heroes deposited each artifact in turn, and the Oracle revealed where each of the giant leaders who had taken the mantle to rule over all giants could be found: the hill giant chief at Grudd Haug, the stone giant Thane at Deadstone Cleft, the Frost Giant Jarl at Svardborg on the Sea of Moving Ice, the Fire Giant Duke at Ironslag below a village of yakfolk, and the Cloud Giant Countess at Lyn Armaal — each with the conch the party seeks.

Before the turning wore off, the heroes marched out of the portal, leaving Eigeron howling for revenge, and into the main temple — where Thistle saw "a wondrous sight, Adimond Kroskas." Thistle prepared to attack, but Adimond Kroskas saw Harshnag and transformed into an Ancient Blue Dragon. Harshnag shouted, "I will distract the dragon. You must go and save the realms." Thistle wanted revenge and Unla Key saw an epic battle he knew he would win, so both hesitated; Vardis and Orsik started for the exit but lingered when their allies stopped. Seeing his warnings fall on deaf ears, Harshnag swung his greataxe at the statue of Annam the All-Father, chipped it, dodged the dragon, and struck again, breaking off a large chunk. The desecration cracked the ceiling and the mountain began to fall. "Flee!" yelled Harshnag. "Your fate lies elsewhere." The heroes ran. The dragon seemed uninterested in them and attacked Harshnag, and as the party rushed down the hallway to their castle, the hallway and the temple where the two monsters fought collapsed behind them.

## Locations
### Blagothkus' castle
Blagothkus' flying cloud castle, parked north of Parnast at the start of the chapter. Its tower holds a control room up a staircase of ice — a chamber of jewels, copper levers and golden spheres whose walls turn transparent and through which glowing runes drift. Esclarotta controls it from the console. Sandesyl's zombies and spawns occupy its courtyard.

### Parnast
The castle is parked in its current location north of Parnast.

### Lyn Armaal
Countess Sansuri's castle, per Esclarotta. The Oracle places it in the sky over untamed moors to the southeast; the Countess's conch is in a secret chest in the highest spire.

### Silverymoon
Thistle's first suggestion for the vampire's meal stop. The vampires later frame House Margaster "in service of Silverymoon."

### Flint Rock
The home of the Uthgardt barbarians, chosen by Sandesyl and Thistle as the feeding stop.

### Eye of the All-Father
The temple complex, with its domed entrance, six pillars carved like giants, a hallway, and the main temple of Annam with its statue of Annam the All-Father. A portal leads into the Eye, where Eigeron's ghost and the Oracle are found. Harshnag's desecration of the statue brings the temple and hallway down.

### Grudd Haug
Per the Oracle, "the den of the hill giant chief," on one of the eastern arms of the great river to the south.

### Deadstone Cleft
Per the Oracle, "a canyon of the stone giant thane," beyond the great forest in the gray peaks to the southeast.

### Svardborg
Per the Oracle, "the berg of the frost giant jarl," among the glaciers of the Sea of Moving Ice to the west.

### Ironslag
Per the Oracle, "the forge of the fire giant duke," below a village of yakfolk on a cold mountainside to the east.

## NPCs
### Blagothkus
A cloud giant, owner of the flying castle. Harried, unshaven and disheveled, he is trapped in an endless argument with his wife, who blames him for helping the Dragons bring Tiamat and for killing his son at the Eye of the All-Father.
