# Chapter 11 - Into the Tower

Date: 2 Tarsakh 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The group stands on the island in what was Mama Mia's bedroom. From the crenelated battlements they work out that this must have been the roof of the Wizard's tower — a tower that has sunk into the swamp. Mama Mia's bed turns out to be a stone table, and across it, in the northwest corner, stands a statue. Wallace looks to Orsik for guidance, Orsik waves his hand, and the young lad cleans it until it is plainly a gargoyle — "and this time, just a statue." Wallace discovers that it has an arm that can be rotated. Unla, who always feels lucky, moves the arm; the table tries to move, and Thistle realizes Mama Mia's pendant can be used as a key. Unla pulls again, and the table opens onto a staircase going down. Wallace, crying "My first tower," runs down first.

The stairs end at a landing with a poem — "Be not faithless / Magic and Knowledge divine" — and a set of symbols. Thistle and Unla determine that the symbols represent gods of the realms and must be put in order: the human hand with a finger pointing upward is Azuth, the single-lit candle is Deneir, and Oghma is a simple blank scroll. Vardis does not recognize the star of Mystra, "a god of wizards," and neither does Unla Key, who has a dim view of the gods because he knows "that he is why the world continues." The failure springs an old trap, and a boulder collides with the heroes in front of the wall. Thistle takes over the guessing, since he can fly away from a boulder, and — "as they say in the Fey, he picked a dandelion" — the door opens.

Beyond is a room 30 feet deep and 25 feet wide, with archways to the left and right. A prismatic-hued will-o'-wisp hovers over a short pillar bearing an offering bowl, before a fresco of a blue, seven-pointed star, and the walls show murals of a woman performing feats of magical prowess. The group now figures out that this is Mystra, and the others give sideways glances at Vardis and Unla. Vardis sees a tourmaline in the bowl and does not steal it; Unla leaves it too, explaining he's a gambler, not a thief.

Heading north, the group passes through areas lit by globes of spun glass glowing with magical light, over dusty stone floors that seem to have formed mounds, in air that is chilly and, oddly, stale and dry. Thistle notices there are no insects or spiders: "weird." The archway north leads to a pillared hall bathed in rainbow light from walls of scintillating light anchored by pillars. Thistle concludes from the arches that the place was once open to the elements before earth and mud filled the openings.

In the hall stand elemental altars. At the jasper altar, Vardis reads the engraving "Grumbar's oath unchanging and true. Earth remains strong, protecting all." Thistle recalls a story from his uncle about Grumbar — "He's the boss of elementals" — and when everyone stares, explains, "My uncle traveled a lot and told me many stories." Thistle shouts, "okay, let's put some dirt on the altar!" Vardis suggests the mud mephit, and Orsik warns Wallace to back away "out of the blast radius." Thistle drops the dirt in the geode and feels a blessing course through his bones that makes him resistant to being hurt by the earth. Orsik hits the ground and concludes, "I guess it's only the people making an offering."

Unla steps up to the quartz wave altar, engraved with Istishia's triumph, drops in some water, and knows that water can not harm him. Vardis goes past the shimmering walls to the tornado-shaped altar of Akadi. Thistle once again remembers his lessons about the Gods of Faerûn and delivers a long account of Istishia; prompted by Thistle, Vardis burns incense, feels a surge of power, and is blessed too. Orsik smirks, "isn't your willingness to offer incense to other gods why you were exiled?" — "Pragmatic."

Orsik, mumbling "I gotta get me some resistance," steps up to the flame altar of Kossuth. Vardis offers a lit candle while Thistle flies out of the room so as not to be there "for the explosion," but the candle and torch have no effect. Unla wonders aloud whether he should set himself on fire and put himself in the offering; "Sounds risky," says Vardis, and Unla decides against it. Burning rope and casting produce flame also fail. Orsik is tempted to smash the altar but reconsiders.

Thistle leads the group south into a wood-paneled guest room with three large piles of rocks, scattered statues, and a layer of fine gravel. The figures' eyes open and the largest statue speaks in grinding sounds; Unla casts comprehend languages and translates: "What is your business here?" Orsik answers with a hand gesture, and the gargoyle smiles: "Yes, I agree with you. His arrogance bound us to protect his tower." A second gesture earns only a stare. Wallace marches through his first secret door in the southeast — not truly secret, given the worn floor — and finds it leads to another secret door; Orsik shouts, "Leave it for later." The door south is locked. Thistle can find and remove a trap but the door is too tightly locked to open without tools, and Orsik mutters, "I am offended that you think I would have thieves' tools."

The group heads back past the shrine of Mystra. In the next shrine a fresco shows a human hand, index finger pointed up, and a bearded, old human statue holds a topaz-capped staff; Orsik recognizes Azuth and is not entirely happy with this god's unwillingness to part with his blessings. Vardis shouts, "I strayed from my God's teachings, but I am not looting." In the shrine beyond, covered in scripts and glyphs, a balding robed statue stands beside a desk with a quill and ink pot. "Deneir," shouts Thistle — not from his uncle this time, but because he "recognized the symbol from earlier." As everyone leaves, a sound behind them reveals a scroll tube holding a scroll of tongues. "I'll take that," says Unla.

Orsik and Wallace enter the next room, whose walls show ways of preserving and spreading knowledge. "Book people," Orsik mumbles. Instead of a statue he finds a desk with a blank, open tome, an inkstand with a quill, and a large diamond paperweight. As he is about to take the paperweight, a voice says, "All should surrender knowledge so it may be shared," and the quill rises. Vardis, from outside, works out that they must write knowledge in the book. Wallace writes "My name is Wallace." Orsik writes "Dwarves are awesome." Vardis writes "berries are good." Thistle writes "spring's first flower!" Unla writes for some time and says, "I know how the world is going to end," but his writing is in a cipher known only to him, called "Bad Hand Writing." The voice answers: the gift is blessed, it does enjoy a good berry, "And Unla Key, that's not how the world ends." Orsik snickers that Unla's whole raison d'etre has been destroyed; the voice says Unla just got some details wrong, and whispers something to Unla Key alone. Vardis picks up the scroll that materializes: "lesser restoration."

The group then goes down the stairs through the secret door in the gargoyle room. Wallace leads to a landing and a moldy door, beyond which rusted metal turns to dust at his touch. A scintillating wall of orange and red radiates heat, inscribed "Sing praises or be tested." Unla is about to sing old show tunes, but Thistle sings a Fey song of love instead. Orsik scowls at the "out of tune screeching," and the irritated ranger marches through and is burned; Orsik smirks. Beyond is a 20' room with a brass brazier in each corner and a circle of arcane symbols. The eastern wall is a wall of white and grey light smelling of incense and ozone and flashing with lightning, inscribed "Songs of our ally may be treated gently." Unla suggests "perhaps the singing is a metaphor?" Thistle dismisses this, guesses "water," sings Istishia's inscription, and is zapped. He swallows some berries.

Thistle calls on the watchers to look for useful information. Wallace finds an inscription and whispers it to Orsik, who shouts, "Look what I found!" Vardis works it out: "Gods of Fire and Air are allies as the gods of Earth and Water. The gods of Water and Fire are enemies, as are the gods of Earth and Air." Singing the fitting praises, Thistle gets through the walls unhurt, and the others follow, repeating the songs Thistle got right. After the remaining two rooms they reach a room covered in mold, where Unla Key finds a foot-long piece of ivory wood, the only thing not crumbled to dust: "a wand of webs! I knew it was magical!" Vardis declares that they will decide later which spell caster can use it, and Thistle agrees.

Thistle checks the next door for traps — "none." — and grumbles: "Probably thought invaders would be dead by now." Unla Key identifies "a laboratory," admires the stone tables as furniture made to resist explosions, and pockets a set of jewelers' tools still in good shape. But the prize is the spellbook. He reaches over the table, grabs all three books, feels glyphs being triggered, and thinks, "oops." The door closes and locks, and two magmin appear. Meanwhile Wallace finds gems and coins totaling 200 GP. Unla Key's last thought is about who Bad Fruul is, why the SEER thinks there may be books in an ancient tower to help them, and that "there is no way the SEER is getting this spell book."
