# Chapter 63 - The heist.

Date: 16 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
Orsik is tormented and at a crossroads. Once a disreputable hired hand known throughout the North, he had found inside himself the leader of the soldiers that defended Parnast, and then, after a ghost aged him, he had become his father. He is now free of his reputation, both externally and internally, but he is conflicted: Bob Saget is in Silverymoon, and the demon is planning to destroy the city. He weighs it aloud. "Should I abandon them to their fate?" The destruction of the city, he reasons, "would be quite the feather in my cap and probably get a notable promotion within the ranks of the Zhentarim." But he asks himself, "Do I want to have to hide my face wherever I go?" The fairy interrupts his reverie: "Okay, I think I found the location of the conch." Orsik listens, then hands over the planning: "Thistle, tell us what to do."

Thistle is shocked; ever since the dwarf got his intelligence boost he has been insufferable. But seeing Orsik distracted, he reluctantly lays out a plan over a map. The first problem is the sheer number of giants. Unla Key offers a fireball, then cloud kill, then domination, and each time Thistle shoots him down, ending with "There are at least 20 Fire Giants." and "They are giants." Unla Key is miffed: "What’s the point of knowing these spells if I can’t use them?" Since a frontal attack will fail, Thistle proposes a distraction. The others will ride the carts that circle the upper level, jump off in the tunnel leading to the pressing mill, sneak quietly into the elevator area and go down the elevator shaft. Thistle himself will break the wheel: "Wiri mentioned that whenever the wheel breaks, the giants rush in." He will magically reduce a key part, and with the giants distracted he will fly through the Vonindod chamber and meet them near the elevator on the lower floor. Orsik nods — but he has not been listening, too busy thinking about his reputation. "So I get into the cart and then down the elevator," he mumbles.

There is one more problem: a fire salamander where the tracks end, which must be killed quietly. Unla Key has no silence spell — just as, Thistle recalls, he had none at the last heist in Silverymoon. "Figures." Irritated but unbowed, Unla Key sends a shimmering lance of power from his head into the creature, stunning it with psychic pain. Thistle's arrows wound it, Unla Key's toll on the dead and Orsik's strike both fail to finish it, and Thistle's arrows end it. "No noise," mutters Unla Key, though he notices Vardis sprawled on the ground. Only then does he notice the dwarven prisoners, who plead, "Please let us go." The group decides the prisoners need not keep filling the carts, and loads salamander parts into the cart behind them.

As the party loads the carts and moves towards the pressing mill, Thistle flies down to the wheel room. A large fireplace stands on the north side; an enormous wheel with wooden spokes dominates the room, rigged to the bucket chain that travels through the forge. Ten shield dwarves in iron manacles, all exhausted, turn it, watched by two ogres clad head to toe in spiked armor. Thistle breaks the wheel at once. Riding the bucket chain, Orsik wonders whether Thistle realises he needs to wait — and the bucket chain stops. "No, he didn’t." Thistle is thrilled: the wheel is destroyed, the Fire Giants are converging on it, and he can hear Duke Zalto rushing up the stairs. Then his heart turns cold: "Oops."

Thistle flies to his stranded allies. "This was your plan?" mutters the very irritated dwarf, who, no longer distracted, remembers why he always comes up with the plans. The apologetic fairy explains, "Well, we had a lot of plans that we were toying with, and it was easy to get confused." Orsik takes the map Thistle drew of the lower level and lays out a new plan: jump from bucket to bucket to the edge of the room with the Vonindod, drop down, head to the elevator room, enter the dining hall from there, "And then hit the bedrooms." Seeing that Vardis is unhappy at the height, Orsik tells him, "I can cast feather fall."

Floating to the ground, Vardis glares at the fairy, who urges them through the elevator shaft into the dining hall. Asked whether the goblins will be there, Thistle answers, "Probably?" The group huddles over intimidating or bluffing their way past; Unla Key offers to cast dominate person, and Orsik growls, "Okay, so you dominated one of them. What about the rest?" In the end a disgruntled Unla Key simply marches through acting as if he is in charge, and the goblins ignore them. Vardis reflects, "They probably see a lot of strange people." Thistle holds the lever of the north door and urges Orsik to push; when the door opens, Thistle takes a bow, and an infuriated Orsik mutters, "Good for you! You did such a good job!" Thistle replies that he moved the door handle nine feet up in the air: "It’s not like we have anyone else who can do that."

Inside is the bedroom. Vardis takes one look and says, "No kink shaming." Thistle surveys an enormous bed framed from crisscrossing iron sword blades welded together, with large chains and manacles dangling above it; a 15-foot-diameter shield of adult black dragon scales on the north wall behind the bed; an iron chandelier whose chain is snagged on a hook above a 10-foot-tall basalt table bearing valuables, with a large iron chest beneath it; two wardrobes of fire-scorched wood; a giant barrel used as a stool before a burnished full-length mirror; and an iron brazier near the door. Before Thistle can steal from the table, Orsik points to the chest: "Open it now." Unla Key casts a knock and opens the chest, and a blast of incendiary fire erupts. When the fire clears, Thistle notices that the mage is dead and the cleric is flat on his back, having tripped trying to get out. "Typical." Thistle is pleased with himself for recommending that they close the door when they entered, and is sure Orsik will be impressed. He is not impressed.

The chest holds a giant-sized adamantine crown set with six black opals, thousands of gold pieces under the ashes, the conch, a javelin of lightning, a wand of the war mage, and adamantine-scale armor. Orsik picks up the magical ring and sighs, "Another ring of jumping." Vardis nudges him: "I probably need one, given how clumsy I am." Orsik wants to leave at once, but Thistle insists on loading the table's treasures into the bag of holding, struggling with a 750-pound hairbrush. "I want to leave. Dude, we just made 15,000 gold. Let’s get the fuck out of here." Thistle shouts, "There are 5000 more right here!" and Orsik answers, "I don’t want to fight 20 fire giants." When Unla Key asks why one more item would bring 20 Fire Giants, Orsik replies, "Because it’s going to take time!" Thistle calls for a vote, and the dwarven artificer shouts, "YOU TAKE A VOTE! I AM LEAVING!" and marches off. As Unla Key reaches for his rock of wonder, Orsik points at the door: "How are you going to open it without me?" Vardis declines the treasure — "Nah. Nothing piques my interest." — and the group trades half-joking ideas about coming back to kill all the giants, ramming them with the cloud castle, or unleashing "Bob Saget’s zombie army". They leave past the goblins setting the dinner tables, ride the elevator up to the top of Ironslag, and Orsik opens the massive iron portcullis barring the exit to the Yak Folk village. He makes the long rope climb into the castle, notes that the zombies are still there, and enters the control tower.

Esclarotta has been waiting anxiously for the 3HPP, making her husband's life miserable with complaints that she should not have to rely on little people for her revenge. When the dwarf enters, she demands, "Is she dead?" Orsik answers, "We have the conch," and she roars, "I sent you to kill the Duchess! IS SHE DEAD?" "Yes, she’s dead!" Esclarotta imagines a suitably gruesome death and is happy, happier still when Vardis sings, "Ding dong, the Duchess is dead." Then comes the shout: "Now let’s kill Sansuri! That horrible excrement of cumulus cloud dares to pretend she is favored among all other cloud giants!"

Orsik's thoughts return to Bob. He tells Vardis, "My son says we should ally with Bob against the city of Silverymoon." Vardis thinks, "His method acting is getting a little bit weird." Unla disagrees: "Let’s kill him." Thistle recalls the original plan, "That we take down Bob and frame house Margaster. To do that, we have to let some chaos reign." Unla Key surprises Orsik by backing it — "I want the riskiest plan." Orsik objects that "There are a lot of things that could go wrong with trying to pin the slaughter of innocent hundreds of people on a greater devil." The fallen cleric of Corellon sides with letting it happen: "Normally, I am with Orsik, but this time, I think we just let it be. Bad things happen." He doubts they would be blamed, and adds, "And I am not sure Silverymoon is long for this world." Thistle warns, "Bob is going to figure out how to pin the blame on us – unless we explicitly fight him." Orsik, thinking of his new reputation, says, "Taking him out would be the best for our reputation." Thistle observes, "We need allies." Orsik lists them — Lord Zymorven, the Hand of Yartar, Urgala from Triboar, soldiers from Everlund, and members of the watch of Silverymoon who are fans of the 3HPP — and Unla Key cannot resist adding Hsing. The real problem, Orsik says, is how to convince people that they did not bring a demon into the city. Thistle has a brilliant idea: go to the Lord of Silverymoon and declare that Bob Saget, their friend, is dead, killed by a demon, and that they have come to deal with it. Asked about truth-detecting spells, he answers, "We are emissaries of Everlund. They won’t use magic." Orsik decides: "We kill Bob." Vardis mutters, "Walking away is always the better option." And Orsik ponders whether there is a reason he had the reputation he had.
