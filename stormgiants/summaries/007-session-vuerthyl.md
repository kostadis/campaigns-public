# Chapter 07  - Vuerthyl

Date: 13 to 20 Ches 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The road to Parnast is not well-traveled. The Zhentarim once controlled it; the Cult of the Dragon took it over in the events leading up to the Battle at the Well of Dragons, then abandoned the route after Tiamat's entry into the North was blocked. The Zhentarim have tried to reassert control over the Black Road through the great desert Anauroch, but their hold remains tenuous. On the 3rd day of the 2nd tenday of Ches, the party was crossing the Dawn pass — the easiest route through the Graypeak Mountains on the Grayvale Run road — when they came upon a massive pair of black bears that saw them as a tasty snack.

Thistle did not entirely stumble on them: he spotted the bears in time to cast "Speak with Animals" and ask, "Hello! How goes it in bear land?" The answer came from both at once — "HUNGRY!" — while Thistle's own pet bear hugged him, saying, "Mommy!" The fairy thought it over, cast a goodberry spell, and dropped the berries in two separate piles. The bears swallowed five berries each, sat down patting their full bellies, and purred contentedly. Asked about dangers on the road east, they could only say that significant threats were coming from behind them; pressed for what, they kept saying danger. Thistle, knowing danger lay ahead and determined to find it, spent the next few hours scanning the horizon as the road wound down into hilly dry country. The weather turned from cold and moist to cold and dry, the vegetation thinned, and Thistle recognised the rain shadow of the Graypeak Mountains.

Out of the dusty hills, two scorpions raced toward Thistle and his companions — whom the chapter describes, in Thistle's undecided view, as friends, co-travelers, "some guys he picked up and can't shake off," or allies in his holy crusade to find the love of his life and get vengeance. Orsik offered, "we could run?" and was answered, "But then they would get the next travelers." Orsik shot back, "When did we start caring about the next travelers?" Thistle grunted and fired an arrow at the lead scorpion, which charged straight into Vardis; Vardis flanked it and landed a hard blow, Orsik brought it to near-death, and Unla Key killed it with a magic missile. The second scorpion missed Orsik with both claws and stinger, Vardis flanked and smashed it with his sword, and then the creature, surrounded by Unla Key, Orsik and Vardis, lashed out at all three. Orsik was grappled, and Vardis was struck, poisoned, and "almost dead forever" — saved only because all that time drinking booze with Orsik had taught his endocrine system to digest poisons more efficiently. Orsik screamed, "VARDIS!" Thistle flew a little higher to stay out of the stinger's range. With Vardis down, the scorpion grabbed the grappled Orsik and fled toward the hills, but Orsik smashed its skull and exulted in his vengeance. Someone proposed, "Let's track them to their nest!" and was met with "Really?" The trail led toward the desert rather than Parnast, and Thistle settled it: "another day."

On the 6th day of the 2nd tenday of Ches the party reached Parnast, the last settlement before the Black Road, once a source of power for the Zhentarim and later the Cult, now abandoned. A few dozen houses surround a small square with a tavern, a stable and a shrine, and a small group of locals stared at the adventurers distrustfully. Vardis mumbled, "Shrine of Axes!" and Orsik, noting the absence of guards, smiled at the tavern's name: "The Golden Tankard!" The sealed doors of what looked like stables drew everyone's attention. Orsik knocked, and a dangerous roar answered. Vardis dismissed it — "you knocked over something" — but Thistle shouted, "It's a wyvern!"

An elderly elven woman came from the temple and asked if they planned to deal with it. When the group shrugged, she asked them not to go in: after the Cult of the Dragon were defeated, they had left this Wyvern in the stables. Thistle perked up at the chance of vengeance against the Cult, but she corrected him — "Oh, no, no. We're looking for you to stop knocking on the door." The last person to go in had been injured and taken days to die, begging for help and healing while the village listened. The Wyvern is chained to the ground, but she laughed at the idea that the chain held it; the only reason it has not broken free is that the villagers keep feeding it. She opened a slit into the stables, and each of them looked in on a wide-open space of carcasses picked clean. When Thistle suggested killing it from afar, she was aghast — it might escape and endanger the whole village. Realising the group was determined, she switched approach: "We like the Wyvern. And it simplifies prison sentences. The threat of the Wyvern is good enough to keep the peace here." The group decided this was a monster for another day.

The elf then offered work: "A merchant Azam is stuck in Vuerthyl, bringing back a religious artifact that we sent into the desert during the war. The middle person, Fiora, said she would find us some mercenaries." Thistle, sure she was holding back, demanded to know about the cult and about Adimond Kroskas; each time she answered that she wanted her idol, and that "what information I may have is only to be exchanged when I have my idol." Thistle backed off, grumbling, "she isn't going to talk until we get her idol." Orsik took in the town — an empty shrine, a wheelwright shop, the stables and the tavern — and got the distinct impression that Parnast is used to a faction controlling it while the locals profit, neutral to all conquerors as long as they can trade. The elf returned once more, sighing, to tell them "the Cult of the Dragon brought a big flying castle over here to move a lot of treasure." Orsik connected it: "that's the castle that crash landed near Waterdeep that convinced everyone that there was some deeper, more sinister plot and led to the alliance to defeat the Cult at the Well of Dragons."

"I need a drink," Orsik said, and marched to The Golden Tankard. The tankard over the door was yellow and the interior gloomy — "false advertising," he thought — and all conversation stopped as he entered. A tall, heavyset man with enormous mutton chop sideburns stepped forward: "What can I get you, travelers?" When told they were fetching an idol for the local cleric, he answered, "Chandra Stol? She's been looking for fools to get her idol for weeks. Azam won't come because of dangers." Then: "I have already spoken to you for two minutes, and you haven't bought anything." Ale was a copper, mead a silver piece. Vardis mused to Orsik, "we could buy the place. A nice turnkey operation?" With a fuller till the barkeep explained that business had been slow, with few folk coming from the east and "many more going east." Shown the treasure map, he claimed to know the place, then guessed at the silver marches, the spine of the world, the Ice Spires (Orsik's contribution) and the Nether Mountains, until the answer came down to "somewhere in the North." Orsik groaned and paid a gold piece for the information, and the keeper introduced himself with a promise of his finest sausage and cabbage: "never let it be said that Ragnar Redtooth isn't grateful to those who do him a kindness!" "Kindness when paid," snarked Vardis; "I'm good with that. I'm transactional," said Orsik. The goodwill ended when Ragnar charged them 5 gold for a dingy, smelly room and another five for the sheets. To "You're treating us like marks!" he replied, "When a poor man sees a fool throwing coins around, he's a fool if he doesn't try to get some more."

The next day the party set out on the Black Road for Vuerthyl. The journey east was peculiarly safe and eerily quiet, the desert hot and miserable beneath massive rolling dunes and an even bigger sky. On the 10th day of the 2nd tenday of Ches they reached the oasis of Vuerthyl, ringed by a motley collection of sun-bleached tents. Orsik found the tent that passed for a tavern; a pair of tieflings, unaffected by the heat, approached him warily, and inside a stringed instrument played over people hunched over food and conversation. A Dragonborn waiter guided Orsik to a private table, said of Azam that "he will be here shortly," and brought water, dates, olive and bread.

Azam arrived: a lean half-elven man with a long, craggy face, dark skin and hair showing Rashemi ancestry. Vardis noticed his impressive mustache; Unla Key noticed the golden-scaled pseudodragon draped around his shoulders, which stirred, blinked with milky irises, sniffed the air, nodded to the man, and went back to sleep. Gesturing to it, Azam said, "Hsing, my patron is most pleased you have come," named himself, and asked them to deliver a statue to Parnast. The statue for the Shrine of Axes had been made in the Anauroch desert by artisans there: "It's not small, and it's not light. I don't know if it's magical, but I don't really care." With Hsing's regal nod of assent, Azam showed them a worn wagon where a stout-looking half-orc woman was wrapping the statue in canvas. It was a complicated piece of woodwork by an artisan who had exiled himself to the desert — "It's five feet tall, four feet wide, and very heavy."

Vardis recognised it as the Triune Queen, the threefold deity Angharradh, and read the inscription on its base, which urges vigilance against She Who Was Banished and defence of the lands of the Fair Folk. "This is the wisdom of Angharradh," he said, feeling a calming presence. Thistle presumed "fair folk" meant the fey; the answer came back "Elves." When Vardis offered that the elves are part fey, the reply was "The better part." — and everyone stared at Thistle, who felt he had said something awkward. "Fine."

Azam told them Hsing would have more work if they completed the mission. To Thistle's joke that dead men get no more missions, Azam answered, "No, if you don't bring the statue. Although Hsing has been known to forgive." Hsing opened his eyes: "when there is a seed, it must be let to grow before it is killed." Finally Thistle asked whether they had seen a Drake named Thuul. Azam was taken aback, but Hsing smiled: "where there is the yellow sky, there is the drake you are looking for."
