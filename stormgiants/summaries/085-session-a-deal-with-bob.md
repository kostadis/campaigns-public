# Chapter 85 - A deal with bob

Date: about 8 Uktar 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
With the deal with Iymrith concluded, the 3HPP stand alone in the empty desert. Orsik pulls out the conch, and the party is again in the Maelstrom. In the throne room, Thistle notes who is present: Imperator Serissa, King Hekaton, and the hill giant guards Tug and Cog.

The King looks at the nearly dead heroes who left just a few days ago and refused his request for help, and he thinks they have been coming and going too freely into his castle. Thistle flies up and tries to deliver his news — "The lair of the dragon and they…" — but Hekaton cuts him off: "You have returned." When Thistle shouts that they found the lair, Hekaton's voice booms over the fairy's words, and the King insists on speaking first: "I am the King. And I get to speak first. I am pleased to see you here." Orsik mutters, barely audibly, "More pleased than me." Hekaton stares at Orsik and tells him, "I know you crave the heroic death at the hand of a dragon, but I am glad to see you alive!"

Thistle grows frustrated at the King's dawdling, and notices out of the corner of his eye that Serissa is annoyed. Vardis notices the same thing and tells Orsik, "She’s pissed." Orsik offers his reading of the Giantess — "It’s like she’s happy to have him here but disappointed, like some kind of daddy issues?" — and when Vardis asks, "When did you become so aware of relationships?", Orsik taps the band on his head, and Vardis nods. The King has not noticed his daughter's discomfort.

When Hekaton finally asks, "Thistle, why have you come here?", the fairy speaks as fast and loudly as he can: "Traitors among giants! Giants are working with dragons! WE KNOW WHERE AND WHEN THEY WILL BE!" Hekaton sighs that his hands are tied, and Orsik snaps, "You’re the king. Untie them!"

That is the spark. Imperator Serissa stands and unloads all her pent-up frustrations from last year: at the King for declaring that her two sisters weren't qualified to be Queen, which encouraged them to find other ways to the crown; at Hekaton for disappearing on a rage-filled quest instead of staying at his post when the Ordning was shattered; at everyone who didn't listen to her that her father was alive; and most of all that her father is not the Giant she remembers. She shouts, "You abandoned me to get revenge! You abandoned your post and left me in a nest of vipers!" The 3HPP stare in shock — and then all reach for a snack, sit back, and watch. Hekaton declares, "I am King, and I make the decisions!", and Serissa answers with all the venom she can muster: "I was the king when you disappeared, and I could have left you to rot."

Thistle coughs, and both giants glare at him — though he was only choking on a goodberry. "My girlfriend," he gasps, and Serissa seizes on it: "EXACTLY! HIS GIRLFRIEND WAS LESS IMPORTANT THAN SAVING THE WORLD!" Thistle coughs again and offers, "I think we can find a win-win here." In that moment Thistle realizes that Hekaton is almost a quasi-deity, his size, power, and strength beyond measure — and that the storm giant is angry not at Thistle or Serissa but at his own bad choices. Seeing a fairy he could squish without effort trying to convince him to do the right thing, the Giant stops and asks, "What win-win?"

Thistle explains: "Your traitors are gathering in one location. It’s a perfect opportunity." Hekaton shouts, "Why didn’t you begin that way?", and Orsik answers laconically that Thistle tried, but the King interrupted him and then argued with his daughter — which sets father and daughter screaming again. When both are spent, Hekaton huffs on his throne. Thistle tells Serissa that Iymrith will be there, and she shouts, "I will take the palace guard and defend our honor, even if our king will sit here like a coward." Another round of screaming follows.

Orsik tries to soothe their hurt feelings. Hekaton feels guilty for his quest to get vengeance for his wife, which led to so much chaos in the world; Serissa feels betrayed by her father. But nothing seems to affect the King — his rage, his guilt, and his pain are too much. Looking for a way out without losing face, he remembers that Unla Key is a diviner. Unla Key tries to stand, then decides the better approach is to lie down, and nods. Hekaton asks him to divine the better plan: "To strike at Iymrith’s lair when all of my enemies are present, or my plan to hire agents of chaos and kill them one by one?"

Orsik is shocked that Unla Key can figure out the right path, which sets off an exchange about past guidance — "You steered us wrong every time." / "No, I steered you to the riskiest path. And that is always the right thing." Unla Key weighs it: the safe play is to do what the king wants; the risky option is to take all the troops from the castle and attack the king's enemies in Iymrith's lair. He doesn't hesitate: "Take everyone and attack Iymrith." Hekaton agrees, and Orsik sets the terms: "We have some more preparations. In 5 days, we will meet at Iymrith’s lair. For now, we have some business to attend to."

Through the King's teleportation chamber, the 3HPP appear in Ascore. Their plan is to summon Bob and pay him to fight against Iymrith, and, as a bonus, to secure the release of the 200 citizens of Silverymoon trapped in a pocket dimension. Vardis casts Planar Ally; a portal opens, and Bob appears. His demonic, raspy voice is softer, his arrogance a little bit gone, and he seems afraid.

"Do you fools know what you have done?" Bob demands. Not the giant civil war, it turns out: "I was referring to the artificer’s decision to use ISDs. This violated the 1489 Treaty on Export Regulation of Interplanar Storage Devices." He explains that bags of holding and portable holes are classified as ISDs, that the Treaty was signed in Sigil between the representatives of the Trading House, Chromax, and the Githyanki, Githzerai, and Mind Flayers, and that an ISD placed in another ISD instantly teleports any entity nearby into the Astral Plane — typically a massively powerful entity, an MPE, that the three empires would want for their armies. Any empire can lay claim to a planet found in violation, and "The three empires have just discovered that Toril violates the treaty. They are planning to come here."

Unla Key needles the demon for being worried. Bob denies it, complains that he got snookered by the oldest trick in the book because he didn't check whether Toril was a signatory, and laments, "Your planet is doomed. And I am stuck on it, thanks to your stupid spell." Unla Key only smiles: he has always known about the infinity of planets and about the end of the world, and nobody ever asked him if it was ‘this’ world. He tells Bob, "All of your demonic powers haven’t given you the power of divination to know what I know." When Bob grumbles that being trapped in the Astral Plane for eons is boring, Unla Key tells him, "It was only an hour." Bob believes him: "It was the longest, most boring hour of my existence."

Orsik asks whether Bob sent a few hundred people to the Astral Plane; Bob says it was a pocket dimension. When Unla Key asks, "Didn’t we try to kill him?", Orsik patronizingly calls it a misunderstanding — they had to end the zombie army but didn't want to hurt Bob. Bob puffs, "I am a powerful monstrous demon." "Of jokes," says the dwarf. "That was a portfolio change, not a demotion," Bob insists, explaining that he would, for all eternity, crack bad jokes at souls that needed punishing — and if that didn't work, resort to more traditional punishments.

Orsik shifts the conversation: "There’s going to be a big battle coming up with a bunch of dragons and giants. And we need your help to slaughter them." Vardis suggests Bob could enslave them, and Unla Key and Vardis goad the demon about weak zombies and finding another demon until he roars, "ARE YOU QUESTIONING BOB SAGET!!!!" Trapped, Bob says, "I need something of value." The 3HPP realize they hadn't planned what to offer; the cloud castle and the Dracolich are both things they don't have. Then Unla Key proposes two zombie blue chromatic dragons hybridized with purple worms, and Bob is suddenly excited: "You have two of Iymrith’s creations?"

Learning of Iymrith's grand reveal in front of her patrons, and that Vardis wants to give her the biggest public humiliation, Bob squeals: "You want me to perform at that event?" He wants speaking lines; Unla Key says it "Depends on how you do in the audition," and Vardis tells him, "Bring that energy." Bob takes offense at being made to audition, and Orsik turns to the others: "I’m with Bob."

The 3HPP realize they have a new plan: they will polymorph Bob into a dragon. Except Unla Key doesn't have the polymorph spell, which means they have to go back to Silverymoon. Bob, meanwhile, delights in the idea of unleashing hell and chaos before a who's who of bad guys who will applaud him as one of Iymrith's creations: "And that would disgrace Iymrith forever? When they discover her fraud? THIS IS PERFECT!" He agrees — insisting it's to prove he's not a joke of a demon, since ever since his new portfolio the rest of the powers of the Abyss have thought him a bit of a joke.

Orsik asks again about the prisoners. Bob can free them for "20,000 gold." "Done," comes the answer, leaving Bob flustered: "That was supposed to be a lot of money." The demon roars a threat — "If I don’t get to put on this performance for the Eons, I will hunt you down and burn you." — and asks Orsik, "You signed one of those DNRRRs?" Orsik thinks the demon has a plan but is unsure what it is.

Bob asks after the promised dragons, learns they are right there and still living, and when Unla Key asks if he is not strong enough, Bob roars, "ARE YOU QUESTIONING MY POWER?" and summons several more demons: "They will be fighting for some time." Asked whether he will summon other demons for the big battle, Bob says, "No, this is my show. I share my stage light with no one." — though if in danger, "I would use all the powers of the abyss to win."

As Bob is about to free the prisoners, Orsik asks where they will appear — "In Silverymoon at the door of the theater." — and, seeing an opportunity to be a hero, asks Bob to time it so they appear in Silverymoon tomorrow at 9 am. Bob smiles.

The next day, the conch sends everyone to the Maelstrom, and the teleportation chamber takes the 3HPP to just outside Silverymoon. In the city they hear rumors about themselves. The disappearance of notables in the fire has caused chaos; the ruse of using the dead zombie bodies worked at first, but it was quickly discovered they weren't the nobles. Lord Methrammar Aerasumé, son of Lady Alustriel Silverhand, is under attack because he was the patron of the play, and everyone's interpretation of the play is different, as if each saw a different play. The 3HPP's arrival in Yartar in a giant cloud castle in the middle of a giant civil war has split Silverymoon between those who blame Lord Aerasumé for trusting the 3HPP and those who blame the 3HPP for everything. Market rumors tell of fire giants hunting adamantine with massive divining rods, Stone giants destroying settlements near Delimbiyr Road, Frost Giants attacking ships on the Coastal Cities and perhaps destroying Bryn Shander, and increased raiding by the Uthgardt against caravans heading to Silverymoon — raiding some say would be less exposed if the current leadership had not abandoned the alliance of the Silver Marches.
