# Chapter 67 - Into the Maelstrom

Date: 26 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens where the fight against the giant lords in the Maelstrom left off, seen through Vardis's eyes. Thuul has just breathed destruction, and Vardis stares with his mouth ajar, muttering, "The fairy's creature can now breathe out death and destruction." He wants the drake to do it again, but the unconscious Thistle needs to be alive for Thuul to breathe again. Down the hallway Vardis's wall of fire still burns, and beyond it his friend Orsik is fighting alone with two giants. The cleric of Corellon worries about the dwarf: "Ever since Orsik became smarter, he hasn't liked his chosen career," he thinks, and wonders whether Orsik has a death wish.

The roar of a giant beyond the wall of fire pulls Vardis back to the moment. He considers flame strike, then remembers the endless debates at the temples in Waterdeep — before his heretical views on the responsibilities of the Clerics of Corellon made him persona non-grata — about how flame strike used to be more potent before Ao weakened that magic's power. It was why he had followed the light aspect of Corellon to use Fireballs. A fireball incinerates the Frost Giant and injures the Cloud Giant. Vardis cowers inside the doorway to the guestroom, then hears the crunch of Count Vaal's morning star on Orsik and wonders whether the dwarf is still alive. He is: Orsik taunts his opponent, "That hurt, but you did swing that morning star with grace and style. So I will give you that."

Vardis points to the downed fairy and urges the guard drake to help Thistle; the creature mumbles and agrees. Out in the great hall Orsik shouts, "This monster is still alive, and we must hurt it a lot." Unla Key cries, "Take that," a shimmering glow surrounds his head, and the cloud giant screams in agony and floats in the air, wobbling. Two hand axes impale themselves on the giant. Vardis thinks, "Good thing I chose to follow the light, eh Corellon?" and sends lances of pure flame toward the Count, jetting like spokes of a wheel from his hands. Thistle moans as the Healing Word's effect wakes the ranger, and one of the half pints unleashes magical arrows on Count Vaal, who barely notices, still reeling from the psychic lance. Vardis dreads Unla Key's turn — "A simple magic missile, and we're done" — expecting the wizard not to do the easy thing. But this time the self-centered wizard finishes the fight with a few blasts of power. Vardis blinks the wall of fire out of existence and finds a bloodied Orsik still standing among the corpses of the giant lords. "Is he happy or sad? Hard to tell."

Orsik's own view is grim: "Still alive," said with satisfaction at living and bitter regret that he is still a fighter. He turns to the plunder. On Vaal he finds a diamond-studded platinum earring, a vest of platinum embroidery, a teleportation conch and a potion of water breathing. On the Fire Giant Duchess Tartha he finds a necklace of gilded Dragon's teeth and another conch; on the Frost Giant Jarl Hellenhild a cloak of white dragon scales and another conch; and from the Stone Giant Thane Braxow he rips out 15 gemstones embedded in his flesh. "12,000 gold pieces and some giant toenails," he tells his friends. When Vardis shouts, "We need to move them from here," Orsik nearly gets angry, then remembers that being the smartest person in the room is about recognizing the right idea from other people and amplifying it. He suggests "The storeroom?" and drags the bodies north past the guestrooms for Giants into the storeroom.

Just in time: Mirran descends the staircase to see why no giant lords are coming up to celebrate. From hiding, Orsik hears her step into the Portal Chamber and mutter, "They are all gone. Well, I suppose they killed those little people and decided to go back to their homelands and spread rumors about Serissa." He can barely overhear Nym screaming something about loose ends at her sister. The 3HPP take a long rest in the guest chambers.

A few hours later Orsik lays out the situation: "Serissa is the current Queen. The current Queen is being undermined by her two sisters, Mirran and Nym." Serissa believes her father is still alive, and her mother was interested in an accommodation with the little people. Under Vardis's stare he admits his plan had been to pose as a suitor Serissa had found through a matchmaker in Waterdeep. Then he proposes, "I recommend a full front assault." Vardis is sad and concerned, Thistle just says no, and Unla Key is already running for the stairs before he pauses and asks "Why?" The answer: "Because we don't all have a death wish, buddy."

With the frontal assault discarded, Thistle casts pass without trace and sneaks alone upstairs to the Great Hall. Unlike the day before, there is no adoring crowd of Giant Lords, just a pair of bored guards. While he wonders how to get past the two hill giants guarding the throne room, Nym slams her fist into the living organ, shouts, "I must go speak to the Queen," and heads for the door. "An opportunity!" Thistle flies close to the ceiling, too small to be noticed, past the guards into Maelstrom's throne room: an amphitheater with stone bleachers overlooking a 50-foot-deep seawater pool, facing three enormous thrones. The middle throne, carved from a solid chunk of obsidian with four blue dragon skulls on its base, floats 1 foot above the floor. "What did Joon say about that throne? Damn it, I can't remember."

On the middle throne sits Queen Serissa, over 20 feet tall, silver-blue hair, eyes the color of a stormy sea, wearing a crown of enchanted silver and a necklace of pearls and sapphires; Thistle realizes there is a reason why she and not her sisters is Queen. Beside her sits Imperator Uthor, a middle-aged Storm Giant with steel-gray hair and a trident crackling with latent electricity, who gazes at the young Queen with perpetual condescension. And on the left, startling Thistle, is Adimond Kroskas. Thistle is tempted to strike, but judges it futile and the wrong time and place.

He flies closer to overhear. Nym mocks Serissa for giving a dwarf a conch for a date — "Was it "Ok Ogre?" or was it "The short for the Tall?"" Serissa snarls, "They are liars," then soothingly, "We agreed. We felt they were assassins and had them killed." Adimond asks, "Can you describe them?" and freezes when he realizes this is the 3HPP, then asks, "Did you find the bodies?" Nym answers, "The Frost and Fire giants eat little people," adding, "I am sure they are well digested." The Imperator, barely hiding his irritation, asks Mirran and Nym to confirm these events. Adimond then tells Serissa that "the experiment to create a new dragon-fairy hybrid is proceeding," promising a force loyal to her that will guarantee the Storm Giant's position in the Ordning. "I like it when the bad guys tell us their plans," Thistle mutters.

Thistle reasons that the blue dragon has not survived this long without an exit strategy, and remembers what Joon said: the Maelstrom has a series of interconnected tunnels, so Adimond's way out is the pool of water in front of them. Serissa dismisses the experiments — "But if we found my father, we wouldn't need them" — and asks for someone she can trust to look for him; the Imperator sighs, "We have sent so many, your highness." Thistle sees that the Imperator likes a Queen he can manipulate, and that Adimond and the two sisters are allies, the blue dragon directing them when to speak and act. Mirran pouts and stamps her foot, insisting the visitors were assassins and that the sisters should be Queen. Serissa screams and tries to rise, but the Imperator grabs her arm. Adimond suggests a change in leadership might be appropriate if the Queen cannot restrain her temper; the Imperator snaps, "Do not confuse your privilege with the right to speak about these matters," and Adimond demurs, "I am just an alchemist doing the Queen's bidding."

The last section turns to Unla Key. After Thistle reports that the great enemy is in the throne room, Orsik again wants to charge, while Vardis and Thistle push back. The group agrees to sneak into the throne room through the waterways that link the Maelstrom. From a pool in the portal chamber they reach an underwater portcullis built for much larger creatures, and the half-pint squeezes through, barely avoiding the barnacles. Beyond it lies the half-mile-wide whirlpool that gave the palace its name, which Unla Key regards with grandiose glee, picturing its 100-foot-deep, 180-foot-wide sinkhole and the iron grate over its 50-foot-diameter drain as "perfect for any of my numerous plans." His reverie breaks when Orsik swims to a tunnel on the floor: the treasure is no longer with the ships but was stolen by some crabs, crabs Orsik intends to kill. "I can't have him have all of the fun…"

## Locations
### Maelstrom
The storm giants' palace, named for the vortex beside it. Its throne room is an amphitheater with stone bleachers, a 50-foot-deep seawater pool and three enormous thrones. Per Joon, as Thistle recalls, the Maelstrom has a series of interconnected tunnels.

### The Great Hall
Where Mirran and Nym play their living organ. The day before it held an adoring crowd of Giant Lords; now only a pair of bored guards, with two hill giants guarding the entrance to the throne room.

### The guestrooms and the storeroom
The battle with the giant lords is fought in the guest wing; Orsik drags the bodies north past the guestrooms for Giants into the storeroom, and the party long-rests in the guest chambers.

### The Portal Chamber
Mirran steps in and finds the giant lords gone. Its pool leads to an underwater portcullis, the party's route into the waterways.

### The vortex
A half-mile-wide whirlpool over a 100-foot-deep, 180-foot-wide sinkhole, with a 50-foot-diameter drain covered by an iron grate with 10-foot-wide gaps.

### Waterdeep
Where Vardis once debated at the Temples, and where Orsik's cover-story matchmaker supposedly worked.
