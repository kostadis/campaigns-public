# Chapter 89 The Fall of Iymrith

Date: about 13 Uktar 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-15; recorded 2025-03-19)

## Summary
The battle against the ancient blue dragon Iymrith reached its fever pitch as the cunning wyrm attempted one final act of manipulation. She turned to Orsik and offered him a false truce, urging him to abandon the fight and throw himself against the other dragons instead: “I'm not going to kill you. In fact, I'm not going to fight you. If you want to die from a dragon, there's three over there. Go kill them.” Orsik rejected the offer. Serissa advanced and struck Iymrith with a boulder for 28 damage before the dragon became invisible and flew toward the flames. The Djinn’s attempted opportunity attack and a Storm Giant’s thrown rock both missed her.

Iymrith did not stay hidden for long. An augmented tail swipe against Serissa revealed her again and dealt 49 damage. She threatened Hekaton directly: “Your daughter will die.” A second, critical strike dealt another 75 damage to the princess, reinforcing the dragon’s demand that Hekaton stand down. Orsik remained confident that Iymrith would not survive another round, but his own four attacks inflicted only limited damage. Hekaton, shaken by the threat to his daughter, agreed to pause for a round and told his giants to do the same. He remained on the battlefield, considering his options.

It was the mages of Silverymoon who ended it. The party established that the five ninth-level spellcasters still had their fifth-level slots available, having previously used third-level Fireballs. They chose five fifth-level Magic Missiles: seven bolts per mage, thirty-five guaranteed hits altogether. After considerable arithmetic, the table settled on 124 damage, enough to defeat Iymrith. Rather than leaving a corpse, the dragon vanished into a puff of smoke. There would be no scales to harvest.

Orsik immediately moved away from the most dangerous part of the battlefield and claimed the victory before the assembled factions. He offered the dragons the wealth while reserving the glory for himself: “You get all the money. I get all the glory.” An Arcana check of 19 established that Iymrith had made an infernal pact providing for this contingency. Her patron remained unknown, and she was no longer on the material plane. The GM explained that her plans there were disrupted for at least part of a human lifespan. The mages, satisfied that Silverymoon’s honor had been preserved, withdrew.

Iymrith’s disappearance did not end the fighting. The Storm Giants moved against the Fire Giants and Frost Giants, while the red, black, and green ancient dragons continued fighting over the treasure. The party learned that helping Hekaton defeat the rival giant leaders could establish his rule and bring peace to the realms. The Cloud Giants intended to wait for a winner, supporting Hekaton if he prevailed without becoming too badly wounded. Orsik complained about Hekaton’s brief withdrawal but conceded that a hero could forgive it. The GM also reminded the party that Serissa had previously argued for supporting them when her father wanted to sell them out.

Vardis cast a sixth-level Fireball into Iymrith’s four remaining clerics, killing three priests and dealing 38 damage to the Archpriest. His Djinn followed with scimitar attacks, and Thistle and Thuul joined the assault. An attempt to recruit the Archpriest failed. He retaliated with Flamestrike, dealing 49 damage to Vardis, who maintained concentration on the Djinn. The Archpriest declared, “My God is more powerful than yours. Iymrith shall rise again.” Further Djinn attacks and Thuul’s two-point acid reaction left him barely alive; Thuul later finished him with a bite dealing acid and piercing damage. Vardis also restored 80 hit points to Serissa, earning Hekaton’s gratitude.

The giant war continued around them. Unla Key’s Chain Lightning struck Duke Zalto and three Fire Giants, with only the Duke saving. Hekaton suffered repeated boulder hits while Serissa and the Storm Giants concentrated lightning and thrown rocks on Zalto. The Duke sought partial cover and used a legendary action to call two warriors to his side, but a Storm Giant’s rock killed him. Hekaton demanded surrender, and one Fire Giant laid down his arms and fled; the others continued fighting.

Orsik cast Sleet Storm to slow the advancing Frost Giants. Their immunity to cold damage offered no automatic protection against the spell’s slippery ground, although the Jarl dashed around its perimeter. Thistle shot the Jarl for 32 damage using Favored Foe, and a Storm Giant struck him with lightning for another 57. Hekaton, lacking both boulders and a recharged lightning attack, advanced and exchanged insults with the Frost Giant leader.

Two Fire Giants then struck Unla Key with boulders for 28 damage apiece, leaving him at 14 hit points. After considering another Chain Lightning and several other spells, he used Polymorph to become a Storm Giant, gaining a form with 230 hit points while recording his original 14 separately. The battlefield fell momentarily silent as the newly towering Unla Key squared off against the enemies who had nearly killed him moments before. He prepared to advance against the Fire Giants as the session ended.

Meanwhile, the dragons’ ongoing battle was resolved in broad exchanges of damage while the party left them alone. The Black Dragon ultimately conceded to the Red Dragon and withdrew, abandoning the hoard. The Red Dragon remained the strongest claimant and wanted the entire treasure. No division of the wealth was agreed, and the giant battle remained unresolved.
