# Chapter 78 - On the Grand Dame

Date: 28 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The next evening, aboard their castle, Esclarotta calls out "We are approaching Yartar." The 3HPP have followed their trail to Yartar and to a gambling boat called the Grand Dame. Imber, a merchant on the docks, had told them that the ship belonged to Lord Khaspere Drylund, who was manipulating votes to become the next Waterbaron. How a minor power's petty politician connects to the disappearance of the Storm Giant King is still unclear. Orsik is certain there is more to it: "There is more to this boat than we think. Someone on the boat must know who could have been involved in a scheme to disappear a storm giant king."

Orsik recalls how Waterbarons are chosen. The post is a lifetime appointment, and an election is held only when the sitting Waterbaron dies, so the incumbent works to keep his enemies divided and his friends close. The result is a peculiar and lethal kind of politics.

The party plans its approach. Orsik names Unla Key as the gambling den expert. Someone asks how much money they have, the answer is "A lot.", and the proposal that follows is "We gamble it all." Vardis glares, and Orsik clarifies that "on point" means offering ideas rather than making all the decisions, adding that "Our biggest problem is getting an invite onto the boat." Esclarotta asks where to land the castle. Thistle suggests sinking the boat and stealing the gold; Orsik answers, "We need information, and dead people can’t give us information." Orsik proposes offering the only known bottle of Champagne du le Stomp, and Thistle, a master of court etiquette in the realm of the fey, lays out the full plan: a cloud giant castle, the bottle, and an announcement that they are there to gamble. "There is nothing like arriving at a Casino in fancy clothes and an expensive transport that screams – take my money." Then they look at their clothes and realise they have nothing fancy to wear.

Vardis, who had to learn to make his own vestments after he was kicked out of the Cult of Corellon, uses the fabric of the Cloud Giantess to sew epically showy outfits for the 3HPP. Orsik's attempts go less well, and when Esclarotta tsks at them he retorts, "I don’t have the practice and skill to create what is in my head." Dressed, the group stands on the tower walls and watches the castle set down near the docks of Yartar.

Thistle flies down first and is met by a series of arrows. The docks are abandoned; the city walls a few hundred feet away are manned by well-armed, apprehensive defenders, confused that a fairy has appeared. Unla Key lands a few seconds later and shouts, "WE HAVE 1,000,000 GOLD, AND IT’S BURNING A HOLE IN MY POCKET! I just ransacked a dragon hoard and stole a giant cloud castle. I WANT TO GAMBLE!!!" He shows off two bags of holding. Before the captain of the guard can shout, a voice from the gates intervenes: "Captain, if they wanted us harm, they would have harmed. Let me speak to them." Lord Khaspere Drylund and his retinue appear.

Orsik presents the bottle and asks whether it will get them onto the Grand Dame. Drylund, confused, promises that the food and alcohol will be comped and hands the bottle to a courtier. Orsik is disappointed that the lord does not recognise how rare it is, but resists the urge to lecture him. Once it is clear that the castle does not herald an invasion, the guard reopens the gates to the docks, and merchants and onlookers flood in. The 3HPP are gently whisked to the Grand Dame, whose owner wants to make sure he gets most of the money these adventurers want to gamble. At the gangway they are asked to give up their weapons, which causes a little drama, but Orsik remembers that he has other options now.

The main cabin has been turned into a gambling hall. A gold-plated wooden goose sculpture stands at the aft with free glasses of fine wine around it, and an unflattering portrait of Lord Drylund hangs across from a spiral staircase leading up to the dance hall. Bets are placed with coin-sized wooden chips called golden geese, each stamped with Lord Drylund's golden goose emblem. Pow Ming, a Shou human who says he is "in charge of everything so Lord Drylund can do his job," asks how many chips they want. When Orsik asks what the high-rollers table requires, Pow Ming says, "You want the Sava tables." Thistle explains that Sava is a drow variant of chess, "a game of skill, deception, and insight," and muses that in a city with this many lethal conspiracies it is no surprise the rich and powerful play it.

Thistle spots a woman in a purple dress with short-cropped black hair and a necklace of black stones. Her white-haired opponent, Lord Hubbard, concedes with a stack of golden geese and tells her that she seemed to know his every move before he knew it himself. Unla Key sits down to face Lady Atalia. She offers to play for 5000, calling herself "a poor lady playing these games of skill, chance, and treachery." Orsik sees that she is playing them; Unla Key believes her. As the stakes climb, Orsik notices that Lady Atalia is probing his mind with telepathy. He pulls Thistle and Vardis out of what he hopes is her range. Vardis notes that they have no magic to counter her, and Thistle suggests she may have information worth trading. Vardis sets a limit: "But not in a dark alley. We do not threaten women in dark alleys." Vardis proposes losing the game and persuading her to share information.

Back at the table, with some 10,000 gold in golden geese at stake, Orsik knows that warning Unla Key about the cheating would only make the diviner more sure of winning. Asking to take his seat fails, and so does sending him for a drink, so Orsik pushes him out of the chair: "I am playing first because I’m smart. I’ve got the headband. I’m only going to come in for 1000. I can win this game because I am smart!" Lady Atalia seems to be losing, but just as Orsik moves to finish her she counters and wins. Orsik accuses her quietly: "Oh, my fair lady, it’s not a fair game when you read my mind." She denies it in an equally dangerous voice. Guessing that she wants no whiff of scandal, Orsik suggests a private conversation, and she answers straight into his mind: "We need to talk. Not here. Follow me when the boat returns to the docks. Nod if you understand me." Orsik leaves the table and stops Unla Key from taking the empty seat.

Later, Pow overhears Vardis saying he wants to meet Lord Drylund, and the party is invited to a private drink in Drylund's office. The office is nouveau riche and gaudy, with an aquarium on a table holding a coral reef and Drylund's pet octopus. Drylund picks up "the last remaining bottle of Champagne du le Stomp outside of Ravenloft" and offers to open it. Orsik, turning pale, steers him toward a lesser vintage, and Drylund decides to save it for when he becomes the Waterbaron. Drylund calls the party's arrival "a godsend and a positive endorsement for my candidacy" but admits that the timing of the election is unclear. When Orsik observes that "People die every day," Drylund mentions a rash of disappearing nobles and says a group of adventurers freed one and that the freed noble magically reappeared next to the father: "Lord Zymorven’s son!" He calls this a terrible blow for the Waterbaron.

Orsik asks who Drylund's rivals are and offers to kill them all. Drylund splutters and refuses, saying too many guilds and assassins would come looking, and he lists the three ways elections are won: convincing people, bribing them with promises, and "holding a loved one’s ransom." Orsik pitches an alliance, saying it would be good to have an entire city on the party's side. Drylund sizes them up and agrees: "When I become the Waterbaron, I promise that Yartar will be friends with the 3HPP. And because of that friendship, the 3HPP has committed to protecting our cities from cloud giants!" He drains his drink and says they should continue somewhere more private, as there are associates he wants them to meet. As Drylund ushers them out, Orsik decides it is time to ask about the coin, and why it might have been found on the bodies of those who murdered Queen Neri.
