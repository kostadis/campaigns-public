# Chapter 08 - Encounters on the Black Road

Date: 21 to about 24 Ches; the Black Road report is dated 30 Ches 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter is told through two documents. The first is Azam's journal, dated the 1st day of the 3rd tenday of Ches, recording that "The travel to Parnast is finally on its way." Azam's caravan has four wagons. One carries food; another the water and a shipment of medicinal herbs; another a shipment of weapons; and another "the statue of Angharradh."

Azam describes desert travel as "slow, hot and full of blistering sun," with "no clouds, no promise of water anywhere." The caravan keeps a strict rhythm. It travels well before sunrise by the light of the moon, "that makes the dunes look like mountains of diamonds," and walks until sunrise. From sunrise until noon it looks for shade, rests through the noon heat, heads out again at sunset until it is time to eat, and then sleeps. "Such is the daily routine of the traveler."

Azam then takes stock of "The middle person's retainers," whom he calls "a curious bunch." There is a fairy "who seems bent on revenge"; the "curious pair of the dwarf Orsik and the elf Vardis"; and Unla Key, "who is convinced that the fate of the world sits on his willingness to take risks." According to Hsing, Orsik "has done things that have made him unemployable," and Vardis' fellow clerics will not allow him in their temples. Hsing tells Azam that these are the heroes he is looking for. Azam shrugs. He remembers them arriving in Vuerthyl "stinking of sweat, covered in sand, sunburned and exhausted," looking "every bit the raggedy adventurer who is down on their luck." He had hoped one of the factions might send an agent, "but instead we get these independents."

The previous night changed his view: "the faith Hsing had in them might be justified." Thistle, the fairy, had proposed sleeping by day and standing guard by night, and Azam judges the decision "wise indeed." Azam went to sleep with Thistle and his friends guarding the caravan. The desert night is "bitterly cold and dry," the hour when camel spiders, sand scorpions, snakes and large-eared hopping mice "come out at night to casually murder each other." He woke to the fairy's shouts and the sound of charging goblins, and saw "the moonlight glinting off the steel of the goblins' weapons." The goblins were after the caravan's goods and water. Because Thistle saw them, they stole nothing: two goblins died, along with "their boss and his foul steed." The other two fled. Thistle argued that they should be pursued "to the ends of the earth," but Vardis objected. The rest of the night passed quietly and the journey continued.

The second document is a report from Fzoul Slovis "on the state of the Black Road from 10th day of the 3rd tenday of Ches." It notes increasing reports of goblins, hobgoblins and bugbears stirring up trouble along the Black Road up to the Graypeaks. On the way from Parnast, Fzoul's group saw a toll being set up on the edge of the desert, manned by hobgoblins who were "decently armed and armored" and "too many for us to fight." He encloses its location and recommends dealing with it. Because the mission was to collect information, the group paid "the toll of 15 GP per camel," and "An expense report has been filed."

Fzoul also reports a caravan near Vuerthyl that had been attacked by goblins. Those goblins "lacked the sophistication of the attacks of the earlier groups." In a pointed note he repeats his argument that "the Zhentarim leadership must take a firmer stand on the Goblin threat to our trade on the black road," warning that "We will not be taken seriously if Goblins threaten traders" and that "The piecemeal attempts have emboldened them." He calls for "a more systematic approach of extermination of the Goblin menace." He closes by naming "a potential new recruit, Orsik," a dwarf "accused of doing many things that make him unemployable as a guard," and states: "I intend to recruit him at the earliest opportunity."
