# Chapter 70 - Seeing through new eyes

Date: 26 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens with Adimond Kroskas’ latest manipulation of the fairy. Thistle is covered in a green, gooey substance, and when he stares at the pool of water he shrieks as the goo evaporates: "This is horrible." The giant queen, the Imperator, the storm giant guards, the other half-pints, and the pint all stare at the screeching fairy. Thistle flies up, stretches each eye open in turn, and shouts – "HE GAVE ME DRAGON EYES!" Orsik coughs, "Is that all?" Thistle admits that he also has true seeing, but his worry is elsewhere: "Yes, that’s cool, but dragon eyes? WHAT WILL I SAY AT THE GRAND BALL? And what will my love say."

The giants squint to see the tiny eyeballs on the 3-foot fairy. Queen Serissa says she is not sure she can see the issue, so Thistle flies into her face, pulls his left eyelid wide, and points at the dragon-like eyeball – "This is an abomination." Serissa squints and mutters, "It’s quite fetching!" The Imperator tries to calm everyone by pointing out that the change comes with a blessing, True Seeing, and Thistle glares at him. Orsik then steps forward to ask whether True Seeing reveals anything different about him, which sets Thistle off: "REALLY? THAT’S WHAT YOU ARE WORRIED ABOUT? HOW ABOUT FOCUSING ON ME FOR A MOMENT? Adimond Kroskas is messing with me again." He sobs that he is not one of Adimond Kroskas’ freakish experiments, and mutters, "I am not a freak." When he finally answers Orsik, the verdict is short: "Nothing’s changed."

Queen Serissa asks to return to matters of importance, and when Thistle flies into her face insisting "THIS IS IMPORTANT!" she smiles and tells him, "You are like the storm that rages." She asks him to focus so they can save her father and get his revenge on Adimond Kroskas. She holds up a wooden coin with a golden goose on it; Unla Key claims to know where it comes from — a chip used on a gambling ship in Yartar called ‘The Golden Goose.’ Unla Key corrects her: "A booze cruiser." He says the ship would not let him in, and he mumbles that some of his companions thought it was because he had no money and was a diviner, while he thinks it was racism. The Maelstrom has a magical device that can teleport them anywhere. Orsik guesses Yartar, and recalls the mess there: the home of the Harpers destroyed by Adimond Kroskas, his own betrayal of the Harpers, and the rescue of Lord Zymorven’s son from a prison where a vampire was involved in some kind of magical experimentation — or, he wonders, torture. Unla Key wants the castle back instead: "I want my castle back." Orsik sighs that they will have to deal with the Vampire, and Vardis mutters, "I have sunbeam."

The group leaves the Queen and is teleported to just outside the control room of Blagothkus’ castle. Thistle enters shouting, "I AM NOT A FREAK." Blagothkus cannot get low enough to see him, so he lifts the fairy to his eye and declares the dragon eyes cool. Thistle pouts, "My arch nemesis did this to me. It’s not cool." Esclarotta, in the soothing voice of a mother to her child, asks if he has any cool powers, and Orsik answers for him: "He can see me for who I am." Thistle snipes that he thought Esclarotta hated dragons and asks, "You -are- a giant, right?" Esclarotta explains that Blagothkus’ infatuation with power and obsession with Dragons brought down the Ordning and killed her son. Blagothkus, the repaired relationship still fragile, says he used to hate dragons but now has a relationship with them: "I wouldn’t invite them for dinner, whereas in the past, I would have them for dinner?" He defends keeping peoples apart — "I think different people want to be with their kind." — and Unla Key turns to Orsik: "Racism." Orsik nods.

Esclarotta reports that the party has been gone several days and the Vampire needed to be fed. Asked whether it fed on little people, the moonstones of the control room turn bright red, and she admits they had a problem, but since the party did not like the Uthgardt, they set the Vampires on a tribe of them. Unla Key declares that all this good vs evil is not working for him: "What I see is a bunch of racists and bullies who are causing harm." When someone suggests killing them all, the control room disappears and the sky becomes visible. Esclarotta asks, "I am assuming that killing is only one option? My husband and I have reformed." Unla Key absorbs the apparent threat — mess with her and hers, and he might fall into the void.

Thistle walks the cloud giants through what happened at the Maelstrom. Both are horrified at the part about Adimond Kroskas. One of them (the chapter does not say which) names the blue dragon he described as the Doom of the Desert, a powerful, ancient blue dragon, and is appalled that she wanted a hybrid fey-dragon and that "that fool Serissa" was working with her. Blagothkus shudders: "We must go find Hekaton. We need his strength to defeat the dragon." Unla Key insists the Vampire comes first — "The Vampire is the worst kind of racist." — and names her: Sandesyl, who wants to turn every dragon into a purer form of dragon. The group debates how to kill Sandesyl, then remembers her offer to leave the castle if they went on a quest for her. Thistle argues the quest might be less life-threatening than attacking a vampire; Blagothkus says this is not the time to remove unwanted guests; Unla Key shouts, "What do we want? It's a vampire-free zone. When do we want it? Now!" Thistle wins the day.

Thistle approaches Sandesyl’s tower in the middle of the day. The vampire hides in a shroud of darkness, unaware that Thistle can see through it. She offers to make him a fey vampire spawn; Vardis quips, "A fey-vampire-dragon? A triple threat!" and Thistle repeats, "I am not a freak!" Sandesyl explains the quest: a tome lost when the apostate Severin attempted to bring Tiamat into the world. She deflects questions about its contents — "Some arcane details of historical importance." — and when Orsik guesses a play and Thistle a romance novel, she tells one of her spawns, "It’s not the final book in the series. This is for official cult business." The spawn rolls his eyes in the dark, and Thistle, who can see him, struggles not to laugh.

The tome is in Xantharl’s Keep, behind an interdimensional puzzle always bathed in bright sunlight. Sandesyl recounts its history: as the ritual neared its climax the Cult split between traditionalists and apocalypse seekers; Severin offered a Blue Dragon mask to a traditionalist who needed the book to investigate it; then Iskander, an inner circle member, betrayed the cult to the Lords’ Alliance, who sent the Heroes of the War of the Dragon to the tower. The heroes secured the mask but missed the tome. "I want that time." Vardis guesses it is a wizard tower with puzzles that reset — "How cliché." — and Sandesyl agrees. Thistle asks what is in it for them, and she answers that the castle is: she is a powerful and ancient vampire with an ancient white dracolich at her beck and call. She admits she holds a privileged position in the castle but cannot control it, because Esclarotta is impervious to her charms; she means to find a dark lair, study the cult’s lore, and re-establish the rituals interrupted by the usurper. Vardis, still wondering about his future after his dismissal from the temples of Corellon, muses on founding an alternative believers cult of the Elf god. Sandesyl adds that the true believers hold that salvation comes not when Tiamat enters the world, but when all dragons are turned into dracoliches and rule Faerûn.

Someone asks about the Doom of the Desert before answering her offer. Sandesyl names her Iymrith, an ancient blue dragon, and her body glitters with lust and passion for the creature; she lets slip that with the tome she could convince Iymrith to embrace the endless life of the Dracolich. She laughs off the idea of a dragon-fey as impossible — then tells Thistle that Iymrith undermined the Giant world to gain access to the Storm Giant’s castle for her experiments on hybridization of dragons, "and fey." Thistle says he must talk with his colleagues. Thistle suggests charging; Unla Key, ever the gambler, argues that a clever ploy has a better chance of success, and Vardis proposes finding out what the Dracolich on the vampire’s leash is really about.

The 3HPP descends 50 feet through icy tunnels of blue ice into the glacier that Blagothkus’ castle is built around, past coins, helmets, livestock, a handful of kobolds and an ogre or two frozen in the walls, to a two-level vault where a vast white dracolich clings to the ceiling above a treasure hoard. Thistle offers his own theory of dracolich origins (a vampiric stork), and Unla Key explains the phylactery: "If you control the phylactery, you control the dragon." Orsik’s "Hello, neighbor." gets nothing; Unla Key mutters, "Never send a fighter to do a wizard’s job." Then Orsik tries flattery — "Oh, great and powerful dragon!" — and the dracolich descends.

She tells her story: "I am Glazhael the Undying." Once Glazhael the Cloudchaser, she ruled the northern skies with Talis; she broke a truce with the heroes of the war of the dragons when the White Wyrmspeaker was captured, and was killed for the first time at the Tomb of Diderius by the dwarf Stroud’s bow. Jorgen raised her as a Dracolich in Xantharl’s tower using the Blue Mask, but she left him and ran to Talis, and Severin was adamant that a Dracolich could not serve the cult. Sent to spy from the Miklos glacier, she met Stroud and the First Faction again; they told her where to find the blue mask, and they lied. A plot to murder the First Faction failed, and when she recovered the cult was destroyed, Talis was no more, and Sandesyl had her phylactery. Orsik asks how she feels about that. "I AM A DRACOLICH," she roars. "I do not bow to anyone." His follow-up — "Oh, so you aren’t at her beck and call?" — draws a torrent of dragon curses. Orsik proposes a deal to kill the vampire. Glazhael fears Sandesyl will destroy her phylactery if she is seen, but agrees to strike at the end: "When you have almost won, I will seal your victory. But not a moment sooner." She knows how to kill "the moon elf romance-book-loving vampire," and will not say how: "That is my secret. Now go."
