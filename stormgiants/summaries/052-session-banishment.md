# Chapter 52 - Banishment

Date: 11 to 13 Eleasis 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The 3HPP, with the goblins led by Tlega and Mirk, enter Maergun’s throne room. It is newly finished, with smooth walls and a massive throne; Drauchanar hangs from a chain, and a cyclops, a displacer beast and the Fomorian wait inside. Thistle stares at the Fomorian and recalls the official textbook of the Seelie kingdom, which he admits is "a tad bit biased": it calls the Fomorians "the most hideous and wicked of all giantkind." He thinks, "Definitely got the ugly, right." He then remembers the official histories of the elves, which tell how the Fomorians "sought to conquer the Feywild and enslave its inhabitants" and were cursed when the fey united against them.

Thuul rushes the Cyclops and bites, and Thistle follows the splash of acid with a pair of arrows. The Cyclops smashes Thuul with his club, but Thuul lives. Tlega, in the corridor behind, shouts to his troops, "Move ahead. I’m right behind you." Orsik glares, then sighs when he realises that is all Tlega plans to do. Unla Key casts a Fireball that burns the giant, but the displacer beast ducks out at the last minute; it then kills Thuul and wounds Thistle with its tentacle. Orsik squeezes between the Cyclops and the Displacer beast and strikes with his infused sword, whose thunder damage hits both creatures, and attacks them again. Vardis, the cleric of light, casts Fireball: the Displacer Beast dies, and the Cyclops and the Fomorian are hurt. The goblins fire at the giant, two arrows hitting. Mirk shouts "Well done!" and, when his decision to do nothing but heap praise causes dissension in the ranks, throws a tantrum, hurls his javelin into the giant and shouts "Satisfied!" The goblins all cheer.

The Fomorian shouts, "You killed my puppy!" Unla Key and the giant trade names for the beast — "Fluffy!", "No!", "Spiky!", "Haerbega!" — and Orsik interjects, "That’s a bad name. You really need a new one!" The Fomorian tries an evil eye on Vardis, who avoids the worst of the effects but not of the club. Thistle, annoyed that Thuul is dead ("More like dematerialized," he reminds himself), shoots the Cyclops dead. Tlega shouts "ATTACK!"; Orsik asks, "Don’t you fight?"; Tlega answers, "We lead!"; and Orsik mutters, "From the rear!" With a cantrip, a goblin arrow, and a devastating set of attacks by Orsik using the Giant Sword of Lord Zymorven, the giant almost dies, and Vardis finishes it with a cantrip. The goblins break into a dance, shouting, "WE ARE THE CHAMPIONS! NO TIME FOR LOSERS! FOR WE ARE THE CHAMPIONS OF THIS CAVE!"

Vardis frees Drauchanar, who claims the victory for his "spectacular leadership" and says he let himself be captured. Vardis, knowing Orsik keeps silent to maintain the illusion that he is Prince Thornacious, says, "Thornacious is always trying to steal the credit!" Orsik searches the body, finds an iron key, and uses it on a keyhole in the throne to reveal a staircase to a treasure vault: turquoise figurines, a brass crown, a carved ivory statuette, a tapestry of elves feasting, a mass of coins, and a potion of greater healing and water breathing.

Thistle notices a secret door to the Southeast and investigates. He finds an excavated tomb decorated with elven nature bas-reliefs, with two duergar cleaning it reverentially and, on a raised platform, what looks like a white stone statue of a sleeping elven woman. A peculiar mind reaches out, and he decides to kiss the statue. Vardis shouts, "Thistle, what are you doing!" Thistle answers, "This looks just like my girlfriend," and kisses it. The statue becomes a disoriented female elf, and Vardis knows at once: "A vampire!" Thistle calls Thuul over to meet "my new friend, Aeglossil." Aeglossil grapples Orsik and bites him, feeding for the first time in eons. Vardis presents the symbol of Corellon, and she snarls in fear at the power of The Protector. Unla Key casts banishment; space and light warp around her, there is a great bang like a door slamming shut, and the vampire is banished into another realm. Asked "How?", Unla Key answers, "I can twist time and space."

With the vampire gone, Thistle flees — flies away — pushed by the sight of so many Derro and his injured state. Unla Key wants to fight, but Vardis talks him out of it, and Orsik and Vardis follow. The Derro remain, waiting for their vampire mistress to return.

In the sunlight of the Feywild, Thistle shouts, "Here’s The Knife." Orsik replies, "That’s no knife," and The Knife insists, "I am not a knife, I am THE Knife. It’s a nom-de-guerre." When Thistle presses about "that nice lady we met," Orsik mutters, "Tomorrow, Thistle, ask me tomorrow." The Knife, still under the illusion that Orsik is Prince Thornacious, shouts Thistle down and fawns over the dwarf, who keeps a straight face: "Yes, it is true. I am very patient." The following day Thistle understands that the lady was a vampire and he had been charmed; Orsik is disappointed that Thistle neither grovels nor shows him more respect. The colorful sprite reappears: "The Fomorian is dead, and Queen Titania is pleased! She would like to give you a gift!" Thistle tries to signal that any gift from Queen Titania comes with strings attached, but Orsik’s greed wins out. The gift is "a Blessed Horn! A magical item of raw and incredible powers." Thistle hears "powers" and takes it, and the heroes go through the open portal.

On the 1st day of the 2nd tenday of Eleasis, Delsephine is waiting on the other side; for her, only a few moments have passed. She stares at the aged Orsik and asks, "Who is this?" Orsik answers, "I am Orsik’s father," and spins a story about being "Orsik, with a c" who gave his son "a nice whupping." She smiles beneath her mask, and Orsik knows she will keep the secret because she understands second chances. On the ship, Vardis names the next destination: Beorunna’s Well. The sage from Candlekeep negotiates a fee of 50 gold, paid as 500 silver, and tells the tale: the spirit mound of the Black Lion and Red Tiger tribes of Uthgardt in the heart of the Druarwood, the legend of Beorunna, the half-ogre’s bones, the altar over a relic stolen from the giants, the two totem poles, the Sunken Grove, and the 250-foot drop with its knotted rope. He suspects the relic lies beneath the blood-splattered altar. Orsik says, "I don’t think they will mind if we dig under it." The sage disagrees, but has placed a modest wager on their success, and will not increase it.

On the 2nd day of the 2nd tenday of Eleasis, the 3HPP arrive near the cave. Thistle scouts: tents of Uthgardt barbarians on a ledge to the east, a manticore to the west, the altar to the south. They try to go down quietly, but the shaman hears them and rouses the Barbarians, who wake the Manticore; it sees Unla Key's 12’ wings and flies at them. Orsik proposes running to the altar as a defensive position. Thistle stays back as rear guard, and Vardis shouts, "NEVER SPLIT THE PARTY!"

In the battle in the cavern, Vardis grumbles, "Not so sure running to get cornered is the best plan we’ve had," and Orsik, still getting used to his 50 years of aging, answers, "We do our best work with our backs against the wall." Unla Key, sure this is not where they will die, tells fate, "I know because I know," and turns toward danger. He casts "Enemies Abound" on the Shaman, who can no longer tell friend from foe and strikes at his own tribesmen. The Manticore looses spikes at Thistle and misses; Thistle wounds it, and it hides behind stalactites. Thistle kills two on-rushing locals from a great height, prompting Vardis to mutter, "One fairy killing machine when it remembers it can shoot more than once." Behind the Altar, Orsik starts digging and asks Vardis for a digging spell; the answer is "No." The barbarians, caught between the fairy and the shaman, hide behind rocks near the rope. A barbarian punches the Shaman out of the spell; the Shaman hugs him and shouts, "Protect the relics!" Orsik then suggests, "Given that we don’t need the altar for protection, how about we just smash it?" and Vardis casts a spiritual weapon: "I love the way you think." The barbarians' short-bow arrows miss Thistle, who is just inside their long range. Unla Key closes on the cowering barbarians; the Manticore aims a volley at him, and Thistle orders Thuul between them, which annoys Unla Key. The shaman begins a ritual spell. A fiery blast kills the Manticore. Vardis’ spiritual weapon misses ("I can’t hit the - literal - side of a barn"), but Orsik’s blades crack the altar into chunks that reveal the ground beneath it: "We have the artifact."

As they run back to the rope, Vardis' bow shot unexpectedly hits, and another fireball leaves only one of the protectors alive. Orsik misses with his longbow, smashes it on the ground and screams, "I hate this bow." The Shaman raises the dead fighters as a horde of zombies; Vardis advises against targeting him, because "These monsters exist even if the Shaman who cast them dies," and another Fireball incinerates the undead. Thistle strikes the Shaman, who falls in a pool of blood, and is annoyed when Vardis asks whether a dragon did it. Unla Key saves his fireball and uses magical darts on the last barbarian, whom Thistle then kills with two arrows. Thistle offers, "Goodberries, anyone?"

Thistle explores the encampment and the manticore’s lair and finds nothing of value. Meanwhile Orsik whispers the magic word "Chaos" to a reluctant Unla Key, and he, Orsik and Vardis stage the battle to blame a rival tribe. When Orsik starts up the rope, rocks fly out of the ground and hit him — the Shaman's ritual protected the area around the rope, and Thistle is attacked by rocks when he tries to fly up — and someone concludes, "I guess we’ll have to fly them up." Artifact in tow, they stand at the lip of Beorunna’s Cave, satisfied that the two tribes will be too distracted to find the real culprit. A few hours later the Shaman, who had faked his death, wakes, foresees a brutal civil war, and decides it is better to die killing the thieves than in another pointless tribal conflict.

Back on the ship, Delsephine cuts off Orsik’s suggestion that she join them with a definitive no. Vardis studies Harshnag’s map, floats the Great Worm Cavern, then reconsiders: "How about the One Stone? It’s closer." Brassik notes it lies in the territory of the Sky Ponies. Vardis wants to see the Sky Ponies, and Orsik, now super intelligent, cringes at memories of Vardis's collection of ‘Sky Ponies’ and the pamphlets. Thistle relays that the Sky Pony tribe and his Uncle Joon had a dispute over honor; Uncle Joon, he notes, "was kicked out of the Court for being a drunk." Paid five platinum, Brassik ("Is it possible to standardize on a single coinage?") describes the Sky Pony spirit mound in the Moonwood, west of the Redrun River: the two-tiered plateau shaped like a pegasus from the air, the 15-foot boulder that is the eye of the sky pony, the encircling stream, and the totem poles hung with the corpses of trespassers. The sages think the relic is the rock; Orsik declares, "It’s under the rock." They wait until morning.

On the 3rd day of the 2nd tenday of Eleasis, Thistle leads them across the river to a beach and up the pegasus's legs to the boulder. Unla Key, staring at the plateau, mutters, "I am a pegasus!" At the boulder he says, "Well, dig," but the ground trembles and two bulettes burst out, each carrying a human rider. Unla Key is momentarily captivated by an idea for a spell, the Point of Power. Orsik is thrown down, then hits the first bulette four times, his green flame leaping to the second. Unla Key stops the armored rider's hold person, Vardis's spiritual weapon kills the wounded beast, and Orsik and Vardis shrug off the other rider's command to flee. Thistle casts Hunter’s Mark and shoots from the sky. Orsik kills the second creature and its rider, whose last words are, "You shall not seize the stone from the cult of the black earth!" Thistle chimes in, "The stone belongs in a museum!" Thistle, Thuul and Unla Key strike the remaining humans from the sky, and Orsik cuts down the last as it tries to run.

As the party scavenges, including a strange stone breastplate, Sky Pony tribesmen appear from the tree line. Vardis holds up a dead assailant and shouts, "We defended the stone from these thieves!" The Shaman hails "The Sky Pony avatar" and asks for the one with the wings of a Sky Pony. Unla Key drops his invisibility and shouts, "I am here. Bow before me." The tribe bows in awe. But the Shaman promises a festival and to leave 20 warriors to guard the totem while bringing a thousand more. Orsik thinks, "Great work, Unla Key; instead of 20, we now have a 1000." Unla Key salvages it by commanding a vision quest: "three days out, then spend a whole day fasting, and then return in three days." The tribe leaves cheering. "So we have seven days to figure out this rock," Unla Key says, and wants to make them a new totem; asked if he is getting soft, he replies that "a benevolent god always attracts more followers than one that does not bestow gifts."

The rock is too heavy for the airship. Unla Key notices trace lines on it, learns from a power how to trace them, and is covered in oozing blisters when the last line is traced, which he cures with cure wounds thanks to his blessed horn. The rock can be shrunk and enlarged thrice daily and can create an effect similar to the wand of wonder. "How glorious!" he exclaims, and the rest of the 3HPP groan. Unla Key uses a stone-shape spell to craft an image of himself where the rock was, and Orsik leaves a magical bean and an enchanted egg in a bowl that creates a water pool when broken. "I hope they figure out how to use this."
