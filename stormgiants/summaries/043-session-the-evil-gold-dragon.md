# Chapter 43 - The Evil Gold Dragon

Date: 30 Flamerule 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The road was hard on the group. Harshnag was grouchy, and Yazmeen was freezing, but Orsik was very determined to follow the treasure map. Because the map pointed almost in the right direction, the rest of the group tagged along — or, as the chapter puts it, "no one wanted to walk away from that kind of haul."

The treasure lay in a cave that happened to be the lair of an evil gold dragon. A pack of crag cats hung around the caves, trying to feast on the remains of the dragon's prey.

The chapter then pauses on the phrase itself — "Evil Gold Dragon?" — to explain that it is "A common misconception" that dragons are good or evil according to the color of their scales. The narrator lays the real cause at the feet of the two dominant religious groups, the Cult of Bahamut and the Cult of Tiamat. Historically the Cult of Bahamut attracted dragons with shiny metallic scales and the Cult of Tiamat drew dragons with primary colors; over time each group bred within itself, and each cult's cultural biases permeated its dragons. For most non-dragons, Tiamat is evil and Bahamut is good.

The narrator calls this "a misunderstanding" and argues that both gods view dragons as the apex species, with others serving the dragons' needs; the difference is the approach. Bahamut holds that having the fewest number of enemies is critical. Tiamat is impatient with anyone in her way and does not care how many enemies she has, because she "has unshakable faith in her final triumph." So Bahamut's dragons are good "because they seem to not want to destroy non-dragons right now," and Tiamat's dragons are evil "because they do not care if non-dragons are killed in pursuit of their aims." Because dragons tend to reflect the group they are brought up in, metallic dragons tend to be good and primary color dragons tend to be evil — "But the keyword is tend."

This gold dragon was one of the metallic dragons who rejected Bahamut's philosophy and the "artificial limits that Bahamut has placed on their freedom of action." He was "a profoundly philosophical Dragon who saw the Cult of Bahamut's hypocrisy and decided to selfishly pursue his own goals." He did not join the Cult of Tiamat either: after millennia of fighting between primary color and metallic dragons, he saw that as "just trading one hypocrisy for another," and the Cult of Tiamat, because of profound doctrinal differences, was not welcoming to metallic dragons. In time, the narrator suggests, he might have found a different path for all dragonkind.

But the 3HPP "were not interested in a deep philosophical debate on good and evil." After a fierce battle, they killed him.
