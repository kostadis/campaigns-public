# Chapter 21 - The Battle Begins

Date: 6 to 8 Mirtul 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary

The chapter opens with Orsik eager for more fighting: "Let’s find some more orcs to kill!" Vardis looks into the southwest cave and finds a dead human, beaten by orcs, lying next to a rock. Orsik muses aloud to Vardis about why orcs sit in their caves rather than rushing to help one another when they hear combat. Unla Key thinks, "I understand their motivations," and offers the suggestion "it might be because of the insulation?" The others all stare at him.

When Vardis walks in, two Orcs hidden in the shadows attack, but he is not surprised; he recognizes them as followers of Yurtrus, the god of death and disease. Orsik rushes in and kills one, only for the creature to come back to life. "No matter," says the Dwarf, and kills him again. Wallace charges in after the other orc.

Meanwhile Unla Key and Thistle, still in the cave with the whelps and the villagers, are attacked by two more Orcs. One hits Thistle with a spiritual weapon; the other hits Unla Key with his spiritual weapon and the spear. Thistle wonders aloud how they hurt his companion so badly, and one of the orcs cannot resist answering: "you have armor, he doesn’t!" Unla Key smirks, "not enough" and answers with a lightning bolt the orcs cannot dodge, for 29 points of damage. Orsik sends Vardis to "go help the halfling." Vardis tells Unla Key to "get ready for my light touch" and heals him. Thistle strikes an orc with his bow, and Thuul's acid leaves the orc dead; then an arrow pierces the orc's defenses for 11 HP of damage with an arrow, and Wallace charges and kills another.

With the orcs dead, Vardis explores the room to the south east of the prison room. A foul-smelling concoction brews over a fire, bones and ash are piled up as an altar to Yurtrus, a wooden frame holds a pale white section of elven skin, and a dead flayed elf lies there. Back in the room he first entered, he finds several gems and gold trade bars.

Wallace, excited by a new feat he has mastered, charges into the last remaining area of the cave complex. The cave is unnaturally dark. He shouts, "guys, there’s someone here." That is the last thing he hears before an orc of the Red Fang drops him. The chapter explains that the Orcs of the Red Fang are assassins for hire and followers of Shargaas. Unla Key looks into the darkness, sees nothing, and casts a fireball for 29 points of damage; the two assassin orcs cannot escape it, and neither can Wallace, who is left clinging desperately to life. Orsik screams "Wallace!" and runs after him. Unla Key raises an eyebrow at Vardis, who says, "I know I have been replaced." Orsik overhears and insists, "you’re BOTH my best friend!"

Standing in the darkness over Wallace's unconscious body, Orsik is hit for 36 points of damage. Thistle rushes in, stumbles around, finds Wallace's mouth and forces a potion of healing down his throat. Vardis sighs and channels the divine power of Larethian to bring daylight to the cave, banishing the magical darkness and causing 16 and 8 points of damage to the orcs. But the other Orc of Shargaas casts darkness again and disappears into it. "I just cleaned that up!" shouts Vardis. Unla Key, unable to see any target, runs into the darkness, under Orsik's legs, over Thistle, past the outstretched arm of one of the orcs, and out the other side facing the other orc. Orsik uses his fighting spirit to kill the Orc assassin, then rushes the last Orc outside the darkness and misses. Wallace — knocked out, burned with a fireball, healed by a fairy — gets up from the ground and kills the last orc: "You thought you were so clever!" With its death the darkness is dispelled.

Wallace points out a chest holding a miniature silver dragon skull and a small sack of loose coins and gems. The group returns to the main room with the whelps and villagers. Orsik asks, "So how much time do we have before the battle?" and is told "Two days." When the answer comes back "Not if we want to bring all the children," Orsik thinks it over and decides he has to do the right thing: "We bring all of the children." Unla Key raises an eyebrow.

On the way to Parnast the group learns that one of the orc whelps is the son of the orc chieftain. The orc who guarded the whelps, thankful her child was not killed, tells them Kurglow would be willing to abandon Bad Fruul to save his child: "He’s a terrible husband, but a good father."

In Parnast, Thistle suggests using Jarlor and Larrish to send a message to Kurglow through the spy channel. Jarlor is not interested until his wife tells him to "grow a spine and do what you need to do to protect your family". He nods and listens as Orsik delivers the worst threat he can come up with: "if you attack, we send the whelp to bed without dessert and will not read a bed-time story!" Jarlor stares, wonders whether to correct the dwarf, and thinks, "never had kids and he’s the boss."

Thistle asks, plaintively, "Is there a Lionshield Coster around?" Chandra explains that the place belonged to the Zhentarim, who believed power came from physical transport of goods, that the transdimensional market the Lionshield Coster's use is not available here, and that "Our poor local merchant Calder has been forced to use the Zhentarim physical transportation of goods." Thistle remembers what Uncle Joon had told him: that the Lionshield Coster was a devotee of KP Trade Equilibrium Theory, which considered all markets across all possible universes rather than a single local physical market, met violent resistance from merchants, and involved a novel application of the Smalistin conjecture — though mathematics was not Thistle's strong suit. The important point is that Calder has no magic to sell. "Sorry about that!" the small halfling had told him.

The next morning the militia arrives at their posts. All paths into the center of town where the well is are barricaded, windows boarded up, and pits dug to slow the advance. The militia is terrified, the women and children nervous, the refugees worried and trapped in Parnast. As the sun begins to set, Orsik steps forward: "my friends, they will not pass, they will not succeed, they will die! We will come through tonight alive, our town preserved!" The jingoistic speech is a rousing success and the militia cheer.

As the moon rises, Orsik and Thistle tell Chandra, "our ace in the hole is wyvern." She asks whether they realize "the wyvern will kill everyone?" Asked whether she could control it, she answers "Um, no." Thistle tries to remember whether he ever learned to control crazed hungry draconic creatures, and Thuul whimpers. Unla Key scans the book that SEER gave him and finds a story of seven warriors who train a town's villagers to fight and all die together in a glorious blood bath, their combined blood being part of a ritual that summons an extra-planar power. He chooses not to share it, and instead shouts, "we must win!"

From his elevated vantage point Thistle watches the edge of the forest, and Bad Fruul appears: sitting on a mammoth, in bone-festooned plate armor, with a helm made from the skull of some unidentifiable beast. Thistle thinks, "I should know that." Bad Fruul looks like a massive dark knight, not like some hill giant. The giant dismounts, removes his helm and scans the battlefield with eyes that gleam with intelligence. Someone exclaims, "That’s the drow!" The giant demands Chandra, "the offspring of the horrid Luistarra", and the "vile statue of Angharradh", threatening the town's destruction after its people are made slaves. Ragnar and Gundalin shout back, "NEVER! The Orsik’s Militia will never surrender one of our own! Go back before you die!" "Then death and destruction it is!" — and the horde of goblins, orcs, ogres, and ettins begins howling and chanting.

The final section shifts to Bad Fruul's point of view. He thinks: "They underestimate me! They think Bad Fruul is dumb! Bad Fruul will rule over all!" He sends eight goblins, two wolfs and their goblin leaders to test the defenses along the main road, dismissing them — "they served their purpose." The goblins fall into the pits and die on the pikes or to the militia attacking from higher ground; the goblin bosses are killed by the archers of Orsik's Militia. Bad Fruul shouts, "but I was told how badly their training went! My spies lied to me!" A wolf tries to go around the pits and scale the wall, but the militia hacks it to bits. Bad Fruul then sends ogres, half-ogres from Mama Mia and two owlbears. The ogres walk around the pits full of dead goblins and reach the walls; the half-ogres are killed by the Thistle Trained Archers. "You were saying, Vardis?" "Nothing." The ogres navigate the pits, but the owl bears do not. Seeing his ogres advance, Bad Fruul tells his lieutenant, "Mama Mia’s ogres have breached the wall, send some more ogres with the owlbear." The chapter ends there.
