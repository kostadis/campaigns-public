# Chapter 77 The end of Sandesyl and Blagothkus

Date: 27 Eleint 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary
The chapter opens in the aftermath of a blast: the cavernous room is lit brilliantly, the ice shakes with a roaring sound, and then there is silence. Orsik shrugs and looks inside the chest, finding thousands of gold coins and hundreds of platinum pieces. He reflects, "Sandesyl chose to live in squalor." While dumping the coins into his bag of holding he finds a ring, studies it for a while, and hands it to Vardis, who looks at it and puts it into his pocket. With the last of her treasure emptied, Orsik stretches his back and says, "We should see the Dracolich."

The cloud giant interrupts: "Excuse me," he shouts. "I need some healing." Thistle observes that he does not think the giant turns to mist when brought to death's door, and so could use some healing himself. Vardis sighs and mutters, "Fine, let's camp and find this undead dragon." Thistle adds, "We have two hours before the vampire reforms."

An hour later the party stands in the glacier's center, inside the lair of Glazhael the Undying, who is surprised to see them living. "Sandesyl is dead?" she asks, and is told, "Mostly. We need your help to finish the job. Do you know where her lair is?" The dracolich smiles, her eyes glowing, and asks whether any of them has the magic to break through the ice. Unla Key casts a fire bolt; Glazhael rolls her eyes, sighs, walks up to the same spot and flicks her tail at the wall. The wall shatters, revealing a plain-looking coffin. "Get me my phylactery," she demands. Thistle is uncertain: "How do we know this is the right coffin?" Glazhael explains that the vampire will be inside, along with her phylactery, and that once she has it she will leave. Orsik and Vardis agree: "It was the deal."

Unla Key cannot wait any longer and opens the coffin. Inside lies the perfectly preserved corpse of Sandesyl, along with a small box inlaid with gold and locked shut. As the box is handed to Glazhael, Orsik asks her, "How do we kill a vampire?" Glazhael laughs, "I don't know." Watching her leave, Unla Key mutters, "I know." Orsik offers the folklore answers — "Wooden stake through the heart? Holy wafers? Sunlight?" — and Unla Key shakes his head, telling him he "went to a second-rate institution for your studies in necromancy" and that "A vampire simply needs to be killed in its coffin. The how is irrelevant." Orsik shrugs. Unla Key then examines Sandesyl and considers the power he could have as a vampire, thinking he might manipulate her using telekinesis. Before he can, Orsik notices what the halfling is up to, pushes him aside, and stabs the vampire.

A little later the 3HPP and Blagothkus are in the control room with Brunvild and a furious Esclarotta. "This is all your fault. You made a deal with dragons!" Brunvild adds, "And a vampire!" and the accusation is repeated: "And a vampire!" Esclarotta has heard from Brunvild the real version of how Blagothkus reacquired his castle, and she is unhappy. Orsik wants to kill Blagothkus because he does not trust him; Thistle agrees, but wants to be sure Esclarotta does not shut down the castle. Then he remembers the ghost in the Eye of the All-Father.

Thistle plays it slowly, feigning that he cannot remember where it was before settling on "the oracle," where they ran across a ghost "that said he was trapped for all eternity." Esclarotta's gems form two narrow slits, with two red gems as pupils that pulsate and stare at Blagothkus. "I believe his name was Eigeron," Thistle continues. Esclarotta cries, "Our son!!! Is a ghost? What does he need to find peace?!" Thistle tells her the ghost sent them, wanting revenge on the person who turned him into a ghost. "Who?" she shouts, and Thistle lets the question hang. Blagothkus whimpers, "That's not what happened. It was an accident." Esclarotta screams in rage, "Kill him! FREE MY SON!!!!" Orsik shrugs: "Happy to oblige."

Before Blagothkus can react, Vardis strikes him with a guiding bolt, Orsik smites him with the sword, Thistle pierces his critical arteries, Unla Key burns him with electricity, and Brunvild finishes the job with one of Blagothkus' old hammers. Esclarotta sobs.

Brunvild then tries to argue that the castle is his by salvage rights. Orsik, having killed one annoying giant, is itching to kill another, and Brunvild, seeing the danger, mutters, "I salvaged the castle. It doesn't belong to me, I just want … " As the two edge toward blows, Esclarotta shrieks, "THIS IS MY CASTLE!" The room goes silent. She declares them passengers: "You saved my son. So you have earned safe passage on my castle." Thistle mutters, "Delighted to be your guests!" Brunvild backs out of the room, muttering that he will find a nice place to sleep — "Big castle, after all!"

Before Orsik can offer any further suggestions, Esclarotta's gem forms two clasped hands and, like a disapproving mother, she asks, "Have you forgotten your mission?" The party runs through its list of goals — putting on a play, framing Silverymoon for looting the Uthgardt artifacts, finding Hekaton, defeating the blue dragon, saving Thistle's beloved — and Orsik cannot resist adding, "Uncover the plot of the Margasters?" She growls, "HEKATON is the priority. We have a clue. It's in Yartar, and we are going there."

Later that day Orsik looks down at the clouds and, below them, the Evermoors. He mutters to Thistle, "It's not that fast." Esclarotta overhears: "Are you saying something about my speed?" Thistle, sensing her annoyance, assures her they are saying nothing about the speed of her castle — "It's a fantastic castle!"

After sunset the lights of Yartar shimmer below. The Grand Dame is still at dock, getting ready for its nightly voyage. Esclarotta wants a plan — land and sink the boat, or sneak around? — and doubts the clue: "Queen Serissa's clue isn't -that- helpful. I mean, a wooden coin from a gambling casino? It could be a coincidence." Thistle answers, "We shall sneak. Take a position up here, and I will fly down." Watching him go, Unla Key remembers that there are vampires in Yartar and weighs immortality: "Perhaps that's a way for me to achieve immortality. To what end? I have seen the end of the world, and I can save the world. Would being a vampire change anything?" He shakes his head. "No, it would not."

A few hours later Thistle returns to the castle deck: "It's a regular boat." It is night and they have time before morning. Thistle frames the problem — a tenuous clue, a chip from a casino — and says they need someone to talk, ideally the ship's owner, Lord Khaspere Drylund. Vardis agrees: "We need to get him to talk." Unla Key proposes sinking the boat and stealing all the money from the bottom of the sea; Thistle points out that it "would get in the way of us saving my girlfriend and defeating Adimond Kroskas," so information comes first. Orsik pats Unla Key on the back: "We have 75,000 gold pieces in our treasure room. Let's get down there and gamble some of it away." Lord Drylund would not let them in last time, he says, but "now he will beg to let us gamble on his Grand Dame!" Thistle thinks, "One roll of the dice, and it will be my Grand Dame."
