# Chapter 81 - Thistle Decodes the Conspiracy

Date: 30 Eleint to the following day 1492 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-06)

## Summary

Thistle remembers Mary, an old lady in Everlund who warned passers-by of the Kraken. She had sent the party on what the chapter calls a "goofball quest" to fetch what she called an artifact but was a trinket. Her group was known as the "Fighters of the Light." Part of the reason they took the quest was that they could not afford the admission fee, which was, at the time, *five* gold pieces. The artifact had lain in "the dark, brooding peaks of the Nether Mountains." Orsik and the team had been intrigued by the legends of lost Netherese magic, and Uncle Joon had noted that the mountains were full of orcs, while the eastern peaks were home to blue dragons chased out of the northern Anauroch desert by the Doom of the Desert. Thistle had ignored that detail at the time. Now he knows who the Doom is: Adimond Kroskas.

Unla Key asks what Thistle found. "Nothing of value," Thistle answers. Orsik is suspicious and asks about treasure, and Thistle quickly shares it. "That's valuable." remarks the mage. The fairy shrugs that there is "No evidence of any written communications," adding, "There's always some written communication." Someone answers, "Were you expecting printed stationery?"

Unla Key wonders whether the Kraken Society merely uses the name or whether some actual Kraken is manipulating events. Krakens were covered at the Nirany School of the Arcane, his alma mater, but as legendary creatures rather than things that exist. Uncle Joon had only given Thistle the Kraken Society's name, but Krakens were famous in the Feywild. Unla Key recalls the sage of legendary creatures: Krakens are "Leviathans of Legend," once fierce warriors of the gods who shrugged off their servitude when the gods' wars ended. Citing Prof BigEyes, he describes the Kraken as "a primeval force that obliterates civilization's greatest achievements as if they were castles in the sand," and notes that cults spring up around such creatures — "Such a thing may have happened with this Kraken." Thistle mutters, "But whether the Kraken Society is affiliated with a Kraken or just plays dress-up is unclear. Any evidence of telepathy?" Unla Key remembers that they had been told of telepathy "over a limited range, like 100 feet."

Thistle thinks aloud: Lord Khaspere Drylund and Lady Atalia lack the resources "to steal a giant and hide him in a boat that can't be tracked." Everyone turns to him, confused. He declares that Iymrith, or Adimond Kroskas, wanted to continue his experiments on fairies, and, pausing for dramatic effect, cries, "LOOK AT MY EYES!!!!" Pow is about to offer a compliment, reads the body language of the rest of the 3HPP, and puts on the appropriate look of horror. Thistle goes on: the dragon needed a patron, and who better than "the desperate Queen of the Storm Giants." Iymrith is probably behind the dismantling of the Ordning itself; with Hekaton out of the way he could work into Queen Serissa's good graces. "The Kraken Society is just a rebranded Cult of the Dragon."

Unla Key tries not to point out the logical flaws, though he wonders how a dragon could manipulate a lost oracle of giants — and, more importantly, how he might get such a dragon's power for himself. He tries to poo-poo the idea: "A dragon with such power? I'm sure the dragon is a patsy. We shouldn't try and kill him but talk." The answer comes back: "Dragons are evil. Did you see what he did to my eyes? DRAGON EYES!" Even the oblivious mage picks up on the tone of anger from Thistle's voice and falls silent. Thistle mutters, "I am not a freak."

Hoping to redirect Thistle from his murderous rage, Unla Key asks, "Could I join the Kraken Society?" The reply: "Do you want to work for a cult that has a remote brain-exploding device?" Unla Key shudders, then suggests, "What about the SEER? We could ask her?" Vardis splutters and Orsik shuts that idea down.

Recovered from his rage, Thistle announces, "Whenever there is trouble, it's a dragon." The 3HPP stares at him. He then rants through his whole theory. The War of the Dragons — "that thing that cost me my eyes" — was brought on by Tiamat, who tore up the North, and that is how Iymrith got the money to start capturing fairies. When the First Faction defeated Tiamat, Iymrith needed a new sponsor. She came up with "the plan of perverting the Oracle of Annam and starting a Giant war," hoping to use Eigeron until "his idiot father, Blagothkus, killed him." She used the Oracle to tell every giant lord that the ordning had been sundered, and the war started — but no giant would take a dragon's help. Desperate, she approached Hekaton, who said no. The Doom of the Desert decided to kill Neri, expecting the King to go ballistic over the Queen's death, but Serissa convinced Hekaton to calm down and research. So Iymrith decided to get rid of the King, kidnapping him and hiding him "on a ship that no one can scry." Without Hekaton, Queen Serissa lacks the credibility to hold the Giant world in order; Iymrith gets an introduction from "the two sisters" with a promise to deliver them the throne, and Queen Serissa agrees to help the Dragon with his "insane experiments." For a patsy and muscle, Iymrith chose "a bunch of dead-end Cult of the Dragon losers who are told that there is a new super powerful big bad that is a KRAKEN!" Sweating, eyes half-crazed, Thistle concludes, "It all fits."

Pow and the 3HPP stare at each other. Unla Key breaks the silence: "Yep, that sounds about right." Vardis asks, "I'm guessing this means we are not going to Everlund?" The answer: "Straight to the northern isles of the Trackless Sea."

Pow turns to his new partners and asks whether they will have time to hire a crew for cleanup. He wants about ten people for basic cleanup and demolition, at about three sp per laborer, doubled "since we are asking them to fly on a castle," plus supplies — "so 1000 gold?" Someone tells him, "If you stay here for a few days, I know some guys." Thistle and Unla Key, wanting to spend their newfound wealth on magic, agree.

The next day Pow has his crew and needs one more day for supplies. The Shou probes about the budget: about 500,000 gold to turn the entire place into a grand casino, but 50,000 gold for a serviceable Casino, with reinvestment making it "the jewel of the realms" over time. The talk turns to theater. Pow remembers the party's theater group, "headlined by that actor Bob, who was always in his demon costume," whose shows he saw in Yartar, and has heard that the Silverymoon theater burned down and Bob disappeared. Orsik explains that the theater did burn down but Bob has only taken a leave of absence. Thistle says they still have the script of the play; Vardis quietly reminds him it is a summoning ritual for a demon they barely averted. Pow is delighted: "Excellent! With Bob and his crew gone, we can use that script to bring in the crowds." He vouches for his hires as "trusted members of the town," though Thistle notes that they all appear to be from Shou and wonders to whom they are personally loyal.

While materials are loaded, Thistle asks Esclarotta what they can do for her. She blushes, and all the control gems pulsate red. She asks that the grass on the tower and the tower itself be cleaned — "It would be nice to have it clean!" — and Thistle shouts to Pow, "Our priority is to clean this tower." She adds a request for "some gold trim on the door with a nameplate," and Thistle tells Pow to "put that on your list."

On the day of departure, as the ship takes off, Unla Key asks Pow, "So if I gamble, can we set it up so I win?" Pow, as if explaining to a small child, answers, "Yes, but then we would lose money." Unla Key is unconvinced. Esclarotta sets the course for the Northern Isles of the Trackless Sea.
