## Identity
Methrammar Aerasumé is the High Marshal of Silverymoon and serves as the High Lord of the city. [ch 033 / 033.11; ch 048 / 048.02] He is the son of Lady Alustriel Silverhand [ch 055 / entry; ch 085 / entry; ch 086 / entry]. He also acts as the patron and producer of the party’s theatrical production. [ch 048 / 048.02; ch 055 / entry]

## Personality and Motivations
Aerasumé presents himself as a pragmatic and politically astute ruler who prioritizes stability and image over transparency. He is generous with funds for projects he deems successful, such as the party’s play, but guards financial details closely, refusing to disclose profit percentages. [ch 048 / 048.02; ch 055 / 055.06] He is defensive regarding the use of magic in Silverymoon, viewing criticism of it as treason, and has stated that magic has served the city for generations. [ch 055 / 055.06] He is deeply concerned with his reputation and political standing; when the play results in disasters, he shifts blame and seeks to control the narrative, offering aid only if Silverymoon’s involvement remains secret. [ch 085 / 085.09; ch 086 / 086.04] He values practical outcomes over moral absolutes, admitting he wishes he had found a scapegoat (a dragon) to simplify the chaos, and makes calculated concessions to maintain power and order. [ch 086 / 086.07]

## History with the Party
*   Methrammar Aerasumé, High Marshal (as identified by High Captain Horix Zoar's grudge), is mentioned by Dral Thelev as the reason Zoar broke the compact and dissolved the alliance with Everlund; Zoar has not forgiven him for abandoning his allies [ch 033 / 033.11].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé is the High Lord of Silverymoon (referred to as "High Lord Aerasumé"), who attends to the production budget of the party's play; he denies the requested 50,000 gold pieces, offering 1,000 gold and the free use of the Royal Theater instead [ch 048 / 048.02].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé is the producer of the play and the son of Lady Alustriel Silverhand; he praises the play's success and pays the party 12,000 gold pieces [ch 055 / entry; ch 085 / entry].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé threatens Thistle regarding his role in the finale, stating no actor small enough was found so a puppet was used [ch 055 / 055.06; ch 055 / moment].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé refuses to debate the risks of magic, stating "Magic has served Silverymoon for generations" [ch 055 / 055.06].
*   Unla Key notes that Methrammar Aerasumé "won’t be too happy" to learn a demon summoned zombies in his theater next to the royal palace; Lord Zymorven jokes about covering up his son's failings with Orsik, implying Aerasumé may be Orsik's father or simply sharing the sentiment of parental burden (evidence ambiguous on specific kinship, but links the two via "son's failings" comment) [ch 064 / 064.09].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé, son of Lady Alustriel Silverhand, is under political attack for being the patron of the play; the city is split between blaming him for trusting the party and blaming the party [ch 085 / entry; ch 085 / 085.09].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé, haggard and eating breakfast, greets the party with accusation: "You have caused nothing but disasters!" [ch 086 / entry; ch 086 / 086.02].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé confirms that 200 notables disappeared during the destruction of the Royal Theater and expresses confusion over the lack of memory regarding the play; he realizes the chaos is connected to the Giants when the party presents evidence against Iymrith [ch 086 / 086.02].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé agrees to attend the rescue ritual, though he is initially reluctant; he offers aid to the party conditional on Silverymoon's involvement being kept secret [ch 086 / entry; ch 086 / 086.04].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé dismisses House Margaster as "an irrelevant house" initially, but agrees to investigate them after Thistle implies they harbor Cambions and after Taern Hornblade supports the suspicion of corruption [ch 086 / 086.04].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé privately promises Thistle that the fairies will become a "goodly race of Silverymoon" in a few months, contingent on the party dealing with Iymrith [ch 086 / entry; ch 086 / 086.07].
*   Methrammar Aerasumé confesses he wished he had thought to blame a dragon, stating, "I am not fool enough to think it was the Dragon and wise enough not to care" [ch 086 / 086.07].
