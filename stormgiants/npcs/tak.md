## Identity
Tak is an ogre distinguished by a bandaged right hand [ch 059 / entry; ch 059 / 059.04]. He is the companion of another ogre, Worug [ch 059 / 059.04]. Tak was found alive in a barred tower within the cloud castle, suffering from starvation alongside Worug [ch 059 / 059.04]. He possesses knowledge of the castle's recent history, specifically regarding the vampire and the dracolich Glazhael, which he leverages as a bargaining chip [ch 059 / 059.04; ch 059 / 059.05].

## Personality and Motivations
Tak is driven primarily by hunger and the desire for survival [ch 059 / 059.04]. He is pragmatic enough to trade information for safety, promising to reveal a secret in exchange for being taken off the cloud castle with Worug [ch 059 / 059.04]. He appears to have a close, possibly romantic or deeply bonded, relationship with Worug, though he reacts defensively and "a little too eagerly" to Vardis’s suggestion of this connection, dismissing it as "ridiculous" [ch 059 / 059.04]. Tak is protective, as evidenced by his offer of the tower as a sanctuary for the party only after ensuring the safety of himself and Worug [ch 059 / 059.05].

## History with the Party
- Tak is an ogre with a bandaged right hand who, along with another ogre named Worug, was found alive and starving in a barred tower within the cloud castle [ch 059 / 059.04].
- Tak identified himself to the party by stating, "My name is Tak, and we are hungry," after Orsik asked if they spoke common [ch 059 / 059.04].
- When asked about their survival, Tak and Worug explained that the other ogres had let in a "purple lady" (the vampire), who then caused chaos and killed the rest of the castle's occupants [ch 059 / 059.04].
- Tak denied with "a little too eagerly" that he liked Worug when Vardis suggested a romantic connection between the two ogres [ch 059 / 059.04].
- Tak promised to reveal a secret if the party agreed to take both him and Worug off the castle [ch 059 / 059.04].
- Tak offered the tower they were hiding in as a refuge for the party, stating they would not leave and that the party could use the tower for rest and safety from the vampire [ch 059 / 059.05].

## Last Observed State
Tak was last seen in Chapter 059, inside a barred tower within the cloud castle [ch 059 / 059.04; ch 059 / 059.05]. After Worug revealed the presence of the dracolich Glazhael within the glacier, Tak stood with his arm around Worug and declared that they would remain in the tower [ch 059 / 059.05]. He offered the tower to the party as a place of rest and safety from the vampire, asserting that he and Worug would not leave [ch 059 / 059.05]. Status beyond this moment is not established in the summaries.

## Relationships
**Worug**: Tak is constantly paired with Worug, identified as the other ogre in the tower [ch 059 / 059.04]. Tak physically touches Worug (arm around him) and refers to them as a collective unit ("we are hungry," "get me and Worug off") [ch 059 / 059.04; ch 059 / 059.05]. When Vardis suggested Tak liked Worug, Tak denied it with visible haste, while Worug questioned why Tak hadn't spoken up, implying an established intimacy between them [ch 059 / 059.04].

**The Party (Orsik, Thistle, Vardis, Unla Key)**: Tak viewed the party initially with terror, screaming when their door was opened [ch 059 / 059.04]. The relationship shifted to cautious alliance after the party fed Tak and Worug Goodberries [ch 059 / 059.04]. Tak treats the party as potential transport off the castle, making promises in exchange for their intervention [ch 059 / 059.04]. He ultimately offers his tower as a refuge for the party, indicating a degree of trust or mutual dependency established during the encounter [ch 059 / 059.05].

**Blagothkus and the Vampire**: Tak, along with Worug, witnessed the chaos caused when other ogres let a "purple lady" (the vampire) into the castle [ch 059 / 059.04]. He observed Blagothkus crying and claiming to have killed his son, noting that Blagothkus subsequently holed up in the control room [ch 059 / 059.04]. Tak’s survival strategy relies on the vampire's inability to enter without invitation, a fact he shares with the party [ch 059 / 059.04].
