## Identity
Grindle is a massive ogre, almost 13 feet tall, who serves as Gralm's chosen champion [ch 015 / entry; ch 015 / 015.05].

## Personality and Motivations
Grindle speaks in broken, simplified language [ch 015 / entry; ch 015 / 015.05]. He is possessive or curious about small beings, specifically inquiring if the tiny Fairy was a "toy for me?" [ch 015 / 015.05]. He exhibits childish behavior, pouting when ordered away by Gralm and screaming "unfair! Unfair!" when defeated by the party's magical advantages rather than physical strength [ch 015 / entry; ch 015 / 015.05].

## History with the Party
- Grindle is introduced as a massive ogre almost 13 feet tall and Gralm's chosen champion [ch 015 / entry; ch 015 / 015.05].
- Grindle participated in the feats of strength and drinking demanded by Gralm to test the party's worth [ch 015 / 015.05].
- Grindle eyed the tiny Fairy and asked if the fairy was a "toy for me?", prompting Thistle to tell him "no touching" [ch 015 / 015.05].
- After the party defeated Gralm's other challenges, Grindle protested the results, screaming "unfair! Unfair!" [ch 015 / entry; ch 015 / 015.05].
- Grindle lost the contest against Unla Key, who used *reduce* to lift the boar and then destroyed a brick wall with a *thunder wave* [ch 015 / 015.05].

## Last Observed State
Grindle was present in Chapter 015, participating in Gralm's demanded contests of strength and drinking [ch 015 / 015.05]. He entered the contests after Gralm introduced him with the phrase "I Grindle" [ch 015 / 015.05]. He was ultimately defeated by Unla Key, who used *reduce* to win the boar dead lift and *thunder wave* to destroy the brick wall, prompting Grindle's protests [ch 015 / 015.05].

## Relationships
Grindle acts as the champion for Gralm [ch 015 / entry; ch 015 / 015.05]. Gralm holds authority over Grindle, ordering him back when Grindle expressed interest in Thistle [ch 015 / 015.05]. Grindle competed against Unla Key, Thistle, Orsik, Vardis, and Wallace [ch 015 / 015.05]. He interacted directly with Thistle, who told him "no touching" in response to his question about the Fairy [ch 015 / 015.05].
