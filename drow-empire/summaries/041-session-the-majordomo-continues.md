# Chapter 41 – The Majordomo Continues

Date: The Abyss, Day 98 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
The chapter opens mid-conversation with Ziamar's majordomo, who tells the party, "we know you were successful because, well, you emerged and not the Drow." KP shrugs and asks for a detailed analysis of the new inter-planar economic system that Ziamar has introduced to Plagueridge Rampart, hinting that the information is critical to the next part of Bayard's plan for the final defeat of the Drow empire. The majordomo is unfazed: "Knazreponnium, we knew that this would be an important piece of information for you." A detailed analysis of the new economic system has been provided on the second floor. He adds that "the abyssal demons are delighted to have you back in our realms and wish you a most pleasant stay," but that "Our Lord Ziamar, however, wishes to remind you that you should not enjoy your stay too much. Your defeat of the Drow Empire is the only thing that assures the continued safety."

As the majordomo is about to leave, KP blurts, "I need one more thing, how do I power this gem?" Holding the gem up, he notices that it glows, and the majordomo smiles malevolently. Later KP recalls something he read at the gnomish library in the Kron Hills: mention of demonic rituals that allowed abyssal power to be transferred from the Abyss to the prime material plane. He quickly concludes that the Abyss powered the gnomes, and that they had made themselves willing recipients or participants of the ritual involving Lolth. KP is frustrated, having hoped to have some of their cool powers; the rest of the group smirks.

KP asks for and receives directions to the best libraries. Bayard, annoyed at having to listen to KP's meandering analyses of inter-planar economics, asks for the name of any reasonable bar. When KP hints that he only wants to take a quick look, Bayard agrees to go with the gnome to the library and then for drinks, observing, "too much library is bad for the hero's health." The chapter explains that Bayard grew up in a part of the Wild Coast that was anti-intellectual and libertarian. Periodic invasions by unsophisticated humanoid tribes had often proven wrong the human armies who claimed superior intellect and battle tactics would be enough; the folk of the Wild Coast "believe in brioche and men whose brioche was buttered," had little patience for intellectuals who studied battle maps, tactics and grand strategy, did not believe in destiny, and believed only in "a strong sword arm and in the will of people to stand against the tide of destruction."

As the party is about to head out into the city, the majordomo blurts, "the only thing I ask is that you do not try or, more importantly, succeed in killing any demon." The group is confused, and Selzyt explains that demons killed on this plane are permanently destroyed. Bayard groans and mutters, "okay." The majordomo jumps in: "exactly! You are quite right, gem! And more importantly, Ziamar, will not be able to guarantee your safety." Bayard notes, "we don't kill unprovoked," and the majordomo replies that he was "just asked to convey Lord Ziamar's message. Consider this the conveyance of that message." KP, still confused, says, "I do not understand why demons would object to some chaos." The answer: "The demons do not care if you kill humans, goblins, gnomes, elves, or any other creature. What they care about is if you kill them! The city garrison is loyal to Ziamar, who is loyal to his father, Lord Freedahl." When KP asks how demons choose successors from their captains, the majordomo warns, "That is a very dangerous and treacherous conversation, gnome," then spews the party line: "as a territory is seized from Asmodeus in the endless blood war, a territory is given to the captains." With that he leaves. The right hand of Ziamar hands a note to KP, who ignores the letter.

The group steps outside into the dark gray night of the Abyss. Young hobgoblins throw a volley of rotten fruit and rocks and flee down a side alley. The gnome, with a wicked twinkle in his eye, remarks, "well, there is no proscription against killing hobgoblins!" Corrin notices more hobgoblins on the rooftops about to throw rocks, and his quick thinking keeps the party from getting hurt too badly, though Rimardo and Bayard are still flat-footed. Four hobgoblin slavers emerge from the shadows, and one immediately recognises Bayard: "Your tactical brilliance makes this a heartrending decision. Killing you is necessary. Your decision to free the slaves has severely impacted our standing within this community." Honor, he says, demands it. "You will die first," replies Bayard. KP tries to tempt them with promises of being the king of a kingdom of Giants; the answer is, "Your offer is tempting, but honor requires we kill you first. If the offer still stands after your death, we will consider it."

Corrin attacks first and severely wounds one hobgoblin before it can do anything but posture. KP considers a meteor swarm but, deciding the destruction of property may not be well received, blasts several hobgoblins with a cone of cold. The hobgoblins hit back hard: KP takes 18 HP of damage, then 26; Corrin, flanked, takes 30 points of damage and then another 26. Bayard kills the injured slaver with his second attack, Rimardo's toll the dead inflicts 17 points of damage, and Corrin kills the hobgoblin who attacked him. KP casts a Ray of Frost from close range; a slaver bashes the gnome prone and hits him for a total of 14 points of damage, and Corrin too is knocked prone and takes 14 HP of damage. Bayard nearly kills another — "And there is no joy in the upper planes." Rimardo casts toll the dead again, Corrin inflicts 53 points of damage, Bayard kills one more hobgoblin, and Rimardo does 13 HP of damage. When KP casts a Ray of Frost, the hobgoblin counterspells, then drinks a potion of healing. Corrin is perplexed and Rimardo wonders why the hobgoblin will not surrender, but Bayard understands: death before dishonor. The hobgoblin sees oblivion as preferable to being defeated a second time. Rimardo finds it all interesting and silly and wonders why the hobgoblins do not just form a gang; Bayard knows that for a hobgoblin, to live without honor is to not live. Rimardo shrugs. Bayard kills the last one and recognises a monster that is with honor but whose choices in life did not make it beyond reproach — "Perhaps this monster had a brioche that was not heavily buttered."

On the hobgoblins the party finds a pair of black pearls and a large yellow diamond. KP's obsession with market forces in inter-planar politics has begun to rub off on Rimardo, who wonders why monsters in this city run around with such valuable gems in their pockets, and whether it is a side effect of the current ruling establishment. A city where demonic half-human spawn rule with demons as city guards is not unusual; hobgoblins carrying precious gems is, and the mystery perplexes him.

A group of agitated humanoids in chain mail rapidly approaches: the former Rangers of Plagueridge Rampart, to whom the party owes some money. Bayard steps forward to negotiate, and Rimardo makes it clear that any cash will come from Bayard's pockets, not his. The Ranger's spokesman accuses "Mr. double-dealing Bayard" and demands, "Where is our money?" Bayard stares blankly. The Ranger explains that they used to be respected members of authority in this town: "We were goodberry farmers." People feared them and needed their berries or died of hunger, "But Ziamar has decided to pursue a different cultivation technique that does not require our services." Ziamar refuses to meet them, and they are convinced it is because of "some prior arrangement he made with you." Bayard shakes his head: "what is all this nonsense? What makes you think that?" The Rangers have been reduced to the fringes of society, sleeping on the street and pursuing odd jobs. "Well, you can work?" Bayard says; as a member of the Libertarian Party of the Wild Coast, he believes that any form of assistance harms the person receiving it. Asked what they would do with the money, the Ranger answers, "Why I would go to another plane." Rimardo points out that "getting to a new plane doesn't require money," and the Ranger replies, "Our pain-and-suffering and our humiliation do. And furthermore, there is the small matter of back wages." Bayard, thinking them moochers, says, "we understand your pain. That does not mean we intend to do anything about it."

Rimardo notes that the party is used to killing monsters that have money, not penniless ones. Bayard objects to paying as a matter of honor, calling it a shakedown, but Rimardo makes the point that they did promise the money, and Bayard lets him take the lead. The hobgoblin rangers say it is not about numbers but about the capitalization of the benevolent fund of the rangers, "commonly known as the pension fund," and after more waffling they request 96,000 gold pieces. Rimardo laughs: "we don't have that kind of money." The Ranger is shocked — "You are adventurers! You're supposed to be pillaging the realms!" Rimardo explains that such money would be kept at a financial institution requiring "two forms of identification and the letter of credit," with the risk of "some time drift that will expire the letter of credit." Persuaded, the Rangers ask whether they have a letter of credit. Rimardo nods and proposes, "we want 20% down payment, so on 96,000 gold pieces, that means we get 18,000." Bayard shakes his head, and Rimardo tries again: "how about 10,000 down and a letter of credit for 150?" KP whispers, "can I just cast plane shift so that we can get over with this?" The Ranger says yes, as does Bayard. Inspecting the line of credit, the Ranger asks, "your money is kept by the cleric Lundbeams?" Rimardo nods, thinking that perhaps these Rangers can deal with their problem cleric.

The group decides to head back to the Inn with the gnomish stew. On the street six haggard halflings in explorer outfits approach, saying they are freed slaves who have no idea where the Drow was sending them, and wishing to present Bayard with a token of their appreciation. The lead halfling says, "let us present you with this a gift, O noble Bayard!" and drops the bag. Rimardo notices just in time and dodges the explosion, as does Bayard, while Corrin smiles in approval at "the rare double-bag assassination ploy." Another halfling screams, "what have we done? What have we done?!"

The explosion attracts four demonic vrocks: "It would appear that you are disturbing the peace." Rimardo looks relieved — "finally, we meet the people who are supposed to be taking care of our city. It wasn't us; it was the halflings." But the vrocks announce, "Lord Ziamar has declared that your actions have disrupted the peace," and order the party to drop their weapons and come to his palace. Rimardo is confused, since this is his city — technically Bayard's, as leader, but close enough. Corrin makes it clear the vrocks do not agree, and Rimardo concludes that Ziamar has decided to make a play for Bayard's city. The vrocks attack. Corrin strikes first, critically hitting the lead vrock, and dodges its hellish rebuke. Another vrock's screech stuns KP, and a vrock throws a bead of force that captures the stunned gnome. Rimardo heals Corrin; another vrock hits Corrin three times for 48 points of damage, and Corrin "smiles the smug smile of someone who has dodged death just long enough to be killed by another vrock." KP stares at the world around him. The demons use their spores to no effect. Bayard kills one of the demons, then turns and hits another vrock for 35 points of damage, where the chapter ends.
