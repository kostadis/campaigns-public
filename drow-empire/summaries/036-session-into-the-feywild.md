# Chapter 36 – Into the Feywild

Date: The Feywild, Day 1 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
Having resolved things with the dwarves of Brungril Forge, the party has only a few hours before the portal continues its path to the next realm subjugated by the Drow Empire. The Failsafe, Selzyt, has told them that the next realm is the Feywild. He has little information about the structure or strength of the Drow presence there, but the previous portal landings make it clear that "violence is apt to accompany your arrival."

When KP learns the destination, he ponders, then perks up: he remembers a book he read when he worked in the great library. Its author, Ronbar Dorhik, is famous for his travels across the planes, and it was this sage's works "on the importance of inter-planar trade as a possible explanation for the emergence of godlike powers" that inspired KP's obsession with the nature of trade in the Drow Empire. KP is excited that the book will now be of use, and the narration calls it "a personal vindication of his obsession with the nature of the planes." While he reminisces, everyone else stares at him waiting for him to say something.

Finally KP delivers his lecture. The Feywild, also called the Plane of Faerie, is "a land of soft lights and wonder, a place of music and death" — a realm of everlasting twilight under an ever-setting sun that never truly sets. Away from the settled areas ruled by the Seelie fey of the Summer Court, the land is "a tangle of sharp-toothed brambles and syrupy fens," perfect territory for the Unseelie fey to hunt their prey. The Feywild exists in parallel to the Material Plane and mirrors its landscape in spectacular forms, and it is home to sylvan creatures and, in its darker regions, to hags, blights, goblins, ogres and giants. Two queens hold court there: Queen Titania and her Summer Court lead the Seelie fey, and the Queen of Air and Darkness, ruler of the Gloaming Court, leads the Unseelie. Their opposition "stems from their queens’ jealous rivalry, not abstract moral concerns." KP then paints a vivid picture of a forest where the colors are saturated and everything is "just a little bit more vibrant and dangerous and off than it should be."

With little left to do but wait, KP and Rimardo discuss their preparations and Corrin pours Drow poison on his blades. The ground shakes, then stops. The group waits for the guardians of the portal; they hear burrowing, and "Like prairie dogs, four quaggoths emerge from the ground." Rimardo summons the Griffin and calls down "the fire and the fury of Istus in the form of a flame strike," but "she was neither furious or fiery," and two of the quaggoths take only 20 points of damage. The quaggoths' fighting style makes it impossible for Rimardo, Corrin and Bayard to attack the quaggoth that attacked them in melee. Corrin disengages and kills the wounded quaggoth that attacked Rimardo, and the Griffin swipes another for eight points of damage while Rimardo and Corrin cheer. Now that the room is no longer full of ex-slave allies, KP is free to use his fireball, sculpting it around his allies so that the whole room bursts into flames.

Bayard, "whose training is both honorable and classical," cannot hit the monster that attacked him, so he steps away and strikes another. "This is not a retreat. This is a tactical maneuver." The quaggoth he leaves behind hits him for six. His honor is rewarded: his second swing is a critical and should kill the monster, "except that some annoying Drow spell caster cast death ward on this monster," and it lives with one meager hit point.

Rimardo prepares to cast word of radiance, but Istus warns him that the spell will no longer have the range she blessed him with; it will revert to its usual range of 5 feet. The goddess had intervened, it is unclear why, and now she has withdrawn her boon. Followers of Istus believe she punishes those who do not recognize and give thanks for her interventions. Corrin is furious and Rimardo is embarrassed; both will need to think hard about how they relate to their patron. Instead of offering prayers, Rimardo shrugs and casts toll the dead on the one-hit-point quaggoth. It is not affected. "Istus is pleased."

It gets worse: that same quaggoth hits Bayard for 13 and KP for 24. "Even the goddess of fate gets bored": when the quaggoth tries to rend Bayard's armor, Bayard rips its arm off his armor. Another monster tries to frighten KP; the paladin's aura of courage protects the gnome from fear but not from 18 points of claw damage. Corrin kills one wounded quaggoth, though his second attack on the death-warded one is parried. The Griffin critically hits a quaggoth and kills it. KP casts thunder wave on one of the remaining quaggoths for 13 points; Bayard walks up and misses; Rimardo kills the last quaggoth with the cantrip.

As the group prepares to go through the tunnels, Rimardo asks Selzyt whether the Drow have established an empire in the Feywild. Selzyt says no: "the Drow do not create empires," they send troops through the portal to plunder and steal. KP, drawing on his study of inter-planar economics, asks, "but an empire or local proxies would make plunder easier?" Selzyt agrees that a proxy is a more efficient way to plunder, and cites the party's own plane, where "a mayor and his beautiful wife, the cleric Lundbeams allied themselves with the Drow." Rimardo replies that the mayor was blackmailed, and that the cleric claimed to be protecting some artifact and playing both sides, "but I think the simpler answer is that she is evil." Bored, he enters the tunnel. Selzyt harrumphs and reminds the party that Tathdax, the Drow Governor-General in these parts, is not particularly loyal to the Empire: "He’s gone native."

At the end of the tunnel is a storeroom of chests full of foodstuffs and various goods from the feywild, with four pools of water. The group finds a glowing tube with gold writing on it; KP examines it carefully but does not open the scroll tube. At an intersection, Bayard heads east and finds a toilet. He goes west and finds a room with eight dirty beds too big for humans, with claw marks — the quaggoths' quarters — where among their valuables is a set of wings made of gold. Heading south, KP and Corrin both notice an explosive rune, and KP dispels it. Bayard advances to the door at the end of the hallway; KP casts dispel magic on the door, and nothing happens. The great hero opens it with KP right behind him, and four Drow ambush the gnome and the Paladin.

Bayard is surrounded by magical darkness, struck for 17 points of damage, and hit by a hand crossbow bolt "coated in Drow poison" that does not affect him. KP casts sunbeam: one Drow is blinded, and the enemies are wounded and at a disadvantage. A Drow casts Faerie Fire on Bayard and KP, who are unaffected. One enemy flanks KP and hits him for 17 HP of damage, but KP's concentration is not disturbed. The chapter ends there, mid-fight.
