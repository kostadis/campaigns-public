# Chapter 34 – Attack on Damrith

Date: Brungril Forge, Day 1 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
The party's attempt to surprise the duergar has failed, so combat begins. KP opens the door and kills a duergar with a fire bolt. Rimardo remembers at the last moment that the Drow and their allies have counterspells and decides to just sit there, but Corrin sings to him, and he swings his mace instead for 14 points of damage. The duergar answers with hellish rebuke for 12. The duergar expand in size, going "from small ugly dwarfs to giant nasty dwarves." Corrin exploits a spent hellish rebuke to kill one, then deals "two measly points of damage" to the next and dodges its rebuke. Bayard misses both swings, and Corrin sings to the tune of a nursery rhyme, "You missed, you missed, you swung your sword and missed." When a duergar tries to rebuke the Paladin, "the Paladin cannot be censured for he is without reproach and has no fear."

KP hides and kills a duergar with a spell from hiding. Rimardo's spell is countered; Corrin kills that dwarf. Bayard takes 11 points of damage, then nine from a javelin — "death by a thousand cuts," he remarks; Rimardo: "you will be fine." A duergar tosses a jar full of spores at Corrin and Rimardo, who are unaffected. Surrounded by two duergar, Bayard kills one with two blows. KP's fire bolt and Rimardo's toll the dead are both countered, and Corrin kills that duergar. Duergar count to three and toss their holey jars like grenades; Bayard is unaffected both times, but KP is stunned until he shakes off the spores. One duergar tries to escape and Bayard's blade catches only air; Bayard kills the one he is still fighting, and Corrin kills the last duergar with his bow.

Marco runs in: "you have killed the duergar and freed the slaves." KP and Rimardo both notice that Marco's accent has changed. Corrin reminds Bayard that killing the slaves is also a viable option — all Bayard needs to do is sing a song — but Bayard is not tempted and orders their freeing. An ex-slave grabs Marco by the shoulders — "you live!" — and Marco announces, "the Drow Empire has been defeated. Now is our chance to free ourselves of the Drow!" When KP asks Rimardo, "the Drow Empire is defeated?", Marco is outraged: "Gnome, how many times do I have to explain to you that certain facts do not need to be shared?" He believes the Paladin will defeat the Drow Empire, and muses whether every clock on every plane is synchronized, before asking the gnome, "can you just say yes?" KP: "Yes!"

Marco asks Bayard what to do next. Asked whether releasing the slaves would cause a commotion that would let them find the true Thane, the answer comes: "It would create a great disturbance!" The slaves run down the corridor, make a great ruckus, and overwhelm the doors to the vast marketplace. Marco admits the plan "had an unforeseen consequence": "Well, we are unlikely to surprise Damrith." A dwarf on a box in the North-Eastern corner of the marketplace bellows that Damrith and his supporters would ally with outsiders to rule over the Dwarves, points straight at the Paladin, and shouts, "but I would rather be a slave to Damrith than a slave to more outsiders!" The group tells Marco it is time to go talk to Fonouc.

Then Fonouc shouts, "what happened to you, Marco? Why would you try to kill me? Did you come back from the abyss just to kill me?" He grabs Marco's sword hand and shakes the sword loose: "this must be some kind of demonic possession!" Rimardo casts hold person on both, and Fonouc's allies surround the party and the two dwarves. KP takes charge of the investigation: "Marco, what are you doing?" — "I dinna know." Fonouc shouts that Marco is "my brother, my friend, my ally!" and blames the party. KP thinks for a moment and casts dispel magic on Marco, who begins speaking with yet another accent, that of a Dwarven Lord: "I have been speaking with this terrible pronunciation for the last several months." The Drow had cast a spell on him to compel him to escape and come here and kill Fonouc, believing his death would end the resistance, and knowing Marco was the only person who could get close enough. "I am so sorry."

KP tells Fonouc, "as the rightful king of this land, I will remand Marco into your justice." Fonouc: "It would be easier if I wasn’t paralyzed." Rimardo releases him. "And there is nothing to judge. Marco was a victim of the Drow, like every dwarf in Brungril Forge." Fonouc asks Bayard whether he has anything to say. "I have nothing to say." For Bayard is a man of action, not a man of words. Prodded for a heroic rallying cry, Bayard replies, "It goes without saying." Fonouc is won over: "Finally, a hero who doesn’t spew the nonsense that is expected of heroes. I finally trust you, Bayard. And I trust your companions." He will enter Damrith's Hall and declare himself the true Thane, "but you must do the fighting and killing." Rimardo asks if he has any soldiers, gold, magic items, "perhaps even a catapult?"; Fonouc promises only that "we have the finest smithies in all the realms," who will happily sell their finest wares once he is Thane.

At the main hall, Fonouc opens the door to Damrith's Hall: "Damrith, you traitor you allied yourself with the Drow and partnered with the duergar, your time as Thane has come to an end." Inside, many dwarves eat at a large table, served by dwarf servants, and in the middle of the room stands Damrith, surrounded by several Dwarven knights. Fonouc walks calmly in, only his boots sounding on the stone tiles: "I am the Thane, I was deposed by this imposter who allied himself with our hated enemies, but his time here is over." Damrith waves a frantic finger: "Guards! Guards!" Fonouc dashes outside before Damrith can kill him, and the party steps in.

Rimardo considers killing the many dwarves in the way so that KP can use his fireballs. They profit from the regime and implement Damrith's wishes, but in the legalistic tradition among the cultists that worship Istus, obeying the law regardless of the law is the highest form of worship; he realizes the bureaucrats are, in their own way, followers of Istus, and since they have no weapons and pose no threat, he decides not to kill them all.

Corrin dashes forward and, through sheer luck, finds a crack in a Knight's armor for 34 points of damage. The knights surround him: one strikes for 10, another flanks and hits critically for 40, another's greatsword clangs on the stone, and the last slashes him for 20. Rimardo's word of radiance does only five points of damage — Istus does not smile on him — and he takes 20 from a Knight. Corrin survives and smiles, because the dwarves have surrounded him, "making them easy pickings for the gnomish mage."

KP enters, notices that the dwarves' armor is special, and recalls that the Drow and their allies seem curiously immune to fire, so he readies a cone of cold. Before casting, he wonders, "is it okay to kill the civilians?" He remembers a campfire discussion in which Bayard said, "War is a terrible thing, and sometimes they just are killed," and Rimardo laughed that "the expectation cannot be that a magic-user will spare your life in the middle of the battle." KP concludes that although he does not have to protect the innocent, he chooses to. He sculpts the spell to avoid collateral damage; the cone of cold deals 44 points of freezing damage to all the Knights and kills Damrith. "The usurper is frozen into a solid block of ice."

Bayard charges in, dodging one Knight and cutting another for 26. Corrin disengages and finds his arrows bouncing off the knights' armor; he remembers the party once had magical arrows but decided to trade them. The praetorian guard converges on KP for 20 and then 30 points of damage. Rimardo casts divine word, killing one Knight and blinding another, and takes 20 points of damage. KP's lightning bolt scorches the dwarves for 37, killing two. Corrin smiles: "parley?" The remaining three knights drop their weapons, and one sheepishly points to the door to the North: "you will have to kill the Drow in that room."
