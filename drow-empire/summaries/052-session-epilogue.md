# Chapter 52 – Epilogue

Date: 15 Fireseek 596 CY (596-01-15; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
The heroes arrive in the city of Fax. "It has been several months. And things have changed." The local police are absent, entering the town no longer requires a bribe if you look tougher than the guard at the gate, and signs everywhere announce that "the Lord Mayor Tolub, the Captain of Fax is in charge." Rimardo decides to talk to Tolub, wondering aloud, "Is he divorced? Is his daughter okay?" At the bridge to the Captain's residence, a pair of guards stop them. "Tolub knows who we are. We saved his daughter." The guards are unsure until the party says, "We are the independent contractors. Tolub will know." "are you the folks that got rid of the Drow back in the day?" "That’s us!" blurts KP. Bayard, full of his tactical brilliance, swaggers, "Is that any way to treat us?" The guards are delighted: since the Drow left, the city is back to normal: "Crime is up! Honorable deeds are down! Corruption is rampant!" KP snarks, "Corruption is the new black!" and Rimardo chimes in, "Just like old times!" State finances are so bad that Tolub plans raids for gold, starting with "some orcish tribes to the south," plus other villages along the way, "no point in letting gold go to waste!" When will they leave? "When Tolub decides, probably when we run out of money." KP admires the honesty and candor of the locals.

The Captain's tower lacks the finish and polish it had under the Drow Xhalh, and the guards look slovenly. Tolub is playing cards with his cronies in the yard, and winning. Drunk, he recognizes "the independent contractors," and when KP says they finished their contract, he panics: "I didn’t sign no contract, that was my wife." Her "crazy ass dealings with the liberation front" are her business, not his. Asked about her 20-year search for the perfect adventurers, he groans: "When you are married to a powerful cleric of Heironeous in possession of a reality destroying artifact, you learn to accommodate yourself to certain of their obsessions." It is, he insists, "of course" a happy marriage, though "The last few yahoos who showed up and had failed didn’t have a good end." KP smugly says they fulfilled the terms of the contract, and Corrin is pleased. "How did you get rid of Lolth?" asks a shocked Tolub. "How dare you question our abilities," says the man with butter on his brioche; "We got help from another god," says Rimardo. "Are you people mad?" "Yes," says KP. Tolub describes his marriage: "we are a power couple. She has the power, and we are a couple. She’s scary." Rimardo wishes him well with his raid, and Tolub admits he has been trying to talk the locals out of "the raid economy." KP offers interplanar trade; the drunk captain stares.

Then Tolub breaks down. "Lundbeams, was a good woman. And then that damned dagger corrupted her!" Rimardo begins to feel sad, realizing Lundbeams is a victim. The former river pirate sobs that after 20 years of marriage he worked out her secret ("it was more like 1+1+1+1+1"), told her to have nothing to do with it, and she laughed at him. She has been protecting the dagger and sending people off to kill those who are after it. "If the gods want to kill each other, why should we get in the way?" He remembers when she was sheriff and he was mayor and "We cleaned up towns." He does not think the dagger corrupted her, but "the idea of the dagger corrupted her": she became obsessed that only she could decide how its power was used. "Lolth was looking for the dagger to kill Corellon. Iuz wanted it to kill St. Cuthbert." "It was the god slayer." KP remarks, "we didn’t need it." Rimardo asks where it is, and Tolub, knowing the cleric wants the power, says, "she keeps it in the altar," warning, "you do not want that dagger." He begs them to tell her to get rid of it and become again "that ruthless law and order person that I fell in love with, man." She is in the temple with the dagger, the archmages and the guards. "maybe now that Lolth is dead, she’ll abandon the dagger?" "Yeah, I think so. We’ll go tell her," says an awkward Rimardo.

On the way, a former member of the WLF stops them: "Wait, you are the independent contractors? You’re alive?" "She betrayed us all." "All she cared about was finding you to fulfill the final contract." He urges, "You’ve got to kill her. You’ve got to rip her throat out," because "She set you up as patsies." "Not the first time this happened to us," says KP, and Rimardo is "not sure I should take it personally." The WLF'er calls them cowards, but declines to come along: "No, I am too scared of her!" When Rimardo taunts, "What was your name again?", the fleeing man shouts back, "KP." KP is confused.

The temple has sturdy wooden doors and stained glass windows. Rimardo proposes walking in, "after all, we’re friends." Inside are guards, powerful clerics, and a mage beside Lundbeams, who toys with a green gem around her neck. "The cleric of Istus lives!" she cries. When Rimardo advances nonchalantly, she stops him: "Stop where you are, Mr. Rimardo, you don’t need to come any closer." The guards take flanking positions. Rimardo protests that they completed the mission: "The artifact is safe for all time from Lolth! You can finally rest! Your 20-year search is over, congratulations." "I know about your deals with Vhaeraun," she replies; a different god told her. "I guess the word does get around in the god community." She says they have disrupted the balance of power among the gods. "that’s us, God Slayers!" says KP, and she corrects him: "You are not god slayers. You are god entrappers. You relied on a magical trinket to imprison her." The chapter explains the rivalry between the god slayers and the entrappers: dead gods reform but do not seek revenge, while entrapped ones do, "a bit of “catch and release” vs. “kill”" going on.

She calls them heroes, worthy of praise and of payment from the teller outside. Rimardo presses: the town has fallen on hard times, yet she pays them? "The gold belongs to the cult, not to the town. It’s here to protect the dagger." "that drunk Tolub has let it rot!" Told he has a drinking problem, she looks sad: "yes, he does." "The dagger happened!" she says of their marriage: "I was the only one standing between chaos and peace." When Rimardo points out anyone could steal the dagger, she says only Tolub and the party know it is here, with real threat in her voice. Rimardo admits he told Istus; KP asks about "that poster I put up around town"; Rimardo adds that they told the townsfolk too. "Everybody knows about the dagger." Tired, fiddling with the gem, she says, "We will have to kill everyone in this town, starting with you four." Corrin is excited. "Talk about shooting the messenger," says Rimardo, while Corrin wonders whether he needs to kill Rimardo first. Lundbeams speaks of the many agents of evil and good, occultists and treasure hunters she has had to kill. "Calm down," says KP, and she stops.

"I won’t kill everyone." She will take her drunken husband and hide again, because "I love him dearly, even if he is a drunk." It all went wrong, she sobs, when she became one of Heironeous’ top clerics and he felt inadequate; she set him up as mayor of Fax to help him off the bottle, but then the Drow Empire came, he could not protect their daughter, and "He feels he can do nothing." "Bayard is a licensed marriage counselor," observes KP, and Bayard, who has been preparing a final strike, is stunned that KP knew about his work in Juste-Havre. "Of course," Bayard says to helping them. Lundbeams offers him the guardianship: "Mr. Bayard, perhaps you wish to be the guardian of the dagger?" Someone answers, "We can shoulder the problem," and Rimardo tells Bayard, "we have come to the end of our business relationship," but Lundbeams clarifies: "I meant you, Bayard. Istus has other plans for Rimardo and Corrin." She offers "the dagger and the organization," for he has "the necessary strength of character, and moral ambiguity to do the necessary thing." "I would call it pragmatism and not moral ambiguity." "Pragmatic goodness?" "Yes." She gives Bayard the dagger. "all of these minions are ours?" asks Rimardo; the archmage answers, "No, Mr. Bayard’s." The temple disappears, its existence having depended on a pact between Heironeous and Lundbeams. "This mess and that dagger are yours," she says, and marches off.

Rimardo raises "the small problem of justice," and KP that Tolub is a criminal with thugs to be dealt with; Rimardo answers KP, "Feel free," and tells Corrin, "we got our money." Lundbeams, who had not left, congratulates Bayard: "now it’s your turn to find independent contractors to fend off the hordes of demonic and angelic powers that seek the dagger. Your problem, not mine." KP wonders about destroying the artifact; Rimardo says, "whatever. We are done. We got our money. Good luck," and he and Corrin collect their fee from the treasury. Bayard calls them good companions; "Mercenaries!" "Yes, mercenaries." His new minions "will take a sword for me." "Bayard is pleased. He finally has the party he deserves." KP is disappointed that the portal is closed, until he remembers he is powerful enough to create his own. When Rimardo suggests legend lore on the dagger, the archmage speaks to Bayard, who declares, "That is the order’s business." Bayard will build "The Knights of the Dagger," with the dagger as its central relic. KP muses that he should catch up with Istus's followers, who "understand the value of money," then concludes that writing a book is still important: "Imagine the trade deals I could make selling my book to interplanar communities?"

A few years later, Tolub is sober; he and Lundbeams have resolved their issues, she is a sheriff again, and Fax "has become a model of order and safety on the Wild Coast." Tolub has become an advocate for the rights of women, and Lundbeams continues to make amends. Bayard's secret order guards the dagger, perhaps near Juste-Havre; its knights wear a dagger on their wrists, kill anyone who asks too many questions, and always eat butter with their brioche. KP is on the interplanar lecture circuit, with a famous publishing arm and school. Istus had planned to make Corrin and Rimardo semi-divine avatars of her order, but they set up a magic show instead, famous across Oerth as *Rimardo and Corrin*. "Istus smiled. Free will, she said to anyone who would listen, does exist." And Gruneel, forgotten guarding a door in Plagueridge Rampart, found a magic user to plane shift him to the Wasted Isle, went back to the village of Alaneth, found a common bond with the lizardfolk, and became a member of the tribe, perhaps even adopted into one of the local clans.
