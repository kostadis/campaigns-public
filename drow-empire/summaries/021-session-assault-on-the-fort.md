# Chapter 21 – Assault on the Fort

Date: 1 Goodmonth 595 CY (595-08-01; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
After much debate, Bayard and his "merry gang of mercenary heroes and assassins" settle on a plan to assault the castle while the bulk of the Drow forces and the Faxian army are busy defending against a surprise attack on their city walls. The party will arrive quietly on the island, climb the bluffs to a plateau, and from there sneak into the castle and take down the forces within. KP tells Bayard and Gruneel that KP and Corrin will enter the fortress and that Rimardo, Bayard, and Gruneel will charge later. "KP is quite clear; there will be a signal."

At 9:00 AM KP and Corrin go in. Corrin, "like an avenging angel of death," hides, sneaks, and assassinates guard after guard, while KP uses the chaos to move to the wall closest to the beach. At 9:10 AM Corrin is finally spotted. KP casts a lightning bolt that kills a pair of clerics, ducks into a room, and is barricaded in by the guards; he smiles and casts a passwall, confident that the party will have seen and heard the lightning bolt and will see the hole in the castle wall. The lightning bolt and the hole are the signal for Bayard, Gruneel, and Rimardo to attack.

"But, no." At 9:05, while Corrin and KP were still sneaking, Gruneel and Bayard had grown bored — "Very bored. Very, very, very bored. And worried. Very worried." Rimardo begged them not to go, but Gruneel was anxious to fight and Bayard was miserable just hiding. Gruneel pointed out that if their friends needed help it would take a long time to get there. Gruneel started to run, Bayard decided the time for hiding was at an end and charged, and Rimardo shrugged and followed. The heroes charged across the plateau in their massive, shiny armor to help friends who did not need help.

Their noise alerted the knights on the bridge that connected the isle to Fax; the knights alerted the guards, and the guards fired arrows at Bayard. The knights charged the party. Bayard and the gang had a head start, but the knights had war horses. So when KP fires the lightning bolt at 9:10, the entire fort already knows the castle is under assault and that reinforcements are coming. Yet Bayard's attack splits the castle's forces: torn between a magic user and an assassin trapped in rooms and two fighters and a cleric charging at them, the defenders leave KP and Corrin alone and focus on Bayard. "Huh, it turns out that Bayard’s heroic decision to charge across an open field was the right decision."

The party enters the fortress through the hole in the wall. The guards spot the entryway and move to surround the heroes, which is precisely the moment KP was waiting for: he unleashes a fireball. The guards still will not flee, inspired by the arrival of the Knights, until Rimardo paralyzes the Knights with a hold person spell. Corrin, Bayard, KP, "and not Gruneel" kill a pair of the paralyzed knights and several of the remaining guards. Unimpressed by Gruneel's archery, Rimardo mutters to himself, "Is there some door you can guard?"

With only one Knight and a few guards left, a distinguished old man rushes into the courtyard screaming, "Enough, enough bloodshed." Rimardo, Corrin, KP, and Gruneel are not interested in a parley, but Bayard is, and asks, "who are you, and what do you want?" The man is Tolub, Captain of Fax. He rants that he has spent too much time cowering in his castle — once "a feared pirate king, a lord mayor, the vanquisher of the temple of elemental evil, and the scourge of the Nyr Dyv" whose name the Wild Coast feared — and that he will not watch more men die because his wife forbids action. Asked by Bayard why he waited so long, Tolub explains that "the Drow leader Xhalh has my daughter, and my wife Lundbeams has demanded we take no action and serve the Drow. She is an immensely powerful cleric, and her wrath is, well, not fun." Rimardo smirks, KP laughs, Bayard sighs, Gruneel is confused because he has no idea who Lundbeams is, and Corrin sharpens his knives.

Tolub's plan is to run out and announce that Xhalh is defeated, trusting his unhappy troops to turn on the Drow — and only then does he ask, "you will kill Xhalh?" Rimardo starts angling for a reward, but Bayard declares, "the honor of Juste-Havre demands Xhalh’s death." Annoyed, Rimardo asks where Xhalh is. Tolub sketches out the area and warns of metal constructs in the antechamber and quaggoths that can cast spells in Xhalh's chamber.

Rimardo sighs and begins the ritual to ask Istus for the power to cast an earthquake: why bother killing Xhalh when he can bring the fortress down? Tolub freaks out — "you can’t destroy my castle!" — and, as Rimardo continues, pleads that killing Xhalh this way means they will never find his daughter. Rimardo wonders why the very powerful cleric of Heironeous can't find the daughter herself; Tolub, sensing the thought, says "apparently our daughter is too well hidden from her scrying." When Rimardo groans, "so we can’t kill Xhalh?", Tolub slowly forms the evil grin that once chilled people as a pirate and says, "oh, you can kill Xhalh. I know a cleric who will be happy to bring him back to life many, many, many times over." He cannot have them destroy Xhalh's chambers, because there may be a clue there to where his daughter is.
