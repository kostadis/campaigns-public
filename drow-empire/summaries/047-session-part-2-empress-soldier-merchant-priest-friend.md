# Chapter 47 – Part 2 Empress, Soldier, Merchant, Priest, Friend

Date: The Abyss, Day 150 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
Drow mages surround the party, and in the distance stands the Drow general Xaren, whom Corrin recently assassinated, come for revenge with new scarring on his face. "I will not be surprised this time, assassin," he hisses. "This time, I will emerge victoriously!" When Rimardo wonders, "where are our allies of House Illal?", the general replies that "there is no House Illal in the open sector"; here the noble houses do not care who lives and dies, and would abandon the party and their plots if it died. Asked what house he belongs to, he answers, "You will find out when we kill you and raise you for the 33rd time." His mages look bored, wearing the look "that every employee has when they are hearing their bosses pep talk for the hundred thousandth time."

Corrin fires a single arrow for 76 points of damage, a blow that should kill. "However, not this time." Xaren gets up, dusts himself off, and a flock of owls casts spells that restore him. He points a gem-shaped magical item at the halfling and casts hold person, but Corrin, under the influence of foresight, is unaffected: "Really?! REALLY!?! Magic users, kill this halfling!" KP walks past a mage into an open courtyard and lets it hit him with its staff, knowing it can then no longer use its counterspell, and turns invisible. Bayard slashes a mage for 31 HP; Corrin takes a flurry of magic missiles for 45 points of damage; a mage casts Evard’s Black Tentacles, which KP moves through; Rimardo quaffs a potion of healing. Corrin's plan is to kill Xaren once to trigger his death ward and let KP finish him with lightning, but Xaren recovers through his death ward, 12 owls (familiars, clearly) heal him, and he shoots Corrin and hides in the shadows out of KP's line of sight.

KP's lightning bolt cuts through three mages instead; one dies and triggers a death ward, and the other two are badly hurt. Bayard kills the mage the death ward revived, and the other is killed and revived by another death ward. The mages answer with more black tentacles, a cloud kill (its caster misty-stepping to a roof), and a lightning bolt through the cloud that electrocutes Rimardo for 33 HP and Bayard and KP for 16 HP. Trapped in the tentacles and breathing the fumes, Rimardo uses his gem of true seeing and finds no owls anywhere. He fumes at Istus that surely there must be one visible owl; she grants him a vision of owls that can fly great distances and enter and leave the combat area. Rimardo sighs. Xaren has prepared well for this. But the cleric has prepared too. He misty-steps to a roof and casts divine word, killing one mage, triggering another's death ward, and deafening the third.

Corrin escapes the tentacles, climbs a roof, kills a mage, and leaves the uninjured one barely alive with 42 HP of damage. Frustrated that "His plan is not going according to plan," Xaren sneaks up on Rimardo for 54 Hp of damage, and owls heal him further. KP, entangled, "breathes in deeply, savoring the poison," misty-steps to a roof, and uses magic missile to trigger the injured mage's death ward; Bayard kills the revived mage, leaving Xaren alone. "Damn you!" he screams. Rimardo heals the party, Corrin bounds across the roofs and hits him, and Xaren disengages ("I shall have my revenge!"), jumps to the ground and slithers into a sewer. He has escaped again, and Rimardo and Corrin are dissatisfied.

The party decides to meet House Do’ty first. On the way they see the usual business of a Drow city and its visitors: mind flayers with their thralls, vampires, and demons. At a corner the crowd disperses, everyone reaches for weapons, and the crowd closes in again, but a street urchin touches Rimardo's hand and invites Bayard to meet house Do’ty at the Perverted Inn. The inn is famous throughout the abyss for being brightly lit and clean, with soft pastel colors and bright flowers, which is horrific for a Drow and pleasant for someone from the prime material plane; its Drow patrons apparently enjoy the experience as "a perverse form of masochism."

Inside, guarded by Drow soldiers, two Drow introduce themselves as Waglossz and Xiltyn, majordomos of the matron of house Do’ty. They say Talaryn and House Zollazo have defied the will of Lolth: "They have allowed Vhaeraunites to prosper amongst us!" Rimardo replies, "we were told not to trust you." "By whom, Vhaeraunites?" Remembering that Valnozz is a Vhaeraunite, Rimardo says, "perhaps." The majordomo builds up steam, spittle flying: "we shall ensure that they are all purged!", followed by a holy war against all the Drow who are not pure. Rimardo starts to think this might not be a bad deal, but the majordomo "sells past the close," promising war "against all who oppose the rightful worship of Lolth!" Rimardo asks Bayard, "an insane Civil War over the purity of faith?" and Bayard nods that an alliance might not be such a bad idea. When Rimardo asks what they need, the majordomo is indignant: "I need nothing from you. Lolth needs nothing from you." Rimardo thanks him for the beer. They then offer "the power of Lolth’s clerics and peace with Freedahl if Lolth so chooses," and slaves, which Rimardo dismisses: "You offer us nothing."

Then they produce a slave: "Sir Ryton of Juste-Havre!" The battered former general of the combined anti-Drow alliance, once mayor of Bayard's Juste-Havre, demands, "Bayard, I demand that you make no deals with these monsters." The majordomo offers to trade him for the party's support; what they want is for the party to "kill Talaryn and destroy House Zollazo." Sir Ryton insists, "There is no honor in a deal with the devil to save me." Bayard looks at him and says, "it doesn’t appear to be a good deal, anyway." Rimardo laughs: "you’re going to let your friend die?" Bayard does not understand the cleric; such a deal would "tarnish the honor of Juste-Havre." The Drow are stunned, since other paladins were known to make such deals; "Other paladins, obviously, did not have butter on their brioche." Rimardo thanks the majordomos and says they will get back to them. The majordomo whines that they will tell House Zollazo the party is here, and Rimardo replies, "House Zollazo knows we are already here."

Bayard suggests going to House Zollazo to "clear the air." Near its compound, Drow warriors invite him: "Bayard, perhaps it is time we cleared the air. Would you follow us?" Selzyt whispers, "do not trust them," then insists, and finally pleads, "They are weak. All they want to do is keep power." In a run-down barracks, two refined, worried, middle-aged male Drow lament "So much carnage caused by four puny humans," then correct themselves when Corrin sharpens his blades: "Three humanoids defeated us." They want "Peace": they will protect the party's plane and make peace with Freedahl, who "can keep his city." Bayard asks whether that is all. It is, except for one thing: the party must announce in a public ceremony that it signed because it realized it could not defeat House Zollazo. Rimardo says, "I do not think you are in a position to make demands," and lays out the Empire's weakness: "your negotiating position appears to be singularly weak." House Zollazo's troops storm out, and the other majordomo says, "House Zollazo has endured and will endure."

On the way to House Illal, a cleric of Lolth shouts, "beware the infestation of the faith of Vhaeraun." The party finds the Drow's unusual fixation on the penetration of the cult of Vhaeraun odd. A squad of Drow warriors brings them to a barracks where the chamberlain, Naerth, announces, "I am the consort of the matron of House Illal." He blames House Zollazo's costly wars on spending too much on magical enhancements of troops, relying on abyssal magic, rather than conventional soldiers: "her magical troops are dead, and we have no armies to protect the city." This answers the mystery of the Drow soldiers' magical superpowers, as was hinted by the gnomes; the other houses paid that cost in gold, not in Drow soldier lives. House Illal wants to return to "the traditional martial ways of the Drow Empire." What it needs: "We need you to kill Talaryn." Then the new conclave will pick a new leader, and House Zollazo, without Illal's military support, will have to bow. Bayard asks, "will the world be a better place because of that?" The answer is no, since the Drow will not rule all that exists, but Illal offers a truce, a peace treaty with Freedahl, and the admission that "we could not take Plagueridge Rampart by force even if we wanted to." As a sweetener, the soldiers who guard the entrance to the Drow House are House Illal's, and will be "particularly weak" if the party allies with them, and the opposite if not. "Fair enough," shrugs Rimardo. Naerth closes: "When this truce is over, perhaps we shall meet your children on the battlefield." Bayard blurts, "I am not sure that is a great thing to look forward to," but realizes it is about the closest thing to a compliment a Drow Lord can give.

On the way to House Noquarth, Corrin talks his way into a pair of barbers' beard-shaving race, comes out cleanly shaven and incredibly happy, and stubble is already forming. In an alley, a street urchin points Bayard to two Drow on a bench: "Perhaps Bayard, we could make arrangements with some of your more financially inclined friends?" KP, Rimardo, and Corrin step forward. House Zollazo's war has cost them "our most important trade route to the prime material plane." KP recalls his prediction that restricting trade would crack the Drow power structure, a view from the revolutionary theory of economics that classical economists ridicule; he likes to remind everyone "that humans are not the only species in the planes." He floats "a radical reorganization of your economy…", away from slave labor and monopoly rents toward free humanoids, trade, and an artisanal class, which the irritated negotiators, part of the ruling elite, will not support. Their offer: kill Talaryn and leave a note with her body crediting them, so that the other houses back them in the conclave, while disowning the note if it is found beforehand. The price is "30,000 GP each." "Not enough," says Rimardo. "Yeah, and we like to assassinate people, but that’s not enough."

KP keeps pressing his economics until Nadal, one of the negotiators, snaps that he will not listen to "a gnome who happens to read a few books on inter-planar economics." "I have not merely read a few books; I have written several." "I am Knazreponnium…" Nadal recognises the author of "the drivel that suggested that the Pareto coefficient did not apply to planes where matter and magic were not aligned with a universal constant?" "You are a hack!" / "Yes, but the technique worked!" "My point exactly, magic changes the equilibrium!" After several hours of debate, Nadal concedes, "Your theories have merit." As a result, Drow societies called the "Nadal-KP Equilibrium" would form, proposing a new model of Drow society to address the limits of the plunder economy; their long-term impact is unclear.

The four deals are weighed. House Zollazo wants the heroes to kiss their ring; House Illal offers help and a truce but would leave the Drow Empire much more potent; House Noquarth offers money, but not enough, and its idea of trade is slavery; house Do’ty offers Sir Ryton and little else, and its victory would mean a civil war of purges, after which a purified Drow empire would be "unified and dangerous." "All the houses are evil. None of the choices are good." Everyone turns to Bayard. He declares, "We will not kiss the hand of Talaryn." Selzyt warns, "Do not be fooled, Do’ty will secure power quickly," but Bayard announces, "It is my nature to support creatures of faith." With everyone staring, he declares for house Do’ty. All the Drow are evil and all the houses corrupt, but with Do’ty he saves his old friend Sir Ryton: "Apparently, House Do’ty did understand what motivates a good Paladin." The heroes head back to House Do’ty and declare that they are ready.
