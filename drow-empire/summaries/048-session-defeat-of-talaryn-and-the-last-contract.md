# Chapter 48 – Defeat of Talaryn and The Last Contract

Date: The Abyss, Days 150–151 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
The decision to support House Do’ty is made, and the heroes approach the house's compound. Just before they knock, Rimardo poses an ethical question: "What if we were to tell all of the Drow noble houses that we support them? We could sleep here and secure all of the rewards from all of the houses." Bayard shakes his head: "That doesn’t sound very aboveboard." Corrin protests, "But they are the Drow! Why did I ever sign up to work for you?", and allows that the party has at least ensured enough power and magic "to be able to deal with a certain cleric."

The party has rested in safety, but its allies cannot shelter it for long; if their aid became known they would probably face annihilation. Talaryn is not idly waiting for the opposition to coalesce. She is marshaling a response within the safety of the palace and will soon launch a campaign of terror to purge threats; within 24 hours, either Empress Talaryn will have been assassinated or the party destroyed. The allies supply information about the palace and Halloran. House Do’ty has an area in its refuge not covered by the city's forbiddance, and there are gaps in the forbiddance within the palace as well. The palace grounds are patrolled by Drow warriors who respond within minutes to an alarm, Talaryn has elite hobgoblins as guards, and there are likely unknown protective measures. "Our favorite independent contractors are once again thrust in the unlikely role of saviors of the multi-verse."

Safely inside the compound, Rimardo and KP plan to use Leomund’s tiny hut to block the exits and an earthquake to shatter the building. The Chamberlain, a 227-year-old male Drow with short straight white hair and cyan eyes, rolls his eyes, grabs his belly, groans, and tells them it is impossible: "we have been preparing for invasion for millennia. Do you think you are the first person to try to use an earthquake spell in the city?" The city was built on this plane because its magic and minerals make an earthquake spell ineffective. Stymied, KP asks the Chamberlain and Selzyt, "well, where is the secret entrance? What is this secret underground sewer? What is that special room we can teleport into?" The Chamberlain reveals a dungeon in the palace that is not protected by a forbiddance spell. KP has teleport memorized, but does not know what the dungeon looks like.

Selzyt makes an offer: "I can tell you what the dungeon looks like if you promise to kill Talaryn. And you promise not to kill whomever you may find in the dungeon." When Rimardo suggests the party step aside to discuss it, Selzyt answers, "I am making this deal with the Paladin." Rimardo laughs; surely Selzyt knows by now that Bayard is "a morally relativistic Paladin"? The narration answers for Bayard: he has always understood the importance of pursuing the greater good and of being honorable, and on the Wild Coast the honor of the greater good comes first. Bayard: "Yes, I promise. Of course, accidents do happen, priorities do change, necessity may cause us to breach this deal." Selzyt: "I wouldn’t expect any other kind of deal." Rimardo laughs louder: "Accidents do tend to happen when you have an assassin on the payroll."

For hours the party questions the Chamberlain, who is patient and not helpful. He reveals that inside the palace there are no drow, only a hobgoblin honor guard, because Talaryn does not trust Drow to protect her; outside, the drow guards are almost infinite, so any attempt on her life must succeed before the hobgoblins can alert them. Asked "where do we go after she’s dead?", he replies, "anywhere in the multi-verse?" Corrin smiles and thinks, "there is a small matter of a certain cleric." The Chamberlain understands the real question: "once Talaryn is dead, the drow noble houses will not care about you. You can leave at your leisure. I do recommend you not linger exceedingly long." Before they leave, Selzyt warns that they must come back to the dungeon afterwards, since "There is no other way to escape." "Foolishness is what we are," says a smiling KP. A snarling Selzyt: "and that is why I brought it up."

KP teleports everyone into the dungeon: a small room with a hallway leading north into an even smaller, well-furnished room that looks recently used. In one corner a stairwell leads up. In a darkened room, Corrin opens the door to the south and sees a praetorian guard and Talaryn. He dodges the guards and hits her for 52 HP of damage. From the center of the room, 12 magic missiles hit Corrin for 45 HP; one of the four praetorian guards flanks him and strikes with a javelin for 39 points. Rimardo peeks through the door and uses his wand of polymorph to turn Talaryn into a frog. The guards laugh. The cleric and the Paladin are confused ("why are they laughing at us and not their frog Queen? Are they guards with no honor?"), and Rimardo says aloud, "maybe she is not the real Talaryn?" An enraged guard attacks Rimardo three times to break his concentration and fails.

While Rimardo stares at the frog, the real Talaryn makes a grand entrance protected by a mirror image spell: "did you really think I could be trounced? Did you really think the drow Empire was so defenseless?" The palace lights come on and the shadows disappear. Her finger of death fails to break Rimardo's concentration; Bayard charges a guard for 81 HP; she uses a legendary action to dispel the polymorphed Faux Talaryn, and KP's sunburst blinds a guard and kills Faux Talaryn. A glyph fails to hold Rimardo and Corrin, but her chromatic orb hits Rimardo for 48 HP. Corrin dispels two of her mirrors, and the palace watch hits him with 12 magic missiles for 45 HP. The blinded guard, not seeing how wounded Rimardo is, attacks Corrin for a meager 13 points, a mistake the chapter calls "the turning point." Rimardo channels divinity to heal himself and Corrin; Talaryn heals her guard; a hobgoblin hits KP for 16 HP; her finger of death hits Rimardo for 58.

KP shouts, "do not worry about the hobgoblins." Bayard kills two guards. Talaryn stuns Rimardo with a glyph, another hobgoblin hits KP for 38 HP, and KP, smiling his cryptic grin, casts a wall of force that cuts the hobgoblins off from the main chamber. Corrin hits Talaryn twice; the palace watch hits him for 50. A praetorian guard reduces the stunned Rimardo to one hit point, prompting centuries of debate among the clerics of Istus over whether the goddess intervened, whether Lolth did, or whether the guard was a secret follower of Vhaeraun. KP taunts a hobgoblin with "a childish gnome-like gesture," enraging it. Then Talaryn kills Rimardo, which those who deny Istus any role cite as proof.

Bayard, unharmed, flanks Talaryn and hits her for 49, destroying a mirror, and takes 22 HP. From the room next door, KP casts a cone of cold: Talaryn's glyphs were placed to stop such spells only if the caster stood in the main room, and the hobgoblins had failed to drive him there. She drops to 1 HP, then uses a glyph to heal herself completely. Corrin brings a Drow guard near KP to 10 HP; the palace watch's missiles are absorbed by the wall of force; a drow warrior hits Bayard for 33HP; the last hobgoblin tries to break the wall with his fists. Bayard kills the last guard and hits Talaryn for 43. KP runs out of his hidey-hole, uses a potion of healing on Rimardo, and drags him out of the war zone. Corrin critically hits Talaryn for 85 HP, and Rimardo uses a mass heal to cure everyone. Talaryn, furious ("Where are those hobgoblins?"), fails to hold Bayard and hits him with finger of death for 36 HP. Bayard hits her and she dies.

"At last, we can find my body!" says Selzyt. Rimardo rolls his eyes and asks the gem, "you are Talaryn? Is that what will be revealed?" "No!" KP dismisses the wall of force, the party runs down into the dungeon, and he raises another to keep anyone from disturbing them. A shadowy doorway appears in the northern wall, and out steps Brorna, an ancient wizard crone of a drow. The green glow in Selzyt's gemstone flows into her. "I am Vhaeraun. I have been waiting a thousand years for someone to get here." Brorna has spent that thousand years sending the Drow on pointless quests; Vhaeraun has been "the failsafe." "I am the masked one!" Bayard: "oh, you are the guy who hates Lolth, and you have been doing what?" Vhaeraun asks whether they thought it a coincidence "that Valnozz showed up magically every time you needed him?" Rimardo shrugs, "Yep," privately crediting Istus. Brorna, Vhaeraun says, hid in plain sight under Lolth's nose, protected his cult, quietly thwarted Lolth's plans, and was "the real power behind the drow Empire, picking matrons, defining strategy."

"Dupes?" asks a frustrated KP. No: Vhaeraun has waited a thousand years for "a set of adventurers who hated Lolth just a little bit more than me" (independent contractors powerful and motivated enough to exact his final revenge on his mother). He shows a small object shaped like an eye with glass in front, "a magic mirror the Drow found for me": placed before a weakened Lolth, it traps her, whereas killing her only makes her rematerialize. "My plan is insane, but it can work." Lolth, suspecting him, has moved her seat of power to a quasi-plane whose only entrance is through the slave pits, as a prisoner of the Drow. KP is delighted: "The slave trade! Mercantilism is the Achilles’ heel of every Empire and will save us!" The plan: Brorna will strip them of weapons and magic, declare she captured them, and have them sent to Lolth's plane, with their gear following in a tiny Leomund’s Secret Chest that an ally there will hand over. Then they break out of the slave pits and assault her place of power.

"What is in it for us?" asks KP. Rimardo lists everything they have already done: "We have completed every contract we were asked to sign." Bayard surprises everyone by agreeing: "My friends have a point. At some time, you must stop. The bloodletting must end." Vhaeraun offers three things: the defeat of Lolth will throw the Drow into centuries of chaos, he can open the treasury of the drow Empire, and he will grant boons. He then oversells, describing Lolth's shield maidens, her power to disintegrate anyone within 60 feet once per round, and the warning that killing her would only bring her back enraged. "Your mission is to trap her."

To Corrin he offers "the magical sword Stryker, a +3 rapier," +3 studded leather armor and "the boon of the rogue, that allows you to turn any miss into a hit." Corrin says, yes. To KP he offers a robe of the Arch-Magi, a legendary staff of power, bracers of defense, and "the boon to cast one more additional 9th level spell" ("Excellent! That is worth the trouble."). To Rimardo, who prides himself on his healing, he gives "the boon of word of recall for mass heal," a rod of absorption and +3 plate armor. Vhaeraun and Bayard exchange words ("You realize I am an evil God?" / "That is a problem. I wouldn’t want anything from you."), and Bayard receives a boon of improved constitution, a +3 avenger taken from a lawful good Paladin whom the Drow killed and quartered (none of them could touch the sword), and their most prized possession, "gauntlets of storm giant strength." KP raises his hand and says yes; Rimardo judges the rewards worth the risk; Bayard mutters, "we are past the point of questioning whether we will do it." When Rimardo chortles, "You make a good argument, we want her dead!", Vhaeraun corrects him: "No, no, not dead! She must be trapped." KP asks how to replicate the mirror ("if you had access to a God that was willing to provide you with some magical power, then you could build such a thing").

Rimardo asks whether the plan can work, since "for this plan to work, Talaryn has to be dead, and she is not." Vhaeraun points to her body on the floor: "oh yes. I knew I forgot something." The masked one stabs Talaryn's body repeatedly and viciously until Brorna is covered in blood. "That should do it." Brorna: "I never liked Talaryn. She was always obsessed with power." When Rimardo asks why they were not okay with the party supporting House Do’ty, the answer is that the house persecutes the cult and is the most fanatical of Lolth's followers, and "I find it delicious that House Do’ty will come to power only to lose it when Lolth is trapped." Rimardo: "It looks like we’ve double-crossed them." "We’ve?" The party realizes it is once again on its own: independent contractors. Corrin smiles; a contract to neutralize a god means Istus must be smiling on him.
