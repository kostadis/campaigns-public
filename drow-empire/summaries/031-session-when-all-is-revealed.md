# Chapter 31 – When all is revealed

Date: The Abyss, Days 2–3 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
The spy Valnozz stands before them. Rimardo is shocked: the last time he saw Valnozz was during the second battle of Vallaki, when the spy told him they were on the same side and then ordered the Drow army to retreat, letting the forces of Primus and Sir Ryton withdraw safely. Rimardo remembers that Valnozz was a follower of Vhaeraun. The spy stares at the party and smiles: "The better question, Rimardo, is not why I am here, but why are you?" When Rimardo calls him an old friend, Valnozz refuses the word: "We are travelers on the same road, but our intentions and our long-term plans are not the same. I am not your friend. You are not mine."

Selzyt pipes up, "excellent! Excellent! The spy has emerged. You must listen to this one." Valnozz congratulates the party on securing "the failsafe" and reveals, "I trained Selzyt myself. For we are both members of the cult of Vhaeraun." KP asks, "tell us more of this Vhaeraun!", and the spy ignores the question. He tells Bayard that when they made their first arrangement near Vallaki he only thought they were stopping Lolth from acquiring a powerful artifact: "I never imagined you would come here and try to seize control of the Drow empire's portal. And never in my wildest dreams did I imagine you could succeed. And yet, here you are."

Then he tells the story. In his telling, the Elves waged war amongst themselves, between those who felt the elves were a master race, destined to rule, and those who wanted to live with the other races; the self-styled master race became known as the Drow. According to the followers of Vhaeraun, Lolth was Araushnee, wife of Corellon Larethian, Chief God of the Elves. Araushnee wanted power, and she and her son secretly encouraged her followers to start the war. Vhaeraun suggested that she create a new persona, Lolth, so she could help her followers without revealing her plans to Corellon. Corellon found out and cast them out; Lolth lost her power, and the Drow were defeated and sent underground. Lolth fled to the Abyss, secured the 66th plane for herself, and transformed herself into the Queen of the Spiders.

Lolth shaped Drow society so that she would have no rivals. Its four principles were the supremacy of the Drow as a race; that to be Drow was to worship Lolth and only Lolth; that clerical magic was permissible and other magic shunned; and that "permanent conflict for supremacy" was necessary for the master race to survive. The most crucial conflict was between the sexes: fearing that love could break her spell, Lolth made only female Drow her priests, and the males, deprived of direct access to the goddess, came to resent the females, who in turn kept them subservient. Love between the same genders "was poisoned too." The males could have used magic, but Lolth's hatred of power she could not control meant very few did. Drow society was organised as commoners, noble houses, and a ruling house under The Empress, a structure that looked orderly but hid permanent strife: commoners wanting to become nobles, nobles wanting to be the ruling house, the ruling house wanting to conquer other realms, and "every so often, some Drow Empress wanted to overthrow Lolth." Because one Empress over all Drow could be a threat, Lolth allowed many Drow Empires, and deprived any realm that grew too powerful of her power. That web is why her symbol is the spider: "For she was the spider at the center of the Drow web."

Lolth still wanted revenge on Corellon and more power, so the Drow fought the Elves and the Dwarves. Their armies had two weaknesses: Empresses would help the forces of good against one another, or a house would sabotage a war effort; and Lolth's hatred of magic meant her armies, "populated exclusively with men," lacked magic users, while the female clerics "did not want to risk their lives in war. That was for the men." As for Vhaeraun, he disagreed with Lolth about the war between the genders and her bias against magic. Lolth assumed he wanted to rule in her place and cast him out, "And there is some truth to that." He found power among the Drow men, and within every Drow Realm there are followers of Vhaeraun, at war with Lolth's. Because of this unending war, the Drow have not been able to conquer the known universe.

KP says, "I am not sure what the problem is." Valnozz explains that the current Drow Empress has spent "staggering amounts of resources" invading the party's plane to find an artifact that Lolth wants, and the Empire is overstretched; she must be stopped. The Drow had waited centuries for the portal to connect to the party's plane, and "There have been prophecies." Pressed on his secrets, he insists, "There are no more secrets between us. I have told you everything," and prefers the words "fellow travelers" to friends. He warns of three problems: they can trust no one, they cannot trust Freedahl, and unless they secure the other sides of the portal they will be overrun by the Drow. When the party mentions their signed contract for a thousand years of peace — "With a demon." — Selzyt, Valnozz and Rimardo all laugh. KP then announces, "the only way to preserve the peace in the realms is to ensure that Lolth’s order is preserved," and the party stares at him, aghast; he argues that she has kept the Drow killing each other for millennia, and that "Another regime might be catastrophic for everyone but the Drow." The reply comes: "Well said, gnome, but to preserve this peace, she must not have too much power."

Everything becomes clear. Lolth used this Drow empire to find a means to bring her armies to Oerth, and they found an artifact that connected the abyss to many other planes. Once the portal connected to Oerth, her armies would invade, and after the conquest they would find the artifact that Lundbeams was now protecting, giving Lolth enough power to finally become the supreme power. But the portal could not be controlled; it would connect to whatever random plane it wanted. The current Drow Empress proposed invading through it anyway, and these invasions have been costly: the ruling house benefits while the noble houses suffer.

Asked what they are supposed to do, Valnozz says, "First, you must secure Plagueridge Rampart, and then you must replace the current ruling house with one of the noble houses." House Illal has suffered most from House Zollazo's endless wars, and its matron is not willing to trust Lolth with that much power; if House Illal were in power, there would be no armies looking for an artifact. They are looking for an opening — "Why the loss of the portal and a city." Heading to the door, Valnozz warns of more pressing problems, "a slave revolt which will be crushed and an ally in Marco who will die," and disappears.

Just as the party is about to rescue Marco, Freedahl appears amid fire, brimstone and sulfur, with a retinue of minor demonic powers. "I have been waiting for a long, long time to take control of this city. Graz’zt is going to be so happy. And I secured it right under Demogorgon’s nose." He summons his forces, the demons rampage through the city, and within a few hours all Drow resistance has been overcome. "The sweet, sweet smell of charred dead Drow," he says, inhaling deeply. Marco arrives and tells Bayard, "we have done what we promised. You have secured the city. Now you must do what you have promised and free the slaves." Freedahl is appalled and tries to protest, but KP, who has studied demonic sociology, says confidently, "these are our slaves, we captured them by force." Rimardo tries to hush him; KP ignores the cleric. Demons respect power and violence, and Freedahl recognises the claim: "you are correct, mage. Might makes right." Rimardo is stunned. KP says, "thank you."

Freedahl sees that until the party secures the other sides of the portal, the deal is incomplete. Marco explains that the portal is connected to four planes: the one the party came from, one near a Dwarven kingdom called Brungril Forge, the Feywild, and a kingdom of Giants. On each is a Drow incursion, and if those Drow Armies are not defeated the Drow empire will use the planes as a staging point to recapture the city.

At the slave pens the party finds four hobgoblins. The largest, Tethir, snarls in disgust at "the slovenly cleric and that Dwarven looking halfling" and tells Bayard, "you look like you must be in charge." With a very straight back and a severe demeanor, he praises the conquest of the citadel: "You demonstrated tactical awareness, executional brilliance, and strategic foresight in securing an alliance with the demonic power despite your hatred." He swears fealty to Bayard and asks whether he would like them to slaughter the slaves. Bayard ignores that and asks how long the slaves have been in the pens; Tethir lists three uses for slaves: a meat shortage, "disposable labor," and serving the Drow houses. Bayard asks that the slaves be led to the portal, and Tethir admires his ruthlessness: "Using the slaves as a meat shield during your invasion of the Drow strongholds, brilliant!" Bayard rolls his eyes — "you are slow, but you’re catching up" — and Tethir reaches for his blade until Rimardo explains, "what Bayard meant is that he sees promise in your tactical awareness." Bayard promises to mentor him, and the tension eases. Before the transfer, Tethir remarks that the party's allies "are too short. And they are too small." KP and Corrin are offended but keep silent.

With the city patrolled by demons and infested with Drow assassins, KP casts Leomund’s tiny hut and the party sleeps safe and sound. In the morning a knock wakes them. Galadher Eleneth, a 245-year-old male Drow in scale armor with a bald head, green eyes and a poorly done piercing on his lip, asks whether Bayard is the new boss; Rimardo laughs, "perhaps even the mayor?" Galadher oversees "the Rangers who collect the good berries" — "The Berry-Rangers?" — and warns that in a few days they will run out of berries. KP explains that without the Drow clerics the city's food supply is at risk; Rimardo points out that in a few weeks this will be Freedahl's problem. When KP wonders if some Drow soldiers are still alive, Rimardo speaks to him "as if he were a naïve little child," and the answer comes that Freedahl "killed anyone whose allegiances were rigid. Unfortunately, this was all of the priests." Galadher's real concern is pay. Bayard waves him toward Rimardo — "Excellent point, you should ask the Treasurer." — and Galadher is told, "In five harvests, we will also pay you." When he asks what power the party has that he should trust, KP, who has worked out that Galadher wants upfront payment, asks whether those who defeated the rulers of this town are less powerful than the Drow they defeated. Galadher concedes, "I had not considered that," and promises to tell his band of berry-Rangers they will be paid.

Before the party reaches the portal, another Drow approaches: Finaeli Talraidal, a 112-year-old male Drow fighter with pasty white skin, grey eyes and a bald head. "You have made a terrible mistake allying yourself with the demons. They will betray you." He speaks for those "who worship the masked one." KP, realising he is a follower of Vhaeraun, insists that he invade the city and take it over — "But we are in the city!" — and Finaeli proposes instead a Regent or steward, to whom the Drow forces are loyal and who has the respect of the merchant guild, to represent the party's interests while they are off the plane. Bayard finds this "all very fishy" and asks whether he is going to betray his own kind. Selzyt can take it no longer: "that’s what they do. They are Drow! Betrayal is what we do all day. It’s our hobby."

Bayard weighs it: this Drow is dishonorable, but the demon is a demon; in the end he will have to kill everyone, but for the moment this Drow may mitigate the mayhem, and if he understood Valnozz, the followers of Vhaeraun are better than those of Lolth. "Very well, you shall be our steward." He clicks his finger and Freedahl appears: "Seriously, you are calling a Drow a gentleman? What’s next, Bayard, you gonna call a donkey, a warhorse?" Bayard answers that he has to believe the Drow can be redeemed. Freedahl stares at him in disbelief at the Paladin's naïveté, but is ecstatic: taking over the portal has been "the easiest thing in the history of easy takeovers," and Bayard is gifting him a scapegoat, an alliance with a sworn ally of Lolth's that Freedahl can twist to his advantage. "By golly Bayard, I think you have a point!" Finaeli and Freedahl enter the citadel to finalise their arrangement. A few moments later, the party enters the portal, sends the hobgoblins back to the slave pens, and prepares for travel through the dimensions.
