# Chapter 23 – Theology and contracts

Date: 15 Goodmonth 595 CY (595-08-15; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
Having defeated an assortment of Drow and shadow demons "using a novel strategy that KP pioneered," the party moves deeper into the caverns built under the ruins of the tower on the Wasted Isle near the Lizardfolk village of Alaneth. Before entering the next room, Rimardo reminds himself why he is still working with Bayard: he wants just enough power to kill Lundbeams. With the shadow demons defeated, the party calls Gruneel back from guard duty, since the situation is no longer perilous.

In the next chamber they fight the local commander of the Drow operation. He brags about his mistress of House Zollazo, insists that unlike Xhalh he will not be defeated, and harps on how the Drow Empire of the Abyss will destroy the party. The fight is touch-and-go: when the party gets too close, he uses his reaction to immobilize them with "a stunning lunging attack"; a shimmering ball of force gives his attacks advantage; and he can seemingly heal himself on demand. KP wonders what magic grants such powers and where such information is held. Rimardo just sighs. Bayard just attacks.

After several rounds "that were increasingly looking grim for the good guys," Rimardo casts Banishment. The Drow is banished, a minute passes, and the Drow does not return. Everyone turns to Rimardo: "Why didn’t you do this earlier?" Rimardo shrugs, and Gruneel, who has endured the bulk of Rimardo's jeers, glares. What Rimardo had missed was that the Drow leader, as he repeatedly pointed out, was native to the Abyssal plane, so banishment would not bring him back.

In Rimardo's defense, the clerics of Istus do not agree on how the banishing spell works, and for some of them, using it in ambiguous situations feels almost like demanding that Istus answer the question — tempting fate. Rimardo has stayed neutral in this debate. KP, though, recalls the dispute between the creationist and evolutionist factions of the Istus cult. The strict creationists hold that "native" means the plane of origin of the species; the evolutionists, that it means the plane of origin of the target creature. The evolutionists point out that Dwarves, created by a god, are not sent to the outer planes for good by banishment; the creationists answer that a god can create a species on one plane and make another its home plane. Each side calls the other's view a violation of Istus's control of fate. "Intriguingly, Istus, who could resolve the issue, does not comment." A Creationist would say the Drow vanished because the Drow are Lolth's creation and she is Abyssal; the evolutionists would say the Drow leader's going to the Abyss proves their reading, and when cranky would "cite Sage Occam, who is famous for his Razor." The debate has at times split the faithful. "But Istus just spins her fates."

The theology does not stop the party from searching the room, and they find some trinkets of some value. The treasure in some measure convinces Rimardo that Istus favors the evolutionists, but as he is neutral he does not change his position. Perhaps, the chapter muses, he belongs to the fatalist faction, which holds that Istus decides the outcome and that both other factions are heretics for trying to decide fate for her. Rimardo says nothing about his position.

The party enters the portal chamber again and finds it has reverted to its original configuration: the lava flow and collapsed ceiling restored, and all the dirt KP excavated and the work of the Lizardfolk gone. The artifact that controls the portal clearly also controls the entry point into the realms it reaches, and seizing control of the device will be necessary to protect the multi-verse.

Deeper in, several bearded devils emerge from the lava flow bisecting the room. Their leader hisses, "as per our contractual obligations, we will kill the intruders who cross the threshold." KP is intrigued, Rimardo and Corrin run behind the door, and Gruneel and Bayard advance. KP asks about the contract — "the contract is covered with an NDA" — and then the NDA: "the NDA is also covered with a non-disclosure agreement. All we can reveal is that we are required to stop anyone who crosses the threshold from entering the portal." The warning clause is apparently standard for this bearded devil faction, meant to prevent unnecessary bearded devil deaths. Noticing that two of the party have left the room, the leader announces, "per the contractual terms that were agreed upon, we are only required to have a single bearded devil per intruder," and two bearded devils retreat into the lava. The heroes wonder whether they can twist the contract to avoid combat entirely. Gruneel, a native of Fax who once kept the books for his uncle's merchant's firm, is particularly thrilled at the chance to outwit "a contract that a devil wrote, and a Drow signed."
