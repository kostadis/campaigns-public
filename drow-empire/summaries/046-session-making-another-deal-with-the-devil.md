# Chapter 46 – Making another deal with the devil.

Date: The Abyss, Days 101–150 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
Morning arrives and the heroes come down for breakfast at the tavern of the gilded dryad, where the hobgoblin tavern keeper greets them with a smile. "Business has been good," he says; their heroic acts have made for better business since the time the Drow ruled the city, "Although the poisonous gas was not good for business. Many died." Some of the best neighborhoods were destroyed, but they are his heroes: they "secured the breach near my tavern and rallied the troops to defend the gates." Corrin's paranoia kicks in, and Rimardo, feeding off it, asks, "why are you being so nice to us?" The keeper explains that a scary-looking Drow wishes to speak to them, and has made it clear that if anything bad happened to them, or if they were not here in the morning, "the consequences would be unspeakable." Corrin sharpens his daggers, Bayard grabs the hilt of his greatsword, Rimardo prepares his spells, and KP wonders how this will affect the inter-planar economic trends he has been observing of late.

The spy Valnozz enters. "So, you show up whenever you want?" Rimardo snarks. Valnozz is unimpressed: "why yes, that is why I am Valnozz, the master spy, also known as, the big finger." He tells them they have done well and unfurls a map. They must travel along this road to a basalt plain, where dragons that are looking for them wait; past the dragons are the walls of Maenerius. It is not possible to teleport or plane shift into the city: "For a thousand years, the Drow Empire has protected Maenerius with spells. The only entrance is the gates." Rimardo observes, "this looks like a great place to ambush us," and Valnozz agrees that "the trick is how do we get you through the gates?" He produces "a permit signed by the Empress herself to let you in the city," adding, "I do not care what you do in the city if it ends with the Empress’s death." Each of the houses has decided the party is a threat and a weapon; each will approach them with an offer, may try to kill them if they refuse, and whichever house they do not ally with "will consider you an enemy till the end of time." Each house may even attack them "just to keep up appearances."

Asked whom to ally with, Valnozz scratches his face: "I do not care. House Do’ty is not to be trusted at all." House Illal has the most power and has suffered the most under Zollazo; many of its finest died because of the party's actions. When Rimardo asks whether they will be upset, Valnozz looks at him as if he had asked the stupidest question in all the planes: "No, that’s not how the Drow Empire thinks." House Zollazo wants the party to kiss its ring, Do’ty wishes to purge all the followers of Vhaeraun, and House Noquarth will make a deal for the right amount of money to preserve their business interests. "that sounds like my kind of house," Rimardo smiles. The spy groans that the noble houses' business interests "are not what you would call socially acceptable business interests."

After many days of travel in the Abyss, the party reaches the outskirts of the basalt plain. A walled city arises from the flat basalt, at least 10 miles across and brightly lit from an unseen source; the sky seems to glow, the temperature rises sharply, there is no cover of any kind, and the bleached bones of creatures that could not reach the city lie about. Despite the light, the city is bathed in shadows "as if it's in perpetual twilight."

An unseen voice with a thick accent speaks from about 30 feet away: "more scavengers come to feast on the carcass of the once fearsome beast?" The speaker introduces himself: "I am Nuzar, noble of the True Gith." The Gith want certain prizes not to fall along with the Drow Empire. KP leads the negotiation in a comic back-and-forth ("You are offering your treasure to us?" / "No."), which establishes that Nuzar wants the red dragons that fly over the basalt plain: "if you let us take the dragons, we will let you live." Rimardo can't help butting in: "Sounds like a great deal to me." The dragons are keyed onto the party's appearance and will attack only them, so the party must stay visible. Nuzar proposes, "We take four, and you take the rest," and when the party generously offers all of them, he blushes "as much as a gith can blush" and explains, "I am so sorry, but we can only take 1 per Gith." Eight young red dragon wyrmlings approach. Nuzar raises his head, the party recognizes him from their time in the portal back on their home plane, and he says, "Thank you for your generosity, and always good to do business with you!"

The battle follows. Corrin eyes Bayard's boots of levitation with envy, remembers his bow, and strikes a dragon with advantage through foresight. Bayard levitates into the air to strike with his sword, because the man without fear and without reproach does not use "the weapon of a coward, the bow." Rimardo's toll the dead is countered. One dragon turns invisible; another breathes fire on Bayard for 24 Hp of damage. KP becomes invisible; Corrin's arrow inflicts 36 points of damage; Bayard slashes a dragon for 16 HP; Rimardo dispels the dragon's invisibility. Bayard is nibbled and covered in flames. KP's lightning bolt electrocutes them all for 29 points of damage and one dies; Corrin's arrow kills a wounded wyrmling for 48 HP. The last two breathe on Bayard for 24 HP, and Rimardo looks at the Paladin and says, "you’re fine." KP hides just enough to cast an unnoticed lightning bolt for 35 points of electrical damage, Corrin kills one dragon, and Bayard's attack on the last for 9 HP leaves everyone staring at the fearless leader. The last dragon counters a spell from Rimardo and flies away; KP wounds it with fire bolt and Corrin finishes it off.

At the grand entrance of Maenerius, KP takes stock of the city through his long study of inter-planar economics and his thesis on Drow Imperial trade. Like all Drow cities ruled by a matron, it relies on plunder; the portal let the Drow invade other planets while keeping their enemies out. Without it, the Drow Empire is severely weakened and no longer powerful enough to take the portal by force, and because of the deal the party signed with Freedahl, the Drow must negotiate with the heroes: "The Drow must sue for peace or be destroyed." KP concludes that the real key to destroying the Drow threat is to ensure the Drow never gets access to the portal. He foresees the slave pens falling below replacement levels, the food warehouses running dangerously low, and a starving city whose defenses weaken; demons could destroy it if they could focus their minds, and only Lolth's favor keeps it alive, but "Lolth does not support the weak." Because Lolth teaches that power exists to serve power, each house is positioning itself for the day after rather than banding together, which makes the Drow "the biggest losers of history." And if any Drow city became powerful enough, Lolth would destroy it.

The city's layout: The Drow House, where the ruling house lives, at the center; the open sector to the north, home to non-Drow inhabitants and free Drow outside the four noble houses; the four noble houses' sectors and compounds south of The Drow House; and the slave pens and warehouses to the south near the walls. The warehouses are exceptionally well guarded, and because of the shortage of slaves due to the loss of the portal, House Zollazo has imposed strict security on the slave pens. KP also realizes that the Empire is fragile because House Zollazo's expansionary policies have been thwarted: the invasion of the Wild Coast was unusually large, its motive was to find an artifact, and the defeat at Fax and the Battle of Vallaki destroyed the Empire's military strength. House Illal supplied most of the troops and officers, was crippled by the failure, and has been, according to Valnozz, a less than willing supporter of House Zollazo ever since. "The Drow Empire is reeling."

The party recalls that the other houses are willing to help them usurp House Zollazo but unwilling to do the job themselves, which makes the heroes "once again independent contractors that can be conveniently dismissed if they fail." Doing nothing is no option either: Freedahl has access to the Wild Coast through the portal, and the promise of protection of his city is the only thing holding back the hordes of demons. KP considers economic sabotage, but the city's protections thwart most of his plans, so he proposes marching up to the gate and announcing themselves to sow discord among the noble houses. Corrin points out that they have enough Drow paraphernalia to disguise themselves, but Bayard declares, "I am Bayard, and I do not sneak into an enemy castle, I walk in." KP adds a twist: announce that they are here to see House Illal, because there is "No point in being independent contractors if we don’t announce to everyone that the bidding can begin."

The walls rise 40 feet from the basalt with no decoration, masonry, or visible artistry, broken only by large gates of dense, imported wood, and are heavily patrolled. With Corrin hiding a little behind, Bayard proclaims himself "without fear and without reproach, a man whose brioche has always been buttered," victor at Fax, conqueror of Plagueridge Rampart and defender of Juste-Havre, invited to see House Illal. The guards rise to attention with arrows pointed at him; one captain readies his command for battle, but another Drow captain demands the order be delayed, and the two argue: "You would dare overrule the decision of House Zollazo?" / "For too long has House Illal paid the price for your matron’s incompetence." Closer in, the party notices that the guards are orcs, good at brutal violence and baffled by the houses' conspiracies. An orc in House Zollazo livery screams, "you are Bayard, the enemy! You must die where you stand," while one in House Illal livery insists, "He is here to see my mistress. You will not prevent his entry!" Bayard persuades the House Illal orcs that the House Zollazo orcs are no match, and that it would dishonor the matron if they did not ensure his safe passage. The Drow captains panic; the gate is left open and unguarded, and the heroes walk calmly through. The captains, now complicit, will keep silent: "After all, who will anyone believe, orcs or Drow?"

Inside Maenerius, the inhabitants are a peculiar mix of the demonic, monstrous, humanoid, and not, and "Anything can be purchased, and everything is for sale." Around a corner a cat that has given birth to kittens meows, a sweet moment until the party notices its eyes glow red. KP decides the red eyes merely reflect the ambient light; the rest of the party is unconvinced and moves on quickly, which is fortunate, because KP has confused the entry on cats with the entry on the Hellcats of Maenerius, a demonic crossbreed. A magic effect dulls every light source to a constant dim light. The busy streets hold everyone from the unarmed to the heavily armored, so the party does not look out of place, "except for the fact that you lack a sign of servitude, and that Bayard’s general goodness glows."
