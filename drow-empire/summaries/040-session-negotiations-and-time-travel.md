# Chapter 40 – Negotiations and Time Travel

Date: The giants' plane, Day 2, then the Abyss, Day 98 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
With Molril defeated and the tower destroyed, Selzyt glows: "I suppose we should head back home to the abyss." KP wants information first. Asked where his body is, the gem says only that it will be found in the capital city. Asked about the library, Selzyt glows and dims: "when we get my body, I will tell you where the library is." He calls KP "the Savior of the failsafe" and promises him all the information he could desire — "But only after you find my body." The rest of the group stares as the two fall into "yet another long-winded discussion about the economic Pareto efficiency of inter-planar Drow trade and its impact on the Minkovski equilibrium."

Mr. Corrin stares at the rubble and glares at Rimardo, worried that the mercenary cleric is acquiring a heart of gold. But Istus must have been pleased with Rimardo's decision, because treasure is found on the creatures they just fought, including "an idol of Lolth’s."

Back at the clearing by the portal, the stone giant greets them: "oh, you are alive." KP: "that is the usual outcome." "Smashing! Perfect! Now quickly go through the portal and ensure that the Drow Empire never comes back." Then the gnomes arrive and surround the clearing. Their leader says the party cannot return to the Drow plane, or "the giant's oppression will be reintroduced." The stone giant leader promises the Giants will protect them; the gnome scoffs, "Do you really think that five Giants will protect you from the 12th brigade of the gnomish Army?" Bayard asks why, if they are so powerful, they were being exploited; the leader explains, "our powers are given to us through the Drow." KP asks what arrangement would not stop their quest. The good gnome says, "I have a different plan."

The Giants, wanting the party through the portal, insist, "Saving the multi-verse, that is what you must focus on," and ask, "who are you to interfere in the free association of labor and capital?" This tweaks the gnomish leader, and the two sides descend into a protracted debate over whose tribe committed which crime first and who killed whom. Just as the party might slip through, someone shouts, "SILENCE!" The elderly gnome asks KP to show them his solution. KP asks first how they get their powers. "This gem is what enables our powers. The Drow Molril would periodically go into the abyss and return with the gem glowing." The gnomes do not know the process, though they speculate it involves the emanations of the abyssal plane. Bayard proposes that the party power the gem in the abyss on their behalf.

The Giants are horrified. Their leader threatens that "a large giant Army" is coming to crush everyone. The gnomish leader replies that stopping the party at the portal is far easier — "And we are ready to die for our cause, you capitalist pig!" — and the two sides fall into "a dialectical debate about the relationship of labor, capital, and freedom." The gnomish leader turns to KP: "Knazreponnium, you look like the kind of gnome who is willing to make a deal." KP is confused until the gnome clarifies, "a deal in exchange for the advancement of knowledge!" — and KP's expression changes completely. The leader sees two paths: the party defeats the Giants, or returns with the powered gem. An aide-de-camp wants to just kill the party and let the Drow reclaim their power. KP calmly offers a better idea: "Come to the abyss with your gem and absorb as much of the power you need to protect yourself?" Bayard looks stunned: "that is a perfect idea." The gnomes never expected that option.

KP asks the leader to use the gem on him afterward. The leader apologetically explains that the gem does not work that way: it requires a ritual in the abyss through the clerics of Lolth, "interdimensional rituals powered by divine magic using the deepest and darkest blackest arts." What the party hears: "mumbo-jumbo, we can’t help you, jumbo -mumbo." The gnomes remain unconvinced — they can stop the party and keep their Drow ally, or join them and risk losing it. KP, raising his finger "as if he were speaking to a three-year-old," tells them they are focusing on the superficial problem: "What you need is a renewable supply of gem magic that you control, and that is what we have to offer." The gnomes soften and add a term: "a small strike force to enter the giant capital and kill the ruling council."

The stone giant screams, "this is an outrage!" With arrows pointed at him, he folds his arms and promises to "remain silent observers to this travesty of negotiations." As KP negotiates plans that involve massacres of many giants, the stone giant threatens to leave and send a strike force against them all. KP asks, "Wouldn’t you like to be the new giant kings?" — then shrugs that they could find some other Giants. The stone giant excitedly raises his hand: "You are looking for a new giant king?" "Or a mayor." "I prefer to be the king." A young gnomish warrior chirps that an Emperor ruled more territory than a king, and the stone giant smiles: "I like the idea of me being Emperor, can you make that happen?" KP nods.

The gnomish leader restates their position: if the party defeats the Drow empire, the gnomes do not know what will happen to the portal or the gem, so "the only way we are willing to let you reach the portal is if you commit to helping us with our succession problem." KP struggles to answer. The stone giant, in "a formal diplomatic and severe tone of voice," announces that he has never supported the ruling council's exploitative system and believes in a system "where there is no exploiter or exploited." A hill Giant shouts, "we want and anarcho-syndicalist commune!" Bayard, "ever the libertarian," is appalled; Mr. Corrin and Rimardo are terrified at the idea of helping a society that does not value gold. Another hill Giant accuses the first of being "a radish – red on the outside white on the inside!", and the stone giant laments that "these internal divisions of the progressive movement" let the ruling elite terrorize the humanoids of the planet. Because it is Giants, the trees vibrate and the ground shakes.

The gnome leader screams over the din, "do we have a deal, Bayard?" Bayard is no fan of the new socialist giant movement, but the current regime is unacceptable: "yes, we do!" Rimardo asks at the last minute, "is there anything that you can do to sweeten the pot?" In the background, Mr. Corrin sharpens his knives with the points aimed at Rimardo, unhappy with the cleric's altruism, and Rimardo is now aware. KP mulls creating "a portal within the portal" — a massive explosion, being thrown into some plane, then plane shifting to the abyss. He hears his old master: "Knazreponnium, the great gnomish library doesn’t have any information to guide you." "But master, if we don’t try new things, how will we learn anything new?" Then he remembers the last time he pursued knowledge the library did not have, and shudders.

"So, Bayard, what do you want to do?" Bayard reflects on his fluid morality and decides it is time for redemptive activities: "we will do the right thing and help the gnomes!" When he declines compensation, Rimardo insists that he and Mr. Corrin get the Paladin's share. The gnomes offer any treasure in the vaults of the giant king; when Rimardo worries there is none, a gnome tells him there is over 400 years' worth of gnomish jewelry seized by the Giants. Bayard ends it: "we will come here and help you once we have defeated the Drow." He reflects that small acts of kindness are part of being a good Paladin.

The party enters the portal and arrives in the abyss. Plagueridge Rampart is in transition: traffic flows in and out, merchants and craftsmen are building an import-export economy around the portal, and buildings once dedicated to the Drow slave trade have become taverns and inns for a new mercantile class. Overseeing the city is Ziamar, son of the demon lord Freedahl, with contingents of demons who hold the social position knights would hold elsewhere; there is concern about their aggressiveness. Ziamar has hired mercenaries from other planes but currently relies on the demon knights. The heroes, "the saviors of Plagueridge Rampart," are given the home of a prominent former administrator and access to the new inns.

The party is confused at how this happened over four days — to their surprise, many months have passed in the abyss. The first thing they notice is that Finaeli Talraidal is dead, his head on a pike: his deal with Freedahl "was not quite as airtight as it needed to be," and his reign as Regent was cut short. Ziamar's terrified majordomo leads them to their new residence and thanks them on behalf of "his Royal highness Ziamar": "You have secured the city and the portal for his father, Freedahl, and the demons of this plane." KP thanks him for taking care of "our Drow problem." The majordomo is outraged — the party left the city in shambles, and "We had to resurrect the good berry production." KP answers, disinterested, "yes, yes." The majordomo goes on: since the Drow empire is not defeated, the portal is not secure; Ziamar is delighted with the defeat of the Drow forces off the plane, but has been trying to reestablish trade, and the chapter breaks off mid-speech.
