# Chapter 7 – The chaotic evil orc betrayed us

Date: 12 Flocktime 595 CY (595-05-12; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
The battle between the party and the thugs continues. The first thug, Guache, attacks Corrin and misses, while Corrin sharpens his daggers, "doing his whole silent but deadly thing." KP uses his magic missiles, one on the owl and two on Guache for 10 HP; the owl dies. Bayard swings his sword and kills Guache, then approaches two Thugs surrounding Rimardo. Bayard "once again chooses to not flank because it is not beyond reproach," but when Rimardo screams at him to move, he decides that not flanking "is beyond reproach unless your ally demands it." His flanking of Ketut results in 8 HP of damage.

An acolyte emerges from the shadows and commands Bayard to grovel. Bayard does not. Rimardo is impressed: "You do not bow down to any evil cleric!" Bayard smugly replies, "Exactly!" The acolyte runs, stops, sees the party smile, and moves to hide, and "the party sighs, having smiled too soon." Corrin notices KP has not positioned himself for flanking, moves in on Knox, does 19 HP of damage, and kills him. Seeing two thugs dead and one injured, the acolyte casts sacred flame on Corrin, who dodges the burst, and the acolyte reflects that "casting a spell that can be avoided on a rogue is not a good idea." Rimardo, about to cast a spell on the Acolyte, remembers that a spell high wisdom can resist is a poor choice against a cleric, and instead targets the second thug, Vilmar, who is held.

The last remaining thug, George, says, "You know what this was supposed to be a smooth snatch and grab," disengages, and runs. KP uses a snowball swarm on Hadden and on Vilmar, who takes 12 HP. Bayard weighs stopping the fleeing thug against killing the one in front of him: "The dilemma is great. But not so much, he grapples George." KP's face says, "Kill the bad guys, KILL THEM!" But Bayard wants to know what is going on, tackles George, and demands, "Speak! Talk! Tell us what you mean!" Corrin approaches the dead acolyte and takes his weapons. Then Corrin and Rimardo kill Vilmar.

KP questions George: "Tell me about your Grab and Snatch!" and asks whether they are hiring. George explains: "I was paid by some people to collect some visitors for the slave market. Nothing personal." The party was supposed to be unequipped and easy pickings: "You were selected." Asked who paid him, George says, "You would never believe me," and KP answers, "Yes, we would never believe you." George: "It was dark-skinned elves." KP: "AH, DROW!" George insists "Drow are fiction, stories we tell our children," though these elves "were unwilling to go out in the sunlight. Which is odd for elves," something he put down to worry about superstitious people thinking they were Drow. Rimardo offers him his life if he leads them to the elves.

A small group of the city watch approaches, intent on their purpose and ready for action. KP welcomes them: "Thank you so much for showing up. We look forward to your help." Corrin tells Rimardo, in his own way, "They are not here to help us." Corrin sees criminals who were made guards and have not reformed, carrying themselves "like a street brawler looking for a fight," as if converted to the watch "one beautiful morning"; the livery looks "very – very new," consistent with the story that the town had no city watch until very recently. KP begins, "Constable …" and Rimardo's look shuts him up. A guardsman asks, "Gnome you wanted to say something?" and KP just says thank you. The guard takes George away. As they leave KP calls, "EXCELLENT JOB! BRAVO! GOOD WORK CONSTABLE!!!" A shop owner, Dante, who runs a grocery, mutters, "He will be on the street in 20 minutes."

The group heals itself and heads toward the barracks to look for Walton. A few minutes later a group of guards surrounds them, and their sergeant screams, "You are under arrest in the name of the great city of FAX!" Bayard asks, "In the name of who?" Rimardo protests that they are friends of Walton, and the Sergeant replies, "Walton gave the arrest order." Rimardo: "What a joker, Walton is!" KP: "It is a mix-up." The Sergeant demands their weapons; Rimardo claims they have none, having handed them over at the city. The guard eyes Bayard's greatsword, gets a look of death, and squeaks. KP threatens, "if you take our weapons, we will shut down the government!" Bayard adds, "And these are not technically our weapons." Then the party agrees to go along: "We are very compliant!" The sergeant forms his men into a protective cordon and they walk on.

Suddenly six figures in black surround the guards, and a female voice says, "Free those men or prepare to die." Rimardo insists, "No, we do not need your help, we are not captured!" A guard shouts, "WE KNEW IT! YOU ARE MEMBERS OF THE WILD COAST LIBERATION FRONT!!!!" Rimardo says they know nothing of "Lundbeams’s games," and the Sergeant is outraged: "You know the Archbishop’s name, and you dare associate her to these terrorists?" The party is confused about who to side with. Bayard wants the side of the law; Rimardo, warned by Corrin, thinks following the guards would not be siding with the law; Corrin's insight tells him the WLF members are the ones who care about their safety. Bayard asks the WLF to back off. One of them answers, "No, you do not get it Walton was paid off by Carc to kill you!" Rimardo: "Walton is not our friend? I feel betrayed!" Bayard: "That is evil!"

It finally clicks. Carc, "The evil orc leader," told them to take the overland route, which Alexia said was extremely dangerous, and to hook up with his friend Walton. Rimardo objects that they secured a significant victory for the Orc Empire, and Corrin asks, in his own way, "What part of evil do you not understand?" The party goes with the WLF. KP asks if they should kill the guards; the WLF leader says no. As the guards run, Bayard calls after them, "Tell Walton we are disappointed! Extremely disappointed by this betrayal." KP adds, "He knows we are coming." Rimardo: "We will kill him later!"

As they flee, the WLF leader Akia refuses to take them to Lundbeams. Rimardo argues they must help Tolub; Akia calls him "a stooge and a member of the ruling class and a toy of the empire." When Rimardo asks why Tolub has not been seen in months, Akia says nobody knows, and Rimardo shouts, "YOU ARE BEING PLAYED! HE’S CAPTURED!" Akia reminds them they owe the WLF for the rescue, and Rimardo whines, "I am tired of owing people shit." Akia announces, "There are Drow!" expecting horror and heroic resolve. Instead KP, Rimardo and Corrin roll their eyes and complain: Rimardo would rather go kill Walton, KP wonders about spellbooks, Rimardo asks why they must always be the ones to kill the Drow, and Corrin notes that someone finally calls the dark-skinned elves Drow only to "sub-contract out the killing, again." While the three argue, the leader of the WLF cell asks the Paladin, "Do you really work with these people?" Rimardo: "Yes, and he pays us well." The WLF leader asks Bayard whether he eats his brioche without butter, "a severe insult to Bayard’s honor."

Akia explains that the party was rescued because they are needed for an operation against the Drow, and that as independent contractors there will be no reprisals: it "will be viewed as a rogue element in the city unrelated to the vital work of Fax." Rimardo: "We are disposable!" Akia: "No, independent operators." Rimardo keeps interrupting to ask about money until Akia demands, "Can I finish?" Akia says the WLF knows the Drow are taking slaves "Because we have communed with our god Heironeous and he confirmed that they are Drow, not dark-skinned elves." They know where the slaves are stockpiled and shipped by boat, and that the Drow intruders keep their gold there. KP: "Slaves are valuable!" Akia looks at him "like the way Che Guevara would have looked at Evita Peron." If the WLF fought alongside them, Akia says, the party would no longer be independent contractors; instead the WLF will make it look like the party figured out that the men who tried to capture them were slavers working for the Drow. Rimardo, shocked: "Those were slavers?" Munir, another WLF member, says, "You guys are slow on the uptake. Paladin, are you sure this is the best you could do for allies?"

Akia lays out Walton's campaign: Carc told Walton to take care of his problem. Walton tried to get them arrested at the gate by starting a riot, but they just stood there; he took their weapons hoping they would protest, but they did not; he sent the slavers, and the party killed them; finally he sent the guard to arrest them, expecting them to be killed either way. "He did not count on the Paladin going with the terrorists when the WLF showed up." KP: "We owe him a visit." Rimardo: "To think he was our friend, even though we never met him."

The objective is "a lovely suburban house inside the city," a three-story house rather than a dungeon, cave or keep. A long rest is ruled out: the Drow will be told of the escape and will leave. The WLF says the Drow feel invincible because they own the town, are vulnerable to sunlight, always go out at night, and are thought to sleep during the day. KP proposes burning the house down; the WLF objects that fire will draw a crowd. The house has shades and boarded windows, and the party thinks they can open the windows to let in sunlight. With the sunlight ending, they attack now, going in through the cellar per the stolen floor plan. Rimardo says Corrin will handle it, "being an assassin and all."

Corrin sneaks into the cellar, finds a Drow guard and three prisoners, a merchant and his two guards, and kills the Drow in one blow. Bayard: "Good job, although I am not okay with the methods." The merchant, Tilde, was captured in the city. He reports, "There was 7 Drow, and now 6," then corrects himself: "Well dark-skinned Elves, they pretended to be Drow," blaming the kingdom of Celene. KP asks about his wares. Tilde stutters, then blurts out, "Slaves." Rimardo: "What? You are slavers?" Tilde, indignant: "Yes! This is Fax, this is the biggest market for slaves on the Wild Coast." His operation was competition to the Drow: "Independent slave operators are not allowed to have a business anymore." Rimardo suggests "Import/export?" and the merchant agrees: "Your words, not the ones I would choose." The two bodyguards say, "We are done," and scamper off. Rimardo, in disgust, tells the merchant, "You can go too."
