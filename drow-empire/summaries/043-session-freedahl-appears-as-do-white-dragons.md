# Chapter 43 – Freedahl Appears, As Do White Dragons

Date: The Abyss, Day 99 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
Freedahl appears. The demon lord looks at his dead spawn and says, "Ziamar has chosen poorly. And that I should be gracious for your destruction of this traitorous hellspawn." KP and Rimardo nod. Then, looking at the Paladin, he adds, "my graciousness will be limited to not executing you for killing Ziamar." After an awkward pause, KP stares at Freedahl unblinkingly: "so, Freedahl, did you know that this majordomo has been your loyal ally this whole time?" The majordomo seizes the opportunity to grovel: "Yes, yes, it is I who warned your champions of the threat that Ziamar presented to your rule."

Rimardo tries to convince Freedahl to make the majordomo the new ruler of Plagueridge Rampart, but Freedahl will have none of it. The Drow were able to subvert a demon Lord loyal to him, so he must stay and defend the city himself — and the party has not completed its side of the bargain: "The deal I made to avoid your world and for you to secure my city from the Drow empire. You have done nothing." Rimardo objects — "now hold on it's not like we have done nothing" — and Freedahl erupts: "insolence! I am a demon lord of the abyss, and if I said you have done nothing, you have done nothing!" KP raises his eyebrows: "well, we did reopen trade between the city and the other planes!" The demon lord concedes that the citizens are most happy, and announces that the city will no longer be called by its Drow name Plagueridge Rampart "but instead by its demonic name Flaka." He lists the city's pleasures: "The fine brothels, the torture chambers, the magic shops full of curios from all the planes, and most importantly of all the fine cuisine."

"gnome?" Rimardo hesitates. Freedahl makes a sour face — "oh no, that was ghastly meat" — and explains that some of the finest restaurants in interdimensional space have opened in Flaka, including one whose chef makes a cuisine called "fusion": elk cut into thin slices and infused with a balsamic glaze, "with a side of mashed potatoes and a sprig of the finest barley on the planes. No demon worthy of his salt would eat gnome meat." Asked what main course they should provide if they invited him to dinner, Freedahl says he needs none of their nutrients, but there is one plane with "an animal with antlers" which, cooked with garlic-infused sauce and crème fraîche on mixed fresh greens, "was to die for." If they could get that meat, he would be delighted to attend. Then: "but enough of this discussion of the menus in restaurants and meals, we have a more serious problem to deal with, the Drow empire." He needs a few days to consolidate and review what Ziamar has done; the party should explore and shop, then return to discuss "what you must do to defeat the House Zollazo and the Drow Empire." Freedahl leaves, and KP insists that they go shopping.

The majordomo tries to ingratiate himself, offering to take them to Tharkun and "his Mystical Ink Scriptorium." The shop is a two-story half-timbered building with dwarf-wrought iron furnishings, full of the constant scratching of magical quills. Tharkun is a short, half-crazed old gnome with an earring in one ear, tattered green robes and a gash over his head. "It is the dragon's fault. I was unable to get a mead this morning. And the soap shortage was dragons as well." Asked which dragons, he answers, "The little ones! The little ones that fly around and confuse people and mess things up," adding, "You can never see the dragons if you look for them." KP studies him — the dilating pupils, shaking hands, sweaty brow, the darting gaze — and notes that tiny dragons in the abyss have never been documented in any book he read in the gnomish library. He concludes that Tharkun is afflicted with abyssal madness.

Rimardo tries a hard bargain — "We will buy every scroll you have in bulk." — but KP is underwhelmed by the scrolls and refuses to buy. As the party walks out, the majordomo discreetly waves at Tharkun, who catches the gesture: "It would appear you want the good stuff." A secret door reveals much more powerful scrolls, but this batch has no mirror image scroll. Tharkun, insulted, says, "As a result, I will sell you that scroll for 1000 gold pieces." KP is outraged: the previous batch of 10 scrolls was 3500 gold pieces, and now he faces paying 1000 gold pieces for a single scroll. In the standoff, KP suddenly looks into a corner — "did you see that?" — and tells the terrified Tharkun that purple dragons are "looking for a new place to relocate," and will bring "over 100 purple dragons, to nest in your fine establishment." Tharkun panics: "they will eat all of my scrolls! I'll sell you my mirror image scroll for a thousand gold pieces!" Rimardo sighs, "we will buy the entire first lot for 3500 gold pieces." "sold!" says Tharkun, eyes twinkling, "And for a moment, no one is quite sure if the gnome was insane." Leaving the shop, KP sees several spells vanish from the scrolls, leaving only arcane power: Tharkun had written spells that did not exist in KP's time-space continuum, and KP had paid for magic he could not use.

Rimardo asks for other magic shops, and the majordomo leads them a hundred feet to an alchemist's shop, a two-story building of tortured, gnarled wood, dripping blood, lit by flasks of glowing oil. Inside is a stout female gnome. Rimardo winks: "Ah, KP, you should get the racial discount." The proprietor snarls, "you do not understand how gnomish business works." She is Xyrona Jolin Qimrya Daphici, 322 years old, with long wavy blond hair shaved on the left side, brown eyes, silky golden skin, 3’2” tall, and her right hand is missing. She drones on about her wares until her daughter appears: "Oh, mommy, mommy, these are adventurers! I want to talk to them." "Anette, this is not the time," says her mother.

When Rimardo asks for a bulk rate, Xyrona is flabbergasted: "There are no bulk discounts or racial discounts amongst the gnomish merchants." She asks the majordomo whether this is the business he will bring her, "and if so, I am not sure I will give you that much of a kickback." Rimardo points out that adventurers offering to buy everything must be rare, and she rounds on him: she stayed in the abyss because "once that portal opened, heroes would be coming in regularly to fight the demonic hordes," and they would all need "Potions! Potions!" She explains that the portal jumps from plane to plane, demons exit and cause chaos, heroes invade the abyss in return, and "That's going to make for great business." When KP asks whether the demons will know she supplied the heroes, she waves him off: "Obviously, you have not read the latest book published by the great gnome sage Davston Davnitorium."

KP loses all interest in potions: "you have a book on inter-planar trade? Can I buy it?" Xyrona offers a significant discount for one of his tomes, but KP will not part with any book, and when Rimardo suggests he could since he has already read it, KP looks at him and Rimardo backs off. Corrin sharpens his daggers. KP tells Rimardo, "You clearly demonstrated a complete lack of skill in trying to get any deal out of a gnome." Chastised, Rimardo walks out and sells the party's gems to a local jeweler. KP proposes having a scribe copy both their books for a swap; Xyrona asks whether he means the local scribe "who has a peculiar fixation on dragons," and sighs on learning KP told him about the purple dragons. Fortunately she has a "purple juice" — "it's just water with some purple coloring" — that Tharkun believes is a purple Dragon repellent. KP and Rimardo laugh heartily.

On the way to the scribe's shop Xyrona asks who keeps the originals; when KP says everyone keeps their own, she cuts the discount to 10% from 20. KP objects that he has more books than she does, and she settles on 15%: "Don't push your luck further." KP learns she is not an alchemist but a middleman, "or middle gnome," procuring potions from salvagers of dead adventurers, adventurers needing cash, magic users and alchemists, and the local authorities who find bodies. Their shared interest in economics, and in inter-planar trade, leads her to a momentous decision: "I will give you a 20% discount." She stares at KP a little too intently, and the rest of the party thinks, "no, this ain't happening."

After buying the potions, the group visits the Cherry and Merry Arm Emporium, a two-story building of enchanted stone with a scorch-marked courtyard, kept by Cherry, a 40-year-old halfling with long wavy grey hair, brown eyes and rough grey skin, tall for a halfling at 3’6”. The majordomo introduces the group as the heroes of Plagueridge Rampart "or Flaka as Freedahl prefers the city be called." Nothing in the store interests them.

Out in the street a desperate messenger rushes up: "they are attacking! Poison gas in the streets, and teams of Drow attacking key defenders." The party is confused that the Drow Empire would attack Flaka, but in the distance there are clouds of green smoke, and much closer are four white dragons. The dragons are invisible but surprise no one, the party being very paranoid about attacks from the sky. Corrin casts air walk from a scroll — "His voice is heard in the worlds of magic, but the world hears nothing." KP casts a magic missile at a dragon, seeing it with his gem of true seeing, and to his horror sees the spell absorbed in a shimmer of blue, which he recognises as some form of an ioun stone.

The first dragon breathes frost on Bayard, Rimardo and KP. Rimardo casts a seventh level banishment; the dragons fail to counter it, but only one is banished, and when another dragon's breath breaks Rimardo's concentration, the banished dragon reappears. Corrin walks up to a dragon, which attacks him with claws and bite. The returned dragon breathes on Rimardo and Bayard again, and this time Rimardo dodges. Bayard climbs some boxes onto a roof and lands a blow on a perched dragon for 36 points of damage. Corrin, with foresight cast on him, stabs a dragon twice with his rapiers for 46 points of damage. KP casts a Sunburst centered 10 feet above his head; two dragons fail to counter it, but "these are no ordinary dragons, but creatures bred in the abyss." KP enters a house; inside, the chapter says, "he sees a family cowering in fear," and the head of the family declares, "now hear this, sir, this is my house, and I will not have you here." KP says he will be gone shortly.

"Istus watches and decides that the strand that is Rimardo's life is not to be cut, yet." Rimardo heals himself and those around him, then bravely hides in another house. The dragon facing Bayard jumps off the roof to be near Corrin and far from the Paladin's sword, and "bites the buttered brioche." Corrin is clawed twice. Bayard, "without fear and without reproach," jumps to the roof across the alley and hits the dragon that tried to escape him twice, using his divine smite to inflict the maximum damage possible, as the chapter ends.
