# Chapter 39 – Choices

Date: The giants' plane, Day 1 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
Selzyt sighs: "It’s one of those planes." Before he can drone on, KP remembers something he read at the gnomish library: "it’s got clams!" The gem pulsates — "No, much worse." The party faces an ethical dilemma: "the Drow whom you call the quote-unquote bad guys, have allied themselves with the quote-unquote good guys." Selzyt really wishes he could air quote. At first the party discounts his tale, Rimardo choosing to disbelieve, but Selzyt reminds them that as the failsafe, people forget and talk about things in his presence. The Drow have allied with the gnomes and the dwarves against their natural allies, the Giants.

A few Hill Giants appear from the edge of the forest and a pair of stone giants on the cliffs overhead. As the Hill Giants are about to speak, KP sneezes loudly, and Rimardo seizes the moment: "hail friends, we have defeated these Drow and secured this for the Giants!" A stone giant puts down his stone and shouts, "no, no, no, he’s a gnome from Tingleville." Rimardo explains that KP is not from here and that they share a common enemy in the Drow, but the giants want their treasure back: "The gnomes stole our treasure. And you stole our treasure. Give it all back!" Rimardo — thinking Istus didn't give them all this treasure so he would hand it back to "a pack of dimwitted Giants" — tries to haggle, then shows them a dead Drow and sneers that the party did their job for them. The stone giant refuses to trust them: "You are allied with the gnomes who are our slaves!"

Rimardo rolls his eyes at a plane "where speciesism is a thing," looks at Bayard, at Corrin, at the sky, and complains to Istus: "really? I mean, it’s 595, you’ve sent me on a quest to save the multi-verse, and you drop me here?" Disgusted, he walks off: "Throw the stones, we’re done here." The stone giant's dull, vacant face transforms. "Sir! Sir! Perhaps we were a bit too hasty!" The superiority, he explains, is "just a trope we use when we encounter outsiders"; really "the gnomes are a ready labor supply, and we use religious imagery and speciesist vocabulary to justify a regime of worker exploitation."

Bayard says, "I agree. This does not feel right." KP mutters that allying with Hill Giants is not the most questionable thing they have done; Rimardo smiles, remembering that they have allied with any demonic or evil power that opposes Lolth; Corrin sharpens his knives as he reflects on Lundbeams. KP cannot resist asking, "exactly why is allying ourselves with Hill Giants worse than installing a demon Lord as the mayor of a city?" Bayard reflects that the structure of labor relations is "a vital tenet of the free peoples of the Wild Coast," not a matter of good or evil but a fact of the economy, "like choosing one brioche from one baker and not another." He turns to the Giants: "I will give you your gold."

Rimardo blurts out that their next destination is Tingleville, and the stone giant warns that "that is exactly in the wrong direction if you are interested in getting rid of the Drow." The map Selzyt uncovered shows they must go north. The stone giant puts a monocle on his eye, takes up a walking stick, and explains that once the Drow are gone, "the Giants of Bolglandia" will reassert their dominance and reinstitute the old labor relations — "a form of labor called slavery." KP is not happy and wonders if there is time to kill both the Drow and the Giants; Selzyt says the Giants are not too deadly, but there are too many. KP reluctantly accepts that he must press on to save the library and secure the information about inter-planar Drow economics from the Drow capital; "His thesis, and more importantly, his reputation as a sage, requires sacrifices on the parts of many."

Rimardo asks the giant, "as a future conqueror," for the techniques used to subjugate the gnomes. "oh, the usual. We burned their villages; we killed their menfolk." The giant adds that the Drow did the same thing here, dividing the giants, and "The gnomes offered them something of more value than we could have." Probing further, Rimardo learns that the Giants meet in their capital city to plan battles against the gnomes; defeating them there would break their dominance, but it would take too much time. "But there is always later, Rimardo thinks to himself." Asked why a small gnome must defeat their enemies for them, the giant explains that the last time the Giants killed a Drow emissary, many giants died and a worse thing happened to the giant King: "He was shrunk." At "only 2 feet tall," the king was stepped on "for his own dignity." Someone points out there are spells for that. "To Molril, then!" "Yes," says the giant, "and we will be here waiting for you."

The party takes a short rest after Rimardo discovers that the map is more representative than accurate: mountains are more like hills, cities more like hamlets, ranges short. Selzyt explains that Drow soldiers are not good at drawing outdoor maps.

Down the mountain pass, a group of well-armed gnomes on riding dogs comes around a hill. KP introduces himself and is delighted to meet fellow gnomes. The lead gnome says the Drow leader Molril has said the party attacked the outpost and killed the Drow commander. "You are correct," says KP. "Knazreponnium, the giants have clearly twisted you against your own kind," the gnome replies. "No, we’re just here to kill the Drow. Then we will be gone." "With that bit of typical KP diplomacy completed, the attack of the gnome begins."

Corrin's charge is parried; a gnome trips him prone and another hits him for 14, and he takes fire damage from their fire shield aura — "a gift from the Drow allies of the gnome, no doubt." Bayard hits the tripping gnome for 22 and takes nine from its aura; another gnome flanks Bayard for 14 plus nine. Rimardo wonders why they did not just lie about their alliance, then remembers they decided to let KP do the talking. KP smirks and casts cone of cold: the dogs die, the tripping gnome survives on one hit point thanks to death ward, and the others take 49 points of damage. Rimardo finishes the death-warded gnome; another drinks a potion of healing. Corrin stands and "pokes air with his rapier," taking 4 HP from the fire shield. Bayard's sword strikes gnome armor without effect, and he begins to wonder how powerful the Giants must be if the gnomes are this powerful. KP's lightning bolt drops the rest to 1 HP each. Rimardo kills the one closest to Corrin; a gnome hits KP for 29, screaming, "damn you!"; Corrin kills one and takes 9HP from the fire shield; Bayard smugly misses the last warrior, is parried, and "to add injury to insult" takes 9 HP of fire damage. Rimardo kills the last gnome.

On the gnomes the party finds a note describing them and orders to kill. KP wonders aloud why not alive; Selzyt says, "if they want to talk to you, they could always raise you from the dead." The party takes stock: killing the Drow lets the Giants reassert their dominance, but if they do not, "the multi-verse itself is at risk, or so says Lundbeams." Bayard, "ever the libertarian," says, "I’m not sure that the gnomes have earned the right for us to risk the entire multi-verse. After all, they did attack us." Corrin stares at him, thinking the Paladin "is willing to sacrifice an entire civilization for his ego." Bayard, sensing the disapproval, adds, "I am not going to go out of my way to get revenge, and I’m also not going to go out of my way to help them." Rimardo is delighted.

At Molril's keep, ravens fly in and out of the tower. KP casts invisibility on Corrin, who sneaks in and sees four ogres in the bottom room and a staircase leading up. Bayard declares the party will hide while Rimardo brings the tower down with an earthquake spell. They try to hide and fail. From 300 feet, Rimardo casts earthquake. The ravens fly out and spot Rimardo; the ogres and the Drow leader Molril come out "with falcons surrounding him," and a fissure under one corner collapses the tower. Rimardo sighs that any treasure is probably lost. It is a moment of introspection for him — he tried to save the slaves, expressed disgust at speciesist justifications, and now sacrificed treasure for the greater good. "Has the heroic Bayard rubbed off on him? Corrin worries. Bayard inwardly smiles."

Molril stares at the adventurers: "how curious you decided to help the Giants against the gnomes. This is an unexpected outcome. Something must be going on in the city. As for you, gnome, it would appear you are without honor like the Drow." KP is silenced.
