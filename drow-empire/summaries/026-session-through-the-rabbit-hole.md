# Chapter 26 – Through the Rabbit Hole

Date: 17 Goodmonth 595 to 18 Goodmonth 595 CY, then the Abyss, Day 1 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
The chapter opens in the portal chamber. Gruneel moves to the door and guards it well; after engaging in combat he decides it is too dangerous and too perilous and stands as far away as possible. KP appears to be under the influence of his more direct persona, "not the one that plays 13-dimensional chess."

As the group waits for the portal to be activated, Selzyt explains what will happen. A significant but not dangerous tremor will come, and once the shaking stops the portal chamber will occupy a space about 60 ft. under the Drow town of Plagueridge Rampart, which the local denizens call Flaka. The Drow will know of the arrival from the mild earthquake and will immediately send burrowing creatures, likely dragons, to create a passage from the surface to the portal chamber.

Rimardo is unhappy and begins to make new plans, alone, because his partner in magical mischief is missing. He decides the right strategy is to use Passwall to escape the chamber directly; the only trick is how to reach the hole in the roof. Istus, meanwhile, is confused about why her cleric does not do the obvious thing and start the passage from a place he can climb from, such as the side walls. KP suggests a wall of force to create a slide the group can climb. As the group debates ways to fly into a hole in the ceiling, Gruneel reminds them that a flying dragon is more dangerous than a dragon in a small room, and Rimardo sneers, "A dragon that doesn’t see us, is the least dangerous of all." Rimardo and Bayard review their options, and they all look grim: if the party flees without killing the dragons, the dragons will follow them up into the city and attack them from the sky.

Gruneel wonders whether they might ask Selzyt for a map of the city. Selzyt is delighted to share it, though he has Rimardo draw it from his memory, so it has errors. Rimardo is delighted to discover there are treasure houses, and Selzyt marks where he thinks the party will come out if they use passwall to escape. When Rimardo asks which layer of the Abyss they will arrive in, Selzyt answers, "A question! How wonderful. The 66th." Rimardo asks whether that is Lolth's layer, and Selzyt, almost rolling his eyes, explains that Lolth used to control the 66th, but "a different demonic power kicked her out of this layer." Before Rimardo can speak, Selzyt supplies "the word you are looking for is AWKWARD!" Graz’zt is the local demonic power that took the layer, and Freedahl, "the demon whose liege lord is Graz’zt," lusts after the portal. Lolth now resides elsewhere, no one is sure where, and the only certainty is that the way is through Maenerius, the capital of the Drow empire in the Abyss.

The party looks horrified at the prospect of taking on the Drow Empire, so Selzyt reassures them by explaining what it is: an area that a small number of Drow control using magic, terror and slaves. It does not rule over other Drow but over other groups, and it is quite different from Human Empires: a collection of Drow houses that align themselves to control an area for some time. When Rimardo tries to interrupt, Selzyt says "please go ahead" and, when Rimardo backs down, will have none of it: "Clearly, my information about the structure of the enemy you must defeat is so less important than any question or observation you would want to make. Ask your question?"
Rimardo asks Selzyt where the party should come out. Selzyt is shocked that it is a good question, and gives Rimardo a location: the site in front of the citadel.

Asked what to expect, Selzyt laughs: "To be slaughtered!" The dragons will fly into the sky and blast them with breath weapons, the other Drow will surround them, and "It will be a glorious death. But it will be a death." Rimardo proposes reaching the surface without the dragons knowing, and Selzyt is unimpressed. The dragons expect to find either the Drow or Selzyt with somebody, "For I am the failsafe," and if they find neither they will fly into the sky searching. But if the dragons were killed in the portal chamber, a long delay would be normal: dragons like to feast on their victims, the sounds of battle are expected, and the Drow will not expect anyone to escape. Killing the dragons would buy "several hours before the Drow would think to see what the heck was going on." Rimardo is confused about what to do next, and Selzyt reassures him: "you are the first group to make it this far," adding that he is an authority because "I literally have bet on my body on this."

Rimardo asks the gem who it is and how it intends to fight. Selzyt says he can't "unless you let me possess one of your team, for example, Gruneel!" Rimardo proposes raising a dead enemy for Selzyt to possess. Asked what happens when the Drow come through the portal, Selzyt says the Drow Empire uses the sending stone to communicate, but he does not know the password, and complains: "they never talk to me. They ignore me." He reasons that they withheld it to ensure he never pulled the stunt he is about to pull, and thinks a previous failsafe used this technique to escape. Rimardo wonders aloud whether a Drow he raised from the dead could be convinced to tell him Lolth's secrets. Selzyt admires his gumption and his naivete and reminds him that Lolth has far worse punishments than death for Drow who betray her. Rimardo says he looks forward to those punishments, and Selzyt corrects him: "You are not Drow, so you cannot betray her! The punishments are much more gruesome but different." Rimardo is curiously not reassured.

Rimardo and Bayard resign themselves to battling the dragons. Selzyt does not want them to abandon passwall: "The idea of passwall is ingenious," because the Drow will guard the holes the dragons went down. "who will be guarding the holes?" asks Rimardo, and the exasperated gem answers, "The Garrison!" Bayard and Rimardo shout in unison, "What garrison?", and Selzyt explains again that Flaka has a Drow garrison that guards the city and is near where the dragons will burrow. He mumbles that "using passwall to avoid the garrison is a very clever idea" and that he wishes he had told "that other group." Rimardo decides from the map to appear in front of the treasure houses. Selzyt reminds them of several hundred slaves who hate the Drow, a group that can distract the garrison while the party secures the city. Rimardo points out the slaves will be slaughtered; Bayard, a military man, says they will be a useful diversion. Delighted with the Paladin's practical morality, Rimardo agrees: "Yes, I could go for a plan that involves the Drow slaughtering the slaves while we seize the city!"

The party hides and prepares. The dragons arrive, look around, and do not see them, so the heroes surprise the four medium-sized white beasts, which lack the glowing red eyes. Bayard has a critical miss, both times. Corrin sneaks up on the second dragon, hits both times for 70 points of damage, and takes damage from a fire shield spell and armor of agathys protecting the dragon. Rimardo's banishment is resisted. KP's direct personality wants a fireball, but the KP that plays 13-dimensional chess convinces him to try fire bolts first; he hits the second dragon for 20 HP, halved to 10 by its fire resistance. Asked to attack the second dragon, Bayard says he cannot reach that far. "Do you have a bow?" asks Rimardo, and the buttered brioche answers with disgust: "No. That would not be honorable!" Everyone stares at Gruneel and realizes that, once again, he chooses to just guard the door.

The man from Juste-Havre does 36 HP with his first attack and 34 with his second, and the assassin and the paladin seem to be engaged in a game of who can top this. He takes 17 HP for his trouble. Rimardo's cantrip fails, and Corrin says in his silent tongue, "Another useless attack from Rimardo. Good for you. Doing nothing again." Rimardo glares and moves to give Corrin advantage. Corrin kills the second dragon, then turns on the dragon Bayard was fighting for 5 HP, and takes 5 HP. The chapter then has the first dragon skitter away from Bayard, and the dragons breathe on the entire party for 22 HP each; Corrin is unaffected, and Rimardo slips and falls into the breath weapon. The three remaining dragons breathe on Corrin and Bayard; Corrin dodges, and Bayard takes 22 HP. Selzyt snorts, "You whine too much. The dragons are dangerous, you said." Rimardo whispers, "We don’t whine that much."

KP casts fireball instead of a lightning bolt, for 37 HP halved by resistance. The dragon Bayard was fighting dies, the fourth dragon takes 17 HP, and the third flattens itself to the ground and takes 8 HP. Bayard hits the third dragon for 25 and 32 HP and takes 8 HP. Rimardo's cantrip is stopped by the dragon's wisdom. Corrin trades blows with the third dragon, and his second attack, about to miss, is changed at the last moment by his luck and hits. The dragons' bites and claws miss. KP's fire bolt kills the third dragon for 15 HP. Bayard hits the last dragon for 28 and 24 HP. Rimardo, deflated by Corrin's vicious criticisms of his ineffectiveness in combat, does nothing; the feeling of worthlessness overwhelms his desire to act. Corrin kills the last dragon.

A voice from the gem says, "Bravo!" Bayard, sword covered in dragon blood, asks, "These were the dragons that were supposed to have enough firepower to obliterate us?" Rimardo blows on his fingers in the universal gesture for "YOU ARE BURNED." Selzyt explains pompously that he ensured anyone still alive was either under his control or severely wounded: "The dragons were just a mopping-up operation!" Bayard laughs. Selzyt insists, "I was an essential part of the Drow defenses." Rimardo asks, "Who is this guy again?", and Selzyt, missing the sarcasm, answers, "THE FAILSAFE!"

The party uses a passwall and then furniture to enter the hole and climb up into the city. Remembering how the young men of Juste-Havre who did not die climbing the rocks used ropes, Bayard declares, "Safety comes first!" When they get out of the tunnel, Selzyt apologizes for being wrong: the party arrives not near the Treasury. The heat of the infernal domain overwhelms them; KP, Corrin and Rimardo feel somewhat exhausted, while Bayard, used to such weather, is unaffected and concludes that his god must be protecting him. They see a Drow patrol pass, and a bar. Selzyt is excited: "KP, this is it!" and "The best place to get a drank in Flaka!" Bayard decides blending into a bar beats standing in the middle of the road. Rimardo doubts a human can blend into a city of the Drow, but the group notices there are precious few Drow; the street holds demons, humans, and an infrequent Drow patrol, alongside a torture store, a store of demonic supplies, and a necromancer supply chain.

The bar's name is the "Tavern of the Gilded Dryad." Rimardo laughs at the sign until he realizes the gilded statue beside it is an actual dryad killed while being gilded alive, her horror and pain frozen in time, and he is saddened. Inside, the tavern is dim and smoky, boots stick to the shiny floor, the bar is mostly empty, and the innkeeper, a hobgoblin, seems happy. Bayard tries Rimardo's opening line, "What is a hobgoblin like you doing in a place like this?", and the hobgoblin grouchily replies, "I work here what else would I be doing? What drink will you have?" Bayard asks for the best and receives four skull glasses of "The best mead!" He sniffs, swirls and studies it until the hobgoblin threatens, "You are not one of those mead tasters?" Bayard says he just likes to appreciate the best kind of mead, swallows the cup, and notes a unique berry taste. Rimardo, an expert in mead from his days wandering the Wild Coast, asks contemptuously, "Berries?" The irritated barkeep explains that "the only food in this town is the damned goodberries that the Drow control," and that the one alternative is humanoid slaves the Drow bring in, which you can buy, kill and eat. KP asks what they taste like; "Depends on the humanoid," and "Gnomes are particularly tangy." KP asks whether he has a recipe to hide the tangy-ness, and the hobgoblin corrects him: "No, to accentuate the tangy-ness." Rimardo learns a Gnome slave costs 20 GP, but there is no Gnome meat on the menu: "No, the only dwarf."

The group pays in Wild Coast currency. The hobgoblin finds the coins odd, and Rimardo claims, "We are from the 115th layer of the Abyss!" The hobgoblin shouts, "these are not abyssal coins!" The cleric asks whether he has been to the 115th layer, and the hobgoblin screams that his partner is from there and would know. Unimpressed by Rimardo's argument, the barkeeper threatens to help the Drow forces, who will pay him 100GP per head. Rimardo realizes the problem is coinage, not gold, and the hobgoblin grins: "if you give me 400 GP of your coins, I could forget that you were ever here!" Corrin tells Rimardo to point out that they are four heavily armed adventurers against one, and the hobgoblin replies, "Are you feeling lucky freaks?" While Rimardo and Bayard confer silently, the barkeep summons two demons, apparently bouncers, and raises the price from 100 GP to 500 GP each. Rimardo and Corrin walk out and the others follow, leaving the hobgoblin shocked and the demons confused that the group is not cowering. Outside, the party starts to run, and moments later the proprietor of the Gilded Dryad runs into the street screaming, "ENEMIES OF THE DROW!"
