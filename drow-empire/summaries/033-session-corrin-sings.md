# Chapter 33 – Corrin Sings

Date: Brungril Forge, Day 1 (??? on Oerth; from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-13)

## Summary
The chapter opens on the aftermath: four quaggoths had massacred 60 of the 150 slaves. Rimardo is still distraught, Corrin still confused, Bayard impressed. The control room holds dead white dragons, slaves and quaggoths, and four tunnels that probably lead into whatever plane the party has landed on. Through one tunnel the group finds a room with a collection of goods, a lava flow and a bridge — identical to the one next to the portal in Oerth in the Wasted Isle. KP stares at the lava and achieves insight: it flows in the same direction as in the last portal room, so there must be a connection between the lava, the elemental plane of fire and earth, and the interdimensional forces that power the portal. He realizes a seminal book could be written, a counterargument to the sages who have long argued these things are not connected. KP is excited. Meanwhile, Bayard examines the bridge and crosses it.

Marco enters the chamber and announces, "the storage chamber of the Drow," then shouts to his fellow dwarves, "we have arrived." Asked whether he is certain this is his plane, he answers that the goods in the corner contain crafts the Drow desire, made in Brungril Forge. Among the knickknacks, bales of food, utensils and weapons, KP finds an exotic copper and silver mask set with jewels worth 1099 gold pieces, and writes in his notebook that "the inter-planar economy of the Drow relies on foodstuffs being brought in from their colonies. Destruction of this food source would cripple the Drow empire." Marco tells him, "you are a funny one," and turns to Corrin, whose appearance has changed because of his belt of dwarvenkind: "you’re short for a dwarf." Corrin stares. "You’re skinny for a dwarf too, but you have a pretty beard."

Bayard leads the party down the hallway, and Marco announces that the dwarves will follow "from a safe distance. Close enough to hear you, but far enough from the fireball." In a neat, clean, empty apartment with a bed and a desk they find a box with gems worth 5000 gold pieces. Rimardo finds a note in Drow and, staring suspiciously at KP, hands it to him — KP has a helm of comprehend languages — while Mr. Corrin stands at the ready in case KP lies about its contents. The note calls the Thane Damrith a useful idiot who chose to benefit from trade with the Drow empire and military support against the Duergar. KP is confused; Marco understands at once: "The Drow will support the dwarves for as long as they are useful, and then they will ally themselves with their natural friends the Duergar."

Asked who Damrith is, Marco calls him "a lying sack of potatoes, a traitor to his Thane," and the dwarves join him in a cacophony of curses until he raises his hand for silence. "Damrith is a traitor. Fonouc is the rightful Thane of Brungril Forge." Damrith allied himself with "that scum-sucking Drow called" — Marco cannot remember the name until someone whispers it in his ear — "Antyln." When Rimardo asks why the dwarves' enslavement did not warn Damrith, Marco explains that there was a coup, and the defeated were sent to the Drow empire to die. "But we have come back with allies that will let us overthrow the false Thane." KP earnestly looks around: "which?" Marco: "We earned this. We fought and bled for you. You owe us this. And the Paladin promised." Bayard looks shocked; Marco assures him they have friends in Brungril Forge who will help.

Selzyt pipes up, "oh dear," and Rimardo, looking at the gem, says in disgust, "you didn’t possess any of the quaggoths we were fighting." Selzyt apologizes: he did not want to reveal his alliance, so that if they died he could keep his role as the failsafe. "I mean, I want to help you, but not die." He explains that on this plane the Drow Empire will align itself with some faction before it kills everyone and seizes everything: "Damrith is using the Drow who are using him." Rimardo proposes sharing the note with the locals to undermine Damrith; Marco answers that they are outsiders, and without Fonouc they cannot rally the dwarves. "I know Fonouc’s alive. If he were dead, I would have felt it in my bones." Selzyt mutters, "Not across interdimensional space, you moron."

The group passes through barracks with neat rows of beds and the quaggoths' resting area, where among the debris, feces and rotting food they find a silk handkerchief inscribed with a create undead spell. They discover a common area with a small shrine to Lolth, recently inhabited, clean, and not hastily abandoned. As they discuss the odd missing Drow, Selzyt speaks irritably from inside Bayard's bag: "they’ve set up an ambush. They know you’re here."

KP casts invisibility and detect magic, listens at the door and hears nothing. Mr. Corrin prepares his daggers with Drow poison and follows him in; unnoticed, they see the markings of a deadly magical trap on the floor. KP dispels the runes, but the spell reveals his position. Corrin attacks a Drow in the southwest corner for 27 HP of damage. A Drow from the North-East corner casts darkness on KP and Corrin; KP plans to hide in it, but the Drow directly north of him can see into magical night and shoots him with a hand-held crossbow. Offended, KP considers sunbeam but casts fireball instead. Bayard smiles inwardly — first Rimardo heals the slaves, and now KP resorts to "brutal frontal violence" — and metaphorically pats himself on the back for his leadership. He pats himself "a little bit too quickly": magical fire heals these Drow, and the injured one is at full strength. KP recalls that fire healed the white dragons and that many Drow loyal to this Empire have magical abilities not common to all Drow.

The Drow hit Corrin for seven and 10 points of damage. Rimardo dispels the magical darkness. Bayard flanks and hits for 17, then positions himself so Corrin can flank, and Corrin hits for 31. A Drow flanks Bayard for seven; the Drow in the northwestern corner casts darkness around Corrin, KP and Bayard. KP now casts his sunbeam, doing 26 HP of damage to every Drow except the one who cast the darkness; the Drow Bayard and Corrin were fighting dies and the other two are blinded. A blinded Drow casts faerie fire, but it affects no one. Rimardo's word of radiance deals 13 HP of damage; Bayard and Corrin each kill a blinded Drow. The last Drow hits KP for seven but cannot break his concentration; KP's sunbeam deals 25, Rimardo's toll the dead 21, and Bayard finishes him off.

Marco finds a map to Brungril Forge: an hour's hike through the under dark. Bayard searches the privy and finds 20 pearls inlaid with silver worth 91 gold pieces each. Corrin smiles at Rimardo, since Istus must be smiling on their adventures, but Rimardo answers, "Istus mocks us. We have all this gold and nothing to spend it on." Marco reminds him they are going to a dwarven city famous for its artisanship in all the realms, and Rimardo asks about a discount. "Rimardo, I Marco promise you a discount if you killed Damrith and put Fonouc on the throne. But if you do not do those things, there will be no discount." During a short rest Bayard realizes he has no spells to heal himself: "now I am just a fighter. No, I am not just a fighter. I am Bayard, a man whose brioche is buttered, who is without fear and beyond reproach."

On the way to Brungril Forge, Marco stops everyone. An adult myconid called Pelynumph appears and speaks with Marco in gestures and sounds, which KP understands thanks to his magical helm. "Marco, it is so good to see you. The myconid needs your help." The Drow Empire has decided that myconid flesh is a delicacy and is slaughtering their kind. Noticing that KP understands, the Myconid spreads spores over the group, connecting everyone — and suddenly everyone stares at Corrin, because now they will finally be able to hear him speak. Pelynumph has been tasked with finding heroes or allies to stop the harvest. He explains that the dwarves are willing allies of the Drow, made powerful by the alliance, while "the Duergar are slavers for the Drow, and the dwarves tolerate them." Bayard looks at him in horror; Marco protests, "Surely my kin is not so jaded with power." Pelynumph: "Marco, they have little choice. Whoever objects ends up in the slave pens like you." Fonouc lives, "but he is unwilling to take direct action." Someone remarks, "He needs independent contractors," and the Myconid gestures as if smiling and says to KP, "exactly!" Pelynumph does not know where the thane is, but any of the farmers in the main hall could find him.

Pelynumph asks whether the group wants to stay connected through his spores. Corrin looks angry — the Myconid will penetrate his most secret thoughts, and worse, they will hear his voice — but after looking at Rimardo and the group, he nods. Marco draws them a map of Brungril Forge. Rimardo asks, "Who are the Carls?" — "these are the noble houses whom we must win over," Marco says derisively.

The heroes decide to investigate the slave pits the Duergar control. Corrin sneaks in, and suddenly everyone hears Corrin's voice. Corrin sings. It is a beautiful voice, and his first words that anyone hears are "I am inside. There are eight." After his superb musical performance, he retreats. Bayard is offered two choices: ignore the Duergar and restore the rightful thane, who will free the slaves, or deal with the Duergar, who are allies of the Drow. "The last time we freed slaves, it caused havoc. Marco, I think we should free the slaves." Selzyt offers, "I could possess one of the Duergar." Corrin agrees, but does not wish to carry the gem, and then sings, "I shall assassinate! I shall assassinate!" The Duergar notice Corrin and shriek, "you thought you could surprise us?" Corrin sings, "I shall not assassinate."
