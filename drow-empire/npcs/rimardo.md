## Identity
Rimardo is a cleric of Istus whose adventuring role combines healing, protective magic, and battlefield control. He presents his work with the party as independent contracting, with treasure an explicit condition of service. [ch 049 / 049.07; ch 004 / 004.01; ch 008 / 008.09; ch 051 / 051.02]

## Personality and Motivations
Rimardo is acquisitive, payment-conscious, and protective of his share. He objects to unpaid commitments and remains willing to pursue additional compensation even when Bayard declines it. [ch 015 / 015.04; ch 025 / 025.05; ch 040 / 040.05]

He favors leverage, negotiation, and practical assessment of danger. His pragmatism can be ruthless: he endorsed using slaves as a diversion despite expecting their deaths, and privately considered an accommodation with the Drow. [ch 013 / 013.08; ch 015 / 015.04; ch 026 / 026.04; ch 029 / 029.02]

His compassion develops unevenly but materially. The deaths of slaves leave him grieving and uninterested in treasure; later, he sacrifices potential treasure for the greater good and accepts that glory can outweigh money. Financial motives nevertheless persist. [ch 032 / 032.04; ch 039 / 039.07; ch 051 / 051.04; ch 052 / 052.05]

He addresses divine power with striking familiarity, approaching Istus with requests before social courtesies and responding to a withdrawn boon with a shrug rather than prayer. [ch 050 / 050.03; ch 036 / 036.03]

The epilogue places Rimardo alive and working with Corrin a few years after the party’s breakup. Their magic show, *Rimardo and Corrin*, is famous across Oerth. They chose this career instead of becoming the semi-divine avatars Istus had planned; a specific residence is not established. [ch 052 / 052.06]

## Relationships
- **Corrin:** His enduring partner and frequent priority in combat. Corrin’s criticism can deeply undermine his confidence, while Rimardo’s growing altruism creates tension between them. Their partnership continues into the magic show. [ch 019 / 019.02; ch 026 / 026.05; ch 040 / moment; ch 052 / 052.06]
- **Bayard:** An employer and companion whose moral priorities repeatedly frustrate Rimardo. Their bond gains reciprocal care when Bayard brings him back from death and Rimardo responds with his strongest healing, but it ultimately ends as a business relationship. [ch 014 / 014.04; ch 045 / 045.04; ch 052 / 052.05]
- **Istus:** His deity, source of healing power, and a direct adviser. Their relationship includes both withheld assistance and substantial intervention; her eventual response to his chosen career affirms his free will. [ch 004 / 004.06; ch 032 / 032.03; ch 050 / 050.03; ch 052 / 052.06]
