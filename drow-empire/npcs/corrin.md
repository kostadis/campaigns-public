## Identity
Corrin is a professional assassin, mercenary, and follower of Istus. Rimardo presents him as the killing counterpart to his own healing role. [ch 004 / 004.01; ch 019 / moment] He later becomes Rimardo’s partner in the celebrated magic show *Rimardo and Corrin*. [ch 052 / 052.06]

His belt of dwarvenkind changes his height, build, and beard; its influence also develops his enthusiasm for Dwarven Opera. His singing voice earns particular praise from Marco. [ch 033 / 033.01; ch 035 / 035.04; ch 035 / 035.05]

## Personality and Motivations
Corrin treats killing as paid professional work and measures success heavily through reward. Insufficient earnings discourage retirement, treasure warrants additional searching, and the contract to neutralize a god becomes his greatest anticipated professional score. [ch 013 / 013.06; ch 018 / 018.02; ch 030 / 030.03; ch 049 / 049.05]

His faith shapes how he interprets fortune: he and Rimardo read success, wounds, and disappointing treasure as signs of Istus’s favor or judgment. He eventually accepts healing as the balance to his own killing, recognizing mercy within Rimardo’s service to their goddess. [ch 019 / 019.03; ch 022 / 022.02; ch 032 / 032.04]

He values tactical discretion and questions authority when orders expose him to danger. He also distinguishes suspicion from evidence, reminding Rimardo that Ziamar’s responsibility for hiring assassins remains unproven. [ch 005 / 005.05; ch 042 / 042.05]

Corrin guards his inner life, reluctantly accepting a spore connection that exposes his thoughts and voice. His humor can be cutting: he mocks Bayard’s missed attacks, and his criticism of Rimardo can undermine his companion’s confidence. [ch 033 / 033.06; ch 034 / 034.01; ch 026 / 026.05]

## Last Observed State
The final epilogue places Corrin a few years after the party’s dissolution, partnered with Rimardo in *Rimardo and Corrin*, a magic show famous across Oerth. They pursue this career instead of becoming the semi-divine avatars Istus had planned. Their precise location is not supplied. [ch 052 / 052.06]

Before that transition, Corrin traps Lolth in a mirror and subsequently collects his fee with Rimardo as the party breaks up. Lundbeams explicitly describes Lolth’s fate as imprisonment. [ch 051 / 051.04; ch 052 / 052.04; ch 052 / 052.05]
