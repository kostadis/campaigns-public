## Identity
Istus is the goddess of fate and Rimardo’s goddess. She appears to him as an old woman spinning wool. Her followers also call her the Lady of the Fate. [ch 032 / entry; ch 042 / entry; ch 050 / 050.03]

## Personality and Motivations
Balance is her stated constraint on divine action: she tells Rimardo that intervening in the battle would upset it, yet grants Corrin an anti-magic field beforehand. Her followers dispute whether that boon constitutes intervention. [ch 050 / 050.03]

She wants conversation beyond requests for aid and pointedly counts Rimardo’s questions and boon. Her manner in their conference is conversational, teasing, and precise about what he has asked. [ch 050 / 050.03]

Her assistance is selective. She denies Rimardo’s request for an escape gate for the slaves, but later provides a useful vision concerning the healing owls. She leaves her followers’ disputes about banishment and death’s door unresolved. [ch 032 / 032.03; ch 047 / 047.02; ch 023 / 023.03; ch 042 / 042.01]

Her followers often interpret wealth, hardship, and survival as signs of her judgment; these interpretations do not establish her motives. Her explicit response to Corrin and Rimardo choosing a magic show over her planned roles for them affirms free will. [ch 015 / 015.01; ch 019 / 019.03; ch 052 / 052.06]

## Last Observed State
In the epilogue set a few years later, Istus smiles and affirms free will after Corrin and Rimardo establish their famous magic show instead of becoming the semi-divine avatars she had planned. Her location is not specified in that account. [ch 052 / 052.06]

## Relationships
- **Rimardo:** Her cleric and direct interlocutor. He channels her divinity for healing, petitions her, and complains to her; she responds with decisions, warnings, visions, and advice. [ch 032 / 032.03; ch 036 / 036.03; ch 039 / 039.02; ch 047 / 047.02; ch 050 / 050.03]
- **Corrin:** A follower and recipient of her anti-magic boon, which persists through her will after he loses concentration. She intended both him and Rimardo for semi-divine service but smiles at their alternative choice. [ch 004 / 004.01; ch 050 / 050.05; ch 052 / 052.06]
- **Lolth:** Istus gives advice and a boon supporting the party’s effort to defeat her. Lolth calls her opponents Istus’ champions. [ch 050 / 050.03; ch 051 / 051.02]
- **Heironeous:** The summaries describe mutual hostility between their clerics; they do not establish personal hostility between the deities themselves. [ch 022 / 022.05]
