## Identity
*   Don-Jon Raskin was the newly appointed overseer of the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine, hired by a consortium in Neverwinter [ch 025 / entry; ch 025 / 025.04].
*   He presented himself as a seasoned adventurer with a career spanning prospector, miner, fur trader, privateer, and whaler, claiming to have discovered the "mother load of all mother loads" [ch 026 / 026.08].
*   He was killed by dwarves in a fatal argument that took place before the party reached the mine in chapter 33, after he intended to dismiss the more expensive dwarves [ch 033 / 033.04; ch 033 / 033.06].

## Personality and Motivations
*   Don-Jon was professionally confident to the point of arrogance, assuring his employers he needed no maps or preparatory information regarding the mine's layout or personnel [ch 026 / entry; ch 026 / 026.10].
*   He possessed a moral streak, expressing objection to the ivory trade and insisting, “I’m not that kind of man” [ch 026 / 026.08].
*   He was easily deluded or overly trusting, believing an Uthgardt barbarian tribe accepted him as a brother, despite Brewbarry recognizing they had treated him as a fool [ch 026 / entry; ch 026 / 026.08].
*   He admitted he knew nothing about the mine, its workers, or its current occupants [ch 026 / 026.10].
*   He prioritized cost-efficiency, deciding to dismiss expensive dwarven laborers in favor of wererats supplied with cheese, a decision that proved fatal [ch 033 / 033.03; ch 033 / 033.06].

## History with the Party
*   Don-Jon Raskin is the newly appointed overseer of the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine, hired by a consortium in Neverwinter, for which the party received an assignment to escort him safely to the site [ch 025 / entry; ch 025 / 025.04].
*   Townmaster Harbin Wester promised the party one hundred gold pieces for the escort, though Falcon also offered the party a share of the mine to investigate the poisoned waters, which Don-Jon was associated with via his employer consortium [ch 025 / entry; ch 025 / 025.04].
*   Vukradin identified that one member of Don-Jon’s employer consortium overlapped with Jenna’s list of Kraken Society associates, but insight checks suggested Don-Jon was a hired doer rather than a conspirator [ch 026 / 026.08].
*   Don-Jon admitted he possessed no preparatory information, maps, or knowledge of the mine’s personnel or layout, having assured his employers he did not need them [ch 026 / entry; ch 026 / 026.10].
*   During the escort, Don-Jon recounted his career as a prospector, miner, fur trader, privateer, and whaler, claiming to have found the “mother load of all mother loads” and expressing moral objection to the ivory trade [ch 026 / 026.08].
*   Don-Jon believed an Uthgardt barbarian tribe accepted him as a brother, although Brewbarry recognized they had treated him as a fool; Don-Jon remained oblivious to Brewbarry’s realization [ch 026 / entry; ch 026 / 026.08].
*   Upon arriving at the mine, Don-Jon was impressed by the Whiskered Gang’s maintenance of the site and considered them a potential labor force [ch 026 / 026.10].
*   Don-Jon initially objected to the party’s proposal to house wererats in the mine due to safety concerns and a lack of budget, but was persuaded by Vukradin that the gang could serve as both residents and workers in exchange for cheese [ch 026 / 026.11].
*   The party arranged for Don-Jon to become a consistent, large-scale cheese buyer from Big Al to secure a stable revenue stream and supply the wererats, though the party declined to explain Don-Jon’s specific motivation for the large quantity [ch 027 / entry; ch 027 / 027.04].
*   Don-Jon had contracted a dwarven clan to work the mine, expecting them to help remove the wererats, but instead found “a sucker in some bard fellow” (implied to be Vukradin) who convinced the wererats to work for cheese [ch 033 / 033.06].
*   Don-Jon intended to dismiss the more expensive dwarves in favor of the wererats, a decision that provoked a fatal argument with the dwarves [ch 033 / 033.03; ch 033 / 033.06].
*   Don-Jon died during this fight, not from a carrion crawler as initially claimed by Zeleen Varnaster; the crawler consumed his remains after he was already dead [ch 033 / 033.03; ch 033 / 033.04; ch 033 / 033.06].
*   The party discovered that restoring Don-Jon would require true resurrection because his body was consumed so completely [ch 033 / 033.03].
*   Zeleen surrendered Don-Jon’s share of the mine to the party in exchange for removing the carrion crawler, which was impeding operations after his death [ch 033 / 033.03].
*   Don-Jon’s chain was recovered from the carrion crawler’s lair, and Vukradin stated it should eventually be returned to his next of kin [ch 033 / 033.05].
- Don-Jon Raskin is deceased; the party discusses resurrecting him through a True Resurrection ritual following the battle [ch 046 / entry].
- The party considers creative alternatives for powering the resurrection, including a fanciful reference to "Red Wizards of Lathander" [ch 046 / entry].

## Last Observed State
*   Don-Jon is deceased; his body was consumed by a carrion crawler after he was already dead after a fight with the dwarves [ch 033 / 033.03; ch 033 / 033.06].
*   In Chapter 46, the party discusses his state, specifically debating the means to bring him back via a costly True Resurrection ritual [ch 046 / entry].
*   The party entertains creative, albeit fanciful, alternatives for powering this resurrection, including a reference to "Red Wizards of Lathander" [ch 046 / 046.06].

## Relationships

My. God, you are as dumb as Don-Jon Raskin" [ch 033 / 033.03; ch 033 / moment].
