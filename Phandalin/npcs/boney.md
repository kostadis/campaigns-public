## Identity
Boney is a sentient skeletal horse and the party’s mount and companion [ch 035 / entry; ch 046 / entry]. Originally named “Barney,” he adopted the name Boney after the party rejected other suggestions [ch 035 / entry; ch 035 / 035.02]. He rejects the classifications “undead” or “reanimated,” insisting instead on the term “unalived,” and occasionally specifies himself as “recently unalived” [ch 036 / entry; ch 051 / entry; ch 051 / moment]. He serves as the party’s meticulous record keeper and insists on his professional dignity, correcting those who label him a “butler horse” by declaring, “I’m not a butler horse. I’m a proud member of the livery staff” [ch 056 / entry; ch 056 / moment].

## Personality and Motivations
Boney displays a loyal, eager demeanor toward his mistress, Soma, whom he accepted as the reincarnation of Lady Alagondar [ch 035 / entry; ch 035 / 035.02]. He is obsessed with noble lineages and succession rights, a hobby developed during centuries spent dead in a tomb studying gravestones [ch 051 / entry; ch 051 / moment; ch 052 / entry]. He possesses encyclopedic knowledge of the Alagondar family tree and critically cross-examines claims to heritage, noting holes in timelines with scrupulous fairness [ch 051 / entry; ch 051 / 051.05; ch 052 / entry].

He prioritizes social etiquette and proper presentation over tactical advantage, often reminding the party of dinner engagements or criticizing their appearance [ch 056 / entry; ch 056 / 056.06; ch 057 / entry]. In combat, he is described by Rimardo as a “committed horse” who does not let his skeletal state affect his work ethic, actively dashing across battlefields to maintain magical silence zones and attacking enemies [ch 046 / entry; ch 047 / moment; ch 046 / 046.04].

## History with the Party
*   Boney is a sentient skeletal horse who initially rose from bones in a southern tunnel niche, expecting to serve Lady Alagondar; Soma convinced him she was her reincarnation, leading him to accept her as “your ladyship” [ch 035 / entry; ch 035 / 035.02].
*   Originally named “Barney,” he happily accepted the name “Boney” after the party rejected suggestions like Mr. Ed, Spirit, Black Beauty, and Butterscotch [ch 035 / entry; ch 035 / 035.02].
*   He was brought into the Dragon Barrow when old and dying, left with a promise of return, and killed by Will-o’-wisps; he retained sentience because he believed Lady Alagondar would return [ch 035 / entry; ch 035 / 035.02].
*   Boney correctly directed the party north toward the Dragon Slayer Sword, recalling he was to be buried “across the way” from Lady Alagondar [ch 035 / entry; ch 035 / 035.02; ch 035 / 035.04; ch 035 / 035.07].
*   The party debated retaining Boney as a sidekick despite his low hit points (13 initially); Soma declared she had bonded with him [ch 035 / entry; ch 035 / 035.02].
*   During a Will-o’-wisp encounter, Boney belatedly remembered the lights had killed him and described them as evil and boring [ch 035 / 035.03].
*   He rejects the labels “undead” or “reanimated,” preferring the identity “unalived” [ch 036 / entry; ch 036 / 036.01].
*   When asked if he knew Lady Alagondar’s ghost, Boney replied, “Nay! Well, yes, I do, but nay!” [ch 036 / entry; ch 036 / 036.03].
*   Boney travels with the party rather than being stabled, having been developed as a sixth-level sidekick [ch 040 / 040.02; ch 042 / entry; ch 042 / 042.04].
*   If destroyed, Boney returns the following day [ch 040 / 040.02].
*   Boney was the only creature initially caught and battered by animated roots in the Cursed Clearing; Soma stayed to free him before being ensnared herself [ch 042 / entry; ch 042 / 042.07].
*   Vukradin cited Boney as a way to test the trap’s effects during the Cursed Clearing encounter [ch 042 / moment].
*   Boney is the party’s skeletal horse and mount, who actively participates in combat by carrying a zone of magical silence on his saddle to suppress enemy spellcasting; he dashes 120 feet across the battlefield to position this zone and provokes an attack of opportunity on a fleeing enemy.
