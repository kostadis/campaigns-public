# Chapter 12: Untitled

Date: 2025-08-22

## Summary

With Moesko finally defeated, the party turned their attention to the fallen anchorite’s body and the crude quarters he had called home. The search immediately revived Vukradin’s concern over whether taking anything would amount to theft, while Soma offered a simpler philosophy: “All money is blood money until proven otherwise.” Resting on the floor beside Moesko, they found an opalescent conch that had once belonged to Miraal, the sea elf whose spirit had been trapped in restless torment. More unsettling still was what lay beneath his armor, crafted from the hide of a giant octopus: a gaping, hollow void where Moesko’s heart should have been, a wound that spoke of something far darker than simple devotion to a storm god.

Although Vukradin argued that they had recovered the conch they came for and should leave, Valphine insisted on searching Moesko’s crude living space. Among scattered prayer skulls dedicated to Talos, storm-weathered gear, and the remnants of his anchorite life, she uncovered a concealed compartment beneath a loose floorboard. Nestled on a bed of blackened seagrass was a palm-sized talisman made from a dark, iridescent substance that seemed to drink the light. It was smooth and cool to the touch, yet pulsed with a faint internal violet glow. One face bore the jagged lightning bolt of Talos, while the reverse concealed a more sinister mark: an inverted eye wreathed in delicate, coiling, tentacle-like lines. Brewbarry, unexpectedly the most perceptive member of the group, was the first to notice the hidden symbol. Valphine sensed a faint divine resonance in the object, but beneath it churned something far more alien—a dominant psionic force that had no place in conventional magic. When she focused upon it, she felt a rhythmic thrumming like a distant heartbeat or the fading echo of a monstrous call from the deep. Despite Vukradin dubbing it the “Talisman of Death,” the party took it with them.

Before climbing higher, Valphine invoked a healing prayer that granted the party the benefits of a short rest and restored their strength. Vukradin described the respite as an exceptionally restorative cigarette, while Soma recovered one of her expended transformations. From the roof, the party could see the shattered masts of five shipwrecks jutting from the western waters like broken fingers. The roof was enclosed by a low stone wall topped with an iron railing, and a ten-foot-tall wrought iron lightning rod reached toward the sky. Soma surmised that the rod had been used to channel the storm’s fury down into the altar below. Vukradin, determined to break anything that might still sustain the tower’s curse, set about smashing it, with Brewbarry lending assistance.

At the lighthouse’s pinnacle, three open archways allowed a pulsating green beacon to spill westward over the sea. Valphine stared directly into the light and was temporarily blinded. In the instant before darkness took her sight, however, she saw the truth: the beacon was not powered by flame or a magical crystal, but by a heart several sizes too large. It continued to beat long after Moesko’s death, and its unnatural pulsations generated the tower’s green light.

Valphine initially argued that the heart appeared to be serving a useful purpose and should remain in place. The party debated whether a heart could itself be evil and how the lighthouse would function without it. Vukradin recalled—through an old song about a lighthouse and its fire—that lighthouses traditionally burned ordinary flames rather than the disembodied hearts of evil clerics. He climbed to the beacon and repeatedly stabbed the heart, initially struggling to hit even the stationary organ, before finally destroying it and plunging the lighthouse into darkness. He then cast a light spell to hold back the black for an hour while the party sought fuel for the beacon.

The obvious source of wood was the wreckage below, although it was soaked and surrounded by sharks. Soma shifted into the form of a giant toad and plunged into the murky waters alongside Brewbarry. Underwater visibility extended only twenty feet. Their stated purpose was to recover wood for the beacon, but they soon began investigating whatever else the sea had swallowed. Aboard the Star-Crossed Lover, Brewbarry found a finely crafted magical halberd still clutched in the skeletal grip of the ship’s long-dead captain. He immediately claimed it while assuring the party that there was “nothing down here.”

The sea was not willing to surrender its treasures without a fight. A hunter shark emerged from the gloom and lunged at Brewbarry. Soma bit into it in giant-toad form, inflicting a savage wound, although the shark repeatedly escaped her attempts to grapple it. From the shore, Vukradin hurled a magical insult through the churning water, doing only a token amount of harm but leaving the predator rattled and at a disadvantage. Valphine called down radiant flame from above. When two more sharks circled in from the darkness, both initially turned upon Soma’s enormous toad form. Their bites tore through her temporary vitality and left her perilously close to collapsing, while Brewbarry finally entered a rage and began cleaving at the predators underwater.

The battle turned when Vukradin wove a magical melody that sent two sharks into an unnatural slumber. Valphine outlined the sleeping predators in shimmering violet light, allowing her companions to strike them with greater precision. The party carefully focused on one shark at a time so that the remaining creature would sleep through its turn. Brewbarry’s blow woke one, Soma finished it with a bite, and the last sleeping shark was overwhelmed by a coordinated assault. Soma briefly grappled the dying predator but rejected the suggestion that she drag it onto land to suffocate, declaring that she had no need to torture a shark. All three predators were ultimately killed, including the meanest of them, remembered in campaign canon as Daggermaw.

The wreckage proved too waterlogged to provide an immediate fire, though Vukradin began dragging wood ashore to dry and remembered that Moesko’s furniture could serve as kindling. With the danger temporarily cleared, Soma and Brewbarry continued their salvage. From the Sea Urchin they recovered a locked chest covered in clinging starfish. Two more locked chests came from the Golden Gull and the Orca. Soma also recovered a remarkable cloak from the Vainglory—a garment that had somehow remained pristine despite its time beneath the waves. Its colors could shift on command, making it perfectly suited to Vukradin’s theatrical sensibilities.

Soma presented the cloak to Vukradin as a potential stage costume, suggesting that its colors could pulse with his music and turn him into “a walking visualizer.” Vukradin admitted that it would look good on him and was plainly tempted, but he hesitated to claim property whose owner might still be found. Valphine, ever practical, opened the recovered chests over his objections, arguing that the contents had to be examined if the party hoped to identify their rightful owners. The chests revealed a delicate nine-inch blue quartz mermaid statuette, 120 electrum pieces, six pearls worth 100 gold pieces each, a wizard’s spellbook, and a Wand of Secrets. Brewbarry carried the cumbersome chests, while the party also harvested shark teeth and meat. Vukradin considered the meat lawfully earned and therefore acceptable to keep.

They brought the opalescent conch to Miraal at last. When it passed into her hands, her spirit stirred with relief. She thanked the party and revealed that Moesko had not acted entirely of his own will: something cold, calculating, and foreign had moved within him, twisting his devotion to Talos. Even in death, Miraal had heard fragmented whispers and dreams from the deep. Her final warning settled over the party like a coming storm: “The true storm gathers not in the sky, but in the depths below Leilon.” With that, she disappeared, finally at peace.

The friendly giant crab, Crabby, greeted the party enthusiastically and rejoiced to learn that Miraal had found peace. When he noticed Brewbarry’s magical halberd, he confirmed that it was the very treasure he had intended to retrieve as the party’s reward. This prompted another argument about where Brewbarry had acquired it and whether he had any right to keep it, leaving Vukradin feeling increasingly isolated in his moral position. Crabby also thanked the party for killing the sharks and eagerly accepted a share of the fresh meat, declaring it delicious. The party prepared to return to Phandalin, with the warning about Leilon waiting beyond their remaining local quests.

## Scenes

### Looting Moesko’s Body and Quarters

#### The party investigates the body of the fallen anchorite Moesko and searches his living quarters for clues and treasure.

- The party first debates whether property taken from Moesko would be legitimate treasure, stolen merchandise, or blood money. Vukradin insists that wealth is rightful only if earned under a valid contract, while Soma answers, “All money is blood money until proven otherwise.”
- The party examines Moesko’s body and discovers he was wearing armor crafted from giant octopus hide.
- The armor is eventually clarified to be hide armor: medium armor with a base AC of 12 and a maximum Dexterity contribution of +2.
- An opalescent conch belonging to the sea elf Miraal is recovered from beside Moesko’s body. It had previously rested on his lap.
- Soma expects returning the conch to bring Miraal peace.
- Beneath the armor, the party discovers a hollow hole in Moesko’s chest. The DM clarifies that Moesko is not a mummy and that no portion of his physical body appears to have been transported elsewhere.
- Vukradin initially argues against further searching: “We came here to do a job, we did the job, let’s go!”
- Soma counters that they still do not know whether the curse drawing ships to the lighthouse has truly been broken.
- Valphine searches the crude living space, which contains scattered prayer skulls dedicated to Talos, storm-weathered gear, and the remnants of Moesko’s anchorite life.
- Beneath a loose floorboard, Valphine finds a small concealed compartment.
- Inside, nestled on a bed of blackened seagrass, lies a palm-sized talisman made from a dark, iridescent material resembling polished obsidian or a fragment of solidified night sky.
- The talisman seems to drink the surrounding light. Its surface is smooth and cool, but it pulses with a faint internal violet luminescence.
- One face bears a crudely etched, jagged lightning bolt—the symbol of Talos.
- Brewbarry notices a second, subtler design interwoven into the reverse: an intricate geometric symbol depicting an inverted eye from which delicate tentacle-like lines unfurl.
- Soma connects the tentacles with Moesko’s giant-octopus armor and wonders whether he worshipped something other than—or in addition to—Talos.
- Valphine senses a faint divine resonance within the talisman, overwhelmed by a more dominant and unsettling psionic energy alien to conventional magic.
- When Valphine concentrates upon the object, she feels a persistent rhythmic thrumming like a distant heartbeat or the lingering echo of a monstrous call from the deep.
- The rest of the party backs away while Valphine examines it. Soma notes that she has only a few hit points remaining, while Vukradin continues questioning why they are spending so much time in the room.
- The party decides to carry the talisman. Vukradin sarcastically names it the “Talisman of Death” and predicts that nothing bad could possibly result.
- The party also debates taking Moesko’s octopus-hide armor. Its usefulness is questioned once it is identified as ordinary medium hide armor rather than splint mail.

## Locations

### Tower of Storms

A lighthouse tower where the party recently defeated Moesko. Its interior contains crude living spaces, prayer skulls dedicated to Talos, storm-weathered gear, and a concealed compartment beneath a loose floorboard. The hidden compartment held a dark talisman nestled on blackened seagrass.

The roof is bordered by a low stone wall and iron railing and holds a ten-foot-tall wrought iron lightning rod that apparently channeled storm energy toward the altar below. Vukradin and Brewbarry began smashing the rod. Three open archways at the pinnacle once allowed a pulsating green light to shine westward over the sea. That beacon was powered by Moesko’s oversized, disembodied heart until Vukradin destroyed it. A temporary Light spell replaced the beacon while the party searched for firewood, with Moesko’s furniture and salvaged wreck timber considered possible fuel.

### Shipwreck Waters

The murky, shark-infested waters west of the lighthouse contain the remains of five ships: the Star-Crossed Lover (T10), the Sea Urchin (T11), the Vainglory (T12), the Golden Gull (T13), and the Orca (T14). Underwater visibility is limited to twenty feet.

The Star-Crossed Lover held a plus-one halberd in the skeletal grip of its captain. The Sea Urchin contained a starfish-covered chest holding a blue quartz mermaid statuette. The Vainglory held a magically preserved cloak. The Golden Gull and Orca held additional locked chests containing coins, pearls, a spellbook, and a Wand of Secrets. Three hunter sharks attacked the salvagers here before being killed and harvested.

### Leilon

A town farther south, mentioned by Miraal as the site of a gathering storm brewing in the depths below it. Her warning implies that the alien influence which corrupted Moesko may be connected to something beneath Leilon. The party expressed immediate interest, but the town remains their destination only after completing the quests associated with Phandalin.

## NPCs

### Moesko

A deceased anchorite of Talos whose body was found with a strange hole in his chest. He wore medium armor crafted from giant-octopus hide and possessed Miraal’s opalescent conch.

His oversized, still-beating heart had somehow been removed and placed at the lighthouse’s pinnacle, where it generated the pulsating green beacon even after his death. Miraal later revealed that Moesko had not acted entirely of his own will. A cold, calculating, foreign presence from the deep had twisted his devotion to Talos toward another purpose.

### Miraal

A sea elf spirit whose opalescent conch was taken by Moesko. Returning the conch allowed her spirit to find peace.

Before departing, Miraal revealed that Moesko was influenced by something foreign rather than consumed solely by the rage of Talos. She had sensed shadows twisting his devotion and continued to hear fragmented whispers from the deep even after death. Her final warning was that the true storm was gathering beneath Leilon rather than in the sky.

### Hunter Sharks

Aggressive aquatic predators that ambushed Soma and Brewbarry near the shipwrecks. Three sharks attacked in total—the meanest of them recorded in campaign canon as Daggermaw.

The first attacked Brewbarry but was badly wounded by Soma’s giant-toad bite and harried by Vukradin’s mockery and Valphine’s radiant magic. The other two concentrated on Soma and nearly exhausted the resilience of her Wild Shape. Vukradin put both to sleep, Valphine outlined them with Faerie Fire, and the party killed them one at a time. Their meat and teeth were harvested afterward.

### Crabby

A friendly, sentient crustacean who greeted the returning party enthusiastically and was overjoyed that Miraal had found peace. He confirmed that the magical halberd recovered from the Star-Crossed Lover was the treasure he had intended to retrieve for the party.

Crabby also thanked the party for eliminating the sharks. He asked whether they would share the meat, accepted Brewbarry’s offer eagerly, and pronounced the fresh shark delicious.

## Items

### Opalescent Conch

A beautiful, shimmering shell belonging to Miraal the sea elf. It had rested on Moesko’s lap before being displaced to the floor. Recovering it was the party’s primary reason for searching his body.

The conch was the key to bringing Miraal’s spirit peace. Upon receiving it, she was able to depart, but not before revealing Moesko’s corruption and warning of danger beneath Leilon.

### Iridescent Talisman

A palm-sized object made from a dark material that seems to drink the light, resembling polished obsidian or a fragment of solidified night sky. It is smooth and cool to the touch and pulsates with a faint internal violet glow.

One side bears a crudely etched jagged lightning bolt of Talos. On the reverse, Brewbarry discovered a subtler geometric design interwoven into the material: an inverted eye from which delicate, tentacle-like lines unfurl.

Valphine sensed a faint divine resonance within it and a much stronger, deeply unsettling psionic energy alien to conventional magic. Concentrating on the talisman produced a rhythmic thrumming like a distant heartbeat or the lingering echo of a monstrous call from the deep. Vukradin sarcastically called it the “Talisman of Death,” but the party carried it away.

### Octopus Hide Armor

A set of medium hide armor crafted from the skin of a giant octopus and worn by Moesko. It provides a base AC of 12 and permits a maximum +2 Dexterity modifier.

The party debated whether taking armor from Moesko’s corpse would amount to theft or legitimate salvage. It was discussed among their recovered goods in the following session.

### Moesko’s Heart

A heart several sizes too large that continued to beat after Moesko’s death. It generated the pulsating green light of the lighthouse beacon and magically blinded Valphine when she stared directly into it.

Valphine initially wished to leave it intact because it served a useful purpose. Vukradin argued that a disembodied heart belonging to an evil cleric could not safely remain and destroyed it after several attacks. The green beacon went dark immediately.

### Plus One Halberd

A magical polearm found aboard the Star-Crossed Lover, still clutched in the skeletal grip of the ship’s dead captain. Brewbarry recovered it and initially concealed its discovery from the rest of the party.

Crabby later confirmed that this was the treasure he had intended to retrieve as the party’s reward. That confirmation did not entirely settle Vukradin’s objections to Brewbarry taking it directly from the skeleton.

### Cloak of Many Fashions

A fashionable magical garment recovered from the Vainglory. Its lack of deterioration despite long immersion in seawater revealed its magical nature. Its appearance and colors can shift on command.

Soma immediately recognized it as an ideal stage costume for Vukradin and suggested synchronizing its colors with his music. Vukradin admitted that it would look good on him and was visibly tempted, but hesitated because its original owner remained unknown.

### Blue Quartz Mermaid Statuette

A delicate, nine-inch-tall carving of a mermaid made from blue quartz. It was packed in straw within a locked, starfish-covered chest recovered from the Sea Urchin (T11).

### Electrum and Pearls

120 electrum pieces and six pearls worth 100 gold pieces each, recovered from the Golden Gull’s locked chest (T13). Vukradin objected to claiming the valuables without first attempting to identify their rightful owners.

The electrum amount is GM-confirmed from the module inventory in the 2026-09-12 Stage 0 ruling, not verified spoken dialogue; the pearls are corroborated by the following session’s tracker discussion.

### Wizard’s Spellbook

A wizard’s spellbook recovered from the Orca’s locked chest (T14), together with the Wand of Secrets. The party currently has no wizard able to make conventional use of it.

### Wand of Secrets

A magical wand recovered, with a wizard’s spellbook, from the locked chest aboard the Orca (T14). It can detect secret doors and hidden compartments.

### Shark Meat

Fresh meat harvested from the three hunter sharks killed during the underwater excursion. Vukradin deemed it “lawfully earned” and therefore morally acceptable for the party to keep.

Brewbarry shared the meat with Crabby, who ate it eagerly and declared it fresh and delicious. The party also considered collecting shark teeth for a necklace.

## Spells

### Prayer of Healing

Cast by Valphine before the party ascended the upper levels of the tower. It affected multiple companions, restored a substantial amount of health, and granted the benefits of a short rest.

Soma recovered 31 hit points in total and regained a use of Wild Shape. Vukradin, who had already spent all his Hit Dice, declared himself much more comfortable with continuing upstairs.

### Light

Cast by Vukradin after he destroyed Moesko’s heart and extinguished the lighthouse beacon. The spell supplied temporary illumination for one hour while the party searched for a more sustainable source of fire.

The party considered drying wood taken from the wrecks and using furniture from Moesko’s quarters as kindling.

### Wild Shape

Used by Soma to transform into a giant toad. The form provided strong swimming ability, underwater breathing, improved jumping, and temporary resilience during the exploration of the wrecks.

In combat, Soma used the toad’s bite against the hunter sharks and repeatedly attempted to grapple them. The additional durability absorbed several brutal bites before the form was nearly exhausted. Soma abandoned the transformation after the battle and healed herself before continuing.

### Vicious Mockery

Employed by Vukradin to hurl magical insults at sharks fighting beneath the surface. The churning water initially made the targets difficult to perceive, leading to a lengthy discussion about whether the spell could reach them.

One casting inflicted only a single point of damage, but the mockery imposed disadvantage and caused the shark’s following bite against Soma to miss. Vukradin later used it to finish the first shark.

### Sacred Flame

Cast by Valphine from the surface, calling down radiant energy upon sharks visible through the churning water. It provided an effective alternative when a crossbow could not be fired reliably into the sea.

Several sharks resisted the spell, but Valphine successfully burned the first predator and contributed radiant damage during the later coordinated assault.

### Sleep

Cast by Vukradin when Soma had been reduced to only a few remaining temporary hit points and two sharks still threatened the divers. Both sharks failed to resist and fell into an unnatural slumber.

The party used the respite tactically, attacking one shark while allowing the other to sleep through its opportunity to act. The spell transformed a dangerous melee into a controlled series of attacks.

### Faerie Fire

Cast by Valphine immediately after Vukradin put two sharks to sleep. Both predators automatically failed to evade the spell while unconscious and became outlined in shimmering violet light.

The glow outlined the sharks and granted the party advantage on attacks against them.

### Healing Word

Considered and held in reserve by Valphine during the shark battle. She planned to use it to revive Soma if the druid fell unconscious after the sharks acted.

The party discussed readying the spell, but doing so would consume Valphine’s reaction and risk expending the magic even if its trigger never occurred. Soma remained conscious, so the emergency healing was ultimately unnecessary.
