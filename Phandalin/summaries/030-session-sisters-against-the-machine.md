# Chapter 30: Sisters Against the Machine

Date: 2026-01-15

## Summary

The party continued their exploration of the ancient dwarven fortress, finishing their survey of the first floor. They confirmed that the mechanical “mosquito” captured earlier was a device for collecting blood samples. They then discovered a storage room filled with serviceable armorsmiths’ and weaponsmiths’ tools that would prove useful for the people of Phandalin, along with privies equipped with plumbing. The barracks held dozens of meager cots scattered about, with two cold fireplaces whose rubble-choked chimneys rose one hundred twenty feet through the mountain. An empty armory revealed that the dwarves had taken all their weapons when they departed, leaving behind only stone weapon racks, headless mannequins, and iron hooks on the walls.

Having thoroughly explored the lower level, the party considered several routes upstairs, including multiple stairways and a burrowed passage. Valphine chose the bottom-right stairwell, which soon proved to connect with another known staircase. On the upper floor, where the time and spatial distortions seemed more pronounced, they heard a curious scratching sound echoing from the north hallway, like a hand frantically scratching on paper. Valphine led the group westward into a chamber where they encountered six out-of-phase dwarven figures who screamed incessantly about “efficiency” and “process.” When Vukradin convincingly claimed that the party had come to help improve the mine’s efficiency, the dwarves became even more agitated, as if the very pursuit of increased efficiency was the source of their torment.

The battle that followed was fierce but brief. Brewbarry charged forward in a rage, promising, “I’ll show you how efficient my halberd is.” His first attempted cleaving strike went badly, but his follow-up attacks cut down one of the spectral dwarves. Vukradin wove magic that slowed all five remaining enemies, making them sluggish and vulnerable. Before the dwarves had all fallen, Valphine moved farther into the room and discovered the Chief Accountant—a luminous dwarven figure who glitched and flickered like corrupted data, her face a mask of shifting geometric shapes with ink-like shadows leaking from her eyes. Her voice was an unsettling blend of a dwarven grandmother’s warmth and screeching static as she declared, “The Ledger! It does not close! The ISD regulator has divided by zero!”

The Chief Accountant smelled the unmistakable traces of Valphine’s family and accused her of carrying “the Sotorra scent” of “bleach and betrayal,” calling her “sister of the sun.” Valphine summoned Spirit Guardians, destroying two dwarves and wounding another. Soma tore through a weakened enemy in bear form, while Vukradin projected an illusory giant adding machine—a Babbage Difference Engine—that the Accountant believed was attacking her. The last of the out-of-phase dwarves fell only after the Accountant had joined the battle.

The Chief Accountant’s retaliation was devastating. Frightened by the illusory machine, she unleashed a terrible banshee wail that echoed through the chamber. Valphine and Vukradin both collapsed unconscious from the supernatural assault, while Brewbarry and Soma remained standing but suffered its lesser effects. Soma attacked the Accountant in defense of the fallen companions, though the bear’s nonmagical strikes inflicted only half their normal damage.

The Accountant subsequently tried to terrify Brewbarry. Then, rather than continuing her attack, she stared at Valphine’s unconscious form with panic. Her spectral hands twitched as she tried to put Valphine back together with blue-white static. The energy could not heal Valphine, but it stabilized her and prevented her condition from worsening. The Accountant cried out that “value attrition” was dropping and that “the auditor” would liquidate her if the sister were found depreciated. She insisted that she had not meant to close Valphine’s account and had only wanted a signature. She then became incorporeal and fled through a closed door into another room. Soma declined an opportunity attack because the Accountant had just tried to save Valphine.

After Soma stabilized Vukradin and the party restored their strength with healing magic, the Chief Accountant cautiously emerged. She explained that Valphine’s sister Aletra, the fourth daughter of the Sotorra family, had made a deal with the king to improve the mine’s efficiency and profit margins. The king had been jealous of Lionshield Coster’s multi-interdimensional merchant operations and their incredible revenue. Aletra promised that installing a special machine would improve everything, but the machine malfunctioned and “all hell broke loose.”

According to the Chief Accountant, Aletra was “a snake” who became angry when told that the books did not balance. When the Accountant threatened to complain to management and inform the king, Aletra laughed at her; soon afterward the machine failed. The Accountant believed Aletra had killed her, possibly because she had discovered the machine’s override paperwork. Although uncertain whether she was truly dead or merely out of phase, she offered the party a deal: she would give them Override Code 77 Beta if they let her go and did not tell Aletra what had happened.

The party debated whether to trust the Chief Accountant. Vukradin worried that leaving a banshee in the fortress would violate their agreement to clear it of dangers and cost them their payment. Valphine argued that the Accountant remained useful, especially while Aletra’s location and intentions were unknown. Brewbarry suggested disabling the machine first and cleaning up anything left afterward. Arcane analysis indicated that shutting down the machine should end the extraplanar effects. The Accountant directed them toward the blinking lights they had already noticed.

The party descended to the lower level and used the back tunnel to reach the machine room. They found a massive contraption of brass, orange organ pipes, spinning copper centrifuges, and twisting mechanical arms bolted to the floor. The device vibrated violently, venting jets of purple steam while a magical voice boomed from its brass horn: “Non-Euclidean geometry detected. Purging, purging.” The air was thick with static electricity that made their hair stand on end, and the machine produced a rhythmic mechanical shriek like a steam train trying to brake on broken glass.

Standing calmly beside the chaotic machine was a drow woman in a high-collared uniform, typing on a glowing keypad with one hand while holding a rapier in the other. Valphine recognized her immediately—not by sight, but by scent. The faint, sickly sweet mixture of lavender perfume and chemical bleach from the Sotorra family poison labs could belong only to Aletra. Above her, four initially visible spectral spiders made of flickering static clung to the ceiling and hissed like white noise. When Aletra turned, Valphine saw the sharp cheekbones and cold violet eyes of the sister who had exposed traitors not for honor, but for profit.

Aletra ordered Vukradin not to move and claimed she was performing contracted repairs on a delicate, specialized machine. Vukradin revealed that the party possessed Override Code 77 Beta. Aletra acknowledged that it was a valid code but insisted that something important had to be removed before they used it. Valphine’s insight and knowledge of her sister exposed the real motive: Aletra’s private sideline involved moving drow poison around the multiverse, and the valuable box trapped inside the machine was almost certainly part of that business.

When Valphine emerged from hiding, Aletra greeted her with characteristic condescension, telling her in Undercommon, “Upper management only, sister. Go play in the mud with the other surface dwellers. The adults have a deadline.” She mocked Valphine for becoming a cleric of Lathander and told the others, “My sister is obsessed with being in pain.”

Aletra explained that the spectral spiders were Rift Weavers—antibody-like manifestations provoked by the machine’s interference with the multiplanar universe. She was currently balanced in an equilibrium with the machine and its guardians. A sudden change in the room’s magical potential could cause the Rift Weavers to attack or destroy the device before she retrieved her cargo. She therefore proposed that the party kill the spiders while she extracted her box; afterward, they could enter the override code and disable the machine. Valphine determined that Aletra was, unusually, telling the truth about the immediate danger, though the box was clearly worth a great deal to her. If the party refused, Aletra warned that she could attempt to extract it without their help—and if she failed to remove it in time, “the whole place goes BOOM.” The session ended with the party leaning toward temporary cooperation while negotiations continued.

### First Floor Exploration

#### The party continues exploring the first floor of a dwarven fortress, discovering various rooms and their contents before deciding to move upstairs.

- The party confirms they previously captured a “mosquito” creature, which is described as a device for collecting blood samples.
- They discover a room that served as storage for dwarven armorers and weaponsmiths. Its tools remain in serviceable condition.
- Vukradin observes that the tools could equip the people of Phandalin when they come to occupy the stronghold.
- Valphine dismisses them as “inferior dwarven tools,” insisting that her own tools are clearly better.
- A discussion of drow labor follows, with the party joking that drow would leave manual work—and the quality of the workers’ tools—to slaves.
- The party finds privies with running water and plumbing, prompting speculation about what might be lurking in the waste channels.

- An armory is found, but the dwarves removed all its weapons and armor when they left. Only empty stone weapon racks, headless mannequins, and iron hooks remain.
- With no further rooms left unexplored on the first level, the party decides to go upstairs.

### Chief Accountant

A luminous dwarven figure who constantly glitches like corrupted data, with a face of shifting geometric shapes and ink-like shadows leaking from her eyes. Her voice combines a dwarven grandmother’s warmth with screeching static. She speaks in accounting metaphors concerning ledgers, signatures, depreciation, value attrition, and division by zero.

She recognizes Valphine through the “Sotorra scent” of bleach and betrayal and calls her “sister of the sun.” She possesses the traits of a banshee, including a devastating wail that reduced Valphine and Vukradin to zero hit points and a supernatural ability to inspire fear. Despite attacking the party, she panicked after incapacitating Valphine and stabilized her with blue-white static because she feared “the auditor” would liquidate her for closing the sister’s account.

The Chief Accountant later reveals that Aletra made a deal with the king to improve the keep’s profits through a multidimensional machine. She calls Aletra “a snake” and claims that Aletra killed her after she discovered that the books did not balance and threatened to inform management. She is uncertain whether she is truly dead or merely out of phase.

The Accountant found paperwork containing Override Code 77 Beta, which she suspects may be the real reason Aletra killed her. She reveals the code while requesting freedom and secrecy, but Chapter 28 does not establish that the party accepts those terms. Although she does not understand the machine’s workings—“I’m an accountant, dammit!”—she directs the party toward its blinking lights.

### Aletra

Valphine’s sister and the fourth daughter of the Sotorra family. She has sharp cheekbones, cold violet eyes, and a distinct scent of sickly sweet lavender perfume and chemical bleach from the Sotorra family poison labs. She is remembered as a sister who exposed traitors not for honor but for profit.

According to the Chief Accountant, Aletra made a deal with the king to improve the fortress’s efficiency and profit margins by installing a machine connected to multidimensional commerce. Aletra claims she sold the machine to the dwarves as part of an unnamed employer’s multiplanar experiments. Its builder and ultimate owner remain unresolved. Aletra conducts a private sideline moving drow poison around the multiverse.

She is found beside the malfunctioning machine in a clean, high-collared uniform, calmly operating a glowing keypad with one hand while holding a rapier in the other. She is intelligent, calculating, condescending, and habitually backstabbing, but Valphine knows there is always a deal to be made with her.

Aletra initially assumes Vukradin is the party’s leader and dismisses Valphine in Undercommon: “Upper management only, sister. Go play in the mud with the other surface dwellers. The adults have a deadline.” She mocks Valphine’s devotion to Lathander and tells the others, “My sister is obsessed with being in pain.”

Aletra wants to extract an extremely valuable box, apparently containing drow poison, before the party disables the machine. She truthfully explains that doing so requires the Rift Weavers to be distracted or destroyed. She proposes temporary cooperation: the party kills the creatures, she removes the box, and they then enter the override code. If they refuse, she may attempt the extraction alone, risking an explosion that could destroy the entire place.

### Rift Weavers

Four initially visible creatures that resemble spiders made of flickering, translucent static. They cling to the ceiling above Aletra and the machine and hiss like white noise. Aletra identifies them as Rift Weavers and explains that they are not animals but constructs or antibody-like manifestations generated in response to the machine’s interference with the multiplanar universe.

The Rift Weavers react when the machine’s system falls out of balance. At the time of the encounter, they are uncertain whether to attack Aletra or destroy the machine. Aletra warns that any sudden change in the area’s magical potential could provoke them. Chapter 29 begins the ensuing combat with six Rift Weavers, so two were not visible during this first view or entered before initiative.

## Locations

### Axeholm - First Floor

An ancient dwarven stronghold being explored and cleared for the people of Phandalin. Its first floor contains a storage room with serviceable armorsmiths’ and weaponsmiths’ tools, privies with running water and plumbing, barracks with dozens of meager cots, and two cold fireplaces whose rubble-choked chimneys rise 120 feet through the mountain.

The abandoned armory contains no weapons or armor because the dwarves took them when they departed. Only empty stone weapon racks, headless mannequins, and iron wall hooks remain. Several routes connect the first floor to the upper level, including ordinary stairs, stairs near the old ballista chambers, and a burrowed rear passage.

### Axeholm - Upper Level

The upper level of the dwarven fortress, where the time dilation and spatial dilation effects are more pronounced than below. A curious noise resembling a hand scratching frantically on paper can be heard from the north hallway. Blinking lights and strange sounds mark the direction of the malfunctioning machine, though a cave-in prevents direct access from part of this level.

### Upper Level Room

A room on the upper level where the party encounters six out-of-phase dwarves obsessed with efficiency and the luminous, glitching Chief Accountant. An adjoining room provides the Accountant with a place to retreat through a closed wall after the battle. From this area, the party also notices blinking lights and strange noises coming from the direction of the machine.

### Machine Room

A hall reached through the fortress’s rear tunnel. Its air feels heavy with static and makes the hair on visitors’ arms stand up. A rhythmic, grinding mechanical shriek fills the chamber, likened to a steam train trying to brake on broken glass.

The room contains a monstrosity of brass, orange organ pipes, spinning copper centrifuges, and twisting mechanical arms bolted to the floor. It vibrates violently, vents jets of purple steam, and announces, “Non-Euclidean geometry detected. Purging, purging,” through a brass horn.

Aletra stands beside the machine at a glowing keypad, holding a rapier. Four initially visible Rift Weavers cling to the ceiling above her, flickering like translucent static and hissing like white noise. This is where Aletra is attempting to extract a valuable box that Valphine suspects is connected to her drow-poison business.

### Phandalin

A town mentioned as the destination for the serviceable dwarven tools found in the fortress. The party expects those tools to be useful to Phandalin’s people when they occupy or make use of Axeholm. The party’s contract also appears to require them to clear the fortress of dangers, undead, specters, and anomalies before receiving payment.

## Items

### Steampunk Mosquito

A captured mechanical creature described as a device for collecting blood samples. The party acquired it during their earlier exploration of the fortress and confirms that it remains in their possession.

### Dwarven Tools

Serviceable armorsmiths’ and weaponsmiths’ tools found in a storage room on the first floor of the dwarven fortress. The party notes that they will be useful for the people of Phandalin when they come to the keep. Valphine disparages them as inferior to drow tools, prompting a discussion about whether drow would personally use tools at all.

### The Machine

A massive magical-mechanical device made of brass, orange organ pipes, spinning copper centrifuges, and twisting mechanical arms. It is bolted to the floor, vibrates violently, vents purple steam, and emits a grinding shriek like a train braking on broken glass. Its brass horn announces, “Non-Euclidean geometry detected. Purging, purging.”

The Chief Accountant says Aletra arranged the machine’s installation through a deal with the king to improve the fortress’s efficiency and profit margins. Aletra claims she sold it to the dwarves for her employer’s multiplanar experiments. Its builder and ultimate owner remain unresolved. Its malfunction produced or intensified the fortress’s spatial, temporal, and extraplanar disturbances.

Aletra says that the machine provokes an immune response from the multiplanar universe, manifesting Rift Weavers when the system becomes unbalanced. She is maintaining an equilibrium with it while trying to extract a valuable box. The Chief Accountant provides Override Code 77 Beta to disable the machine, and arcane analysis suggests that doing so should end the surrounding extraplanar effects.

### Override Code

Override Code 77 Beta can be used to disable the machine. The Chief Accountant learned it from paperwork Aletra left behind and failed to hide carefully. The Accountant suspects that possessing this information may be why Aletra killed her.

Aletra recognizes 77 Beta as a valid and useful code but insists that the party must not enter it until she retrieves her cargo. Vukradin uses his knowledge of the code to undermine Aletra’s attempt to present herself as the machine’s sole authorized repairer.

### Drow Poison

A substance that Aletra is apparently attempting to extract from the machine inside a valuable box. It is part of her private business of moving drow poison around the multiverse. Valphine identifies this as the only sort of enterprise likely to draw Aletra personally to a malfunctioning interplanar machine.

The sickly sweet scent of lavender perfume and chemical bleach associated with the Sotorra family poison labs allows Valphine to recognize Aletra before clearly seeing her. The party jokes that poison may have legitimate uses and that it is a standard export from Menzoberranzan.

## Spells

### Slow

Cast by Vukradin on five out-of-phase dwarves. All initially fail their saving throws. The spell reduces their Armor Class by two, limits them to either an action or a bonus action on their turns, and prevents creatures with multiple attacks from making more than one attack.

The effect makes the dwarves easier to hit and limits their assault on Brewbarry. Vukradin later ends the spell by shifting his concentration to the illusion of a giant adding machine attacking the Chief Accountant.

### Spirit Guardians

Cast by Valphine after she moves into the chamber containing the out-of-phase dwarves and discovers the Chief Accountant. Valphine designates her allies as immune to its effects.

The guardians kill two out-of-phase dwarves immediately and wound another, then inflict further damage as Valphine advances. Valphine describes the effect with the declaration, “Bathe in the glorious light of the thunder.” The spell also damages the Chief Accountant before Valphine’s concentration ends when the banshee wail knocks her unconscious.

### Prayer of Healing

Cast by Valphine twice after combat, restoring five hit points to the party the first time and 13 hit points to everyone the second time. Together with additional healing used to revive the unconscious characters, these spells allow the group to recover before negotiating with the Chief Accountant.

### Bardic Inspiration

Given by Vukradin to all party members after the fight with the Chief Accountant, providing inspiration dice that can be added to later rolls. The distribution occurs while the party finishes healing and prepares to continue exploring.
