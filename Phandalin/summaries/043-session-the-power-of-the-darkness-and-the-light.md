# Chapter 43: The Power of the Darkness and the Light

Date: May 5th, 2026

## Summary

Before the fighting resumed, the table clarified how Vukradin’s preaching would affect the orcs. They began hostile, and a successful appeal could move them first to neutral—causing them to hesitate or refrain from attacking—and then to receptive, at which point they might decide the battle was no longer theirs. A persuasive speech alone would not automatically convert them. Orcs one, five, and six began the battle neutral because of Vukradin’s earlier appeal.

The anchorite proved a fearsome foe, calling down a crackling bolt of lightning that tore through Vukradin, Brewbarry, and their centaur companion Xanth in a single devastating strike. All three made their saving throws and absorbed 14 points of damage each. Xanth cursed in his own dialect, shook off the pain, and returned fire with two longbow shots, one of which wounded the cleric for 6 damage.

Then Soma charged in giant elk form, her hooves thundering across the earth as she struck the anchorite. Her ram dealt 12 damage and sent him crashing prone. With the cleric vulnerable, Boney the skeletal horse surged forward and struck him for another 13 damage. Brewbarry then entered a rage and crossed the melee despite the orcs’ attempts to stop him. His first halberd strike dealt 24 damage and left the anchorite with only 3 hit points; his second, made with advantage against the prone cleric, finished him.

Before killing the anchorite, Brewbarry had already cut down orc 7 with an attack of opportunity. The table ruled that, when played in Stéphane’s absence, Brewbarry would normally use Great Weapon Master because recklessly committing to the strongest possible blow suited him. Vukradin protested that he was trying to win the orcs over, demanding, “How are we gonna win them over if you’re slaughtering them?” Brewbarry’s defense was simple: the orc had charged the party.

With their leader dead, the warband began to fracture. Some orcs fled outright, while others continued fighting from fear, hunger, confusion, or stubbornness. The giant elk drew particular attention: the orcs viewed Soma both as a dangerous beast and as an enormous prize piece of meat. Even neutral orcs hurled javelins at her, while Boney disturbed them as an undead abomination.

Vukradin continued preaching as the battle disintegrated around him. Through Universal Speech and his use of Orc, he appealed to successive groups, moving several from hostile to neutral and then receptive. Valphine tried a more forceful approach, commanding the orcs in their own language to kneel before Lathander’s power. Her intimidation had mixed results: one hostile orc hesitated, but one previously neutral warrior concluded that the Drow cleric was a joke and returned to hostility. The orcs’ distrust of Drow, the presence of an undead horse, and the party’s confused mixture of mercy, radiant spectacle, intimidation, and dismemberment all complicated the attempted conversion ceremony.

After one receptive orc attacked Boney and fled, orc 2 calmly demanded money from Vukradin, saying, “Give me money and I let you live.” When Vukradin replied that followers of Lathander did not make such demands, the orc struck him for 9 damage. The DM observed that Vukradin could have handed over a few copper pieces and allowed the would-be robber to walk away. That same orc would soon abandon Talos, join the party, and take the name Prutha.

Soma then withdrew to reduce the bloodshed. She absorbed an opportunity attack with the temporary hit points of her giant elk form, voluntarily dropped Wild Shape, climbed onto Boney, and rode the skeletal horse away from the fighting. In doing so, she removed both “the abomination and the delicious meat” from the orcs’ immediate reach.

The remaining resistance rapidly collapsed. Brewbarry killed orc 9, only remembering afterward that the party wanted nonlethal blows. “Oops, sorry,” he said as the consequences became apparent. He was more careful with orc 10, knocking that warrior unconscious with a deliberate nonlethal strike. Other orcs fled after seeing their leader killed and their attacks repeatedly thwarted. One receptive orc paused amid the brilliant light, decided that life was too short, and ran after Vukradin told him, “It’s okay, you can go. Just tell your friends about Lathander.”

As the last conscious resistance faded, orc 2 set his great axe slowly on the ground after Vukradin made one final appeal. “We friend,” he offered. Vukradin blessed him in Lathander’s name, and the warrior began to walk away with much to consider. Then he looked back at Valphine—the Drow cleric wreathed in sunlight—and something shifted behind his eyes. He reasoned aloud that Talos offered power, but Lathander had just demonstrated greater power. A Drow following the sun god was proof beyond doubt. He rushed toward Valphine and declared, “I follow Drow Cleric of Lathander!” The party had gained its first convert.

The party learned that the orc’s name was Prutha. Suggested replacement names included Thrall and “Prutha the Thrall,” but Vukradin objected that the term was inappropriate for their new friend, and Prutha retained his own name.

As Vukradin became engrossed in conversation with Prutha, the others searched the fallen anchorite and recovered a potion of greater healing. Valphine and Brewbarry began stringing up the cleric’s corpse as a warning. Prutha approved of the display because Valphine’s interpretation of Lathander emphasized ordered, overwhelming power. Vukradin disapproved of the corpse’s treatment but said nothing, concentrating instead on Prutha’s story.

Prutha revealed that his great uncle had been the shaman of his clan. The Talosians killed him to break the clan’s resistance and force its warriors into service, denouncing the shaman as weak, an abomination, and “thunder meat.” Prutha served because the alternative was death. Across three skirmishes, he said, he had never killed by choice.

His great uncle had also taught him that “Dawn comes for everyone, even those born in shadow.” Prutha explained that his people traditionally greeted the sunrise with a bowed head and a bone whistle. He drew his uncle’s whistle and blew its single, painfully loud note, then began to cry. A successful insight into his reaction revealed that he had not performed the ritual in a very long time. He finally told the party directly, “My uncle blew this whistle. They killed him for it. I did not blow it until now.”

The moment deeply affected the party despite jokes about recruiting Prutha for their band, teaching him the triangle, and the whistle’s limited musical range. Valphine recognized that the old rite could become part of her evolving Lathandrian worship. She embraced the ritual openly, resolving to absorb rites associated with the sun and incorporate the whistle into her faith. Prutha responded with renewed devotion.

Prutha then kicked the dead anchorite and contrasted Talos’s failure with Valphine’s victory: “Talos strong! Uncle weak. He dead. Drow in sun, powerful, Talosian, weak.” He proposed that they go kill the other Talosian clerics. When the party asked whether their followers could instead be converted, Prutha agreed that anyone who abandoned Talos could be spared, but those who continued to follow Talos should die. The party summarized the new doctrine as “Convert or die,” a formulation Valphine readily embraced.

Despite the violence of Valphine’s theology, she did not feel Lathander drawing away. Instead, the god seemed closer to her. Her clarity, focus, and emphasis on the overwhelming strength of the sun appeared to hold Lathander’s interest.

After the battle, Valphine healed Xanth for 13 of the 14 points he had lost to the lightning bolt. The party also discussed placing Lathander’s blessing on Boney, joking that divine energy might cause the undead horse to burn or cringe in pain.

The party then reached the Circle of Thunder. Xanth, their centaur guide, paused at the edge of the treeline and announced that his work was done. He told Vukradin that their paths now separated, then tapped Soma on the shell and said that she had been blessed by Meril and would do great things. He gave Meril’s Staff one last lingering look—making the party slightly uncomfortable—before “hooving off” into the forest.

The party approached the ninety-foot hill as ominous storm clouds gathered overhead. From the treeline below the summit, they could see a great ring of ancient standing stones against the darkening sky. Within the henge, two ghastly figures danced in a slow and terrible ritual while twelve small, twisted creatures of bark and twig capered around them.

The party remained below the hill and debated its approach. The two figures were identified as Talosian anchorites who could not be converted, while the surrounding creatures were twig blights—awakened plants with little intelligence and no apparent openness to religious persuasion. The party considered the Necklace of Fireballs, though Vukradin insisted it was not theirs to use and refused to entrust it to Soma. They also discussed Thunderwave, Vukradin’s cone-shaped Spray of Cards, and a Spirit Guardians strategy combined with rapid movement. No attack was launched before the session ended. The Circle of Thunder was waiting, and the storm above it was only growing darker.

## Locations

### Circle of Thunder (Gully Approach)

A 60-foot gully with a ridge on the opposite side where ten orcs and an anchorite of Talos were positioned. The site of the party’s fierce skirmish with the Talosian warband. The terrain allowed the orcs to charge, flank, or throw javelins while the party crossed toward their leader.

The encounter was introduced as ten orcs, but inconsistent token numbering—including duplicate labels and later relabeling—prevents a reliable individual tally. It was here that Soma knocked the anchorite prone in giant elk form, Brewbarry killed him, and the surviving warband scattered or surrendered. Valphine and Brewbarry later strung up the anchorite’s corpse as a warning.

### Circle of Thunder (Hilltop)

A ninety-foot-tall, tree-covered hill topped with a large ring of standing stones. Ominous storm clouds gather overhead as two ghastly Talosian anchorites perform a dark ritual dance within the henge. Twelve small twig blights caper around them.

The party remains below the summit at the treeline, having not yet committed to an assault. The anchorites and twig blights have been deemed unsuitable for conversion, leading the party to consider area-of-effect magic.

### Neverwinter Wood

The forested region through which the party traveled to reach the Circle of Thunder. Xanth guided them through the wood after warning that the white dragon’s disruption had driven followers of Talos into the area.

Earlier in the journey, the party confronted illegal loggers and found the charred pine tree hung with twig dolls and beating pig hearts. Xanth departed into the wood after bringing the party within sight of the Circle of Thunder.

## Items

### Potion of Greater Healing

A potent magical restorative found on the defeated anchorite of Talos after the skirmish at the gully. It was discovered while Vukradin was occupied speaking with Prutha and Valphine and Brewbarry searched the body.

The party now holds two potions of greater healing—one carried before this session and one looted here—plus one potion of superior healing and one scented potion of healing.

### Bone Whistle

A traditional orcish religious instrument carried by Prutha. His clan greeted the dawn by bowing and sounding such a whistle. Prutha’s whistle had belonged to his great uncle, the clan’s shaman, whom the Talosians killed while breaking the clan’s resistance.

The whistle produces a single piercing note rather than a range suitable for ordinary music. Prutha had not blown it since his uncle’s murder. When he finally sounded it after abandoning Talos, he wept and said, “My uncle blew this whistle. They killed him for it. I did not blow it until now.”

Valphine recognized the rite’s religious power and openly incorporated it into her evolving worship of Lathander.

### Necklace of Fireballs

A magical necklace held by the party and discussed as a potential weapon against the twelve twig blights at the Circle of Thunder. Soma repeatedly noted that the party did, in fact, possess a means of casting Fireball.

Vukradin argued that the necklace was not truly theirs to use and refused to entrust it to Soma. The party also observed that the twig blights were not clustered closely enough for a fireball to catch all of them efficiently.

### Meril’s Staff

The staff Soma inherited from her mentor Meril. Xanth regarded the staff with visible interest throughout his farewell and looked at it one final time before departing. He told Soma that she had been blessed by Meril and would do great things.

Cast by Vukradin when an orc’s great axe attack would have hit him. The spell forced the orc to reroll, turning the hit into a miss.

Silvery Barbs also granted Brewbarry advantage on his next attack roll. Brewbarry used that advantage on a reaction attack of opportunity against orc 7, helping him land a Great Weapon Master strike that killed the orc.

Vukradin later used Silvery Barbs against orc 3 as it attempted one final attack before fleeing; the reroll turned that hit into a miss as well.

Table ruling: the advantage from Silvery Barbs may be used on a reaction attack because it applies to “the next attack roll.”

### Universal Speech

Used by Vukradin so selected orcs could understand him for an hour. He initially included three orcs and later added four more.

The party subsequently learned that the orcs already spoke Common, though speaking in Orc remained more persuasive. Separate Persuasion checks, resolved through the table’s social-attitude system, moved several orcs from hostile to neutral or receptive.

A receptive attitude meant an orc no longer wanted to fight; it did not itself constitute religious conversion. Vukradin ultimately converted Prutha through a final successful appeal after the battle had collapsed.

### Lightning Bolt

Cast by the anchorite of Talos in a line that struck Vukradin, Brewbarry, and Xanth simultaneously.

The spell dealt 29 damage before saving throws. All three targets succeeded against DC 12 and took 14 damage each. Xanth cursed in his own dialect, then returned fire with his longbow. Valphine healed 13 of Xanth’s lost hit points after the fight.

### Emboldening Bond

The table used a non-concentration effect it called “Bless,” apparently Valphine’s Peace Domain Emboldening Bond. The table stated that the effect lasted 10 rounds; the standard Emboldening Bond feature lasts 10 minutes. While a bonded creature remained within 30 feet of another, it could add a d4 once per turn to an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw.

The feature remained active throughout the battle. Brewbarry used its bonus to turn his first heavily penalized Great Weapon Master attack against the prone anchorite into a hit, dealing 24 damage and leaving the cleric with 3 hit points.

The recap therefore preserves both the table’s recorded duration ruling and the likely rules identity rather than treating them as identical.

### Thunderwave

Discussed by Soma as a possible area-of-effect option against the twelve twig blights at the Circle of Thunder. The party considered whether the enemies’ positions would allow the spell to catch enough of them to justify its use.

### Spirit Guardians

Discussed by Valphine as a strategy for the upcoming confrontation at the Circle of Thunder. The proposed tactic involved activating Spirit Guardians and moving rapidly into the mass of twig blights so that approaching enemies would be caught in the spell’s damaging area.

## NPCs

### Xanth

A centaur guide who led the party through Neverwinter Wood to the Circle of Thunder. He warned that the white dragon’s disruption had driven followers of Talos into the forest.

Xanth fought beside the party at the gully. He survived the anchorite’s Lightning Bolt, cursed in his own dialect, and answered with two longbow shots, one of which dealt 6 damage. His later volley missed entirely. Valphine healed 13 of the 14 damage he suffered.

At the Circle of Thunder, Xanth announced that he had fulfilled his promise and that his path now separated from Vukradin’s. He tapped Soma on the shell, declared that she had been blessed by Meril and would do great things, and lingered over one last look at Meril’s Staff before galloping away.

### Jenna Roscoe

A representative of the Lord’s Alliance who attempted to recruit the party, as mentioned in the session recap. Her pitch was met with Brewbarry’s thunderous burp, making his opinion unmistakable.

### Anchorite of Talos

A clawed Talosian leader who commanded the orc warband at the gully. Valphine’s display of radiant holy energy enraged him, and he branded her a false cleric before ordering the attack.

He cast Lightning Bolt through Vukradin, Brewbarry, and Xanth, dealing 14 damage to each after their successful saves. Xanth wounded him with an arrow, after which Soma charged him in giant elk form, dealt 12 damage, and knocked him prone.

Boney struck the prone anchorite for 13 damage. Brewbarry then dealt 24 damage with his first halberd strike, leaving the anchorite at 3 hit points, and killed him with a second attack made with advantage.

The party recovered a potion of greater healing from his body. Valphine and Brewbarry then strung up the corpse as a warning, and Prutha repeatedly kicked it while renouncing Talos.

### Prutha

An orc warrior formerly forced into service by the cult of Talos. During the battle, he was identified as orc 2. He charged Vukradin at the battle’s outset and later attempted to rob him, demanding, “Give me money and I let you live.” When refused, he struck Vukradin for 9 damage.

After the anchorite died and the other orcs fled, Vukradin defeated Prutha’s resolve with a final persuasive appeal. Prutha lowered his axe, declared “We friend,” and accepted Vukradin’s blessing.

Prutha concluded that Talos offered power but Lathander had demonstrated more. Valphine—a Drow wielding the sun’s light—became his proof that Lathander’s power was beyond doubt. He rushed to her and declared, “I follow Drow Cleric of Lathander!”

Prutha’s great uncle had been the shaman of his clan. The Talosians killed the shaman to break resistance and conscript the survivors, calling him weak, an abomination, and “thunder meat.” Prutha fought in three skirmishes because the alternative was death, but claimed never to have killed by choice.

His uncle taught that “Dawn comes for everyone, even those born in shadow” and observed a traditional dawn rite using a bone whistle. Prutha inherited that whistle but had not sounded it since his uncle’s murder. He finally blew it after converting, then wept.

Prutha approved of Valphine’s emphasis on ordered divine power and joined the party. He vowed to help destroy the remaining Talosian clerics. He was willing to spare followers who abandoned Talos, but endorsed death for those who refused to convert.

### Boney

A skeletal horse serving as a Warrior sidekick to the party. In the fiction, Boney bites, using the sidekick’s two-handed longsword attack statistics because a horse cannot carry a shield.

Boney charged through the melee and struck the prone anchorite for 13 damage. Several orcs targeted him because they regarded an undead horse as an abomination, even after becoming neutral or receptive toward the party.

Brewbarry intercepted one attack intended for Boney and took the damage himself. Soma later dropped her giant elk form, mounted Boney, and rode him away from the battle to remove both the undead creature and the orcs’ prospective meat from temptation.

The party discussed placing Lathander’s blessing on Boney after the fight, joking that holy power might cause him to burn or cringe.

### Twig Blights

Twelve small, capering plant creatures surrounding the Talosian anchorites during their ritual at the Circle of Thunder. They were identified as awakened plants with Intelligence 4 and a neutral evil disposition.

Soma could not use animal speech to communicate with them, and the party concluded they could not be converted. They were therefore treated as likely fodder for Thunderwave, Spirit Guardians, the Necklace of Fireballs, or another area-of-effect attack.

### Anchorites of Talos (Circle of Thunder)

Two ghastly spellcasters performing a ritual dance within the stone henge at the top of the ninety-foot hill. Twelve twig blights caper around them beneath the gathering storm clouds.

The party considers the anchorites committed Talosian clerics who cannot be converted. No contact or combat occurred before the session ended.
