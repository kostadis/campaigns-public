# Chapter 18: Freeing Big Al, Petunia and watching a ranch burn

Date: 04-03 of Tarsakh 1495

## Summary
Vukradin halted the party at the sight of four brigands, easily recognizable as Carver’s disposable crew, and announced his intention to parley — foolishly, in Soma’s assessment, which she registered as “a slow puff of air against the inside of my shell.” She asked the question whose answer she already knew: was he going to send Corbin, the terrified one? He was. Corbin, “the lost boy,” went out as emissary and tried to smooth-talk his way in, but his fear was too loud, and the bandits drew steel.

Soma transformed into a brown bear and closed the distance, her claws connecting with the first bandit and killing him outright — she could feel the blood and life being ripped from him. She sensed Brewbarry become big somewhere nearby and leverage it with primal fury, dispatching a second instantly. Vukradin, in what Soma noted approvingly as finally using his magic for utility, put a third to sleep. The last fled, and nobody chased him: too often, Soma reflects, they have seen people escape only to be pursued and killed, and while killing is necessary, too much of it disrupts the balance. That left a captive, which is the perfect source of information.

The prisoner woke tied up and surrounded — the menacing bulk of Brewbarry, the heavy shadow of Soma’s bear form, and Vukradin’s own dramatic presence — and cracked instantly. His name was Skippy, he was terrified, he smelled strongly of fear, and he gave up everything. He didn’t know where the Carver was, but he knew there was an ambush prepared for them at Butterskull Ranch. Vukradin, who always assumes spies, demanded to know how the Carver had known they were coming, and got an answer that was worse than a spy: “It’s boringly predictable.” The Carver knew that knocking out the ranch would generate a quest, and that a quest would walk adventurers straight into his trap, because he knows Harbin uses the quest board to hire adventurers out. The ambush, Skippy added, was led by Borg the Hammer, a half-orc enforcer. Pressed further on how the Carver was so well informed, Skippy considered silence, looked at the blood on Soma’s claws, and reconsidered: one of Carver’s henchmen is Sister Kaella, the Spymaster, who runs their spy network in Phandalin.

Speed now mattered, but Vukradin insisted on returning the prisoner to Phandalin — days of travel, during which Borg might lose patience — because he would not leave a prisoner sitting there defenceless. Corbin offered to guard him instead, and then asked the question Soma found ridiculous: “Vukradin, can I take his armor? If I take his armor, is it conflict-free armor?” Soma pinched the bridge of her snout at yet another round of the loot debate, her own view being that armour acquired through threat or capture is plainly stolen merchandise. Vukradin defended his principles with a flourish — “We all have our challenges, things that we have to face” — and ruled: no armour, and they have to face the Borg. Corbin, who Soma thought wanted to hide in a shell he does not have, then produced a genuinely good idea: what if he took care of Skippy here? Vukradin was dubious. Corbin was insistent, promising twice to take care of him. Both Soma and Vukradin could see he wanted revenge, and Vukradin’s condition was that Skippy had better not be dead when he returned.

The air near Butterskull Ranch felt heavy to Vukradin, not with mist but with malice. They bypassed the main trail entirely for a stealthy approach from the north, and he kept his movements precise, focusing not on the combat he dreads but on the certainty that this was an opportunity for honest coin — a path guided by information bought with minimal violence, which he considers the artistic path. They crept past the charred remains of the barn and smithy, and then he saw the bodies scattered near the outbuildings, orcs and humans both, already swarming with flies. His single-word reaction was *Plunder*, and his gorge rose; this casual aftermath of slaughter is precisely what he despises. And then the chapter’s private test: others would loot these corpses, and that money would buy his studio, and here, far from his friends, nobody would ever know. He corrected himself instead. “It is not what I do in front of anyone that sets my moral compass. But what I do when no one is looking. I will not scavenge them. That is not who I am and it is not my style.” He also read the corpses tactically — the fresh wounds on the orc bodies were ripped and mangled, suggesting something much larger and more vicious than human bandits, which confirmed Borg the Hammer was here, or something big.

He slipped to the back of the house, found more dead orcs and open windows, and worked the cellar door, which groaned faintly but yielded to his sleight of hand. The cellar was earthy and cool, lined with shelves of peculiar skulls made of butter, and held a large figure tied to a chair with a burlap sack over his head. Vukradin worked the ropes and whispered a question — “Are you Big Al Kalazorn?” — and got a startled, relieved yes. Alfonse “Big Al” Kalazorn was the man they had been sent to find, and his first question was whether there were more of them. Vukradin assured him it was a rescue mission. Big Al explained the sequence in a hushed rush: a band of orcs raided the farm first, and then the rot-tusk ogres, led by the half-orc Borg the Hammer, showed up, killed the orcs, and seized the property. Vukradin drew the conclusion aloud — “I’m thinking that’s what’s going on. They kept you alive as the bait, huh?” — since Borg had meant the quest to draw them into a trap.

Then, urged toward the stairs, Big Al stopped. “I really need my cow,” he said, utterly distraught. “She’s my prized possession. Without her, you know, the Butterskull Ranch is just another…” Vukradin recognised a negotiation and accepted the job, reasoning that since the cow was not here they would have to leave and go look for her. The offered reward was astonishing: a suit of mithral chain mail in exchange for finding the cow. Vukradin, being Vukradin, verified the provenance before he would accept — “How did you come by Mithral Chain Mail, Big Al?” — and was told simply, “I bought it.” Big Al is a retired veteran and former Sheriff of Triboar, the predecessor to Darathra. The chainmail was clean, untainted by blood or banditry. Fair trade.

They rushed him back to Corbin at the remote camp, primary objective secured, with the contract still demanding a cow. Soma was immediately practical: this is where Speak with Animals might come in handy, and she could ask the pigs whether they had seen it. Within the hour her efficient magic produced the prize — Petunia, bearing the crucial stamp “Big Al’s Cow” — and she was whisked off to safety.

Then the real objective began. They retreated to a nearby hill to observe, Vukradin selecting a spot roughly 250 feet from the farmhouse and reasoning aloud that they did not need to be that near it. They waited patiently, prioritising the hunt for Borg over a frontal assault, because they were there to track the villain back to his source rather than crash his party — a caution Vukradin frames as the price of honest adventuring.

Valphine, who finds the wind at the ranch a minor irritation that only enhances the strange glorious discomfort of the Overbright, settled in for what she considered the actual entertainment. Below them Borg the Hammer emerged: a large half-orc in chainmail carrying a shield, his armour efficient even if his tactics are crude. He found the bandit who had fled the skirmish at Conyberry — Bandit Number 4 — and began to scream at him. Valphine could not make out every word at that distance, but the fury was palpable, and she noted that this Borg is disciplined, unlike the chaotic brutes one usually meets in the crude overworld. He was furious that the adventurers had not walked into his predictable trap, and he solved the problem of incompetence by executing the fleeing bandit on the spot.

He shouted again and three large figures lumbered out of the farmhouse: rot-tusk ogres, wearing the insignia of the tribe the party had dealt with before, the ones seeking revenge for the ogres they had previously killed. But they did not fight. Instead Borg turned to something Valphine found quite delicious — gratuitous violence — and started looking for a way to burn the place down. The farmhouse and the cornfields caught, flames high, a harsh orange glow across the fields. This was not necessity, Valphine notes, but rage: Borg venting his wrath on the environment because the party had denied him his bloodshed.

Brewbarry, concerned about chasing the enemy, asked whether they should try to save the farm. Vukradin’s contribution addressed only the reward — “It might affect your chainmail” — and Valphine stifled a laugh, since mithral chain mail can survive fire. She cared little for the ranch’s wooden structure and less for the sentiment attached to it: fire is transformation, fire is purification, and there is a raw satisfying power in watching such efficient destruction. The spectacle read to her as a brutal commentary on the failure of the Carver’s plan, and she silently agreed with its efficiency. Borg destroyed the farmhouse, the cornfields, and the gardens.

Then he did the one thing that betrayed real cunning: he pointed the three rot-tusk ogres in a direction entirely opposite to the one he took himself, splitting his forces amid the rage. Vukradin spoke for the party — “I think we follow Borg” — and Valphine agreed, on the grounds that the trail of the furious half-orc is the trail to the true centre of the intrigue, and that the pursuit of the schemer is always more rewarding than the destruction of his tools.
