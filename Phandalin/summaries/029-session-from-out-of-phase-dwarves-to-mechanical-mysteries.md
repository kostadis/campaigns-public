# Chapter 29: From Out-of-Phase Dwarves to Mechanical Mysteries

Date: 2026-01-07

## Summary

The session resumed with the battle in the mustering hall of Axeholm already underway as the party fought the twisted dwarves caught in an interdimensional nightmare. Although they continued casually calling the creatures “ghouls,” the DM repeatedly clarified that they were dwarves trapped in “a state of animated suspension” by warped dimensional forces. Valphine fired her crossbow at one of the out-of-phase creatures, bringing it to the brink of death. Soma began the session paralyzed in her powerful bear form after a dwarf’s unnatural touch in the previous session, but her constitution proved strong enough to shake off the effect at the end of the dwarf’s next turn. Together, the party systematically eliminated the remaining dwarves, with Brewbarry delivering crushing blows and Vukradin supporting his allies with inspiring words. Two previously turned dwarves had fled and were still somewhere in the fortress, though their exact location was unknown.

After the combat concluded, Valphine took ten minutes to tend to the party’s wounds, channeling a Prayer of Healing that restored ten hit points to everyone. Soma noted that she still had roughly fifty minutes remaining in her bear form. The group then began exploring the strange fortress. The spatial dimensions continually shifted at the edge of their vision: objects approximately thirty feet away appeared to draw closer, only to stop, while the space ahead collapsed back into normal proportions as the party advanced. Blinking lights flickered on the upper level, the light of Lathander itself seemed somehow unreal, and a pervasive stench of death hung in the air.

The mustering hall had apparently been damaged by an earthquake. Its eastern half was strewn with debris and counted as difficult terrain. The party discovered a narrow, rubble-strewn tunnel leading upward, only two feet wide and five feet high—large enough for an ordinary humanoid, but not for an enlarged or raging Brewbarry. There was brief discussion of scouting with Soma’s familiar before everyone remembered that it had been eaten during an earlier encounter and dissolved into magical dust.

Valphine led the way through the cramped passage to an upper level, initially leaving Soma below to guard against the return of the fleeing dwarves. There they found a ransacked room filled with wrecked cots, chairs, tables, and other ruined furnishings. A blackened fireplace dominated the eastern wall, its chimney rising twenty feet to the outside world. Investigating the chimney, they discovered it opened onto the mountain face above the route by which they had originally entered Axeholm. It offered a useful point of orientation, but no enemies or discoveries, so the party returned downstairs.

Their methodical search carried them through a stair chamber analogous to the room that housed Axeholm’s winch. The stairs showed no evidence that the fleeing dwarves had passed that way. Farther east, the party found earthquake damage, collapsed caves, and signs that the twisted dwarves had been digging through the rubble.

The search then brought them to what had once been a dwarven shrine. Its walls were carved with images of dwarves carrying censers and observing religious rites. The eastern section had collapsed, leaving rubble and gnawed remains scattered across the floor. Among the debris, the party discovered a platinum amulet suspended from a thin but sturdy gold chain. A successful Religion check identified it as a symbol of Moradin, the good-aligned dwarven god.

They also found an unlocked steel coffer weighing five pounds. Valphine inspected it carefully and established that it was exactly what it appeared to be, though the group had to be reminded that “unlocked” did not mean “open.” Inside, packed securely in silt, lay a Potion of Superior Healing. The discovery prompted a debate over ownership. Vukradin argued that the dwarven relics belonged to Axeholm’s rightful owner and should be surrendered to Townmaster Wester for Phandalin’s benefit, while others suggested that the Privy Council, on which Vukradin served, could hold the valuables in trust.

Continuing their exploration, the party encountered a locked door. Valphine used her thieves’ tools to open it with a modest but sufficient effort, revealing an entirely empty fifteen-foot-square room. Her keen insight suggested that it had once been used for storage, then deliberately cleaned out before being locked again. No secret doors explained the oddity, leaving the party to wonder, “Why would you lock an empty room?”

Returning through the mustering hall, they again saw the strange blinking lights above, though they heard nothing associated with them. South of the hall they passed through a defensive kill zone with ballistas positioned to fire on intruders. It was no longer dangerous because the dwarves who would have operated the weapons were absent—or had become the very creatures now roaming Axeholm.

The party then reached a grand, soot-stained pillared hall. Valphine quietly opened the door and spotted two more twisted dwarves standing out of phase in its center. A dark fireplace stood at the eastern end, while a deep southern alcove held a dais and limestone throne flanked by granite statues of dwarven warriors. Between the throne and fireplace was a hallway with stairs leading upward.

The party won the element of surprise. Soma, somehow managing a bear’s version of stealth, struck first and mauled one dwarf to death before it could react. Valphine, Brewbarry, and Vukradin wounded the second, with Vukradin’s Bardic Inspiration turning one otherwise failed attack into a hit—albeit for only a single point of damage.

Their early success was interrupted when a horrifying figure emerged from behind the throne: what had once been Axeholm’s Dwarven King, now fused into a three-headed monstrosity. Another ordinary twisted dwarf entered from elsewhere in the chamber. The corrupted King had far more vitality than the others and could lash out with a claw and three separate bites.

As the battle unfolded, the party became aware of the chamber’s deeper wrongness. A low, tooth-rattling hum filled air that should have been silent, like a massive heart beating within the stone. Dust did not settle but vibrated in perfect, unnatural geometric circles. At intervals, the center of the room rippled like heat haze. The twisted dwarves screamed in pain when these distortions passed through them, then recovered and continued fighting.

The King immediately attacked Soma. His claw struck and paralyzed her, allowing his three mouths to descend upon the helpless bear with advantage. The assault exhausted her wild shape and left Soma unconscious in her normal form. As the players observed, being knocked out provided an unfortunately effective “anti-paralysis effect.”

A lesser dwarf moved toward Brewbarry while muttering about “Efficiency,” but Brewbarry’s reach allowed him to strike it as it approached. Once all of the damage was correctly reconciled, the creature had been killed before completing its attack. Soma succeeded on her first death saving throw while Valphine weighed healing her against controlling the remaining undead.

Valphine chose Turn Undead. The nearly dead lesser dwarf succumbed and fled up the fireplace, while the King resisted. Brewbarry entered a rage and enlarged himself, squeezing through the doorway to confront the monarch. Vukradin granted him further inspiration and attempted to assault the King’s mind with Phantasmal Force, conjuring an image of Moradin attacking the corrupted ruler. Despite the King’s poor intellect, he narrowly resisted the first illusion.

The King advanced into Brewbarry’s reach and suffered a devastating twenty-two-point critical strike, yet continued forward and attacked. Brewbarry resisted the paralysis and, thanks to his rage, reduced much of the physical damage, taking seven points in total after the King’s claw, bite, and strange necrotic time-dilation effect were resolved.

Valphine then rushed to Soma and restored ten hit points with Cure Wounds. Brewbarry inflicted another eleven points of damage, and Vukradin attempted Phantasmal Force a second time. This time the spell took hold: the King believed a giant bear was attacking him and suffered fourteen psychic damage. Even though the illusion occupied his attention, the battered monarch screamed “the expert” while targeting Valphine, then turned his claws and all three mouths upon her. Every attack missed.

The chamber vibrated violently once more and then abruptly became still. Soma, back in the battle, delivered the final attack that killed the three-headed horror. The party immediately advanced to fifth level—not because they had fully secured Axeholm, the DM stressed, but because they would need that strength for what came next.

Searching the fallen King, the party found a signet ring bearing Axeholm’s crest: a handaxe clutched in a dwarven gauntlet. The ring was worth five gold pieces, but more importantly, its construction revealed that it also functioned as a key to an unidentified lock. Valphine retained it on behalf of the Privy Council and the future citizens of Phandalin.

The party continued into Axeholm’s dining room, where a carved stone table fifteen feet long and five feet wide stood between equally long wooden benches. Two iron chandeliers hung overhead, their candles long since melted away. North of the seating area were a sooty fireplace and an alcove containing a rough-hewn pit descending sixty feet. Although initially eager to descend in search of anything valuable for Phandalin, the party learned that this was simply the fortress’s refuse chute, where food waste had been dropped to rot. A search through its muck produced nothing and would have ruined any fashionable clothing worn during the attempt.

The adjacent kitchen contained metal dishware, utensils, drinking mugs, and a remarkable stone sink with indoor plumbing. Its faucet still produced hot water that did not smell of sulfur. Brewbarry sampled and examined it with the attention of a wine taster and determined that it was ordinary water fed by a natural hot spring. The party chose to leave the kitchenware in place for the citizens who might eventually occupy Axeholm.

Finally, the party entered the smithy, where rusty anvils stood before two blackened hearths once used to fashion and repair weapons and armor. A fluttering noise came from the southernmost fireplace. When Valphine investigated, the source panicked and flew out: a tiny mechanical mosquito-like creature with a steampunk appearance and a small glass vessel containing blood.

The creature attacked, but Valphine acted first and chose to capture it rather than destroy it. She subdued it with a nonlethal blow. Vukradin’s examination revealed an unnatural construct that offended his affinity for the natural, musical, and aesthetically pleasing. Markings on its body appeared to indicate quantity, suggesting it had been built to collect and measure samples. With that unsettling device in their possession and much of Axeholm still unexplored, the session ended.

## NPCs

### Out-of-Phase Dwarves

Dwarves caught in an interdimensional twist, appearing out of phase and attacking the party. Although frequently called “ghouls” during play, the DM emphasizes that they are dwarves trapped in “a state of animated suspension” within an interdimensional, twisted reality. Their claws can paralyze victims, leaving them vulnerable to follow-up bites. They appear largely mindless and unwilling or unable to retreat except when compelled by Turn Undead. Some fled during earlier combat, and one later escaped up a fireplace after being turned.

One of the lesser dwarves muttered “Efficiency” as it moved to attack Brewbarry. The creatures also reacted painfully when the pillared hall’s air rippled, screeching before recovering.

### Dwarven King

A twisted, three-headed version of what was once the Dwarven King of Axeholm. He emerged from behind the limestone throne in the pillared hall and possessed substantially more vitality than the lesser corrupted dwarves. His fused form could make one claw attack and three bite attacks, and his claw was capable of inflicting paralysis.

The King paralyzed Soma and knocked her unconscious in a single turn. He endured a twenty-two-point critical reaction attack from Brewbarry and continued advancing. He resisted Vukradin’s first illusion of Moradin but succumbed to a later illusion of a giant bear. After shouting “the expert” while targeting Valphine, he attacked her with every available natural weapon but missed completely. Soma ultimately delivered the attack that killed him.

The King carried a signet ring bearing the Axeholm crest. The ring was both a modestly valuable artifact and a key to an unidentified lock.

### Steampunk Mosquito

A tiny, odd-looking creature with a steampunk aesthetic, resembling a mechanical mosquito fitted with a small glass vessel containing blood. It was found fluttering inside the southernmost smithy fireplace and attacked after Valphine’s investigation startled it.

Valphine knocked the construct unconscious with a nonlethal blow and retained it for examination. Its body bears markings associated with quantity, and Vukradin concluded that it was an unnatural sampling device designed to collect and perhaps measure specimens. Its creator, intended targets, and connection to Axeholm’s dimensional disturbances remain unknown.

A narrow, rubble-strewn tunnel two feet wide and five feet high provides access to the upper level.

### Axeholm Upper Level Room

A ransacked room on an upper level of Axeholm, accessible through a narrow tunnel two feet wide and five feet high. It contains wrecked cots, chairs, tables, and other furnishings left behind after the twisted dwarves trashed the chamber.

A blackened fireplace is set into the eastern wall, with a chimney rising twenty feet to the outside. The chimney opens directly onto the mountain face above Axeholm’s entrance and winch route rather than onto a conventional roof. No enemies were found there.

### Axeholm Shrine

A chamber that was once a dwarven shrine, evidenced by wall carvings depicting dwarves carrying censers and observing religious rites. The eastern section has collapsed, filling the area with rubble and gnawed remains, including those of dwarves.

The party found a platinum amulet of Moradin and an unlocked, five-pound steel coffer here. The coffer had been packed with silt to protect a Potion of Superior Healing.

### Axeholm Locked Room

A fifteen-foot-square empty room secured behind a locked door. Valphine picked its lock and concluded that it had once been used for storage but was deliberately cleaned out before being locked again. It contained no secret doors or other obvious explanation for why an empty chamber had been secured.

### Axeholm Pillared Hall

A death-scented chamber featuring pillars, a dark soot-stained fireplace at its eastern end, and a deep southern alcove. The alcove contains a dais with a limestone throne flanked by granite statues of dwarven warriors. A hallway with stairs leading upward lies between the throne and fireplace.

The chamber is affected by severe dimensional disturbances. A low, tooth-rattling hum like a massive heart beating inside the stone fills the air. Dust motes vibrate in perfect geometric circles rather than settling, and the room periodically ripples like heat haze. These pulses cause the corrupted dwarves visible pain.

The hall was the site of the battle against the three-headed Dwarven King and several lesser out-of-phase dwarves.

### Axeholm Dining Room

A large room featuring a carved stone dining table fifteen feet long by five feet wide, flanked by equally long wooden benches. Two iron chandeliers hang above the table, their candles long since melted away. A sooty fireplace stands north of the seating area.

An alcove contains a rough-hewn pit descending sixty feet. The pit served as a refuse chute into which Axeholm’s occupants dropped food and other waste to rot.

### Axeholm Kitchen

A dusty dwarven kitchen equipped with metal dishware, utensils, drinking mugs, and other surviving implements. The party left these supplies in place for Phandalin’s future refugees.

A stone sink has a working faucet that produces hot water. Brewbarry determined that the water is ordinary, does not smell of sulfur, and is naturally heated by a hot spring. The plumbing is an uncommon and impressive surviving example of dwarven engineering.

### Axeholm Smithy

A room containing two blackened hearths and a pair of rusty anvils, once used by Axeholm’s dwarves to fashion and repair armor and weapons. A fluttering noise from the southernmost fireplace revealed a tiny mechanical mosquito-like sampling device, which Valphine knocked unconscious with a nonlethal blow and retained for examination.

## Items

### Platinum Amulet

A platinum amulet on a thin but sturdy gold chain, found among the gnawed remains and rubble in the dwarven shrine. A Religion check identified it as a symbol of Moradin, the good-aligned dwarven god.

The party debated whether to surrender it to Townmaster Wester as the representative of Phandalin, return it to Axeholm’s rightful owner, or allow the Privy Council to hold it in trust.

### Steel Coffer

An unlocked steel coffer weighing five pounds, found in the dwarven shrine. Valphine’s careful inspection revealed no danger, though the coffer still had to be opened despite being unlocked. It was packed with silt and contained a Potion of Superior Healing.

### Potion of Superior Healing

A potent healing potion found nestled protectively in silt inside the steel coffer in the dwarven shrine. Soma immediately claimed “dibs,” and the party debated its ownership, but the recording does not establish its immediate holder.

### Axeholm Signet Ring

A signet ring found on the defeated Dwarven King. It bears the Axeholm crest, depicting a dwarven gauntlet clutching a handaxe. The ring is worth five gold pieces.

Its shape and construction indicate that it also functions as a key for an unidentified lock. Valphine retained it on behalf of the Privy Council and Phandalin’s future interests in Axeholm.

## Spells

### Prayer of Healing

Cast by Valphine after the mustering-hall combat. The spell took ten minutes and restored ten hit points to every member of the party. Because the healing interval was much shorter than a short rest, Soma retained approximately fifty minutes of her bear form.

### Sacred Flame

Cast by Valphine during combat against a wounded out-of-phase dwarf. The target was required to make a DC 14 Dexterity saving throw, failed despite its Dexterity bonus, and took enough radiant damage to fall to two hit points.

### Turn Undead

Used by Valphine during the battle in the pillared hall with a DC 14 saving throw. The Dwarven King resisted, but one badly injured lesser dwarf was successfully turned and fled up the fireplace without attacking.

### Cure Wounds

Cast by Valphine after the Dwarven King paralyzed Soma, destroyed her bear form, and left her unconscious. The spell restored ten hit points, returning Soma to the fight in time to deliver the final attack against the King.

### Phantasmal Force

Cast twice by Vukradin against the Dwarven King.

The first casting attempted to make the King believe Moradin was attacking him. Despite the creature’s negative Intelligence modifier, it succeeded on its save by one and resisted the illusion.

The second casting created the image of a giant bear attacking the King within the spell’s ten-foot cube. The King failed its save, treated the phantasm as real, and suffered fourteen psychic damage. Although the illusion distracted him, he still chose to attack Valphine; all four of his attacks missed.

### Bardic Inspiration

Used by Vukradin to grant inspiration dice during both major combats. In the mustering hall, an inspiration die converted Brewbarry’s failed attack into a hit that dealt eight damage. Brewbarry’s advantage on that attack came from separate combat circumstances, not from Bardic Inspiration.

In the pillared hall, inspiration again converted a miss into a hit, though that attack dealt only one point of damage. Vukradin later granted Brewbarry another inspiration die as the barbarian enlarged himself and moved to confront the Dwarven King.
