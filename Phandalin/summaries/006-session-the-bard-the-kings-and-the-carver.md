# Chapter 6 The Bard, the Kings, and the Carver

## Scenes

### 006.01 Vukradin 01-02-Tarsakh 1495

Vukradin, ever the theatrical one, taunts the grotesque creature before them. “We have it outnumbered\! It’s stunned\! We shall kill it before it lays a hand on us\!” His voice booms, filled with an almost reckless confidence that, at times, borders on delusion.

But the battle is not going as planned. Valphine shoots the mimic with her hand crossbow, steadily wearing it down while keeping well clear of Soma’s more indiscriminate magic. Brewbarry, the stalwart warrior, struggles in vain to pull his halberd free, the weapon stuck fast inside the creature. His attempt fails, leaving the mimic to munch on it for another round.

Valphine watches with horrified confusion, wondering why the barbarians of Uthgardt were once considered such terrors of the North. Then she remembers that they were ultimately crushed by the city empires and concludes that Brewbarry’s inability to wrest a halberd from a sticky creature may explain why.

“Everybody has a bad day,” someone offers in Brewbarry’s defense.

“But he wasn’t drinking,” comes the answer.

Soma’s Poison Spray dissipates harmlessly around the creature, leaving it utterly unaffected. She had considered Ice Knife, but Valphine has already taken care to remain outside the likely blast radius.

“Ice Knife is nice when there’s a crowd,” Soma observes.

“Yes, a cloud of your friends,” comes the correction.

The mimic misses its strike at Brewbarry, then moves with Brewbarry’s halberd still embedded in it.

“You will not touch us\!” Vukradin declares, casting Vicious Mockery. The mimic makes its saving throw and remains unaffected.

“Such a wise mimic,” someone remarks.

“I don’t think the mimics have feelings to be taunted,” Soma says.

“Who are you to judge?” Vukradin replies. “As a druid, I would think you’d be open to the possibility of mimics being part of the general ecosystem. Or are you one of those druids?”

At this point, Soma, usually reserved and focused on the immediate threat, mutters under her breath, a rare show of frustration. “Unnatural magical creations that have no place in the material world.”

Vukradin, with his usual impeccable timing for tangential conversations, turns to her, a glint of curiosity in his eyes. “Wait, you’re a naturalist druid?”

Soma looks at him, her expression a mix of disbelief and annoyance that he would choose this precise moment, mid-battle, to discuss theological taxonomy. “Yes,” she replies, her voice clipped. “Constructs, extra-planar creatures, are all abominations. Disruptions to the natural order.”

Vukradin, unable to resist, presses on, a mischievous glint in his eye. “What about Aasimar’s and Tieflings?” He pronounces the words with a dramatic flourish, clearly relishing the debate.

Soma looks at him, truly confused this time. “What are those?” she asks, her brow furrowed. The question, delivered with genuine ignorance, clearly takes Vukradin by surprise.

Vukradin, momentarily flustered, mutters, “Aasimar are touched by Celestials, Tieflings by the Abyss\!” He gestures vaguely upwards and then downwards, as if sketching the celestial and infernal realms in the air.

“I’m touched by the gods,” he insists.

“Actually, no, you’re genetically linked to—” someone begins.

Soma, however, remains unimpressed by his poetic flair. She looks at him with a steady, pragmatic gaze. “You’re just another creature born into this world,” she states, her voice calm and unwavering. “There is no celestial touching here.”

Vukradin’s face contorts in annoyance. His poetic license, a cornerstone of his self-proclaimed identity, had been challenged. “Let me explain something. I’m a bard, and I take certain poetic license. Please don’t literal splain me.”

“For a celestial being, you’re pretty naive,” comes the reply.

Meanwhile, oblivious to the philosophical debate raging behind him, Brewbarry is about to unleash a powerful, albeit desperate, punch with his bare fists when the others warn him that striking the mimic directly might leave him stuck to it as well.

“You have no ranged weapons,” Vukradin says.

Brewbarry remembers that he has a handaxe.

“But it might eat my handaxe,” Brewbarry protests.

“We’ll help you get it back.”

“And your halberd.”

“You’ll get everything back when it dies.”

And then, with a final, decisive strike, Soma finishes it off, her earlier philosophical musings giving way to efficient, deadly action. The creature she regards as an unnatural blight on the material world is no more.

“First level characters for the win,” someone cheers.

“Yay\!”

### 006.02 Soma 01-02-Tarsakh 1495

“Let’s see what else is here,” I muttered.

We had killed one shapeshifter among the barrels, but there was immediate disagreement about whether we should search for more.

“What if there’s more?” someone asked.

“More money, right?” came the answer.

Vukradin wanted to leave before curiosity created another problem. “Look, I support your like high and mighty ideals. I do, but in this case, let’s just get the money.”

Everyone stopped to appreciate the rarity of Vukradin enthusiastically accepting a reward.

“Wait, hold on. It’s so rare that he actually takes reward. We should celebrate this.”

“This is organic free trade gold,” Vukradin explained.

“What does organic free trade gold mean?”

“That’s the type of gold he’s allowed to receive.”

“It’s not made from animal byproducts.”

“It’s vegan gold.”

“No innocent people were harmed in the making of this gold,” Vukradin clarified. “And the gold was not stolen or ill-gotten.”

And on cue, I heard Vukradin question my choices, “You are going to steal gold?”

I wanted to hide inside my shell. “If you want to live the impoverished life, that’s your choice. I am not opposed to some gold of questionable provenance.”

More precisely, I told him, “You’re more than welcome to take your morals and enjoy the poverty life. I’m not trying to convert you.”

And I pointedly turned my back on him.

But I was out of luck; he wasn’t done with me. “I am rich\! I have sending stones\!”

“Do you know how valuable those are?” he demanded.

“About 50%,” someone replied.

Vukradin insisted that the stones would eventually make thousands of gold. Valphine continued talking to him, “But I thought you were going to use them as part of your music?”

The possibilities multiplied: stereo performances, performances in several taverns at once, perhaps even the foundation of Vukradin’s future studio. Then someone reminded him of the stones’ actual limitation.

“No, they can only be used for a single short message.”

“So basically, it’s Vine.”

“It’s like the Twitter equivalent.”

I remembered a particular bird in the forest that would sing a single note all season, and how everyone would wait for this note, and wondered whether I should tell Vukradin this. And I remembered that I hadn’t been paid, and decided not to.

After we finished checking the room with the chests, I followed Vukradin to the inventors, where Fibblestib and Dabbledob were very excited to see us.

“Where have you been?” they asked. Then they remembered. “Oh, yes. You went to find the shapeshifter. Did you find the shapeshifter?”

“We brought you a shapeshifter,” we told them. “We found one among your barrels.”

We handed them the proof of the monster’s death, and they immediately started discussing what kind of construct they could create from the creature’s entrails.

“This shapeshifter—I think we could use this shifter ichor to build some new devices,” one said.

“Yes, I could not agree more,” said the other.

Within moments, they were arguing over precisely which mechanical devices could be made from the remains.

“This is how the abominations that walk the north came to be. Inventors who showed no concern for the natural world.”

But I kept my mouth shut.

When someone asked whether the inventors had the promised wizard’s hat, they explained that only the kings could award it. We had merely stopped because the inventors were on the way.

And so I followed Vukradin to the King’s room, where we were to receive our prize.

### 006.03 Valphine 01-02-Tarsakh 1495

I watch as Vukradin attempts to negotiate with the two Kings. And I sigh.

King Gnerkli greets us with relief, and we present the corpse of the shapeshifter. King Korboz is immediately vindicated. The danger had been real all along.

King Korboz complains that King Gnerkli “Doesn’t listen to him and believe a single word he has to say.”

“See, you didn’t believe a single word I say,” he snaps. “That’s what you always do, doubting me.”

And I am about to cut their heads off when Vukradin begins to amuse me.

“I find it rude that you didn’t believe your king.”

At which point Gnerkli shouts, “I’m also the king. We’re co-kings here. This is a partnership. This is not a monarchy.”

“Co-kings?” Vukradin asks. “How do you guys make any decisions?”

“We’re a partnership. We work together.”

I stifle a laugh.

“And when you disagree? There is no decision?”

I can sense the tension. “Celestial? He sounds more like a Tiefling.”

Gnerkli explains, “We get into this room and we argue and we argue until one of us wins.”

I can barely keep it together; this is too ridiculous.

Vukradin won’t stop. “How do you decide who wins? Rock, paper, scissors?”

“The best argument wins,” insists Gnerkli.

“And who decides which is best?”

King Korboz, who has recovered from his fear, explains, “Together.”

“Sounds like you have to come to a consensus.”

I now have to insert myself: “You need a clear approver structure.” I have seen cultures like these and have enjoyed tearing them apart. They pretend to be a commune, but have more betrayals, violence, and politics than even the Drow Noble Houses.

Will Vukradin explode their happy commune?

And he does. “I’m wondering, like, if you had three kings, like, would that become exponentially more difficult? Four kings, five kings.”

At which point Gnerkli cuts him off. “Our culture demands that we have two kings because we do not believe in the despotic, autocratic decision-making of a single individual who could be rendered—”

He turns pointedly toward Korboz.

“—temporarily insane and terrified out of his shirts because of a simple shapeshifter.”

“That you didn’t believe existed\!” Korboz retorts.

I wonder where the Bard is going with this, and he twists the knife expertly. “So if you want to install a new leader, you need two new leaders. You need to install them together.”

“No, no. If one passes away early, we install another one.”

“So you always have a senior and a junior.”

“No, there’s no seniority or juniorness. They’re both—”

“But one has been king longer than the other one.”

“Yes, yes, but that, according to the way we—this is a very fluid dynamic and relies on a lot of trust.”

“So let me get this straight, if I have been King for a decade, and this new guy becomes King, I have to treat him as an equal and take orders from him?”

“Well, there’s no taking orders. We have to agree.”

“Why would I want to agree with a guy who just joined the conversation five seconds ago?”

King Gnerkli mutters, “Where did you learn all this about government?”

Vukradin supplies his name and spins a tale of his expertise and training, fictional no doubt. “I get to learn all sorts of things at performing school and acting school and theater school and diplomacy school.”

“Oh, you’re educated in matters of these things,” Korboz says.

“I am.”

And on cue, King Korboz shouts, “See, I told you, as the senior king, I should have preferential—”

“I am proud that I have now disrupted the system of government,” Vukradin announces.

Gnerkli groans, “Oh, are we doing that again?”

And at that point, I smile. The two kings will be at each other’s throats for months if not years.

Vukradin cuts them off, “Did you have a wizard hat for us?”

King Gnerkli, angry at Vukradin, shouts, “Here\! And go\!”

We take the hat and leave, an exit someone aptly compares to “farting in an elevator and getting off.”

As I walk past the two inventors, I realize that they have been overhearing us.

“What exactly did you tell them?” they ask.

Vukradin explains that he merely had some questions about their government. “Just asking questions, guys.”

Fibblestib tells Vukradin, “I’ve always been fond of a bicameral legislature with a unitary executive and an independent judiciary.”

Dabbledob, trying to one-up him, explains, “I prefer more kings\! That way we could have a vote\!”

She argues that two kings inevitably produce deadlocks; more kings, or perhaps a legislature capable of casting a tie-breaking vote, would be an improvement.

Vukradin tries to create more chaos in a way I can appreciate, and says, “I think you should be king\!”

Soon the inventors suggest that perhaps everyone could be a king. They are less interested in assuming the throne themselves, however, than in building a voting machine that would be resistant to manipulation.

“Not tamper-proof, just tamper-resistant.”

“There’s no such thing as the perfect, you know, tamper-proof systems.”

“Tamper-evident is what you really mean here, just to be clear.”

That creates a fresh argument over whether the device should be tamper-proof, tamper-resistant, or tamper-evident—and whether it would be expected to resist a ninth-level Wish spell.

By the time we leave, Vukradin has created a strain of republicanism inside the dual monarchy.

### 006.04 Soma 01-02-Tarsakh 1495

The tortle is pleased that she is a guide for Vukradin. His tab continues to grow, and at some point, she will want to be paid, but she is fine for now.

The journey back to Phandalin is uneventful. There are no wandering monsters, no ogres trying to eat anyone, and no dragons descending from the sky.

“I’m not just a snack,” someone remarks. “I’m a whole damn meal.”

Arriving in Phandalin, she leads him to the Townmaster. The Townmaster’s Hall is once again closed for business. When we knock, Harbin Wester again assumes a dragon has come for him.

“If this is a dragon knocking, I just have to tell you that I am a scrawny, unappetizing, old, fatty, gristly piece of meat. You do not want to eat me.”

We convince him that we are not a dragon and present the hat as a powerful magical item which will surely help him defeat the beast. Even Vukradin cannot put much conviction behind the claim.

Harbin studies it. “What is it?”

“This is what they had,” Vukradin explains. “We asked them for something to help us. We explained the whole story about the dragon, how it’s coming to the town, how we’re all in danger. Do you have anything you could spare? And they said, well, mostly we make things. Like they have this crazy thing that spun around and shot arrows at people. And they said, we also have this magic item, and it seems like you want a magic item. So this is what they gave us. It’s, I guess it’s the best we could hope for. It’s the best we could do.”

Harbin accepts the hat and pays the promised fifty gold, carefully writing a receipt as he removes the money from the town’s coffers.

“Oh, he actually keeps records.”

“Yes, of course I keep records. What do you think I am?”

With our second task complete, we all feel the benefit of hard-earned experience. Soma gains the ability to assume animal shapes; Vukradin announces that he is now proficient in stealth and sleight of hand specifically so that he can recover stolen property.

“I just want you guys to know that I now have proficiency in stealth and sleight of hand, and this is so I can steal back all of the stolen items and give them back to their rightful owners.”

“Steal back from whom?”

“From whoever stole them in the first place and not from those who may have acquired them afterwards.”

“Whoever illicitly currently has them in their possession. These details matter.”

With the fifty gold in their pockets, split four ways, and the sun setting, she heads off to spend the night in the Stonehill Tavern.

Surprisingly, the Bard performs well. After so many lackluster attempts, his music is finally good enough to impress Toblen Stonehill.

“I’ve been practicing a lot,” Vukradin says.

Toblen tells him that the performance reminds him of a curious tale from Triboar, his old home.

“There was a play in Triboar. It had as its main actor someone who was always dressed in character as a Demon, but had the most ridiculous name, Bob. His performances were legendary\! And your performance reminded me of that.”

“It was a legendary performance,” Toblen says. “Everybody came out from out of town to come see it. It was like advertised left, right, and center.”

He had missed opening night but attended several of the later performances. He is careful to qualify his praise: “I mean, your music wasn’t anywhere near it, but it reminded me of that.”

The Bard perks up, for he, too, had heard of this play. It had had a spectacular run several years ago, although it ended. He had been trying for years to get the script.

“Whatever happened to that play, musical extravaganza?”

Toblen explains that the production toured the coast after leaving Triboar, and he eventually lost track of it.

Vukradin shouts, “Maybe my studio could do a revival.”

The tavern keeper looks at him. “You’d probably have to go south to Triboar to find out. I honestly don’t know.”

“Okay, well, let’s put that on our list. I want to do a revival.”

The search for the lost musical and its script joins the group’s growing list of ambitions.

### 006.05 Valphine 02-02-Tarsakh 1495

The night passes without an attack. In the morning, as we prepare to leave, we notice a half-elf eating breakfast. He is about five feet four, with a round face, short wavy auburn hair, a very short beard, grey eyes, and smooth pink skin. He wears a tan chiton tunic and loose brown pants and looks like a miner.

“You mean he’s underage?”

“No, no, no.”

“I might certainly not talk to him then.”

The half-elf gives Valphine a disapproving small-town stare when he realizes that the drow is a cleric of Lathander.

“But you’re a drow.”

“Well, yes. He’s gloriously painful.”

With little else to say to the suspicious miner, we leave Phandalin for Umbrage Hill.

I was annoyed. We were going to save a woman’s life, at our risk, for a mere 25 gold. It seemed absurd. But Lathander’s Cleric had cautioned me about this.

But then Vukradin, once again, surprised me. “We’re going to save this woman, and the townmaster’s only promised us 25 gold, and I guess that’s his prerogative, but I really think this is worth more. We should insist on, you know, additional payment, I think, for this service we’re doing.”

My interest was piqued; did the others agree with me?

“Bargain on the price of a life. Is that what I’m hearing?”

“I think he’s negotiating after he’s done.”

Soma, who cares about all life, disagreed, of course.

But Vukradin muttered, “But my music studio, like, 25 gold, I mean, come on. These other things we did were 50 gold each.”

I rolled my eyes. This fool could solve all of his problems if he would just do what we are allowed to—take gold from monsters, also known as creatures that Lord Neverember thinks don’t belong in his territory.

Soma, again, tried to thwart his natural impulses when she said, “Sometimes the reward is knowing you made the world a little better.”

When Vukradin dismissed her, she muttered, “I just have a kinship with life. I don’t want anyone to die.”

I heard that and almost coughed, as I thought of her Ice Knife. The Tortle explained that 25 gold was plenty.

“Really?” Vukradin asked.

“Well, Vukradin over here is, you know—”

“I’m just very impressed with that, with your price points.”

“Things in life aren’t that expensive.”

The Tortle continued, “I don’t have aspirations like a music studio.”

“You don’t?”

“No, I just—a new teapot would be nice.”

“Okay. I guess that’s kind of okay. We could ask for a teapot. That seems good. Twenty-five gold and a teapot.”

And so the talking continued as we made our way to Umbrage Hill to save Adabra.

*Some time later.*

The chill wind bites at my face as I pick my way carefully along the narrow trail winding through the rocky hills. The morning light falls over the jagged stones and scraggly bushes, and the silence feels heavy, too heavy. I grip my sword tightly, senses straining for any sign of movement.

Then, out of the corner of my eye, I catch a flicker—a shadow slipping behind a boulder—my heart hammers.

Something is amiss, although I cannot identify what.

“I got a bad feeling about it,” I think, but I say nothing to my companions. I am suspicious, not yet alarmed. We are in the open country, not the Dark Realms, and I refuse to treat every moving shadow as an ambush.

But I dismiss this.

I am not in the Dark Realms, worried about every shadow.

### 006.06 Soma 02-02-Tarsakh 1495

Bandits. Yes, the noise is bandits. Valphine had this tense look on her face, but said nothing. Maybe she sensed something? Perhaps she didn’t. But these bandits would have gotten the jump on us if they had not made so much noise.

Four of them occupy the surrounding hilltops, crossbows ready. Most of us spot the danger in time, but Vukradin is caught completely off guard.

Vukradin shouts that we’re going to die.

“Oh, we’re going to get obliterated. This is going to be awesome.”

“It’s just four bandits, man.”

“Yeah, but they’re all going to go ahead of us in the initiative.”

I can sense his fear. I am old, and I have seen much, and I am not going to die today.

The first bolt strikes me. Before another bandit can loose, I call upon the new power growing inside me.

“Well, hold on a moment. So I’m going to think this is right. So I’m going to Wild Shape into a giant wolf spider.”

And then I am shaped like a spider. This new power of mine is quite strange. I can think and feel as myself, and I am also wholly spider.

“I’m a spider. Rar.”

With spider-like ease, I quickly climb up and strike my enemy. My fangs pierce him, and venom enters his blood. The poison does not fell him outright, but it leaves him barely standing.

The bandit slashes me with his scimitar. The blow lands, but this spider’s body gives me a second reserve of life; beneath it, my own body still bears the wound from the first crossbow bolt.

I take a glance behind me, down below, where the rest of my group is. A bandit aims at Valphine. She’s hit hard.

Another bandit chooses among the cleric, the barbarian, and what he apparently considers “the dilettante druid.” His shot misses Valphine. A fourth bandit also fires on her, inflicting another painful wound.

To my right, I see the Barbarian take note of a bandit on a hill. Like Brewbarry has done for so many years in the wild, he climbs up and swings his halberd. The blow lands as a devastating critical hit, killing the bandit instantly.

It lands with such force that someone jokes the bandit “exploded.”

“Does the barbarian have a signature roar?” someone asks.

“Hoo-ha\! Hoo-ha\!”

“Well, it’s not quite as catchy as ‘Go for the eyes, Boo,’ but I’ll come up with something.”

Why isn’t Vukradin doing anything? Oh, right, he’s in shock. They are so young.

But this bandit is going to die here today. And her bite release crushes his hand, and the poison leaves her glands.

The bandit dies.

“All life is precious, but, you know, maybe not the bandits.”

All life is precious, but not a bandit who is trying to kill me.

I turn around to see what to kill next.

The two surviving bandits take one look at the enormous spider and the blood-covered barbarian and decide to flee.

One of the bandits takes out his crossbow, misses, and starts to run. He’s afraid. And he should be.

And another bandit, seeing my form, runs screaming—“Spiders\!\!\!\!”

One of them has arachnophobia so severe that his hand trembles as he fires.

Vukradin screams at the one who attempted to hit Brewbarry, “He’s taking stolen treasure with him. Get him.”

And of course, the Drow reaches casually with her hand-held crossbow and strikes the fleeing bandit in the back.

“That’s such a—so on brand,” someone observes.

“We’re assholes.”

Vukradin runs forward until the fleeing man is within range and cuts him down with Vicious Mockery.

“I did something,” he celebrates.

And so I turn my head to that poor bandit who was afraid of spiders. And there is Brewbarry, covered in blood, sprinting to get next to him.

“Let him go\!” I shout. “He’s not going to hurt us anymore.”

But realize I am speaking like a Spider. And wonder, what does that sound like?

I sit down.

### 006.07 Brewbarry 02-02-Tarsakh 1495

The last bandit is trapped beside me. Terrified, he swings his sword and misses.

Then he recognizes the marks of the Uthgardt. He begins blubbering about how Uthgardt barbarians killed his family. The sight of me has pulled him back into memories of his village’s destruction.

My rage consumes me. And yet, there is something about this man’s fear. And I think that he has lost his tribe. And I feel sadness.

I drop my halberd and shout, “Friends.”

He looks at me, and I can tell that he has suffered at the hands of my people.

“I am so sorry that the Uthgardt hurt you. They were mean to me, too.”

The man stares at me, a look of confusion on his face. He was expecting to die, not get an apology.

“What do you mean you’re apologizing?” he asks.

“I had a hard time with the Uthgardt too. They kicked me out.”

The man stares and says, “Look, man. I just—I had some bad times. Like, you know, but you’re the barbarians. They pillaged my village. They destroyed everything. And I had no money. So when the Carver said he’d get me a job, I took it.”

He looks at me and sobs, “I’m not a bad person. I don’t want to be doing this. I never wanted to be doing this.”

“You should go have a drink,” I tell him.

He confesses that the Carver has been demanding more and more money and resources from those working under him. He has never met the Carver directly and served beneath the dead leader of this particular band.

When I describe my own exile, he looks at me with dawning recognition.

“Wait, you were kicked out of your group?”

“Yeah, because of the invaders.”

“Brother,” he says.

I have found another exile.

### 006.08 Vukradin 02-02-Tarsakh 1495

The bandit has a boss. I must learn more.

“You should join us at dinner and tell us about the Carver?”

Brewbarry has invited the man to share a drink, and Soma returns to her natural form so that the terrified bandit does not have to negotiate while staring at a giant spider.

I stare at him, a bit of menace, a bit of kindliness. The form of an Aasimar can always be helpful.

“Who is the Carver?” we ask.

“What’s there to say. He’s the big boss. And he’s big. I’ve never met him. I just worked with Jax.”

The Carver is the local boss—or at least that is what this bandit was told. He recruits desperate people into a broader bandit operation, then demands increasing amounts of money and resources from them.

The bandit passes along the description he was given: “He’s a thick, powerful man, built like a backcountry butcher’s. His shoulders are broad, his neck is trunk, and his hands are calloused and heavy. You know, he stands with a slight stoop, not from weakness, but like a wolf about to spring.”

He studies Brewbarry.

“Are you the Carver?”

“I’m the brute squad.”

“He’s only one man.”

“He is the brute squad.”

The bandit, Corbin, looks at me like a puppy who has found its momma. Brewbarry and Corbin have both lost their people, and that common wound has done what threats could not.

Corbin is still frightened that the Carver will find and kill him.

“Dude, no, I can’t be—I gotta run. Like, man, the Carver’s gonna find me and he’s gonna kill me.”

“No, no, no, no, no,” we assure him. “We can protect you. He can’t get you here.”

Corbin looks to Brewbarry as though he has found a new tribe. Someone declares, “Brewbarry has acquired a pet.”

“A pet?”

“A follower,” comes the correction. “Remember followers from first edition?”

I smile. And then he asks, “Can I be with you guys?”

I smile, “Of course.”

Perhaps Brewbarry is forming a horde. Corbin may even begin painting himself to resemble his new leader.

The Carver, meanwhile, almost certainly possesses stolen wealth waiting to be reclaimed. A new purpose takes shape: reclaim all the blood money from the Carver.

### 006.09 Soma 02-02-Tarsakh 1495

We search the three dead bandits.

There’s a scroll. I pick it up. It is a clumsily penned parchment, discolored by bright light, accompanied by a tablet of dry, compacted dirt traced with symbols left by burrowing insects. It contains a druid spell.

“Excellent. Dibs.”

And then I hear the annoying voice of Vukradin reminding me that this really should be returned to its rightful owner.

“No, no, no, no, no, no, no, no, no. That is stolen goods.”

He insists that life is precious, but so is property. I argue that the scroll should stay with the only person among us able to use it, but Vukradin tries to persuade me to surrender it.

He rolls a sixteen. I make the opposed roll, then use the Bardic Inspiration he gave me to try again, but I still fail to beat him.

“I guess that scroll goes back to the people.”

I stare at him in disbelief, and then realize he has a point.

Am I any better than a bandit if I keep this?

Between us, the party has apparently developed two firm principles: all life is precious, and all property is precious.
