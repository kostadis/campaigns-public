# Chapter 36: Silencing the Siren’s Warning

Date: 2026-03-10

## Summary

Deep within the northern chamber, surrounded by the ancient bones of a dragon, Valphine claimed the Dragon Slayer Sword she had located at the end of the previous session. It rested atop a dragon skull in the direction Boney had indicated, across from where Lady Alagondar had been laid to rest. Brewbarry and Valphine debated who should carry the glowing blade while Vukradin and Soma remained in the western Sepulcher chamber. Boney, the sentient bone horse who had initially bonded with Soma, watched on with quiet amusement. When the party joked about shoveling after him, Boney observed that this was “one of the problems I don’t have these days.” He rejected being called undead or reanimated, preferring the term “unalived.” The mood was triumphant, but it would not last long.

Valphine, who had picked up the sword, was about to give it to Brewbarry when an invisible stalker launched itself from the shadows. It slammed into her for seven points of damage before its second strike missed, gaining its attack before the party could return to normal initiative. The creature was a summoned elemental, the kind wizards send to guard precious objects or eliminate those who have angered them. Valphine recognized the technique as commonplace among the wizards of Menzoberranzan, where magical wards are maintained specifically to keep such creatures out.

Vukradin was in the Sepulcher chamber with Soma. Hearing the commotion in the northern chamber, he rushed to help, but the distance left him unable to reposition his lingering Cloud of Daggers immediately. The stalker struck Valphine again, inflicting another brutal twenty-one points of damage, then moved away invisibly. Valphine and Brewbarry both swung at the disturbance, but neither connected.

Soma advanced into the chamber and carefully studied the air. She noticed the slight atmospheric disturbances produced by the stalker’s invisible body and moved her beam of pale moonlight over it. The creature failed its Constitution save and suffered twenty points of radiant damage, although Moonbeam did not reveal its form. Brewbarry followed with a blind attack that struck for ten more damage.

Valphine, sensing that the stalker wanted the sword, attempted to outline it with Faerie Fire, but the creature resisted the spell by the narrowest margin. She then tossed the Dragon Slayer Sword toward Brewbarry. Vukradin urged him not to pick it up until the guardian had been dealt with. Brewbarry hesitated, looking down at the prize they had fought so hard to obtain, and asked, “But we just fought all these monsters to get the sword.” Reassured that they could retrieve it afterward—and that he could not use it until he had attuned to it anyway—he left it on the floor.

That proved a wise decision. With no one carrying the blade, the invisible stalker stopped attacking. It moved out of the magical hazards and hovered over the dropped sword in a silent, “come-and-get-it” posture, revealing that its purpose was to prevent the weapon from leaving.

Vukradin and Soma exploited the opening. Soma declared, “I’m gonna moonbeam this sword,” and scorched the guardian again. Brewbarry landed two solid blows for a total of twenty damage, while Valphine struck it with her crossbow. Vukradin repeatedly repositioned his Cloud of Daggers over the stalker, slicing through its unseen form.

The stalker finally abandoned its passive defense and lashed out at Brewbarry for twenty-two points of damage. Brewbarry invoked Stone’s Endurance, reducing the blow by nine. Soma shifted Moonbeam onto it once more, leaving it on the brink of destruction. Brewbarry prepared to finish it, imagining the Dragon Slayer Sword in one hand and a glass of wine in the other, but the party realized that an earlier opportunity attack might already have killed it before its final blow landed. In either accounting, the invisible stalker was destroyed.

Brewbarry approached the sword cautiously and asked, “Can I pick it?” Once Vukradin assured him, “Dude. It’s good. You’re good,” Brewbarry finally claimed the Dragon Slayer Sword for real.

The party healed and took a short rest in the aftermath. As they sat admiring their hard-won prize, a ghost materialized in the chamber. The sorrowful specter of Lady Alagondar did not attack or speak. She simply looked at the party sadly and stared toward the Icespire Mountains. When Vukradin asked Boney whether he knew the ghost, the horse answered, “Nay! Well, yes, I do, but nay!” Lady Alagondar’s silent gaze reminded the party that the white dragon was reputed to dwell in those mountains, although it did not give them a precise location.

Vukradin returned to the party’s other obligations. They still intended to travel to Neverwinter and find the art collector connected to the stolen goods. Xanth, meanwhile, had previously offered to lead them to the lair of the evil half-orc spellcasters, where they hoped to recover treasure without provoking another unnecessary conflict.

But things were not as they had hoped.

Outside, the centaur Xanth stood wounded and bloodied in the fading light as the sun began to set. Then Sister Kaella stumbled into the clearing. Her cloak was missing, her armor had been slashed, and she was gasping for breath. She fell to her knees and raised empty hands toward the glowing sword in Brewbarry’s possession.

“You survived! Thank the gods! You have to listen to me,” she cried. She warned that the Carver was no longer a man but a hollow shell speaking to his sword “in a tongue that makes your ears bleed.” He was marching on Icespire Hold that very night to bind the white worm. If the party failed to stop him before he finished his chant, “he will wear that dragon like a suit of armor.” She added that he would burn Phandalin to the ground merely to test his new weapon. Although he had begun marching that night, Icespire Hold remained far enough away that the party could reach it if they hurried.

Vukradin, however, had long viewed Sister Kaella as a threat. She was an agent of the Carver, and he regarded the party’s alliance with her as a pretense meant to get them closer to their enemy. Valphine had a separate, secret intelligence arrangement with Kaella and questioned why they would attack her when she might still be useful, suggesting that they capture her for the party’s prison or torture chambers instead. Now Kaella was weakened and vulnerable, and Vukradin saw the perfect moment to strike. He succeeded in concealing his hostile move from Kaella long enough to surprise her, although Valphine noticed what he was about to do.

Vukradin paralyzed Kaella with Hold Person. She tried to warn him, “You have no idea what you’ve just done, you idiot,” but the table ruled that paralysis prevented her from speaking, silencing the warning. Soma objected to simply beating a helpless woman and asked, “Could we… do we have to kill her? I mean, at least, like, bring her to justice?” The party therefore switched to non-lethal attacks. Soma struck Kaella with her staff, Valphine missed, and Brewbarry delivered two automatic critical hits that knocked her unconscious.

They had little time to dwell on the consequences. As soon as Kaella fell, an explosive or blinding device landed among Brewbarry, Soma, and Valphine. Soma and Brewbarry resisted it, but Valphine was temporarily blinded. Jax, identified as the last of the Carver’s agents, emerged with several thugs and a hulking Rot-Tusk Ogre—the same clan with whom the party had created a blood feud during their first hunts through the forest.

Jax addressed Vukradin directly: “Thank you for dealing with that little bit of business, Vukradin.” Then he added, “I just want you to know, it’s not personal. It’s just business.” One attacker attempted to net Vukradin and initially missed, but another successfully trapped him, leaving him restrained and unable to move.

Even caught in the net, Vukradin was far from helpless. He cast Hold Person under the table’s played ruling and paralyzed both Jax and the Rot-Tusk Ogre. Soma assumed her great bear form and tore into the immobilized Jax, whose paralysis turned her attacks into critical hits. Still temporarily blinded, Valphine summoned Spirit Guardians. Under the table’s played ruling, she exempted her allies despite being unable to see them. Her radiant aura engulfed Jax, the ogre, and several thugs, burning them and slowing their movement.

Brewbarry entered a rage, crossed the battlefield, and attacked Jax twice. The paralyzed agent could not defend himself, and Brewbarry’s hits became devastating critical strikes. The surrounding thugs recognized that breaking Vukradin’s concentration was their best chance. They converged on the restrained spellcaster and struck him repeatedly. Vukradin used Silvery Barbs to turn one successful blow into a miss, suffered eighteen points from two other attacks, and then took another seven-point hit. He passed every concentration check, leaving Jax and the ogre paralyzed.

Unable to break free of the net with brute strength, Vukradin decided to “go out like a bang.” He unleashed Spray of Cards in a fifteen-foot cone against three of the thugs surrounding him. One was killed outright, while the others were badly wounded.

Soma continued mauling Jax. Although one claw strike missed, Valphine’s Spirit Guardians finished the Carver’s agent as radiant power swept over the battlefield. Under the table’s played ruling, Valphine deliberately stepped out and back into position so that the emanation could re-enter the enemies’ spaces and trigger another round of saves and damage, burning through more thugs. Brewbarry then turned on the still-paralyzed Rot-Tusk Ogre and killed it with two crushing critical hits before it ever managed to attack.

With Jax, the ogre, and most of the thugs dead, the last surviving henchman fled. Brewbarry received an opportunity attack but missed, allowing the thug to escape. He nevertheless sent the fugitive off with one of his prepared battle lines: “Try not to die too quickly, I want to enjoy this.”

When the dust settled, the clearing was littered with the Carver’s fallen agents. Sister Kaella remained unconscious nearby and might no longer be willing to cooperate after the party’s assault. Worse, only after the battle did Vukradin ask whether they knew where Icespire Hold was. The answer was immediate: Sister Kaella did. Valphine could only observe, “See, I told you we could use her. I told you.”

The party stood victorious, the Dragon Slayer Sword gleaming in Brewbarry’s grip, but they would now have to revive and persuade the woman they had just paralyzed and beaten unconscious if they hoped to reach Icespire Hold before the Carver completed his ritual.

## NPCs

### Boney

A sentient bone horse that initially bonded with Soma during the previous session after she dared to approach him. Soma insists that she will not use him for personal gain and that he chose the bond himself. He is partially alive—or “unalived,” as he prefers—and can now join the party in combat without his former extreme initiative penalty. He humorously noted that cleaning up after him and “irregularity” are no longer problems. When asked whether he knew Lady Alagondar’s ghost, he answered, “Nay! Well, yes, I do, but nay!”

### Invisible Stalker

A summoned elemental creature, typically used by wizards to eliminate targets or guard objects. Valphine recognized such creatures as a familiar danger in Menzoberranzan, where magical wards are used to repel them. It ambushed the party after Valphine acquired the Dragon Slayer Sword, attacking her repeatedly and later striking Brewbarry.

The stalker’s behavior revealed that its purpose was to prevent the sword from being removed: once Valphine dropped the blade and Brewbarry was persuaded not to pick it up, the creature stopped attacking and hovered over it in a “come-and-get-it” posture. Soma tracked its atmospheric disturbances and damaged it with Moonbeam, although the spell did not reveal it. Vukradin repeatedly moved Cloud of Daggers over its position, while Brewbarry and Valphine landed direct attacks. The party eventually destroyed it.

### Lady Tanamere Alagondar

A sorrowful spectral figure that appeared after the party secured the Dragon Slayer Sword and took a short rest. She did not attack or speak. She looked at the party sadly and then stared in the direction of the Icespire Mountains, reminding them that the white dragon was reputed to dwell there. Her gesture indicated only a general direction and did not replace the need for more precise guidance.

### Xanth

A wounded and bloodied centaur present outside the chamber as the sun began to set. He had previously offered to take the party to the lair of evil half-orc spellcasters associated with Talos. During Jax’s ambush, the party briefly wondered which side Xanth would take, but he did not intervene in the recorded combat.

### Sister Kaella

An agent of the Carver and the Carver’s spymaster. Vukradin regarded the party’s alliance with her as a pretense meant to get them closer to their enemy, while Valphine had a separate, secret intelligence arrangement with Kaella. She stumbled into the clearing missing her cloak, with slashed armor, badly disheveled and gasping for breath.

Kaella warned that the Carver was marching on Icespire Hold to bind the white worm, intended to wear the dragon “like a suit of armor,” and planned to burn Phandalin. Vukradin attacked her while she was weakened, paralyzing her with Hold Person. Soma insisted that the party use non-lethal force, after which she and Brewbarry knocked Kaella unconscious.

Out of character, the GM explained that Kaella had combat abilities and had been intended to help the party survive Jax’s ambush. She also remained their immediate source of directions to Icespire Hold. The party would need to revive and question her, and she might no longer be willing to cooperate after their assault.

### The Carver

A powerful antagonist described by Sister Kaella as no longer fully human: “a hollow shell talking to his sword in a tongue that makes your ears bleed.” He began marching toward Icespire Hold that night to bind the white worm through a chant or ritual. If successful, he intends to wear the dragon like a suit of armor and burn Phandalin to test his new power. Although he departed that night, the journey to the hold is long enough that the party can still intercept him if they hurry.

### Jax

Described as the last of the Carver’s agents, Jax ambushed the party with several thugs and a Rot-Tusk Ogre immediately after Sister Kaella was knocked unconscious. He thanked Vukradin for “dealing with that little bit of business” and insisted that the attack was “not personal” but “just business.”

Jax had Armor Class 16 and 84 hit points. Vukradin paralyzed him before he could act effectively. Soma mauled him in bear form, Brewbarry landed multiple critical strikes, and Valphine burned him with Spirit Guardians. Jax remained paralyzed after repeated failed saves and was ultimately killed by the party’s combined assault.

### Rot-Tusk Ogre

A large, brutish creature that fought alongside Jax and his thugs. The party had created a blood feud with the Rot-Tusk Ogres during its earliest forest encounters. This ogre was paralyzed alongside Jax by Vukradin’s spell, repeatedly failed to shake off the effect, and suffered radiant damage from Valphine’s Spirit Guardians. Brewbarry ultimately killed it with two critical hits before it ever had the opportunity to attack.

### Thugs

Several henchmen accompanying Jax. They used thrown nets and a blinding effect to disrupt the party. One successfully restrained Vukradin, after which several surrounded and attacked him in an effort to break his concentration and free Jax and the ogre.

Despite multiple successful blows, Vukradin passed every concentration check. His Silvery Barbs converted another hit into a miss, while Spray of Cards killed one thug and badly injured two others. Valphine’s Spirit Guardians burned through the remaining attackers. All but one were defeated; the final thug fled after Brewbarry missed his opportunity attack.

## Locations

### Northern Chamber

An ancient chamber containing the bones of a dragon, where the Dragon Slayer Sword was found resting atop a dragon skull. It lay north of Boney’s niche, in the direction he had indicated. The chamber was also the site of the invisible stalker’s ambush and Lady Alagondar’s later spectral appearance.

### Icespire Mountains

A mountain region where the white dragon is reputed to dwell. Lady Alagondar’s ghost stared sadly in this direction after the party secured the Dragon Slayer Sword. The party interpreted her gaze as a possible reference to the dragon’s rumored home, but it did not reveal a precise location.

### Icespire Hold

A stone fortress in the Icespire region. Sister Kaella warned that the Carver had begun marching there that night to bind the white worm. The journey is long enough that the party can still intercept him by traveling quickly. Kaella knows the route, but the party knocked her unconscious before asking for directions.

### Phandalin

A town that Sister Kaella warned the Carver intends to burn to the ground after binding the white dragon, merely to test his terrible new power.

### Neverwinter

A city the party intends to travel to in order to find an art collector and address the stolen goods in its possession. Those plans were interrupted by Lady Alagondar’s apparition, the appearance of the wounded Xanth, Kaella’s warning, and Jax’s ambush.

## Items

### Dragon Slayer Sword

A powerful +1 sword found resting atop a dragon skull in the northern chamber, north of Boney’s niche and in the direction he had indicated. Its removal triggered the attack by an invisible stalker tasked with preventing the weapon from leaving.

Valphine initially picked up the sword and later dropped it once the guardian’s objective became apparent. The stalker immediately hovered over it, allowing the party to target its position with Moonbeam and Cloud of Daggers. After the guardian was destroyed, Brewbarry cautiously took possession of the blade. He must attune to it to gain its magical benefits, and wielding it requires him to surrender the superior reach of his halberd. The party therefore considered reserving it specifically for the confrontation with the white dragon.

## Spells

### Cloud of Daggers

A concentration spell used by Vukradin to create a swirling cloud of magical daggers. It remained active from the previous battle with the will-o’-wisps. During the invisible stalker encounter, Vukradin initially could not move it onto the creature because he was too far away and could not see its position. After entering the northern chamber and detecting the stalker’s atmospheric disturbances, he repeatedly repositioned the cloud as a magic action, dealing automatic damage whenever it entered the creature’s space.

### Moonbeam

A concentration spell used by Soma to create a beam of pale moonlight that deals radiant damage. It remained active from the preceding battle. Soma detected the invisible stalker by observing its atmospheric disturbances and moved Moonbeam over it, dealing twenty radiant damage on the first failed save.

Moonbeam did not reveal the stalker; its form-changing effect would only matter if the target were a shapeshifter. Once the guardian hovered over the Dragon Slayer Sword, Soma could target it without another Perception check, declaring, “I’m gonna moonbeam this sword.” She moved the beam over it repeatedly until it was near death.

### Faerie Fire

A spell cast by Valphine that can outline enemies in shimmering light and prevent them from benefiting from invisibility. Valphine used it as a familiar drow response to the invisible stalker, but the creature succeeded on its Dexterity save by one, so the spell did not outline it.

### Hold Person

A spell cast by Vukradin on multiple occasions during the session. It was first used to paralyze Sister Kaella, preventing her from acting. When she attempted to warn him, the table ruled that the paralyzed condition prevented her from speaking.

Under the table’s played ruling, Vukradin later used the spell to immobilize both Jax and the Rot-Tusk Ogre. Both repeatedly failed their Wisdom saves. Paralysis granted advantage on attacks against them, and successful attacks from within five feet became automatic critical hits. Vukradin maintained concentration despite being restrained and suffering several attacks, allowing the party to destroy both enemies before they could respond effectively.

### Spirit Guardians

A spell cast by Valphine that summons spectral guardians in a fifteen-foot radius around her. Valphine cast it while temporarily blinded, positioning the aura to catch Jax, the Rot-Tusk Ogre, and nearby thugs. Under the table’s played ruling, she designated her allies as unaffected despite being unable to see them.

The spell inflicted repeated radiant damage throughout the battle. Under the table’s played ruling, enemies made Wisdom saves when the emanation entered their spaces or they began their turns within it, and Valphine’s movement away and back triggered another round of saves and damage. This killed or badly injured several foes and helped finish Jax.

### Silvery Barbs

A reaction spell cast by Vukradin to force an attacker to reroll a successful attack against him. While restrained and surrounded by thugs, he used it to turn one successful hit into a miss. This helped him preserve both his health and his concentration on the spell paralyzing Jax and the Rot-Tusk Ogre.

### Spray of Cards

A spell used by Vukradin in a fifteen-foot cone while restrained by a net and surrounded by thugs. After failing to free himself with a Strength check, he declared that he would “go out like a bang” and caught three enemies in the cone. One failed its save, took the full twenty-five damage, and died; the other two succeeded but still suffered twelve damage each.
