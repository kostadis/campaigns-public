# Chapter 25: When Boars Are More Than Just Boars

Date: 2025-12-09

## Summary

The session opened with the realization that the party should already have advanced to level four after completing the Butterskull Ranch and Loggers’ Camp quests. The players updated their characters and discussed feats, ability-score increases, and possible combinations such as Observant and Tavern Brawler. The GM assured the group that powerful choices would not be a problem: “if it becomes an issue, I know how to make the monsters harder.”

The party arrived at the Falcon Hunting Lodge with their collection of prisoners and rescued companions in tow. Among them was a disoriented Teega the Smith, two Emerald Enclave guards freed from captivity, and a criminal named Shrimpy. Falcon greeted them with surprise, particularly pleased to see his friend Vukradin had returned safely. When Shrimpy asked whether he could finally leave, the party refused and presented him to Falcon as “a genuine criminal.”

Teega, still confused and suffering from severe memory gaps, asked who the party was and why she was there. Careful observation of her muscular arms, hands, and fingers revealed that she was a blacksmith. She explained that the last thing she remembered was working at the Wayside Inn, a roadside tavern complex with an attached forge near the High Road and Triboar Trail. Vukradin recounted the story of her capture and rescue, emphasizing the party’s heroism; although his performance was merely average, Teega was still impressed.

When asked where she had been found, Teega admitted that she had experienced blackouts even before her captivity. She remembered being drawn away by the promise of the Kraken, whose followers offered peace after the wars of the giants, dragons, barbarian hordes, the Lords’ Alliance’s reprisals, and the demonic plague. That peace was described first as “the sweet oblivion” and then as the promise that everyone would be unified into a single whole. Teega recalled that a man passing through the Wayside Inn had told her about the Kraken’s promise, but she could no longer remember his appearance.

Valphine harshly challenged Teega’s willingness to follow such a promise, telling her, “If you’re that easy woman, you need to ponder on your life choices.” Teega defended herself as a victim, prompting the declaration that there would be no victim-blaming. Vukradin took a gentler approach, advocating for Lathander and offering Teega work as a blacksmith for the church. The party also entertained the more mercenary possibility of returning her to the Wayside Inn first in hopes of receiving a reward. Teega declined immediate recruitment, saying that she wanted to return home but might visit the church later. Her questions about whether Lathander could stop her bad dreams suggested that the cult’s influence had left deeper scars.

Through careful observation and medical examination, the party determined that Teega’s mind had been manipulated through psionic means rather than conventional magic. Her brain had effectively been rewired, leaving gaps in her memory and making her vulnerable to the cult’s influence. Given the party’s knowledge of the Underdark, Mind Flayers were an obvious suspect, but Brewbarry’s unexpectedly excellent Arcana insight established that the symptoms did not match their methods. The precise source of the psionic manipulation remained unknown.

Falcon then shared troubling news about one of his investments. The waters around Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine, fifteen miles northeast of Phandalin, had reportedly been poisoned, threatening to make mining impossible. The party already had a contract to escort the new overseer, Don-Jon Raskin, safely to the mine for a reward from Townmaster Harbin. Falcon explained that Don-Jon was involved with a Neverwinter mining consortium and admitted that he had personally invested in the venture at a discounted rate in exchange for scouting and consulting work.

Vukradin pressed Falcon for details about the scale of his investment and who would pay the party to investigate the poisoned water. Falcon initially floated a reward of fifty gold pieces, but admitted that the investment had “crimped” his liquidity and that he had no cash available up front. After extended negotiation—and jokes about angel investing, stock swaps, and literally obtaining fair-trade gold—Falcon offered the party a share of the mine if they solved the poisoning. The prospect delighted Vukradin, who enthusiastically declared his intention to make the mine successful.

The party also revisited the Woodland Manse quest, where orcs and half-orcs had occupied an old manor. Falcon had promised boots of elvenkind as the reward. The party immediately imagined less conventional uses for the boots, including carrying beer in them so that one could drink silently at work.

The Emerald Enclave guards insisted that they were no longer prisoners and needed to report to the Enclave and Lyra about the planar disruption, mind-manipulating society, orcs, and the cult of Talos active in the forest. After negotiation, they agreed to remain at Falcon’s Lodge for two nights to rest and recover, provided the party paid for their stay. Vukradin instead persuaded Falcon to accommodate everyone for free, citing their future business relationship. Falcon agreed, though he warned, “This is the last time. I’ve got a business to run.”

Falcon preferred that the party address the gold mine because his own income was at stake, dismissing the Woodland Manse as a problem another adventuring party could handle. The party, however, considered the boots too useful to pass up and chose the manse first. Early the next morning, at five o’clock, they set out along the trail Falcon indicated. They also noticed that the mysterious Drow woman interested in artifacts had departed from the lodge.

After a ten-mile journey through the forest, the party arrived at a foggy clearing containing a crumbling two-story manor almost hidden beneath thorny ivy. The structure stood on a six-foot-high stone foundation, with a balcony above its main entrance. Wild pumpkins grew in patches around the building, and several wild boars fed among them. The boars snorted contemptuously as the adventurers approached, and Soma answered with an equally contemptuous snort.

Soma used her ability to communicate with animals to question the boars. The first ignored her, but a second complained that “new boars” had recently arrived. These newcomers did not speak boar and showed no respect for the elaborate etiquette governing territorial pumpkin patches. The talkative boar explained that, although he and the boar to the north had not spoken for years, they respected one another’s patches. By contrast, a third boar to the south was “ruder than rude” and had appeared only a few days earlier. The party remembered that Anchorites of Talos could assume animal forms and immediately suspected the newcomer.

Valphine briefly argued that the party should try to parley with the manor’s inhabitants before attacking. The others decided that Moonbeam would provide a decisive test. Soma called down radiant moonlight on the suspicious third boar. It failed to resist, suffered radiant damage, and was forcibly returned to its true form: an Anchorite of Talos.

Combat erupted immediately. The genuine boars began retreating, while the talkative one protested that the adventurers were violating proper protocol and sought reassurance that they did not intend to seize his pumpkin patch. The party promised that their goal was only to clear away the false boars, leaving the pumpkins to their rightful claimants.

Valphine struck the revealed Anchorite with a devastating Guiding Bolt. Brewbarry raged and charged, but even with the bolt’s magical advantage, both dice for his attack missed. Soma shifted into a combat form and wounded the Anchorite further. Vukradin attempted to contain it with Silence, but the Anchorite resisted the party’s efforts to stop it from responding.

The wounded Anchorite stepped out of the Moonbeam. As it withdrew, Brewbarry landed an opportunity attack, further reducing its remaining strength, though Soma’s attempt missed. The Anchorite then shouted loudly, alerting another shapeshifter nearby, and unleashed a devastating Lightning Bolt through Valphine, Soma, and Brewbarry. The spell inflicted thirty-five points of damage before saves and reductions. Brewbarry invoked Stone’s Endurance to reduce the blast by thirteen, while Valphine made her save and took seventeen. The battle remained unresolved when the session ended at the top of the next initiative round, with at least one additional shapeshifter now alerted.

## Scenes

### Leveling Up and Character Development

#### The GM informs the party they should have leveled up to four after completing previous quests, and the players select new feats for their characters.

- The party begins the session believing they are still level three, only for the GM to realize they should already be level four.

- The GM confirms that completing the Butterskull Ranch and Loggers’ Camp quests earned the advancement and accepts responsibility for not telling the players earlier.

- The players discuss whether level five or level eight is where fifth-edition characters become overpowered, with the GM joking that he has researched how to handle that problem.

- Valphine considers the Observant feat, which can provide expertise in Insight, Investigation, or Perception. The group recommends Investigation or Insight because Perception is often handled passively.

- The players discuss feats and ability-score increases, including raising an odd Wisdom score and the interaction between Tavern Brawler and a druid’s Wild Shape attacks.

- Soma ultimately chooses a straightforward Wisdom increase rather than Tavern Brawler.

- The GM permits Tavern Brawler to work with suitable forms possessing paws or hooves and assures the players, “if it becomes an issue, I know how to make the monsters harder.”

- The party updates maximum hit points and related level-four statistics during the later combat.

- Before Teega says anything, the party studies her. Her heavily developed arms, fingers, and general build identify her as a blacksmith.

- She remembers working at the Wayside Inn, near the High Road and Triboar Trail, before abruptly finding herself in a cart.

- The Wayside Inn is not merely a drinking establishment; it is a roadside complex with a forge extension where Teega repairs horseshoes and performs ironwork for travelers.

- Vukradin recounts the story of Teega’s capture and heroic rescue. His performance is only average, but Teega is still impressed and happy to speak with him.

- When asked where she was found, Teega admits that she had experienced blackouts for a long time.

## NPCs

### Falcon

A hunter and ranger who owns the Falcon Hunting Lodge. He is surprised but pleased to see the party return and accepts Vukradin’s claim that they are friends.

Falcon provides information about the poisoned waters surrounding Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine. He said that Don-Jon was involved with a Neverwinter mining consortium that had invested in the mine and revealed that he had obtained his own discounted stake by supplying local scouting and consulting expertise. The investment was substantial enough to crimp his liquidity, leaving him unable to provide a significant cash reward up front.

Falcon initially suggests fifty gold pieces but ultimately offers the party a share of the mine if they resolve the poisoning. He openly admits that his interest is financial and that the party already knows better than to mistake him for a purely heroic benefactor. He prioritizes the mine over the Woodland Manse because the former directly threatens his income.

He permits the party and its companions to stay at his lodge for free after Vukradin appeals to their prospective business partnership, although he warns that this is the last complimentary stay.

### Teega the Smith

A physically powerful woman whose muscular arms, hands, and fingers reveal her trade as a blacksmith. She worked at the Wayside Inn, a roadside tavern complex with an attached forge, before being abducted.

Teega initially does not recognize the party or know why she is traveling with them. She remembers working at the inn and then awakening in a cart. She admits to suffering blackouts even before her capture.

A traveling man introduced her to the Kraken cult’s promise of peace following the Sword Coast’s seemingly endless wars and disasters. The cult described that peace as “the sweet oblivion” and promised that everyone would be unified into a single whole. Teega cannot remember the recruiter’s appearance.

The party determines that Teega’s brain has been manipulated through psionics rather than magic. The effect is not consistent with Mind Flayer domination, although its true origin remains unknown.

Teega views herself as a victim and reacts defensively when Valphine criticizes her choices. She is intrigued enough by the Church of Lathander to ask whether its teachings might stop her bad dreams, but she declines immediate recruitment. She wants to return to the Wayside Inn first and says she may visit the church later.

### Emerald Enclave Guards

Two guards who were previously captives but regained their free status following the death of their captor. They object to being counted among the party’s prisoners.

The guards urgently want to report to the Emerald Enclave and Lyra about a planar disruption, a society capable of manipulating minds, orcs active in the forest, and the cult of Talos. Although they assure the party that its reward will eventually be delivered, they place reporting these dangers above immediate payment.

They agree to rest at Falcon’s Lodge for two nights. If the party has not returned by then, they intend to declare themselves sufficiently healed and depart.

### Shrimpy

A criminal prisoner still being transported by the party. Unlike the Emerald Enclave guards, Shrimpy remains in custody. Upon reaching Falcon’s Lodge, he asks whether he can leave; the party refuses and introduces him to Falcon as “a genuine criminal.”

### Don-Jon Raskin

The newly appointed overseer of Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine. The party has an existing assignment to escort him safely to the mine, for which Townmaster Harbin has promised one hundred gold pieces.

Falcon described Don-Jon as connected to the Neverwinter mining consortium that invested in the mine. The party did not establish that Don-Jon personally owns any portion of it.

### Talking Boar

A genuine wild boar living among the pumpkin patches at the Woodland Manse. Soma can understand him through Speak with Animals.

He is deeply concerned with territorial etiquette and initially regards the party as ignorant outsiders. He and another boar to the north have not spoken in years, but they continue to respect each other’s pumpkin patches.

The boar complains that recent newcomers neither speak boar nor observe the proper protocols. He identifies the southern boar as a particularly rude newcomer who appeared only a few days earlier, enabling the party to expose it as an Anchorite of Talos.

When Moonbeam reveals the shapeshifter, the boar protests that the attack is “not how protocol is handled.” He seeks reassurance that the party does not intend to seize the newly available pumpkin patch and retreats once satisfied that the adventurers are interested only in removing the false boars.

### Anchorite of Talos

A shapeshifting cultist of Talos initially disguised as the southern wild boar at the Woodland Manse. The genuine talking boar identifies it as rude, unfamiliar with boar language, and a recent arrival.

Soma’s Moonbeam forces the Anchorite to resume its true form and inflicts eight radiant damage. Valphine’s Guiding Bolt, Soma’s attack, and Brewbarry’s opportunity attack then wound it further.

The Anchorite nevertheless escapes the Moonbeam, shouts to alert allies, and lines up Valphine, Soma, and Brewbarry for a devastating Lightning Bolt. Its warning appears to alert at least one additional disguised shapeshifter near the manor.

## Locations

A hunting lodge owned and operated by Falcon, serving as a temporary base for the party. Falcon provides quests, regional intelligence, food, and accommodation here.

The party leaves Teega, Shrimpy, and the two Emerald Enclave guards at the lodge while traveling to the Woodland Manse. Falcon agrees to house the group without payment after Vukradin invokes their future business partnership, though he insists that he still has a business to run.

The mysterious Drow woman interested in artifacts is discovered to have left the lodge before the party sets out.

A crumbling two-story manor reached by following a roughly ten-mile forest trail from Falcon’s Lodge. It stands in a foggy clearing and is almost hidden beneath thorny ivy.

The manor rests atop a six-foot-high stone foundation and has a balcony above its main entrance. Wild pumpkin patches surround the structure, with genuine boars and shapeshifting Anchorites of Talos intermingled among them.

Orcs and half-orcs are believed to occupy the manor. The first exposed Anchorite shouts a warning during combat, apparently alerting at least one additional shapeshifter nearby.

## Items

### Boots of Elvenkind

Magical boots promised by Falcon as the reward for investigating and clearing the Woodland Manse. Their normal appeal is silent movement and improved stealth.

The party devises a more mundane use: filling the boots with beer so that one can drink silently at work without attracting an employer’s attention. This becomes known as “stealth drinking,” summarized by the observation, “Modern problems require modern solutions.”

## Spells

### Speak with Animals

Used by Soma to communicate with the wild boars around the Woodland Manse.

The first boar ignores her, but the second explains that unfamiliar “new boars” have recently appeared. These newcomers do not speak boar and violate the territorial customs of the pumpkin patches. The information allows the party to identify boar number three as a likely shapeshifter.

### Moonbeam

Cast by Soma over the suspicious southern boar in a five-foot radius.

The disguised Anchorite fails its DC 14 Constitution save, takes eight radiant damage, and is forced out of its boar shape. The revelation initiates combat.

The Anchorite subsequently steps outside the beam to avoid further damage. Soma later loses concentration after being struck by Lightning Bolt.

### Guiding Bolt

Cast by Valphine against the revealed Anchorite of Talos.

Valphine rolls 24 to hit and deals twenty-nine radiant damage. The spell grants advantage on the next attack against the target, though Brewbarry rolls a five and a four and misses despite that advantage.

### Silence

Cast by Vukradin in an attempt to prevent the Anchorite of Talos from calling for aid or casting Lightning Bolt.

Under the table’s adjudication, it fails to prevent the Anchorite from shouting or casting. The Anchorite subsequently alerts allies and retaliates with Lightning Bolt.

### Lightning Bolt

Cast by the badly wounded Anchorite after it steps out of Moonbeam and shouts a warning.

The Anchorite positions the spell to strike Valphine, Soma, and Brewbarry in a single line while missing Vukradin. The spell deals thirty-five points of damage before saves and reductions, an unusually high roll.

Valphine succeeds on the DC 12 Dexterity save and takes seventeen damage. Brewbarry reduces the full thirty-five damage to twenty-two with Stone’s Endurance. Soma is also struck and loses concentration on Moonbeam, but her exact remaining hit points are not established.

### Stone’s Endurance

A racial ability used by Brewbarry after he is struck by Lightning Bolt.

Brewbarry rolls eleven on the d12 and adds two, reducing the incoming damage by thirteen. He therefore takes twenty-two damage instead of thirty-five.
