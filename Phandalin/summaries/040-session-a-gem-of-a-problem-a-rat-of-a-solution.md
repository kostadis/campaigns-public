# Chapter 40: A Gem of a Problem, A Rat of a Solution

Date: Apr 18th, 2026

## Summary

In the aftermath of their victory over the young white dragon at Icespire Hold, the party took time to reflect on their growing power and the new abilities they had earned through their trials. Everyone except Vukradin gained a level; Dave was absent, so the GM played Vukradin during the session. Valphine reviewed Emboldening Bond, a Peace Domain gift she had possessed for several levels but had overlooked, which mystically links willing allies and allows each bonded creature to add a d4 to an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw once per turn. When asked why she had never used it, Valphine admitted simply, “Because I forgot,” prompting comparisons to the earlier discovery that Thistle could fire more than one arrow.

The group also discussed Protective Bond, the sixth-level improvement that allows a bonded protector to teleport to an ally’s side and absorb a blow meant for them. They considered whether Brewbarry, Soma, or another durable companion would be best suited to intercept such attacks. Valphine examined a protective spell capable of establishing a safe zone or temporarily imprisoning a creature, but its one-minute casting time made it awkward in direct combat. She ultimately selected a potent healing aura that, once cast, could continue restoring an ally at the beginning of each turn without consuming another action, though its concentration requirement placed it in direct competition with Spirit Guardians.

The party also learned that their recent choices had drawn divine attention. Valphine sensed that Lathander approved of the new direction being taken by his church and now regarded her as important to his purposes. Brewbarry’s growing power likewise reflected his increasing awareness that the world contained lies, cheating, and magical manipulation. His distrust had roots in the incident that led to his exile: Thistle, the Seelie fairy from his backstory, used a Goodberry to sedate him and desecrated sacred ground. This session added that Thistle also stole an Uthgardt artifact while Brewbarry was guarding it. Since entering civilization, however, Brewbarry had discovered wine and beer and concluded that his sober youth had been thoroughly wasted.

Before leaving Icespire Hold, Soma turned her considerable skill to the grim but rewarding task of harvesting the slain dragon’s remains. With extraordinary precision, she rolled two natural twenties and extracted a basket-sized pouch from deep within the dragon’s throat—a reservoir of magical frost that could be unleashed with devastating effect. She also carefully detached supple, interlocking scales from the dragon’s hide, plates that a skilled blacksmith could one day fashion into formidable armor resistant to cold and attuned to the presence of white dragons. Each harvested component was valued at roughly 1,675 gold pieces in its raw state.

The party learned that the breath pouch could either be expended as a single devastating attack or used in a proposed reusable craft referred to at the table as Dragon’s Wrath. Initial estimates placed that work at an alarming 20,000 gold pieces, though a correction suggested a rare version made from a young dragon’s pouch might cost closer to 2,000. The exact finished item and crafting recipe remained subject to a final GM specification. The scales could become very rare dragon scale mail, but that project remained in the 20,000-gold range. Such prices were far beyond the party’s current treasury of roughly 700 gold, though bargaining, quests, trades, or returning other recovered relics might reduce the cost.

With their prizes secured, the party began the two-day journey toward Phandalin, choosing to stop first at Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine. They were greeted by the nervous were-rat leader Zeleen Varnaster and her Whiskered Gang, who called out, “Hey, boss!” and assured the party that everything was in order. Yet an investigation revealed that the mine was utterly silent. No picks struck stone, no carts moved, and no dwarves were working.

The dwarven miners had gone on strike under the direction of their superstitious foreman, Dwolin the Grout. Dwolin refused to dig another inch until an Earthstone Gem could be found to bless the mine. He explained that a gold vein was shy and had to be treated with absolute respect or it would hide. Horia, the dwarven leader, conceded that Dwolin had many rituals and eccentricities but insisted that he was the finest seeker of gold she had ever known.

Valphine, unimpressed, flooded the mine with intense magical daylight that made everyone’s eyes hurt before delivering a devastatingly flat interrogation of Zeleen. She repeatedly demanded to know whether the dwarves were actually mining, inspected an empty minecart, and established that the were-rats had done nothing to acquire the gem holding up production. When Zeleen protested that the gang had been hired to guard the mine rather than undertake fetch quests, the party reminded her that guarding an unproductive mine served no purpose.

The confrontation culminated in a brutal assessment of the were-rats’ performance: if they consumed the party’s cheese while producing no gold, they were nothing but a waste of cheese. Zeleen promptly agreed that recovering the Earthstone Gem fell within the gang’s contractual obligations and dispatched several were-rats into the orc-controlled forest. She ordered them away with panicked urgency: “You, you, go! Why are you here? You heard what the boss said. Go get the emerald!”

Brewbarry and Valphine negotiated a secret contingency with the pragmatic Horia without Vukradin’s knowledge. Horia’s original plan was to use fifty gold pieces to secure a convincing fake gem that would satisfy Dwolin’s rituals and get the miners back to work. When reminded that Vukradin’s contract contained a strict no-skimming clause, she proposed a formal “Brewbarry addendum.” Brewbarry suggested signing it in her blood when he claimed to be out of ink, and Horia quickly decided that the addendum could be considered signed without further ceremony. They agreed that the were-rats’ expedition would remain Plan A and the false gem Plan B, with the fifty gold returned if the real gem arrived first. Horia strongly suggested that the arrangement remain hidden from Vukradin.

The party continued to Phandalin, where townspeople spilled from the smithy, Barthen’s Provisions, and nearby homes to cheer their returning dragon-slayers. Children rushed forward to high-five them, and the dragon parts provided tangible proof of their victory. Townmaster Harbin Wester seized the moment to praise Vukradin’s extraordinary talents and “undefatigable support,” presenting the triumph as the natural result of his own wise decision to establish a privy council of heroic adventurers.

Harbin promptly ordered a festival on the town green, and townspeople began assembling a celebration area. The innkeeper promised Brewbarry two barrels of his very best wine—not merely the good stuff he normally served—and Brewbarry replied, “I’ll be the judge of that.” Valphine’s followers also greeted their leader ecstatically with cries of “Oh, the glorious blinding pain!” as they prepared for further acts of zealous devotion.

The celebration was tempered by an urgent summons from Ser Kaelen. He greeted Vukradin as an “associate member of the Lords’ Alliance”—a verbal slip at odds with Vukradin’s established membership in the Order of the Gauntlet—and named Brewbarry, Soma, and Valphine of the Cult of Lathander. He then told Soma they needed to speak privately about the Emerald Enclave. Fearing spies, he led the party to the Temple of Lathander—the Searing Pain of Justice—while preparations for the festival continued outside.

There Ser Kaelen revealed that Adabra, the absolutist naturalist who had long been a thorn in the party’s side, had uncovered the truth about what had transpired at the Whispering Grove, including the killing of the treant and Vukradin’s deception. Adabra had called a conclave of local druids and intended to argue that the party’s reckless interventionism had brought ruin to the forests. She sought to frame the defenders against the dragons and planar incursions as the source of the land’s suffering.

Ser Kaelen argued that Adabra was not evil but profoundly misguided. She had witnessed the devastation caused by the wars without personally experiencing the planar manifestations that precipitated them. Consequently, she mistook the defenders and their destructive methods for the underlying threat. Her passion had nevertheless attracted enough support to pose a genuine danger to the party and to the communities that depended on druidic assistance.

The Lords’ Alliance did not wish to intervene, while the Order of the Gauntlet lacked the personnel after the War of the Dragons, the War of the Giants, and other recent conflicts. Ser Kaelen therefore placed the burden squarely on the party. He told Soma that Meril’s Staff carried deep ritual symbolism among the druids and might be enough to tip the conclave in their favor, despite Soma’s protest that she was “barely a druid” herself. Vukradin’s silver tongue might also prove useful.

Ser Kaelen cautioned that some attending the conclave might demand an evaluation of whether Soma should retain Meril’s Staff at all. Soma responded quietly but resolutely that they were not taking it from her, adding that it possessed sentimental value as well as power. Kaelen agreed, comparing the staff to his conviction that no order could take his sword. He then extended the comparison to Brewbarry’s halberd, calling it a very fine weapon. Brewbarry warned him, “You don’t want to have a conversation with it, trust me.”

With the festival still ringing outside and the weight of a new conflict settling over them, the party prepared for a diplomatic confrontation that could determine whether the region’s druids remained allies against the planar threat—or turned against the heroes of Phandalin.

## Scenes

### Leveling Up and Strategic Planning

#### The party gathers to discuss their growth in power and analyze new spells and abilities following their recent victory.

- The party members, except for Vukradin, gain a level in experience. Dave is absent, so the GM plays Vukradin during the session; Vukradin is humorously denied the party’s “Fair Trade Organic Experience points.”
- Brewbarry discovers that he can add elemental damage to his weapon, while Soma notes that she can now inflict radiant damage.
- Valphine reviews Emboldening Bond, an existing Peace Domain feature she had forgotten she possessed.
- Emboldening Bond can be forged a number of times equal to Valphine’s proficiency bonus, currently three times per day. It lasts ten minutes and does not require concentration.
- Each bonded creature can add a d4 no more than once per turn to an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw while within range of another bonded creature.
- The bond benefits willing creatures; its flavor about people “at peace with one another” prompts a table joke about whether the party actually qualifies. Soma is judged to be at peace with everyone except the absolutist naturalists and their allies, while Vukradin is described as genuinely and absolutely naive in his desire to be friends with everyone.
- When asked why she had not used the feature earlier, Valphine answers, “Because I forgot.”
- The oversight is compared to the party’s earlier realization that Thistle could shoot more than one arrow. Valphine admits, “I’m playing one-dimensional chess these days,” and Soma corrects her: “You’re playing checkers.”
- The group discusses Protective Bond, the sixth-level improvement that allows a bonded protector to teleport next to an ally and intercept damage meant for them.
- The party considers whether Brewbarry, Soma, or another high-hit-point companion would be best positioned to absorb incoming attacks.
- Valphine considers the utility of a reversible protective circle for creating a safe zone or imprisoning eligible extraplanar creatures. The table discusses how it might work outside or within an active planar disruption, leaving any campaign-specific interaction for a later ruling.
- The spell’s one-minute casting time makes it unsuitable for direct combat, but useful when defending a location, protecting a ritual, creating a temporary refuge, or imprisoning a creature for questioning.
- The table’s creature-type discussion becomes confused. Ordinary drow and elves are Humanoids rather than Fey, while actual Fey are among the creature types such a circle can affect.
- Valphine ultimately selects a healing aura spell that restores hit points when cast and again at the start of subsequent turns without requiring another action.
- The aura requires concentration, creating a tactical choice between sustained healing and Spirit Guardians.
- Spirit Guardians is judged more useful against groups of nearby enemies but less effective against a flying dragon.
- Soma also considers searching for an owlbear to use with her expanding nature abilities. Its challenge rating is currently too high, and because an owlbear is a Monstrosity rather than a Beast, using it with ordinary Wild Shape would also require a house rule or different ability.
- Valphine receives a sense that Lathander approves of the new direction of his church and now considers her important to his purposes.
- Brewbarry’s developing powers are tied to his growing recognition of deceit and magical manipulation in the world.
- The party clarifies that Thistle—the Seelie fairy who sedated Brewbarry with a Goodberry and desecrated sacred ground—also stole an Uthgardt artifact while Brewbarry was guarding it. Together, these details explain his expulsion and his strong reaction to seeing a Goodberry.
- Brewbarry has not yet selected which Uthgardt tribe he belonged to.
- Since entering civilization, Brewbarry has discovered quality wine and beer and now regards his earlier abstinence as “a wasted youth.”

### Harvesting the Dragon

#### Following the dragon’s defeat at Icespire Hold, the party harvests valuable components from the young white dragon’s remains.

- The party discusses harvesting body parts from the slain young white dragon, joking that the actual fight felt anticlimactic because the heroes made their saving throws.
- Soma performs a Nature check with advantage and rolls two natural twenties. With her bonus, both results would have been 22.
- Brewbarry also rolls a respectable 18, “not bad for a barbarian.”
- Soma remarks, “This is not a time I wanted to spend to double crit, but I guess we just perfectly harvested that dragon.”
- Valphine summarizes the operation as, “We put him in a bathtub and Organ harvested him.”
- The party successfully extracts a basket-sized dragon breath pouch from the back of the dragon’s throat without rupturing it.
- The pouch contains magically produced liquids capable of recreating the destructive frost breath of a young white dragon.
- The pouch is initially described as a one-use weapon comparable to a very powerful fireball and a “get-out-of-jail-free card.”
- The raw breath pouch is valued at 1,675 gold pieces.
- The party later learns that the pouch can either be expended once or used in a proposed reusable craft referred to at the table as Dragon’s Wrath.
- An initial crafting estimate of 20,000 gold is corrected after the young-dragon rarity is reconsidered; a rare young-dragon version may cost approximately 2,000 gold pieces to craft, but the exact finished item and recipe still require a final GM specification.
- Soma also harvests supple scales that have not yet fully hardened through repeated molting.
- The scales are carefully detached in the interlocking arrangement needed to create a functional suit of dragon scale mail.
- The raw scales are likewise valued at approximately 1,675 gold pieces.
- Dragon scale mail made from them would be medium armor with AC 15 plus the wearer’s Dexterity modifier, to a maximum bonus of +2, potentially suitable for Brewbarry.

As a magic action, its wearer could discern the distance and direction of the nearest white dragon within thirty miles; once used, that sensing ability could not be used again until the next dawn.
- Because dragon scale mail is a very rare item, the initial crafting estimate is approximately 20,000 gold pieces.
- The party currently possesses only about 700 gold, but the cost could potentially be reduced through negotiation, trades, services, quests, or returning other recovered items for rewards.
- The group jokes about the scales as part of an “ascension kit” and discusses the preferred colors of dragon scale mail in NetHack.
- Ser Kaelen’s earlier message made it clear that the party was needed urgently in Phandalin because of trouble involving the Emerald Enclave.
- After some debate—and acknowledged “light railroading”—the party decides to return to Phandalin before traveling to Neverwinter.
- The journey is expected to take two days, and the party chooses to stop at Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine along the way.
- The party confirms that Soma’s skeletal horse, Boney, remains bonded to her. If destroyed, Boney returns the following day.

## Items

### Dragon Breath Pouch

A basket-sized organ harvested intact from the back of the young white dragon’s throat. It contains magical liquids capable of reproducing the dragon’s destructive frost breath. The raw pouch is worth approximately 1,675 gold pieces and can be expended as a powerful one-time weapon or used in a proposed reusable craft referred to at the table as Dragon’s Wrath. The corrected estimate for a rare young-dragon craft is approximately 2,000 gold pieces, but the exact finished item and recipe require a final GM specification.

### Young White Dragon Scales

Supple, flexible plates harvested from the young white dragon’s hide in a pattern suitable for interlocking armor. The raw scales are worth approximately 1,675 gold pieces. A skilled blacksmith could fashion them into very rare medium dragon scale mail with AC 15 plus the wearer’s Dexterity modifier, to a maximum bonus of +2, resistance to cold damage, and advantage on saving throws against dragon breath weapons. As a magic action, the wearer could discern the distance and direction of the closest white dragon within thirty miles; once used, that sensing ability could not be used again until the next dawn. The preliminary crafting estimate is approximately 20,000 gold pieces, though quests, bargaining, and trades could reduce it.

### Earthstone Gem

The gem required by the dwarven foreman Dwolin for his ritual to bless the gold vein before mining can resume. Dwolin believes gold is shy and will hide unless treated with proper respect. Several were-rats have been sent into an orc-controlled forest to retrieve a real gem, while Horia has authorization under the secret Brewbarry addendum to spend fifty gold pieces on a fake version as Plan B.

### Meril’s Staff

A magical staff of great symbolism and power among the local druids, distinct from Soma’s Staff of Birdcalls. Ser Kaelen does not fully understand its ritual significance but believes it may tip the balance at Adabra’s conclave. Some druids may challenge Soma’s right to retain it. Soma refuses to surrender it, citing both its power and sentimental value.

### Brewbarry’s Halberd

A large, fine polearm carried by Brewbarry and described as a “big, steppy, pokey thing.” Brewbarry taps it meaningfully against his hand while urging the were-rats to act. Ser Kaelen admires the weapon, but Brewbarry warns him against attempting to have a conversation with it.

## Spells

### Daylight

Cast by Valphine during the confrontation at Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine, filling the area with intense magical light and causing everyone’s eyes to hurt. It reinforced her already intimidating presence while she interrogated the were-rat guards about the silent mine.

### Spirit Guardians

Discussed by the party as a powerful concentration spell with high utility against multiple nearby enemies. Valphine weighs it against a potent healing aura, noting that different encounters favor different choices. It is considered less effective against a dragon that remains airborne.

### Revivify

Briefly discussed by the party as a vital utility spell worth selecting, with no one able to turn it down.

## NPCs

### Zeleen Varnaster

The nervous leader of the Whiskered Gang of were-rats guarding Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine. She initially insists that the dwarves have been working hard despite the mine’s complete silence. When confronted, she argues that the gang was hired to protect the mine rather than go on fetch quests. After being labeled a “waste of cheese,” she accepts the party’s interpretation of her contractual obligations, addresses them as “Mr. Boss,” and sends several were-rats into the orc-controlled forest to retrieve the Earthstone Gem.

### Dwolin the Grout

The superstitious dwarven foreman of Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine. He refuses to resume work until the Earthstone Gem is found and used in a ritual to bless the operation. Dwolin believes a gold vein is shy and must be treated with absolute respect or it will hide. Despite his eccentric rituals and trinkets, Horia calls him the finest seeker of gold she has ever known.

### Horia

A pragmatic dwarven leader at the mine who vouches for Dwolin’s unmatched ability to locate gold while acknowledging his many superstitions. Her original solution to the strike was to spend fifty gold pieces on a convincing fake gem. She negotiates a secret “Brewbarry addendum” authorizing the expense despite Vukradin’s no-skimming clause and suggests that the arrangement remain hidden from him. She describes herself as a stickler for rules, procedures, and contractual obligations.

### Harbin Wester

The townmaster of Phandalin, who publicly credits Vukradin’s talents, support, and management for the defeat of the dragon. Harbin uses the victory to validate his own decision to establish the party as a privy council, then organizes a festival on the town green in their honor.

### Ser Kaelen

A man of sword and action associated with the Order of the Gauntlet. He greets the party during Phandalin’s celebration and leads them to the Temple of Lathander for a private warning about Adabra’s conclave. He believes Soma’s staff and Vukradin’s diplomacy may sway the druids. Though he admits limited understanding of druidic rituals, he recognizes that the loss of local druid support would leave nearby communities more vulnerable to planar threats.

### Adabra

An absolutist naturalist who has discovered the truth about the Whispering Grove, including the treant’s death and Vukradin’s deception. She has called a conclave of local druids to argue that the party’s interventionism has harmed the forests. Ser Kaelen describes her as misguided rather than evil: she witnessed the devastation caused by defensive wars but did not personally experience the planar incursions that provoked them. Her campaign may cost the region valuable druidic allies, and some among her coalition may challenge Soma’s right to retain her staff.
