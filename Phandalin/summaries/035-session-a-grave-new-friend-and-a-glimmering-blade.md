# Chapter 35: A Grave New Friend and a Glimmering Blade

Date: 2026-02-25

## Summary

The party ventured into a narrow secret passageway within the Dragon Barrow, eager to find the legendary Dragon Slayer Sword. Vukradin, driven by excitement, insisted on leading the way: “I am excited to get the sword!” His haste proved costly when he stepped on a hidden, five-foot-long pressure plate in the two-foot-wide tunnel, causing the walls to collapse inward and bury everyone except Soma in dirt and stone. While the trapped adventurers worried about suffocation, Soma used her connection to the earth to extract her companions from the debris. She then painstakingly excavated the rest of the blocked passage with repeated uses of Mould Earth, cheerfully dismissing concerns about “burning” a cantrip: “Burning cantrips? Oh, no. Absolutely, we should burn cantrips! Yes! Not my cantrips!”

As they emerged from the cleared tunnel, Soma discovered the bones and rotting saddle of a horse resting in a southern niche. When she approached to investigate, the skeletal remains knitted themselves together and rose as an undead horse. Through magical communication, Soma learned that the creature expected Lady Alagondar and believed it had been brought back to serve her on an important quest. The party cleverly convinced the horse that Soma was Lady Alagondar reincarnated. After briefly wondering whether a polymorph spell explained her appearance, the loyal steed accepted the reincarnation story: “Anything is possible, your ladyship. Do we ride at dawn? Do we go to fight evil? Are we ready for triumph?”

The horse lamented the loss of its mane and several other anatomical features. After suggestions including Mr. Ed, Spirit, Black Beauty, Butterscotch, and “a horse with no name,” it embraced the name Boney. Boney explained that he had been left in the dungeon with the promise that someone would return for him, only for the Will-o’-wisps to attack and kill him. He believed he was to be buried “across the way” from Lady Alagondar and directed the party northward toward the likely resting place of the sword. The group immediately began discussing whether Boney could become a permanent martial sidekick despite having only 13 hit points.

The party pressed forward with Soma attempting to sense magical auras to avoid traps and locate their prize. Brewbarry took the lead but fell into a non-magical trap, suffering 12 points of damage before an invisible Will-o’-wisp materialized, shocked him for another 9 points, and fled through solid rock. Boney belatedly remembered the lights and warned that they were evil, boring, and not very talkative. More importantly, he remembered that the wisps were what had killed him.

As a precaution against the wisps, Vukradin created a 20-foot-radius zone of absolute silence in the corridor, hoping it might prevent the creatures from calling reinforcements. Soma tended to Brewbarry’s wounds with healing magic, restoring 25 hit points. Valphine moved beyond the silenced area, readied Turn Undead, and successfully drove off the first wisp when it next appeared.

Seeing the path open, Vukradin charged ahead to find the sword. He raced down the corridor and turned west into a tomb, misunderstanding Boney’s directions to go north. Brewbarry recognized the mistake, but the zone of magical silence prevented any warning from reaching the bard. Vukradin then plunged into another pressure-plate trap, taking 19 points of damage and losing concentration on Silence. Worse, another Will-o’-wisp emerged from the shadows.

The battle intensified as the ethereal creatures became invisible, phased through walls, and attacked from unexpected angles. Vukradin struck one with a reaction as it flitted away, dealing 6 points of damage. He later opened the sarcophagus into which it had retreated and caught it in the damaging area of a Cloud of Daggers, although four disastrous damage dice produced only 4 points of damage. Soma arrived to help and destroyed the same creature with a 12-point Moonbeam.

During the fight, Vukradin opened the southeastern sarcophagus, which held a dead wizard, and recovered a Necklace of Fireballs containing seven beads. The northwest sarcophagus held a dead bard buried with a magical Lute of Illusions; the other two sarcophagi contained the moldy bones of adventurers.

Meanwhile, Brewbarry continued north and found another suspicious blinking light. He attempted to expose the hidden wisp by using Thaumaturgy to brighten the magical light, but this only made the light brighter. Adopting a more practical approach, he deliberately triggered the nearby pressure plate with his halberd, jumped across the resulting pit, entered a rage, and destroyed the exposed Will-o’-wisp with two attacks.

With the immediate Will-o’-wisp threats defeated or driven away, Valphine carefully navigated past the remaining trap and reached the northern chamber. There, among the ancient bones of a dragon, she found its skull resting on the floor. Atop that grim pedestal lay their prize: the Dragon Slayer Sword, exactly in the direction Boney had indicated. The party had successfully located the sword, though they had not yet secured it, arranged a Legend Lore reading to identify its history, or determined whether taking it would satisfy their requirements for fair trade.

## NPCs

### Boney

A skeletal horse that rises from bones found beside a rotting saddle in a southern tunnel niche. Boney believes he was brought back from the dead to aid Lady Alagondar on a quest and that he was connected to the defense of her sword. The horse is sentient; at the table, Soma successfully communicated with him after casting Speak with Animals despite his undead nature.

Boney initially fails to recognize Soma as Lady Alagondar, but the party persuades him that she has been reincarnated. He immediately accepts Soma as “your ladyship” and enthusiastically asks whether they will ride at dawn, fight evil, and achieve triumph.

He was old and dying when he was brought into the Dragon Barrow. His handlers left him there with the promise that they would return in a few days, but Will-o’-wisps attacked and killed him. Boney retained his sentience because he remained convinced that Lady Alagondar would eventually return.

He is missing his mane and other features, being fully skeletal, and does not want to discuss all of his absent anatomy. Suggested names include Mr. Ed, Spirit, Black Beauty, Butterscotch, and Boney. He states his original name as “Barney,” but happily embraces Boney instead.

Boney has only 13 hit points in his current form. The party nevertheless wants to retain him, possibly as a martial sidekick, and Soma declares that she has bonded with him. He remembers that he was supposed to be buried “across the way” from Lady Alagondar, allowing him to direct the party north toward the sword.

### Lady Alagondar

A figure from the past whom Boney believes he was brought back to help. Boney addresses her with great loyalty and expects that her return will lead to a dawn ride against evil and eventual triumph.

Boney recalls that he was to be buried “across the way” from Lady Alagondar and associates her with the sword hidden in the Dragon Barrow. The party convinces Boney that Soma is Lady Alagondar reincarnated, which he accepts despite her radically different appearance.

## Locations

### Dragon Barrow

The dungeon where the party is searching for the Dragon Slayer Sword. It contains narrow tunnels, hidden pressure plates, collapsing walls, pit traps, sarcophagi, Will-o’-wisps, and the bones of a dragon. Several of its traps are entirely mechanical and therefore cannot be detected with Detect Magic.

### Secret Passageway

A narrow, two-foot-wide tunnel to the west. A concealed, five-foot-long pressure plate midway through the passage causes its walls to collapse inward when triggered. Vukradin activates the trap while rushing forward, burying everyone except Soma. The cave-in initially blocks all forward movement, forcing Soma to excavate the tunnel with repeated uses of Mould Earth.

### Southern Niche

An area beyond the collapsed tunnel where the bones and rotting saddle of a horse rest. When Soma approaches, the bones reassemble into Boney, a sentient skeletal horse formerly connected to Lady Alagondar. Boney’s recollection that he was to be buried “across the way” from her provides the party’s best direction toward the sword.

### Sepulcher Chamber

A western chamber containing four sarcophagi and guarded by a Will-o’-wisp. The northwest sarcophagus contains a dead bard buried with a Lute of Illusions. The southeast sarcophagus contains a dead wizard and a Necklace of Fireballs with seven beads. The other two sarcophagi contain the moldy bones of adventurers. A pressure-plate pit trap lies along the route into the chamber.

### Northern Area

The area north of the Southern Niche, reached by crossing another trapped corridor and passing a pair of sarcophagi. The bones of a dragon lie within the chamber, with some remains embedded in its walls. The dragon’s skull rests on the floor, and the Dragon Slayer Sword lies atop it.

## Items

### Dragon Slayer Sword

A long sword found resting atop a dragon skull in the northern area of the dungeon. It is the object of the party’s quest and lies in the direction Boney indicated.

The party has located but not yet secured or examined it. They hope to arrange a Legend Lore reading and learn enough about its history to determine whether taking it can be reconciled with Vukradin’s requirements concerning fair trade.

### Necklace of Fireballs

A magical necklace found in a sealed sarcophagus in the southeast corner of the Sepulcher Chamber, alongside a dead wizard. It contains seven beads that can be thrown individually or all at once to cast Fireball. The first bead deals 8d6 damage, and each additional bead adds 1d6; all seven together deal 14d6.

The party recognizes that it may be extremely useful against the Will-o’-wisps, although Vukradin retrieves it without immediately using it.

### Lute of Illusions

A magical lute found with the remains of a dead bard in the northwest sarcophagus of the Sepulcher Chamber. It had previously been identified during the familiar’s exploration of the room and is recovered by Vukradin during the fight with the Will-o’-wisp.

## Spells

### Mold Earth

Used by Soma to clear the collapsed tunnel walls after Vukradin triggers a pressure plate, freeing the party members buried in dirt and stone. Soma first clears enough earth to ensure that her companions can breathe, then excavates the entire blocked passage to permit forward movement.

Because Mould Earth is a cantrip, Soma can repeat it without consuming spell slots, leading her to mock the suggestion that continuing would require “burning” cantrips.

### Speak with Animals

Cast by Soma before she successfully communicates with the newly reanimated skeletal horse Boney, learning about his claimed past service to Lady Alagondar and his death at the hands of Will-o’-wisps.

Because Boney is undead rather than a Beast, the successful communication is a table ruling. It allows Soma to greet him, question him about the sword and nearby dangers, and participate in convincing him that she is Lady Alagondar reincarnated.

### Detect Magic

Cast as a ritual by Soma to sense magical effects within 30 feet for 10 minutes, helping the party locate the sword and avoid magical traps, though it requires concentration.

The party establishes that the caster immediately senses nearby magic and can use a Magic action to perceive an aura around a visible magical creature or object and identify its school. The spell is blocked by one foot of stone, dirt, or wood, one inch of metal, or a thin sheet of lead. It does not detect the dungeon’s non-magical pressure plates and pit traps, nor does it identify the Will-o’-wisps themselves as magical creatures.

### Silence

Cast by Vukradin as a precaution against the Will-o’-wisps, creating a 20-foot-radius sphere where no sound can pass. Vukradin hopes that it will prevent the creatures from communicating or summoning others.

The spell complicates the party’s coordination and would interfere with Valphine’s spoken prayer for Turn Undead, forcing her to position herself beyond its radius. It also prevents Brewbarry’s warning from reaching Vukradin when the bard runs west instead of north. Vukradin loses concentration after taking 19 points of damage from a pit trap.

### Turn Undead

Used by Valphine as a readied action against a Will-o’-wisp. Valphine positions herself outside the Silence effect so that she can speak the required prayer or command and presents her holy symbol when the creature becomes visible.

The wisp fails its Wisdom saving throw against DC 15 and is forced to spend its turns moving as far away from Valphine as possible. The party avoids damaging that turned wisp so the effect will not end prematurely.

### Thaumaturgy

Used by Brewbarry to brighten a suspicious magical light in an attempt to expose or provoke an invisible Will-o’-wisp. Under the table ruling, Thaumaturgy successfully makes the light brighter, but it neither reveals the invisible creature nor causes it to react. Brewbarry concludes that the experiment was useless.

### Cloud of Daggers

Cast by Vukradin on a Will-o’-wisp hiding inside an opened sarcophagus. The spell creates a swirling cloud of daggers in a small area and does not grant the creature a saving throw.

Vukradin casts it at 2nd level for 4d4 slashing damage, then rolls four ones, dealing only 4 points. The spell remains active through concentration until Soma adds Moonbeam and destroys the wisp.

### Moonbeam

Cast by Soma on the Will-o’-wisp already caught in Vukradin’s Cloud of Daggers. The creature fails its Constitution saving throw and takes 12 points of radiant damage, destroying it.

The spell creates a five-foot-radius beam of pale moonlight, requires concentration, and can be repositioned with an action.
