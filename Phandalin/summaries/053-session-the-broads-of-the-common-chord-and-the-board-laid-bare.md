# Chapter 53: The Broads of the Common Chord and the Board Laid Bare

Date: 2026-08-25

## Summary
The party’s departure from the Spire of the Morninglord was interrupted by the arrival of Aurelan Vance, a representative of the Counting House who had been inspired by Valphine’s recent sermon to take action. Breathless from rushing over from the bank — patting himself down and gathering himself into proper decorum — Aurelan asked after the correct appellation (“Is it Mr. Brewbarry? I’m sorry, I’m not familiar with Guardian norms”), was invited to simply call the goliath “Brew,” and offered in return that his friends call him Ori. He shared what he had uncovered about a gnome named Bimble, producing a piece of paper with the correct spelling for the benefit of a party that immediately and persistently mispronounced it “Bimbo.” Bimble had been carried on a commission maintenance roll for eleven years, but payments had stopped abruptly seven tendays ago — “on the day, not the tenday” — with no severance, no final reconciliation, and no forwarding instructions. “That is not how a contract ends,” Aurelan declared. “Brew, that is how a contract is abandoned!” Since then Bimble had drawn nothing: not banked, not borrowed, and not left the city. Aurelan concluded with the quiet certainty of a man who only knew one kind of question: find who feeds the man, and you have found the man. Brewbarry darkly suggested the gnome might be “feeding worms” instead, but promised his banker friend they would get to the bottom of it.

As the party made their way through the Protector’s Enclave, a passerby stopped to stare at Boney, Brewbarry’s skeletal horse, still wearing Lady Alagondar’s tackle. The man was convinced he had seen such a horse before, puzzling over the memory with growing agitation — asking Boney directly, at Soma’s insistence, whether he had ever been “fritting around outside of the walls of Neverwinter,” and even speculating it might have been one of Boney’s children or siblings (“Wait, are his kids skeletal?” Soma wondered) — only to eventually realize, with some embarrassment, that he had simply seen Boney earlier that same day outside the service where “that charming cleric gave a speech.” Vukradin, exasperated (“Stop talking to strangers, please”), was answered by Soma’s cheerful creed: “Stranger’s just a friend you haven’t met.” The party moved on, leaving the man to his confusion.

Their destination was The Board Laid Bare, a modest restaurant in the Blue Lake District that Soma had long considered a home away from home. At the door stood Bellows, a warforged host of mechanical precision and absolute humorlessness, who recited the table options — a window table with the best view of the tower, and a corner table “preferred by guests who do not wish to be observed,” which he recommended — before recognizing Soma and informing her she had been absent for exactly seventy-three days. He escorted the party to their tables (two window tables pushed together, Brewbarry occupying nearly one on his own) with the same detached efficiency he applied to everything, presenting a handwritten menu of fresh fish, vegetables, potatoes, and soups at prices that seemed impossibly low given the city’s rampant inflation — pre-disruption prices, which Soma cheerfully rationalized as “farm-to-table” and Vukradin as “Organic Fair Trade!” Asked directly whether he knew a gnome named Bimble, Bellows did not.

Before the party could settle in, Lim — the tortle chef and proprietor — emerged from the kitchen carrying a tray of food she had already decided they wanted, set it down, and sat with them. “You came back? I thought you’d gone somewhere northern.” She caught up with Soma warmly, sized up the great Vukradin (“I don’t like your music... It’s not even useful”), and revealed that his old venue was now the hottest ticket in town precisely because he used to play there. She mentioned that the Tower District had grown quieter in recent weeks — “not safer. Quieter is different” — and that three weeks prior, people dressed as Many-Starred Cloaks associates had entered the Cloak Tower by the basement stairs and spent the day there, emerging with nothing. In the kitchen, she and Soma shared a private laugh over the turtle soup: the number of people who still believed the restaurant sold it was too funny, since they *had* once sold it — “but it was for a different clientele.” While Soma and Lim reconnected, Brewbarry and Valphine turned their attention to Bellows, whom Valphine’s keen eye had marked as someone who could not tell a lie, pressing him about the restaurant’s suspiciously affordable supply chain.

Bellows answered with characteristic precision, offering to elaborate on any axis requested: “When they arrive, how they arrive? What do the crates look like? Are they stamped? How many crates? What’s contained in the crates?” He explained that deliveries arrived nightly between the third and fourth hour after midnight via a rear service lane connecting to the city’s aqueducts, brought by two men, sometimes three, never the same two — six or seven couriers oscillating through the rotation over the last thirty days. The crates were wooden, rope-bound, wet on the underside. He noted, with complete indifference, that the order had been nine crates since midsummer though the restaurant used only eight — a discrepancy he had raised twice. The ninth was left in the lane and was gone by morning — never billed to the restaurant, never explained, and never questioned: “It does not come inside. It is set down, and it is not there in the morning. I have not asked. It is not billed to us.”

Brewbarry followed Soma into the kitchen — halberd in one arm, dragon-slaying sword in the other, bathrobe billowing — under the pretense of culinary curiosity and confronted Lim directly about her suppliers, spinning a cover story about getting into business himself and needing efficient delivery partners. Lim was reluctant, calling it a trade secret, and flatly denied any knowledge of a ninth crate — she ordered eight. She described her suppliers only as a group keeping local businesses afloat, believers in fair trade and organic goods who refused to be trapped by the economic policies of Lord Neverember and the Neverwinter Commission — a description that sounded, the party noted, less like House Margaster and more like revolutionaries. When Brewbarry lost patience and threatened to simply stake out the delivery, Lim finally broke down — because she liked Soma — and admitted that her goods were moved through the sewer system beneath the Blue Lake District. Vukradin’s horror (“So you’re telling me that the fresh food that you serve to your customers comes from the sewer”) was met with a sharp correction — “No, I said it gets *moved* through the sewer” — and Brewbarry’s diplomatic gloss: “Yeah, it’s just transit, you know?” She had made a deal with the suppliers after they approached her at the market, drawn in by the promise of affordable goods at a time when rising taxes were crushing local businesses. She asked Soma, with quiet desperation, not to scare them away and not to ambush them — she had a business to run, the food was good, the prices were good, and they were not bad people. Soma’s disappointment was plain — “You could have just raised your prices... those types of people, they make demands in the future” — and Lim’s hurt response cut deep: “You’ve changed. I remember a different Soma.”

Valphine, ever sharp — and, as a child of Menzoberranzan, hardly one to find underground logistics alarming — pieced together what the others had gathered. Seven weeks ago the displacement manifold went dark and regional distribution collapsed; Lionshield’s store stopped receiving, and the party still carried Linene’s request for help with supplies. House Margaster now controlled the port. Yet the Board Laid Bare’s prices had never moved, because Lim’s supplier was never on the manifold. A third trade route existed — one that had outlived the collapse, paid no harbor authority, and moved goods at the third hour after midnight through service lanes and aqueduct steps. The party suspected that Bimble, the missing gnome, was surviving by intercepting the mysterious ninth crate each night — “Who’s feeding Bimbo? He’s eating the ninth box,” as Vukradin put it. They weighed surveillance options: Soma could wild shape or summon an unassuming familiar (a rat, fittingly, for the sewers), and the party recalled that where there are rats there are were-rats — and that they had a standing business relationship with the were-rat Zeleen from the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine, whose people may well have hailed from Neverwinter. With hours to spare before a midnight stakeout could be arranged — the dinner with Lord Neverember could wait a night, as could tea and intrigue with a certain lord — the party decided to make use of the time and headed toward the Common Chord.

The walk through the city was unsettling in small ways. A woman in the Blue Lake District was seen locking a door she had already locked. A wizard’s apprentice tried to sell the party authentic many-starred cloak thread — decorative, not soft, though Brewbarry’s impeccable logic (“if the fabric is made of threads... so what makes this fabric soft is the thread, right?”) earned him an inspiration on the spot. Citizens argued loudly, and correctly, in the streets that rents had doubled. Two rumors circulated: that Lord Neverember had taken a drow advisor — news that made Valphine sit up sharply — and that Lathander’s Death was playing at the Common Chord. And then, posted on a wall near the Common Chord, the party spotted a bill advertising a performance by Lathander’s Death — in two nights — at the very venue they were approaching. No one in the band had agreed to it. Along the way Brewbarry unveiled a new track he’d composed on a napkin at lunch — a heartfelt ballad to Lim’s turtle soup (“a bowl of warmth from good ol’ Lim’s own hand... a taste of home that’s beyond all compare”) — which the band gently declined as off-brand. “It’s a marketing jingle,” Valphine observed. “Call him Yoko and move on,” said Soma.

The Common Chord had transformed entirely since Vukradin’s early days playing three consecutive nights to sparse crowds of ten or fifteen — eleven, really, but always Brewbarry, who was the only patron whose money Vukradin ever kept, having refunded everyone who failed to rate the show a ten out of ten. It now boasted a six-week reservation list and an atmosphere of fashionable excitement, with fans cosplaying as Vukradin and chanting the band’s name. Brewbarry immediately began working the crowd, taking pre-orders for the merchandise he was wearing, while Valphine’s dragon scale armor drew wide-eyed stares from every corner of the room. When the door staff hesitated, the question of who exactly was going to stop Brewbarry from taking the stage answered itself.

Petra, the half-elf proprietor who had given Vukradin his first gig when no one else would, emerged to greet them — “I discovered this guy” — with the gleaming eyes of someone who had just watched her investment pay off. She admitted she had been booking a cover band under the band’s name — a man who played Vukradin’s songs on stage every night — always telling audiences it wasn’t the real Vukradin, but profiting handsomely from the association all the same. She offered his usual fee; Vukradin, threatening to sue for his likeness with Brewbarry looming behind him (“Are you trying to rip off my friend?”), did not take her coin: five hundred gold was named as the value of the infringement and squared by one exclusive performance in two nights, granting Petra rights to the cover act in perpetuity so long as she always disclosed it — “I respect the image of the one, the only, the great Vukradin,” she swore. A revenue-share gambit floated by Brewbarry — fifty percent of the house on the night of the concert, gross, the band insisted, never net — Petra took, shaking on it before anyone could change their mind: “You’ve learned something, Vukradin. You’re not the same guy I used to remember.” He then squeezed an additional twenty-five gold pieces out of her on the spot to sign the commemorative plaque she had installed at his old table — now polished, couched, and enshrined. She paid immediately, pupils practically replaced by coins, and marveled at what Brewbarry had done to the nicest guy she used to know.

The band performed a teaser set that night — nearly derailed when Vukradin launched into the soup ballad before Brewbarry redirected him to the correct track, “Vault of Silver Lies” — and when Valphine’s contribution came out discordant and off-key on a natural one, the crowd erupted: the screeching imperfection perfectly echoing Vukradin’s beloved early avant-garde style. Riding the energy of the room, and after a brief internal debate in which Brewbarry’s proposal to sing about the evils of Lord Neverember was vetoed (“Lord Neverember is our ally against Margaster”; “the enemy of our enemy is our friend”), the party launched into a second performance with a sharper purpose — Vukradin lacing it with magic, Valphine lending Bardic-boosted blessings. The song was witty and pointed, its lyrics skewering House Margaster’s predatory grip on the city’s trade routes and the suffering it had caused ordinary people. The crowd picked it up eagerly, humming and repeating the tune as the night wore on, the seeds of dissent planted quietly but firmly in the hearts of Neverwinter’s citizens — every patron leaving the tavern nudged toward Brewbarry’s stated goal: that House Margaster be known as the most evil organization in the city.

The party settled in afterward, the Common Chord’s food and drink far superior to anything Brewbarry remembered from his early visits — the swill of old replaced by genuinely good beer, wasted somewhat on a goliath whose stated palate was “anything that will get me drunk is good,” and who happily kept both the good beer and the cheap replacement Petra swapped in. “Pearls before swine,” Soma observed. The evening had been productive in ways that went beyond coin — they had uncovered a smuggling network beneath the city, identified a likely hiding place for the missing gnome, and turned a room full of strangers into unwitting allies against House Margaster, all before midnight had even arrived.

- When the door staff balk at letting the band on stage, the question answers itself — “Who’s gonna stop Brewbarry?” — and a 17 Strength check settles it.
- Vukradin confronts the proprietor, Petra, about the sign advertising a performance he never agreed to. She denies posting it — “That was more of a cover band idea than the real Lathander’s Death” — revealing a man who plays Vukradin’s songs on stage every night, always disclosed as not the real thing.
- Vukradin threatens legal action for his likeness — “I should sue you for a percentage of all your profits” — and calls in muscle: “Brewbarry, can you dispute for me, please?” Brewbarry towers over Petra: “Are you trying to rip off my friend? Making profit on his image and reputation?”
- Petra refuses the asking price twice — “I can’t afford 500 gold pieces for a one-night performance” — and never agrees to pay it. A 23 Persuasion and the looming goliath close a different deal: 500 gold is named as the *value* of the past infringement and squared by one exclusive performance in two nights, with Petra licensed to keep the cover act in perpetuity so long as she keeps disclosing it. No coin changes hands. “I respect the image of the one, the only, the great Vukradin.”
- Along the way Brewbarry floats a revenue share — “We get 50% of whatever earnings she makes while we sing” — prompting Petra’s instant “Is that after or before expenses?” and the band’s hard-won wisdom: “You always want gross” (“that is where Hollywood…”). Petra shakes on it before minds can change, drawing Vukradin’s protest: “No, on the gross, on the gross!” Her verdict: “You’ve learned something, Vukradin. You’re not the same guy I used to remember.”
- Petra pleads her history — “I discovered you, man. I was there at the beginning. I was the one who gave you the gig. Nobody wanted to put you on stage, actually” — a point Soma concedes on her behalf.
- Vukradin’s old table — really just where Brewbarry sat — is now polished, set with beautiful couches, and bears a plaque: “Vukradin created his music here.” Asked to sign it, Vukradin replies, “Well, that’ll be extra,” extracting 25 gold pieces paid on the spot, as Petra despairs: “Brewbarry, what did you do to this guy? This guy used to be the nicest guy!”
- The band performs a teaser set — one song only, at Petra’s insistence — nearly opening with the soup ballad before Brewbarry redirects: “That’s not the one about Lord Neverwinter. There’s one called Vault of Silver Lies, that’s the one you want.” Valphine’s natural 1, combined with Vukradin’s 14, comes out discordant and off-key — and the lack of harmony and off-cue screeches perfectly echo the original early Vukradin genre. “Very avant-garde.”
- Brewbarry then proposes weaponizing their popularity in song. His first target — the evils of Lord Neverember — is vetoed by the band (“Lord Neverember is our ally against Margaster”; “The enemy of our enemy is our friend”), so he pivots: “So let’s sing about the evil of House Margaster. And how corrupt they are. Plant that seed into the people’s mind. ... Everyone who leaves this tavern tonight needs to think that House Margaster is the most evil organization.”
- Vukradin offers to cast — “You want me to cast some spells, too, to, like, really alter their minds?” “Yes, yes, let’s do that” — but no enchantment is ever cast; the GM checks (“You’re not doing a special magical performance tonight, right?”) and the second performance runs on a straight Performance check, boosted only by Bardic Inspiration and Bless (raw rolls of 14 and 7 lifted to 19 and 13). It lands: a witty lyric about Margaster and the trade routes profiting off the people’s inflation, which the crowd picks up and hums along to.
- The party settles in to eat, noting the tavern’s transformation: in the old days the Common Chord had no food and cheap booze Brewbarry once loved and now remembers as undrinkable swill; tonight the food is fancy and the beer is genuinely good — though when Brewbarry declares “Anything that will get me drunk is good, that’s my definition of good,” Petra reclaims the good beer, and Brewbarry keeps both anyway. “Pearls before swine,” Soma sighs.

## Locations
### Spire of the Morninglord
A prominent temple in Neverwinter where the party had recently concluded a sermon — one inspiring enough to move Aurelan Vance, a banker, to a night of forensic paperwork. They were departing, having given the Necklace of Fireballs heirloom over as promised, when intercepted by Aurelan. Cullen Sharpe and House Margaster continued to grate on the party’s nerves in an epic way.

### Protector’s Enclave
A central district of Neverwinter where the party encounters a confused passerby who claims to recognize Boney, and where citizens are heard complaining — loudly and correctly — that rents have doubled.

### Bluelake District
An area of Neverwinter where many businesses appear shut. The party observes strange behavior from citizens — including a woman locking a door she had already locked — encounters a wizard’s apprentice selling many-starred cloak thread, and finds The Board Laid Bare. A sewer system beneath the district serves as the smugglers’ route into the city.

### The Board Laid Bare
A local restaurant in the Tower District of Neverwinter known for its affordable food and tortle cuisine, run by the chef Lim and hosted by the warforged Bellows. The handwritten menu offers fresh fish, vegetables, potatoes, and two sauces — no alcohol — at pre-disruption prices that defy the city’s inflation. Seating consists of a corner table “preferred by guests who do not wish to be observed” and window tables with the best view of the tower. It features a small kitchen and a rear service lane connecting to the city aqueducts, through which smuggled goods are delivered nightly at the third hour after midnight. The restaurant once genuinely sold turtle soup — “for a different clientele” — a fact Lim and Soma still laugh about, since people keep believing Soma when she tells them it’s on the menu.

### Tower District
An area of Neverwinter that has recently become quieter but not necessarily safer — “quieter is different,” as Lim puts it. Three weeks prior, people dressed as Many-Starred Cloaks associates were seen entering the Cloak Tower by the basement stairs. Something in the neighborhood has changed.

### Cloak Tower
A notable structure in the Tower District where suspicious individuals dressed as Many-Starred Cloaks associates were recently seen entering the basement and spending the day — emerging, by Lim’s account, with nothing.

### Blue Lake District Sewers
An underground transit network used by smugglers to move goods into Neverwinter without paying harbor authority fees or taxes. Lim’s suppliers use this route for nightly deliveries. Where there are sewers there are rats — and where there are rats, there are probably were-rats, who tend to know each other; the party’s contact Zeleen may open doors here.

### The Common Chord
A tavern and music venue in Neverwinter that has transformed from a struggling dive bar — no food, undrinkable swill for beer, room for 40 with an average crowd of eleven — into a trendy, high-end establishment with a six-week reservation list, fancy cuisine, and genuinely good beer. It profits off Vukradin’s reputation via a nightly cover act (always disclosed as such, Petra insists) and features a commemorative plaque at the polished, couch-flanked table where he supposedly composed his music — in truth, the table where Brewbarry sat.

## NPCs
### Aurelan Vance
A representative of the Counting House who has become an informant for Brewbarry — originally recruited via an intimidation check the goliath insists was “just making friends.” Inspired by Valphine’s recent sermon (“It inspired me to action”), he dug into suspicious paperwork regarding the missing gnome Bimble overnight and rushed to share his findings with the party, arriving huffy-puffy and out of breath. Now on first-name terms: “My friends call me Ori.” His analysis is pure banker: no severance, no reconciliation, no forwarding instruction is not how a contract ends — it is how a contract is abandoned.

### Bimble
A gnome who was on a commission maintenance roll for 11 years until payments abruptly stopped seven tendays ago — on the day, not the tenday — with no severance or forwarding instructions. He has not banked, borrowed, or left the city, suggesting someone is keeping him — or that he is surviving by other means, such as stealing the mysterious ninth crate. The party, despite being shown the correct spelling on paper, insists on calling him “Bimbo,” and has named the quest accordingly: “Who is feeding Bimbo?”

### Boney
Brewbarry’s skeletal horse, currently wearing Lady Alagondar’s tackle, who attracts confused attention from a passerby in the Protector’s Enclave. Boney answers questions when asked directly — he has “been grazing all over the place” — and the encounter briefly raises unsettling questions about whether Boney has children, and whether they too are skeletal.

### Bellows
A precise and humorless warforged host at The Board Laid Bare. He tracks guest attendance with mathematical accuracy (“You haven’t been here in 73 days”), recommends the corner table to guests who do not wish to be observed, and has argued with Lim many times about her word-of-mouth-only policy. Valphine’s insight marks him as someone who could not tell a lie. He observed that nine crates are delivered nightly but only eight are brought inside, with the ninth disappearing by morning — a discrepancy he has raised twice, never asked about, and notes is not billed to the restaurant. He knows the crates (wooden, rope-bound, wet on the underside) and the couriers (six or seven men, rotating, never the same two) but does not involve himself in how the crates are procured.

### Lim
A tortle chef and proprietor of The Board Laid Bare, and an old friend of Soma’s — the two used to cook together and still trade recipes. She values word-of-mouth business (“There will be no advertising”), dislikes Vukradin’s music (“It’s not even useful”), and once genuinely sold turtle soup to a different clientele. She has maintained a secret supply chain through smugglers who approached her at the market, justifying it as a necessity under Lord Neverember’s rising taxes: the food is good, the prices are good, and “they’re not bad people.” She denies all knowledge of the ninth crate — she orders eight. Her reunion with Soma sours into mutual accusation: “You’ve changed. I remember a different Soma.”

### Zeleen
A were-rat with whom the party has a prior business relationship, mentioned as a potential lead into the sewer networks beneath Neverwinter — the were-rats of Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine may themselves have been from Neverwinter, and were-rats tend to know each other.

### Petra
A half-elf proprietor of the Common Chord who gave Vukradin his start when no one else would book him — “I discovered you, man. I was there at the beginning.” She has since capitalized on his growing fame by booking a nightly cover act (always disclosed, she insists) and enshrining his old table with a plaque, and settled 500 gold pieces’ worth of past infringement not in coin but with one exclusive performance and a perpetual likeness licence — though she did pay 25 gold for a signature on the plaque, on the spot, with pupils like coins. She parries a revenue-share proposal with “Is that after or before expenses?” and closes deals faster than minds can change. Her verdict on the new Vukradin: “You’ve learned something... you’re not the same guy I used to remember” — and, to the goliath she blames for it: “Brewbarry, what did you do to this guy?”

## Items
### Lady Alagondar’s Tackle
The riding gear currently worn by Boney, Brewbarry’s skeletal horse. A passerby in the Protector’s Enclave notices the tackle — “there was something about the gait, something about the look” — and uses it to identify the horse as one he had seen earlier that day outside Valphine’s sermon.

### The Ninth Crate
A mysterious wooden, rope-bound crate, wet on the underside, that is delivered nightly to the rear of The Board Laid Bare along with eight others, but is never brought inside. The order has stood at nine crates since midsummer though the restaurant uses eight; Bellows has raised the discrepancy twice. It is set down, is gone by morning, and is not billed to the restaurant — Lim herself denies ordering it. The party suspects it is being used to feed someone in hiding, possibly Bimble: “He’s eating the ninth box.”

### Many-Starred Cloak Thread
Decorative thread sold by a wizard’s apprentice in the Blue Lake District, claimed to be authentic material from the Many-Starred Cloaks. Not soft — a property Brewbarry finds theoretically impossible, since surely the thread is what makes a fabric soft, a piece of Brewbarry logic so pure it earned him inspiration.

### Vukradin’s Plaque
A commemorative plaque placed on a polished table at the Common Chord — flanked by beautifully set couches — marking it as the spot where “Vukradin created his music.” In truth it marks where Brewbarry used to sit. Petra paid Vukradin 25 gold pieces on the spot to sign it, after his opening position: “Well, that’ll be extra.”

## Spells
### Bardic Inspiration
Vukradin used his musical magic to bolster the performance of his bandmates during the subversive performance at the Common Chord — both bandmates adding d8s that lifted raw rolls of 14 and 7 to 18 and 11 — helping the band’s anti-Margaster message land effectively with the crowd.

### Bless
Valphine layered Lathander’s blessing on top of the Bardic Inspiration during the second performance, nudging the final rolls to 19 and 13 and helping carry the witty anti-Margaster lyric into the crowd’s humming repertoire.
