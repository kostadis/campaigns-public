# Chapter 62

Date: 2026-11-10

## Summary

Concerned that their element of surprise might be lost, the party made their way to House Margaster's warehouse on the Eastern Quay. Their intention was reconnaissance: search for information, evidence, or a way to free Bimbo.

The warehouse was a suspiciously nondescript wooden structure standing thirty feet tall. The party circled it and found no back door. Vukradin climbed onto the roof with practiced stealth and discovered several skylights, choosing an entrance as far from the front office as possible. Soma prepared to enter through another opening in rat form. Vukradin initially considered Greater Invisibility but saved his single casting because it would last only a minute. The location was familiar: this was the warehouse where the party had previously encountered Cullen Sharpe and House Margaster.

While Valphine paused to pray near the front of the building, a messenger emerged from the office and addressed her as “Your Reverence.” The associates of House Margaster wished to make her an offer, with no obligation to sign anything. The messenger knew nothing about its contents; Valphine's insight confirmed that he was simply a courier. Brewbarry put his huge hand on the man's shoulder and told him to return to the office and leave them alone. Valphine nevertheless wanted to hear the proposal, provided she could easily escape, and brought Brewbarry with her.

Inside the front office, they found an imp clerk named Mnemazar wearing an awkward pair of spectacles. The clerk referred to the Cullen Sharpe matter and Adrian's terrible week before presenting legal waivers with bureaucratic indifference. Brewbarry crumpled his copy and tossed it over his shoulder. Valphine scrutinized the requirements to be counted in and out, wondering whether leaving without a count-out might leave her legally on the premises and indefinitely under House protection. That remained her interpretation, not a promise from the clerk. The document also provided for a dead visitor's remains and obligations to revert to the house for continued performance. Neither visitor signed.

Meanwhile, Soma and Vukradin entered through the skylights. The warehouse contained ordinary commercial goods with carefully maintained paperwork. The DM emphasized that House Margaster was a shipping firm: the cargo was legal, and the party had found no stolen goods. Moving toward the back, Vukradin triggered a glyph of warding. Its Thunderwave explosion dealt seven damage and brought guards running.

Mnemazar remained completely calm when the blast reached the front office. Asked whether the clerk intended to respond, the imp explained that counting visitors and obtaining signatures were the front office's responsibilities. Brewbarry settled the matter by raging and striking the clerk twice with his two-handed weapon. Mnemazar dissolved into ochre dust, and Brewbarry vaulted the desk into the warehouse without signing a waiver.

Four veteran guards responded with crossbows, longswords, and repeated declarations that the intruders were not on the manifest. One ran directly past Soma without recognizing the rat as a threat. Valphine remained at the threshold and cast Doomtide, engulfing two guards in a twenty-foot-radius sphere of inky fog. Vukradin turned invisible and continued toward the stairs, but his audible movement still allowed guards to shoot at him with disadvantage.

The skirmish became what Soma repeatedly called a clown fiesta. Curious about a loading crane, she dashed over to inspect a device unlike anything she knew from the forest. Brewbarry endured sword blows, then triggered another thunderous glyph while trying to topple cargo onto a guard. Blasted ten feet backward, he used Stone's Endurance to reduce the damage, shook off the impact, and charged again. He successfully trapped one veteran beneath fallen crates, though the hoped-for domino collapse did not spread through the warehouse. Valphine's Guiding Bolt missed.

Vukradin continued dashing toward the stairs and triggered two more glyphs, taking twenty-seven and twenty-four damage before falling unconscious. Soma reverted from rat form and conjured spectral wolves near a veteran. The pack set off two glyphs, injured and displaced the guard, and was destroyed by the explosions.

A veteran stabilized Vukradin and began carrying him toward the back rather than killing him. Cullen Sharpe then contacted Valphine through Sending: the deal remained available, the party should come downstairs, and they should remember to sign the waiver for their safety. The group chose to de-escalate. The DM clarified that signing was optional. Brewbarry carried Vukradin, and the guards stayed upstairs as the party descended without an escort.

On the way, Valphine noticed a door opening onto a floor that did not exist, though an unsuccessful Arcana check left the anomaly unexplained. In the records room, a ceiling sign declared that death in service did not terminate service. Bimbo's contract was available, but its non-disclosure provisions were still blacked out. The House Commission restoration contract, however, was readable.

Soma's natural twenty uncovered a crucial loophole. House Margaster's claim covered restoration involving a house signatory's labor or any component on its manifests. A person who had never signed with the house could perform the repair using a component outside its specified supply and keep that repair beyond Margaster's ownership. A marginal note read “Resonator Leilon.” The party proposed bringing Bimbo along for expertise while doing the work themselves, though whether his guidance would preserve the loophole remained unconfirmed.

After reviving Vukradin, the party met Cullen Sharpe and Adrian in an adjoining room. Both appeared unmistakably as devils in fine, fashionable clothing. Adrian dismissed the unpleasantness at the other warehouse as past business and remarked that the promotion suited them.

Cullen presented Valphine with the Instrument of Patronage. Initially private to her, the contract promised resources, money, permits, construction assistance, supplies, and House Margaster's backing for her religious order. The house would not direct its doctrine, deity, or work. In return, she would have to supply a particular name as soon as she first learned it; the identity of that name would be specified later. She shared the document with her companions.

Vukradin warned that he would leave if Valphine signed. When the devils offered him top-tier studio funding, he immediately refused. Cullen dryly acknowledged that they had anticipated his position and had chosen not to drug the ink. Vukradin suspected the requested name would eventually require betrayal of a confidence. Valphine concluded that she already had her armies underway and did not need the offered support. Her drow caution about infernal bargains and devotion to Lathander settled the decision.

Cullen and Adrian expressed mild disappointment; Adrian reminded Cullen that he had predicted her refusal. The DM emphasized the discovery beneath the offer: the devils wanted this information badly enough to finance a cleric's mission against evil. They had targeted Valphine because they thought she might sign, not because she alone could eventually learn the name.

Valphine felt Lathander's blessing and a moment of religious fervor after resisting temptation. Her eyes glowed brighter than before. Brewbarry proposed leaving. The session ended with the party holding a possible route around Margaster's repair claim, no signed bargain, and a recently revived Vukradin.

## Locations

### House Margaster Warehouse

A nondescript wooden structure approximately thirty feet high on the Eastern Quay. It serves as a headquarters for the powerful trading house and features a front office, basement records room, roof skylights, a large loading crane, and numerous hidden glyphs of warding. An exterior search finds no back door.

The warehouse stores mundane goods such as cheese, wine, and textiles, all carefully documented on manifests. The DM explicitly establishes that the cargo is legal. Stacked goods impede movement, and some can be toppled onto opponents. Small brass plates are set into the floor.

The party recognizes the site from its earlier encounter with Cullen Sharpe. Its interior also contains an unexplained architectural anomaly: a door opening onto a floor that does not exist. Below the warehouse, the records room contains signed House documents and the ceiling warning that death does not terminate service.

### Warehouse Front Office

The administrative entrance where Mnemazar processed visitors and legal waivers. It contains a desk, previously observed crates, and a door into the main warehouse.

Mnemazar treats signatures and counting visitors in and out as the office's responsibility, remaining indifferent to an explosion elsewhere in the building. After killing the clerk, Brewbarry vaults the desk to enter the warehouse.

### House Margaster Waiver

A visitor document requiring signatories to be counted out upon departure. Visitors counted in but not counted out remain legally on the premises. It offers insurance for ordinary warehouse hazards while excluding hazards visitors bring themselves.

Its death provision states:

> “The undersigned's remains and obligations revert to the house for continued performance.”

Valphine explores whether remaining legally present might extend protection indefinitely, but that interpretation is not confirmed. Brewbarry destroys his copy, and neither signs. The DM later clarifies that proceeding without signing is possible.

A large mechanical leverage device with ropes and pulleys for handling goods delivered by cart. Several people can reposition it; it has no engine.

Soma investigates it in rat form, fascinated by unfamiliar machinery. The DM suggests it might help someone cross the area or strike a nearby veteran, but she does not operate it.

A House archive document detailing Bimbo's service. Its non-disclosure provisions remain blacked out even in the records-room copy, preventing the party from learning the concealed terms.

An unredacted machine-restoration agreement concerning the Model 1486. Its ownership provisions cover repairs involving House signatories' labor or any component on House manifests.

Soma identifies the loophole: someone who has never signed with House Margaster can perform the repair using an outside component and keep it beyond Margaster's claim. A marginal note reads “Resonator Leilon.”

The party proposes using Bimbo's expertise while performing the work themselves. Whether his involvement as an adviser preserves the loophole remains unresolved.

### Instrument of Patronage

An infernal contract offered to Valphine promising House Margaster's financial, material, and political backing for her religious order. Available support includes permits, construction, and supplies. The house would not direct the order's work, doctrine, or deity.

The price is a specific name, to be delivered immediately upon Valphine's first knowledge of it. The house declines to identify the required name during the meeting. Valphine shares the document and ultimately refuses to sign.
