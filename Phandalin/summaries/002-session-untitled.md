# Chapter 2: Untitled

Date: 2025-05-14

## Summary
In the city of Neverwinter, four unlikely souls found themselves drawn together by desperation, curiosity, and the promise of easy coin. Vukradin, a serious-minded Aasimar musician, had spent years playing his carefully composed pieces at a piano bar to patrons who simply did not care. Despite working seven days a week, he never managed to make ends meet, and his dream of opening a proper music studio — a place to “play his serious music with other people who are serious with him and not just piano bar patrons” — felt further away than ever. When a flyer appeared on the streets of Neverwinter calling for adventurers to head south and help secure the hinterlands on behalf of Lord Neverember, Vukradin saw no other choice but to answer it. He had heard there was a lot of money in adventuring, and he was, by his own admission, very desperate.

Among the regulars at Vukradin’s piano bar was Brewbarry, a large and boisterous Goliath barbarian who had been cast out of his Uthgardt tribe for his fondness for drink. Where the other patrons shrugged at Vukradin’s music, Brewbarry was utterly transfixed by it, having grown up with nothing but Uthgardt music — which is just drums — his entire life. The sweeping melodies and complex harmonies struck him as the music of the ages, and he became Vukradin’s most devoted — and only — fan, the sole patron ever to score the music a ten out of ten on the bar’s survey, and one who took genuine offense when others failed to share his enthusiastic appraisal. Lonely and simply wanting someone to hang out with, Brewbarry followed without a second thought when Vukradin announced he was heading south, eager for both the company and the adventure.

Traveling out of Neverwinter with them was Valphine Sotorra, a Drow cleric who had been driven from the Underdark after the great city of her people was devastated by demonic forces. Emerging into the sunlight for the first time, she had found the experience nothing short of religious, and had taken up the faith of Lathander, god of the dawn, with the fervor of a true convert — the clerics of his Neverwinter temple were ecstatic to have a Drow among them, proof of their god’s righteousness. But she was equally drawn to pain, and Neverwinter proved far too civilized for her tastes: no assassinations, no torture racks, no open-air slave markets, no conspiracies to destroy other noble houses. It was all very boring. The flyer, she decided, promised something more painful. Just outside the city, the three met Soma, an elderly Tortle druid in her sunset years who guided travelers through the surrounding wilderness. Soma offered her services as a guide; Vukradin, having no coin, offered to “pay you in a song,” and the unlikely quartet set off along the High Road toward the south.

Before leaving Neverwinter, Vukradin had a memorable exchange with a city guard about the ethics of adventuring. Lord Neverember, with a limited budget and limited resources, had come up with a brilliant scheme: hiring adventurers as contractors, “because they have fewer benefits,” with the standing convention that anyone doing a good turn for the kingdom kept whatever gold and treasure they found — tax-free, as Vukradin was quick to note. The guard begged that whatever the party did, “for the love of goodness, all that is good, do not bring anything to his lordship,” and explained that adventurers made their money by defeating monsters and taking their treasure — “It’s an excellent system.” Vukradin raised pointed questions about where the monsters got the money from (“I don’t know. And to be quite honest with you, Lord Neverember and the guard don’t care”) and whether the treasure rightfully belonged to the heirs of the monsters’ victims. The guard, increasingly exasperated, warned him that “You aren’t going to be very wealthy in this adventuring business if you care too much about these details,” then drew a very literal line: the laws of Neverwinter ended at the city wall, and everything beyond it was, as he put it, “the gray.” He even cheekily suggested that any unclaimed treasure could be donated to the city guard retirement fund, “where we promise to look aggressively for anyone who might claim this treasure,” and only after a suitable amount of time would it fall into the fund itself. The guard’s parting shot — asking whether Vukradin was quite sure he wasn’t a paladin — followed the bard all the way down the road.

After several days of travel, the party arrived in Phandalin, a quiet frontier town of forty or fifty simple log buildings nestled in the rocky foothills of the snow-capped Sword Mountains. Crumbling stone ruins surrounded the newer structures, hinting at a much grander settlement that had existed in centuries past. The town had no walls and no garrison — Soma noted there was no natural defense at all, not even a river — and most of the adults kept weapons within easy reach. Its residents were quiet, hard-working folk who had come from distant cities to eke out a life amid the harsh wilderness: farmers, stonecutters, blacksmiths, traders, prospectors, and children, all of whom welcomed visitors with coin to spend or news to share. Across the street the party passed the Lionshield Coster, the famous interdimensional store where one used to be able to buy everything — though rumor had it their supply chain had been disrupted.

The party made their way to the Stonehill Inn, a modest two-story roadhouse at the heart of town, where the friendly proprietor Toblen Stonehill welcomed them warmly — even fetching a sturdier chair for Soma, and quoting a price of ten copper pieces for the boiled water she requested for her own tea herbs. Vukradin, having no coin for a meal, offered to perform in exchange for food. When Toblen asked for “one of the more fashionable tunes from Waterdeep or from Neverwinter... or even Triboar,” a joking request for Freebird visibly upset the innkeeper, but Vukradin recovered by playing his own creations, rolling a 17 on Performance and earning himself a glass of ale. His follow-up rolls — a 15 Perception but only a 4 on Insight — meant that whatever Toblen truly thought, Vukradin came away convinced the innkeeper adored the performance; as the DM put it, “Sometimes it’s good to be an idiot.” Over drinks, Toblen shared troubling news: “once again, the orcs have come down from the mountains to prey on the lowlands. If Neverwinter doesn’t send help soon, the orcs will overrun Phandalin and destroy everything we work so hard to rebuild.” Toblen, who had lived in Triboar and heard of the disaster the Uthgardt barbarians brought there, eyed Brewbarry warily and asked whether he was one of those barbarians — Vukradin vouched that “He really loves music,” Soma offered “I’ve known him a week. He seems fine,” and Toblen graciously declared that “The past is the past. The future is the future.” He also mentioned, almost as an afterthought, that there was a small dragon problem in the area; when Vukradin asked whether they’d get paid for killing it, Toblen noted, “I’m told the dragons have hoards.” He directed the party to the quest board outside the Townmaster’s Hall for any paying work.

The board outside the boarded-up Townmaster’s Hall listed three tasks posted by Townmaster Harbin Wester, each offering a reward for services rendered: the Dwarven Excavation, Gnomengarde, and Umbrage Hill. Soma briefly favored visiting the midwife at Umbrage Hill — obviously the cheapest job — and Valphine leaned toward the gnomes, but the Dwarven Excavation’s guaranteed fifty-gold payment for delivering a simple warning won out; Vukradin marveled, “I haven’t had that much money in my whole life.” The debate turned to ethics along the way — the notice about prospectors “seeking treasure and relics” prompted Vukradin to sputter, “They’re grave robbing? What is wrong with this place?” and to note with alarm that the Townmaster himself seemed willing to participate in the thievery scheme. Soma’s verdict: “This is a sketchy town.” Still, after some debate about ethics, rewards, and the general shadiness of the whole enterprise, the party settled on the Dwarven Excavation quest. The journey took them roughly fifteen miles — a day’s hike — into the foothills, with Soma considering preparing Goodberry and planning to live off the land while Vukradin still had two uneaten rations. At the site, a canyon with eighty-foot rocky walls opened up to reveal a ruined settlement at its end. A great twenty-foot wall of black stone with a broken gate marked the entrance, one door hanging by a single hinge and the other gone entirely.

Inside the canyon — past three heaps of rubble and a thirty-foot temple facade whose open doorway was flanked by crumbling, life-sized granite statues of cloaked dwarves with evil grins on their weather-worn faces — the party found two Shield Dwarf prospectors named Dazlyn Grayshard and Norbus Ironrune, who announced at once that “this is a private excavation, and everything that we’re doing, we’re not sharing.” They warmed up once the party delivered Townmaster Harbin’s warning, which Vukradin rendered with characteristic bluntness: “There’s a white dragon coming here to kill you.” Norbus explained that they had found startling evidence of an ancient dwarven settlement buried by an avalanche and an old temple dedicated to Abbathor, the evil dwarven god of greed. Valphine’s Religion check (a 14, against Vukradin’s 4) supplied the lore: during solar eclipses Abbathor is appeased with sacrifices of blood and gemstone, and on one occasion the god found the sacrifices of this temple’s priests lacking, causing the earthquake and avalanche that buried the settlement. “Yes, yes, yes, that’s exactly right,” Dazlyn confirmed — though when Valphine compared it to the gods of the Drow cities, he added, “I’ll have you note that Dwarven gods are not like your drow gods.” Both dwarves were visibly startled to encounter a Drow cleric who worshipped Lathander, the god of light — a combination so unlikely that Norbus flatly declared “I’m telling you, she’s lying,” even as the holy symbol around her neck said otherwise. The prospectors offered the party a pair of sending stones — magical objects “basically like walkie-talkies” — as payment if they would “go in there and deal with any nasties and beasties.” Vukradin was unsure the stones were worth anything, though he guessed they might fetch a couple hundred gold; Soma pointed out they would let Vukradin project his music to two ends at once, and Brewbarry lit up at the idea of listening to music all the time. The dwarves also confessed they had seen an ochre jelly inside and run out before facing it — “I don’t know whether what makes a dangerous monster or an easy monster,” Norbus admitted, but “You’ve got swords and you know how to cast spells and you have a buff shell.” Vukradin extracted a promise that all treasure would be returned to its rightful owners, Soma dryly warned the dwarves not to consider *themselves* the rightful owners, and when asked how green the party was, the answer was: level one, a whole life spent studying music and nothing else. As Vukradin put it, “When your alternative is starving, you’re willing to take some risks.”

The party entered the temple with Soma at the front of the marching order — “I’m the toughest. Also, I got nothing to lose. I’m, you know, late in life.” Valphine’s passive Perception of 15 immediately revealed a secret door in the foyer, and Vukradin volunteered to enter the narrow passage beyond it first (“I’ve never been through a secret door. That sounds cool.”), reasoning his armor class of 13 would keep him fine. Farther down the passage, an Investigation check turned up a second secret door before the party explored a chamber to the east. The chamber was partially collapsed and filled with rubble, apparently shaken apart by the same ancient earthquake that had buried the settlement. There was nothing of value inside, and the unstable ceiling made further digging feel like a gamble not worth taking — the DM warned that continued digging could disrupt things, and Valphine judged the whole exercise unproductive.

Turning their attention to the rest of the complex, the party found a side passage that appeared to loop back toward the entrance in a confusing arc — “This doesn’t seem like a very useful passage,” Soma observed, “It looks like it takes us right back to where we had been” — raising questions about the temple’s original layout. Soma, who still had “architectural questions about this place,” pushed for a closer examination of the collapsed room, which revealed protruding stone blocks built into the walls — defensive positions designed to give fighters cover from an entrance that no longer existed. The room had once connected to another section of the temple to the northeast, but that passage had been sealed off entirely by the collapse. The party stood at the edge of the known, peering into the darkness of a ruin that clearly held more secrets than it had yet given up, with the prospectors’ warning of an ochre jelly somewhere inside and reports of a white dragon somewhere in the region.

## Scenes
### Character Introductions and Backstories
#### The players introduce their new characters and discuss their backstories while the DM prepares the virtual tabletop.
- Vukradin is introduced as a serious musician from Neverwinter who is struggling to make ends meet at a piano bar, playing music that patrons don’t seem to appreciate.
- Dave elaborates: Vukradin “plays in a piano bar seven days a week and somehow never manages to make any money,” dreams of opening a music studio to play serious music with serious people, and is trying adventuring only “because he’s heard that there’s a lot of money in it.” He doesn’t want to be an adventurer — “he wants to be a musician... But sometimes you got to do the things you have to do.”
- A Tortle Druid named Soma is introduced as a guide in her sunset years, leading travelers through the wilderness. Wade clarifies she is “a lady turtle,” and that “In her sunset year, she’s just leading tours around the forest, guiding adventurers to other places.”
- Brewbarry is introduced as a Goliath barbarian from the Uthgardt tribes who was kicked out for being a drunk — Gary pitches him as “a little bit of a... dopey giant sort of character” who “just wanted some friends” and answered the flyer because it “offered, you know, like someone to hang out with.”
- Valphine Sotorra is introduced as a Drow cleric of Lathander, drawn to the light after being driven out of the Underdark. Gary notes bluntly: “She’s masochistic. She likes pain.” Her backstory is negotiated at the table — the main Drow city “was completely pulverized by demons that ran wild,” her first exposure to the sun was a religious experience, and the clerics of Lathander in Neverwinter were ecstatic to have a Drow convert.
- Gary struggles to find Valphine’s hook — “I don’t think she sees herself as a hero” — until the DM lands on it: Neverwinter is too civilized (“There’s no assassinations... no torture racks... no open-air slave markets... It’s all very boring”), so “when she sees the flyer, she decides that this would be more painful.” Gary: “Yeah, okay. There we go. That’s a motivation.”
- The group establishes their initial connections: Brewbarry became Vukradin’s biggest fan at the piano bar, Valphine traveled out of Neverwinter with them, and Soma met the trio outside the city and offered to guide them toward Phandalin.
- The DM proposes that for Brewbarry, who grew up with Uthgardt music “which is just drums,” Vukradin’s pitches and tones are “the music of the ages” — he is “not just your biggest fan, but he also gets really offended when other people don’t share in the enthusiastic appraisal of your music.” Dave: “Me too.”
- Vukradin explains his desperate need for money to open a music studio, leading him to take up adventuring after seeing a flyer posted in Neverwinter — and recalls that Brewbarry was “the only patron who ever gave me a 10 out of 10 on the survey” the piano bar used to run.
- Vukradin, lacking a piano on the road, resigns himself to flute and clarinet in camp; the group discusses drum lessons, but percussion is dismissed as “really boring” while Soma insists “It makes the song.”
- Table meta: the group reminisces about the prior campaign — Wallace is not only around but has kids now, Orsik is helping take care of them, and Gary once famously played a cleric and a paladin “at the same time... they kept possessing each other’s bodies.”

### A New Beginning: Setting Out from Neverwinter
#### The party discusses the ethics of adventuring with a city guard before setting out from Neverwinter toward Phandalin.
- The flyer’s actual wording, per the DM: “with the end of the War of the Giants and the War of the Dragons, the hinterland of Neverwinter is in need of adventurers and heroes to bring peace and prosperity” — and “are you adventurers? Heroes? People who want to make a change? The kingdom, Neverwinter needs you.” Vukradin continues to insist that the flyer promised easy money.
- Lord Neverember’s scheme is laid out: limited budget, limited resources, so he hires contractors — anyone who does a good turn for the kingdom keeps whatever gold and treasure they find. Vukradin: “Oh, no, it’s tax-free income, is what you’re telling me.”
- A city guard explains that adventurers are paid by looting monsters rather than receiving a salary from Lord Neverember — he himself is “of course, an official city guard and get paid on a regular basis, have benefits” — and begs the party: “please, for the love of goodness, all that is good, do not bring anything to his lordship.” Soma had likened the alternative to “a cat delivering a dead mouse.”
- Vukradin expresses concern about the ethics of keeping stolen goods that might rightfully belong to heirs of the monsters’ victims — “I can imagine the monsters got the money from killing people... So we’ll be returning the money to their heirs” — and challenges the guard directly: “I’m just very confused about why we would be trafficking in stolen goods. Aren’t you a guard?”
- The guard argues that the laws of Neverwinter do not apply beyond the city walls, calling the area beyond ‘the gray.’ Vukradin’s summation: “This seems like very grayish to me.”
- The guard jokingly suggests donating unclaimed treasure to the city guard retirement fund for safekeeping — “where we promise to look aggressively for anyone who might claim this treasure. And only if after a suitable amount of time would it fall into the city retirement fund.”
- The party sets out along the High Road, which is better patrolled following the recent wars with dragons and giants, traveling with a merchant caravan.
- Brewbarry, already Vukradin’s most enthusiastic fan from the Neverwinter piano bar, follows him south and becomes his traveling companion. The DM calls him “very talkative” and immediately adds “Not”; Brewbarry communicates “with notes.”

### Arrival in Phandalin
#### The party travels south along the High Road, turns east onto the Triboar Trail, and arrives at the frontier mining town of Phandalin.
- The party arrives in Phandalin, a town of 40 to 50 simple log buildings nestled in the foothills of the Sword Mountains, approaching from the west along the Triboar Trail after turning east off the High Road.
- Crumbling stone ruins surround the newer buildings, hinting at a much larger settlement in centuries past.
- The party notes the town has no walls and no garrison, with most adults keeping weapons within easy reach. Soma finds it “actually kind of weird. There’s like no natural defense. There’s no [river], nothing like that.” (The ASR reads “water riffer.”)
- The DM’s reading adds: the residents are “quiet, hard-working folk who came from distant cities to eke out a life amid the harsh wilderness... farmers, stonecutters, blacksmiths, traders, prospectors, and children,” and “visitors are welcome here, especially if they have coin to spend or news to share.”
- The group spots the Stonehill Inn and the Townmaster’s Hall as they enter the village.
- A Lionshield Coster is visible along the road — “the famous interdimensional store where one used to be able to buy everything” — though rumored to have supply chain disruptions. (Table banter blames “Damn COVID” and tariffs.)

### Arrival at the Stonehill Inn
#### The party visits the local inn to rest and gather information from the proprietor.
- The party enters the Stonehill Inn and meets the short, friendly proprietor Toblen Stonehill, who starts cleaning a table and says, “please sit. Rest your weary legs. How can I help you?”
- Soma orders tea — “I have the herbs myself if you don’t” — and Toblen, incredulous (“You want me to boil water and give it to you?”), asks 10 copper pieces for the boiled water. Sizing up the large Tortle, he also fetches “the sturdier chair.”
- Vukradin offers to perform music in exchange for a meal, rolling an impressive performance (a 17) that earns him a glass of ale from Toblen. A joking offer to play Freebird visibly upsets the innkeeper, who had hoped for “one of the more fashionable tunes from Waterdeep or from Neverwinter... or even Triboar,” but relents: “if you’re willing to have one of my creations, I think I could be even entertained by one of yours.”
- Vukradin presses “But did you love it?” — but with a 15 Perception and only a 4 on Insight, he cannot judge Toblen’s sincerity, and the DM rules: “You got the impression that he adored your music.” Valphine, meanwhile, “was bored with all this display of music.”
- Toblen shares rumors about orcs descending from the mountains to prey on the lowlands, warning that Phandalin could be overrun if Neverwinter doesn’t send help: “the orcs will overrun Phandalin and destroy everything we work so hard to rebuild.”
- Toblen, who lived in Triboar and got word of the Uthgardt disaster there, eyes Brewbarry — “You wouldn’t be one of those barbarians, would you?” Vukradin vouches for him (“He really loves music. He’s not like the other barbarian”), Soma adds “I’ve known him a week. He seems fine,” and Toblen declares “all is forgiven between us. The past is the past. The future is the future.” Wade’s table verdict: “He’s following a hashtag not all barbarians.”
- Toblen also mentions a small dragon problem in the area — “just be careful and aware that we do have a small dragon problem.” Vukradin: “Do we get paid if we kill the dragon?” Toblen: “I’m told the dragons have hoards.” (Corrected homophone; the ASR reads “hordes.”)
- After the party says it cannot afford rooms, Toblen directs them to the Townmaster’s Hall quest board to find paying work — “We’ll have to go collect some coin.”

### The Phandalin Quest Board
#### The party examines the local job board outside the Townmaster’s Hall to decide on their first adventuring task.
- The party approaches the Townmaster’s Hall, which is currently boarded up, and finds a quest board posted outside — jokingly imagined as bearing “the yellow exclamation mark above it,” standard MMO fashion.
- They review three available tasks: the Dwarven Excavation, Gnomengarde, and Umbrage Hill.
- The party debates the ethics and rewards of the various jobs, noting that the selected Dwarven Excavation quest offered 50 gold pieces. Vukradin is staggered: “Wow, 50 GP. I haven’t had that much money in my whole life.”
- Soma briefly favors visiting the midwife at Umbrage Hill (“That is obviously the cheapest one”), and Valphine leans toward the gnomes as “the most straightforward,” but the Dwarven quest’s guaranteed cash and simple warning objective decide it. Gnomengarde also names a 50 gp payment, but only if the party brings back something useful it does not keep; Vukradin’s table logic remains: “Why would we do something that pays less? That doesn’t make any sense.”
- Vukradin balks at the excavation notice’s mention of prospectors “seeking treasure and relics” — “They’re grave robbing? What is wrong with this place?” — and at the Townmaster’s participation in the Gnomengarde acquisition scheme: “Wait, he’s going to participate in this thievery scheme?” Soma: “This is a sketchy town.”
- After deliberation, the group decides to pursue the Dwarven Excavation quest to assist dwarven prospectors. The formal task is to deliver a warning; Soma jokes that it is “literally just delivering a letter.”

### The Journey to the Excavation
#### The party travels from Phandalin to a dwarven excavation site in the foothills of the Sword Mountains.
- The party travels approximately fifteen miles toward the Sword Mountains, a journey of roughly a day (one hex on the map is five miles; “that’s a day’s hike”).
- A party member considers scavenging for food using survival skills rather than consuming rations — Soma: “I’m more of a scavenger, live off the land” — while Vukradin still has two uneaten rations.
- Soma reviews her spell options for the trip, confirming Goodberry is available to a level-one druid and considering replacing Longstrider or Ice Knife with it (“Ice knife, I’m never going to use that”).
- The group arrives uneventfully at a canyon with eighty-foot rocky walls and a ruined settlement at its end.
- A twenty-foot-high wall of black stone with a broken gate marks the entrance to the settlement, one stone door hanging precariously by a hinge and the other missing entirely. Soma speculates: “I’ve heard stories of a white dragon flying around here. Perhaps that killed the gate.”
- Soma briefly floats going back and just saying they delivered the message — but Vukradin objects “that would be dishonest,” and Soma concedes: “We’re here to heal the land, not to, you know, be another blight on it.”

### Meeting the Prospectors
#### The party enters the ruined settlement and meets two dwarven prospectors excavating an ancient temple.
- The party enters the courtyard — making no attempt at stealth (“I had no intention of being stealthy”) — passing a partially collapsed 10-foot wall, heaps of rubble hewn from the canyon wall, and a thirty-foot-high temple facade whose ten-foot-high open doorway is flanked by crumbling, life-sized granite statues of cloaked dwarves, evil grins on their weather-worn faces.
- Dazlyn Grayshard and Norbus Ironrune confront the party, initially defensive about their private excavation: “I’ll just have you note that this is a private excavation, and everything that we’re doing, we’re not sharing.”
- The party delivers the warning from Townmaster Harbin Wester regarding a white dragon sighted in the area — Vukradin’s version: “There’s a white dragon coming here to kill you.” Soma deadpans “Here there be dragons,” then “Well, okay, guys. We did it... Let’s go get our money.”
- Norbus identifies the buried settlement and its temple to Abbathor; a successful Religion check supplies the history of the sacrifices, earthquake, and avalanche, which Dazlyn confirms. Valphine (rolling 14 to Vukradin’s 4) recalls that “during a solar eclipses, Abbathor is appeased with sacrifices of blood and gemstone,” and that the god, finding this temple’s sacrifices lacking, caused the earthquake and avalanche — “Yes, yes, yes, that’s exactly right.”
- Valphine wonders if she knows Abbathor from the Drow cities — she doesn’t; she is projecting what an evil drow god would do (“there’s no news here”) — and Dazlyn bristles: “I’ll have you note that Dwarven gods are not like your drow gods.”
- The prospectors express shock and disbelief at encountering a Drow cleric who worships Lathander, the god of light — Norbus insists “I’m telling you, she’s lying,” and demands, “Are you really a cleric of Lathander?”
- The dwarves offer the party a pair of sending stones as payment if they will go inside and deal with any dangerous creatures lurking within — “if you were willing to go in there and deal with any nasties and beasties, we would be willing to give you two sending stones.” Soma counsels hearing them out: “Talking is a free action, right?” The DM: “What about listening?”
- Pressed on what they’ve actually seen (“You just said, you know, take care of some nasties and beasties. What have you seen?”), Norbus admits to the ochre jelly: “We ran out before we could face them,” and “I don’t know whether what makes a dangerous monster or an easy monster. You’ve got swords and you know how to cast spells and you have a buff shell.”
- Vukradin extracts terms: “All of the treasure, you call it, relics, all of that stuff, you’re going to be returning that to the rightful owners, correct?” Soma adds, “Make sure they don’t consider themselves the rightful owners,” then teases the young party — “How green are you?” “You kids, you whipper snappers” — to which the answer is level one and “My whole life studying music and nothing else.”
- The DM reminds the players there is no obligation to do this now — but the party has already made the day’s journey, and Vukradin settles it: “When your alternative is starving, you’re willing to take some risks.”
- Before the party goes any deeper, Norbus volunteers the warning “it’s only fair that we tell you what we did discover” — the ochre jelly — since, as the party notes, “We did ask.”

### Exploring the Ruined Temple
#### The party begins to explore the interior of the temple, discovering secret passages and a partially collapsed chamber.
- Soma takes the front of the marching order — “It’ll be me up front... I’m the toughest. Also, I got nothing to lose. I’m, you know, late in life” — while Vukradin volunteers to enter the secret passage first: “I’ve never been through a secret door. That sounds cool. I’ll go first.”
- Valphine’s passive Perception reveals a secret door in the foyer — her passive 15 surprises the table, prompting the DM to note that Gary’s characters always have high perception (“Gary, you’re a trope”; “Aren’t we all?”).
- Vukradin heads through on the strength of his defenses: “C of 13, I’ll be fine.” (transcript garble; AC of 13)
- The party explores a chamber to the east that was partially collapsed by an earthquake, finding nothing of value — the DM’s text reads, “An earthquake collapsed part of this room, which holds nothing of value,” to which Vukradin protests, “That’s where we’re stopping? Come on.”
- At the end of the next hallway, Valphine sees nothing; despite apparently failed active checks, the GM reveals a second secret door, though the transcript does not preserve why it was found — as Wade jokes that instead “ochre jellies falls on your heads.”
- The group investigates a side passage that appears to loop back toward the entrance, causing confusion about the layout — Soma: “This doesn’t seem like a very useful passage. It looks like it takes us right back to where we had been,” and later, “There must have been something in that earthquake room at some point.”
- The party debates digging out the rubble; the DM warns that with enough digging “things could get disrupted,” and Valphine calls it off: “I think we should look around more. This seems unproductive.”
- Soma persists — “I still have architectural questions about this place that make this room very interesting” — asking whether it was a room or a mine shaft, and how finished it is; the DM reaches for the books.
- While examining the partially collapsed room, the party identifies protruding stone blocks designed to give defenders cover, suggesting the room once connected to another section of the temple to the northeast. Soma notes the contradiction — “There’s only one entrance in the chamber” — which is exactly the clue: the former northeast entrance is now blocked and inaccessible.
- The party considers clearing rubble to find hidden exits but decides to look for more productive paths first, wary of disturbing the unstable structure. The session ends here, mid-exploration — “But it’s no longer accessible.” “Yes.” “All right. Well, thanks, guys.”

## NPCs
### Lord Neverember
The ruler of Neverwinter who is hiring adventurers as contractors to secure the hinterlands and re-establish trade to the south, offering them the right to keep whatever gold and treasure they find. With limited budget and limited resources after the Wars of the Dragons and Giants, he “came up with a brilliant scheme, hiring adventures... he’s going with contractors because they have fewer benefits” — an arrangement Vukradin gleefully recognizes as tax-free income. Per his guard, adventurers should under no circumstances bring monster trophies to his lordship. Dave’s table reaction to the name itself: “Was he literally named Lord Neverember?... And a Gygax special?”

### Neverwinter City Guard
An official who explains the contractor nature of adventuring to the party, arguing that the laws of Neverwinter do not apply beyond the city walls and jokingly suggesting unclaimed treasure be donated to the city guard retirement fund — “where we promise to look aggressively for anyone who might claim this treasure.” He is candid that neither he nor Lord Neverember cares where the monsters got their money, warns Vukradin that caring too much about such details is bad for the wealth, begs the party not to deliver anything to his lordship, and lands the session’s signature line by pointing at the city wall — the gray begins there. His parting suspicion: “You, sir, are you sure you’re not a paladin?”

### Toblen Stonehill
The short, friendly human proprietor of the Stonehill Inn in Phandalin. He welcomed the party, listened to Vukradin’s music in exchange for ale, and shared local rumors about orc raids from the mountains and a dragon sighting in the area. He charged Soma ten copper for boiled tea water (fetching her a sturdier chair first), was briefly upset by a Freebird joke before warming to Vukradin’s own compositions, and prefers fashionable tunes from Waterdeep, Neverwinter, or Triboar. Originally from Triboar, where he got word of the Uthgardt barbarian disaster, he was momentarily wary of Brewbarry before magnanimously forgiving all barbarians: “The past is the past. The future is the future.” He has rooms to rent, which the party cannot yet afford.

### Harbin Wester
The Townmaster of Phandalin who posted quests with varying rewards on the board outside the Townmaster’s Hall — the Dwarven Excavation, Gnomengarde, and Umbrage Hill. The selected Dwarven Excavation quest promised 50 gold pieces for delivering the warning; the Gnomengarde notice separately offered 50 gp for anything useful brought back that the party didn’t keep for themselves, prompting Vukradin’s horror that the Townmaster would “participate in this thievery scheme.”

### Dazlyn Grayshard
A Shield Dwarf prospector partnering with Norbus Ironrune to excavate an ancient temple of Abbathor. Initially defensive about the private excavation (“everything that we’re doing, we’re not sharing”), he warmed to the party after they delivered the dragon warning, confirmed the temple lore recalled by the party (“Yes, yes, yes, that’s exactly right”), and hired the party to deal with its dangerous creatures. He took mild offense at Valphine’s comparison of Abbathor to Drow deities: “Dwarven gods are not like your drow gods.”

### Norbus Ironrune
A Shield Dwarf prospector excavating the ruins of a temple to Abbathor. He introduced himself and his partner, explained they had found “startling evidence of an ancient dwarven settlement buried by an avalanche,” and — while professing that normally he “would object terribly to anyone excavating and digging into... the Dwarven Dead” — justified this dig because the temple belongs to the evil god of greed. He joined Dazlyn in discussing the site, warned the party about a dangerous ochre jelly they had seen somewhere inside the temple (“We ran out before we could face them”), admitted he can’t tell a dangerous monster from an easy one, flattered the party’s swords, spells, and “buff shell,” and together with Dazlyn offered sending stones as payment for clearing the ruins. He flatly refused to believe a Drow could be a cleric of Lathander.

## Locations
### Neverwinter
A major city on the Sword Coast that has recently survived wars with dragons, giants, and barbarian hordes — strategically placed far enough from the Uthgardt hordes to avoid the worst of the tribal wars that followed. Lord Neverember rules here and is hiring adventurers as contractors to re-establish trade and civilization to the south. Vukradin worked at a piano bar here before taking up adventuring; the city also hosts a temple of Lathander, where Valphine’s conversion was joyfully received. Valphine found the city far too civilized — no assassinations, no torture racks, no open-air slave markets — and therefore boring.

### The High Road
The main trade route between Neverwinter and the south, recently made safer by increased patrols following the recent wars. The party traveled it uneventfully with a merchant caravan.

### Phandalin
A frontier mining town of 40 to 50 simple log buildings nestled in the rocky foothills of the snow-capped Sword Mountains. Crumbling stone ruins surround the newer structures. The town has no walls and no garrison — nor, as Soma noted, any natural defense such as a river — and its quiet, hard-working residents (farmers, stonecutters, blacksmiths, traders, prospectors, and children) keep weapons within easy reach. Visitors are welcome, especially with coin to spend or news to share. The town was overrun by orcs many years ago but has since recovered, and is currently threatened by orc raids from the mountains and a dragon sighting nearby.

### Stonehill Inn
A modest two-story roadhouse at the center of Phandalin with several rooms for rent on the upper floor, run by the friendly proprietor Toblen Stonehill. Vukradin performed music here in exchange for a glass of ale; Soma’s boiled tea water cost ten copper and came with a sturdier chair.

### Townmaster’s Hall
A boarded-up building in Phandalin featuring a quest board outside where adventurers can find work posted by Townmaster Harbin Wester — three quests at the time of the party’s visit. Vukradin briefly threatened to walk into the boarded-up hall itself before Valphine noted that would look suspicious.

### Lionshield Coster
A merchant outpost visible along the road in Phandalin — “the famous interdimensional store where one used to be able to buy everything” — rumored to be experiencing supply chain disruptions.

### Dwarven Excavation Site
An ancient dwarven settlement and temple complex dedicated to Abbathor, buried by an avalanche in the canyon foothills of the Sword Mountains. The canyon’s rocky walls rise eighty feet; the site features a broken black stone gate (one door on a hinge, one missing), a partially collapsed ten-foot wall around the courtyard, three heaps of rubble hewn from the canyon’s back wall, and a thirty-foot temple facade whose ten-foot-high open doorway is flanked by crumbling, life-sized granite statues of cloaked dwarves with evil grins on their weather-worn faces. The interior holds at least two secret doors, a passage that appeared to loop toward the entrance, and a collapsed chamber with defensive stone blocks facing a now-sealed connection to the northeast. An ochre jelly lurks somewhere within.

## Items
### Adventuring Flyer
A notice posted in Neverwinter on behalf of Lord Neverember, calling for adventurers and heroes to head south to secure the hinterlands and re-establish civilization — “with the end of the War of the Giants and the War of the Dragons, the hinterland of Neverwinter is in need of adventurers and heroes to bring peace and prosperity.” The promise of easy money lured Vukradin and others toward Phandalin, and Vukradin continued to insist that the flyer promised it.

### Sending Stones
A pair of magical stones that function as a communication device, similar to walkie-talkies. Offered by the dwarven prospectors Dazlyn and Norbus as payment if the party agrees to clear the ruined temple of dangerous creatures. Vukradin was genuinely unsure whether they were worth anything, then guessed the party “might be able to get like a couple hundred gold pieces for them”; Soma pitched them to the bard as a way “to project your music to two ends at one time,” which set Brewbarry wondering whether he could listen to music all the time.
