# Chapter 28: Where Reality Unravels: Breaching Axeholm

Date: 2025-12-24

## Summary

The session resumed at the entrance to the ancient dwarven fortress of Axeholm, carved into the base of a mountain fifteen miles south of Phandalin, as Soma’s rat familiar began scouting within. The party’s quest was to open the long-sealed fortress and make it safe for habitation, as Townmaster Harbin Wester had warned that the people of Phandalin might need to evacuate there if a dragon attack became imminent. The promised reward was 250 gold pieces. Brewbarry immediately looked for the easiest possible interpretation of the assignment—“They said open, not clear, so as soon as Axeholm is open, we can go back”—but Vukradin reminded him that the fortress also had to be safe for habitation. Talk soon turned to killing the dragon once the party reached fifth level, summarized as “Dungeon before the Dragon.”

The rat discovered a winch mechanism inside the entrance that could lift the portcullis gate. As it explored, the party heard unsettling sounds emanating from within: a constant low-frequency ticking like a clock speeding up and slowing down, and the scratching of quills on parchment coming from inside the walls themselves. There were no visible holes from which the noises emerged; they seemed to emanate from the stone. Working together, the party had the rat loop a rope around the winch and then pulled from outside, raising the gate after several minutes. Soma promptly joked, “Guys, quest complete, let’s go,” while Valphine suggested putting up an “open for business” sign. Beyond the entrance stood ballistas trained through arrow slits, prompting the party to avoid standing directly in their lines of fire. Brewbarry then began smashing through an inner portcullis door, a task expected to take roughly an hour even with help.

While the others battered the barrier, Soma sent the rat through the defensive works and into the main hall. It found a forty-foot-high chamber supported by four thick stone pillars, with a heavy layer of dust across the flagstone floor, double doors, high arrow slits, and a small eastern balcony twenty feet above the floor. Movement through the chamber felt deeply wrong: the rat could seem to travel twenty feet while experiencing the distance as only a single foot. The light within the fortress was equally unnatural—mathematically cold, static, and devoid of normal flicker or shadow.

When several doors opened, grotesque creatures emerged that had once been Axeholm’s dwarven inhabitants. These out-of-phase beings had parchment-like skin covered in shifting ink, gold coins embedded in their flesh, and mouths sewn shut with golden wire or sealed with red tape. Their limbs moved asynchronously, making each creature appear to possess six hands and six legs. Soma used Meril’s Staff to sense the corruption and recognized the same sort of planar anomaly the party had previously encountered in the forest. The staff indicated that reality itself had been warped around the dwarves and that the experience had driven them mad. Valphine determined that they were not dead or undead, although her power to turn undead would still affect them. When Brewbarry called into the fortress—“Is there anybody in here?”—the creatures gave no coherent answer. They only repeated, “Efficiency. Efficiency. Efficiency.”

When Brewbarry finally smashed through the door, combat erupted with six of the distorted dwarves. Valphine stepped beside him and invoked Lathander: “May the glory of Lathander deliver you the glorious pain of the light.” Her divine power turned three of the creatures, leaving them frightened and incapacitated despite their unusual nature. Soma created a Moonbeam to burn two of the advancing dwarves, while Vukradin inspired Brewbarry and attacked with Starry Wisp. Brewbarry raged, becoming Big Brewbarry, and cut down one of the creatures while exploiting his halberd’s reach to strike others as they moved past him.

The battle escalated when eight more dwarves became visible. They had been moving through the fortress’s static light in a way the party could not initially perceive, then materialized in front of the adventurers while continuing to mutter about efficiency. Soma met the new wave with Thunderwave, catching four creatures in a cube of force, injuring them, and hurling them ten feet south. Vukradin advanced into the hall and killed one with his rapier; rather than leaving an ordinary corpse, the dwarf blinked away as space folded in upon it.

The distorted dwarves’ speech suggested fragments of some broken bureaucratic process. During the battle they muttered about “Inspector,” complained of “Lacking the appropriate tool,” and said, “Inspector lied.” Their paralyzing attacks manifested in similarly administrative terms. When Brewbarry resisted one, he felt himself abruptly knocked into cold storage before a voice in his head rejected him with the words “Incorrect packaging.” When Soma later succumbed, she experienced herself as a statue being packaged in a box, sealed, and delivered like an object rather than a living creature.

Soma’s paralysis left her helpless as the dwarves swarmed her and landed critical blows. She fell unconscious, ending the paralysis but also causing her rat familiar to disappear with a disturbing squeal. Valphine brought her back to consciousness with a Healing Word that restored nine hit points. Soma then transformed into a bear, gaining the resilience needed to rejoin the fight, and tore through one of her attackers with two powerful strikes. Earlier, she had also cast Earth Tremor, knocking several dwarves prone and shattering the floor into permanent difficult terrain.

The party continued cutting down the warped defenders. Vukradin finished weakened targets, Valphine mixed healing with radiant attacks, and Brewbarry used his reach, reactions, and cleaving strikes to punish creatures approaching from the darkness. Although the adventurers had substantially reduced the second wave, one of the remaining dwarves struck Soma’s bear form and paralyzed her again. The session ended at the top of the initiative order with “Parabear” helpless before the remaining creatures.

## NPCs

### Harbin Wester

The Townmaster of Phandalin who tasked the party with opening Axeholm and making it safe for habitation, offering a reward of 250 gold pieces for completion. He warned that if a dragon attack is imminent, the people of Phandalin might need to evacuate and take refuge in Axeholm. He also pointed out the danger of an ale shortage if the dragon and orcs are not dealt with, which particularly motivated Brewbarry.

The party also joked about Vukradin’s political aspirations in Phandalin. Although the office of mayor does not exist, Vukradin remains convinced that a new constitution could create it, dismissing lesser ambitions such as town comptroller or assessor because those offices would not let him enact the reforms he believes are necessary.

### Out-of-Phase Dwarves

Grotesque creatures that were once the dwarven inhabitants of Axeholm, now transformed by a planar anomaly and warping of reality. They have parchment-like skin covered in shifting ink, gold coins embedded in their flesh, and mouths sewn shut with golden wire or red tape. They appear to have six hands and six legs moving out of phase with reality.

They are neither dead nor technically undead but are mechanically similar to ghouls, and Valphine’s Turn Undead can affect them. Meril’s Staff indicated that their apparent extra limbs reflect their movements across warped reality and that the anomaly has driven them mad.

They are largely unresponsive to speech and ignore ordinary creatures such as Soma’s scouting rat. Their own broken vocabulary centers on bureaucratic or industrial phrases, including:

- “Efficiency. Efficiency. Efficiency.”
- “Lacking the appropriate tool.”
- “Inspector lied.”
- “Incorrect packaging.”

Their paralyzing attacks do not feel like ordinary bodily paralysis. Soma felt transformed into a statue, packaged into a box, sealed, and delivered. Brewbarry’s successful resistance felt like briefly entering cold storage before being rejected for “Incorrect packaging.”

When killed, at least some of the dwarves do not fall normally. One struck by Vukradin’s rapier blinked away as space folded in upon it. Others appeared momentarily “phased” when badly injured.

The light around them is mathematically cold and does not flicker naturally. They can move through that static illumination while remaining difficult or impossible to perceive, then abruptly materialize as they shift into alignment with the party’s reality.

## Locations

### Axeholm

An ancient dwarven fortress carved into the base of a mountain, located fifteen miles south of Phandalin. It has been sealed for years, and the party has been tasked with opening it and making it safe for habitation as a potential refuge from dragon attacks.

The fortress’s entrance is protected by a portcullis operated by an interior winch. A second portcullis blocks further progress and cannot simply be picked; Brewbarry and the others had to spend roughly an hour battering it open.

The interior exhibits pervasive signs of planar distortion:

- Distances feel out of phase, so that apparent and experienced movement do not agree.
- A constant low-frequency ticking resembles a clock alternately speeding up and slowing down.
- The scratching of quills on parchment seems to come from inside the walls.
- The light is mathematically cold, static, and shadowless.
- Creatures can move invisibly or imperceptibly through the light before materializing.
- Meril’s Staff identifies the effect as a planar anomaly similar to one previously encountered in the forest.

The western bulwark contains seven ballistas aimed through arrow slits. The party initially passes through their lines of fire cautiously, concerned that the fortress’s defenses might still operate.

The main hall has a forty-foot-high ceiling supported by four thick stone pillars and a floor covered in thick dust. It contains double doors, high arrow slits in the northern and southern walls, and a small stone balcony extending from the eastern wall twenty feet above the floor.

Soma’s Earth Tremor shattered part of the hall’s stone floor, leaving the affected area as difficult terrain at the end of the session.

### Phandalin

A town where the party has acquired a reputation and where Townmaster Harbin Wester resides. It lies fifteen miles north of Axeholm and faces potential threats from a dragon and orcs.

The party has become sufficiently well known that the DM described them as having acquired a reputation in Phandalin and its environs. Vukradin continues to aspire to become mayor despite repeated reminders that Phandalin has a Townmaster rather than a mayor. The possible loss of Phandalin’s ale supply remains an especially urgent concern for Brewbarry.

## Items

### Rope

A standard piece of adventuring gear used by the party in conjunction with Soma’s rat familiar to operate the winch mechanism and raise the portcullis at Axeholm’s entrance.

The rat was able to loop or secure the rope around the mechanism while remaining within one hundred feet of Soma. The party then pulled the rope from outside, allowing them to operate a winch that the rat could not turn through physical strength alone.

### Winch

A mechanism discovered inside the entrance of Axeholm by Soma’s rat familiar, used to lift the main portcullis gate. The party attached a rope to it and pulled to raise the gate over the course of several minutes.

Its discovery allowed the adventurers to enter without attempting to lift the heavy portcullis directly, something even a very high Athletics effort had failed to accomplish.

### Ballistas

Seven large crossbow-like defensive weapons in Axeholm’s western bulwark, aimed through arrow slits to defend the portcullis approach.

The party took care not to stand directly in front of them while entering. The rat could crawl around and through the defensive positions, allowing Soma to inspect the chambers beyond while the others remained behind cover.

### Meril’s Staff

Meril’s Staff is a magical staff wielded by Soma that can detect unnatural things and discern corruption within thirty feet. When used in Axeholm, it revealed the presence of a planar anomaly and warping of reality, confirming that the out-of-phase dwarves were once the fortress’s inhabitants who had been caught in the rift and driven mad.

The staff gave Soma the same general impression she had received from the anomaly in the forest. It also helped establish that the creatures’ apparent multiplication of limbs came from movement through warped reality rather than ordinary transformation or undeath.

## Spells and Abilities

### Rat Familiar

Soma continued scouting with the rat familiar she had summoned before this session. The familiar was used to explore the entrance, discover the winch mechanism, and assist in manipulating it with a rope to raise the portcullis. The rat could move within one hundred feet of Soma and relay sensory information back to her.

During the hour spent breaking down the inner portcullis, the rat patrolled the reachable rooms, inspected the ballistas, entered the main hall, and watched the distorted dwarves approach. The creatures ignored it, and the planar anomaly did not visibly affect it.

When Soma was knocked unconscious, the familiar disappeared with what the DM described as “a very disturbing squeal.”

### Turn Undead

Valphine invoked the glory of Lathander to turn the out-of-phase dwarves, causing three of them to become frightened and incapacitated despite them not being technically undead. The divine power worked against these planar-warped creatures, forcing them to flee.

Her invocation was: “May the glory of Lathander deliver you the glorious pain of the light.”

The effect confirmed the unusual overlap between the dwarves’ state and undeath: they were not undead, but magic specifically intended to turn undead still affected them.

### Moonbeam

Soma created a five-foot-radius beam of radiant moonlight that damaged out-of-phase dwarves caught within it. The spell lingered, causing ongoing damage to creatures that started their turn in the beam or entered it during their movement.

Soma moved the beam during later turns to catch new targets as the second wave materialized. It killed at least one badly wounded dwarf and reduced several others to low health before Soma’s paralysis broke her concentration.

The spell’s ability to reveal shapeshifters did not expose any ordinary polymorph effect. The DM clarified that the dwarves were planar-shifted rather than polymorphed.

### Bardic Inspiration

Vukradin used this ability to grant Brewbarry—and later himself—an extra d6 die that could be added to an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, providing magical encouragement in combat.

Brewbarry received the first inspiration before entering his rage. Vukradin later inspired himself while advancing into the hall and preparing to fight with his rapier.

### Starry Wisp

Vukradin cast Starry Wisp at one of the first-wave dwarves, dealing eight points of damage and leaving it badly wounded for Brewbarry to finish.

### Thunderwave

Soma unleashed a cube of thunderous force that dealt damage to multiple out-of-phase dwarves and pushed them back ten feet. The spell’s impact was powerful enough to affect four creatures at once.

All four targeted creatures failed their saving throws, took fourteen points of damage, and were driven south. The party initially discussed the effect as a cone before correcting the area to a cube, which made catching all four creatures easier.

### Word of Radiance

Valphine emitted a burst of divine radiant energy against the out-of-phase dwarves standing in front of her. One resisted the magic, while another took radiant damage and muttered something about an inspector.

The spell let Valphine strike nearby enemies without moving deeper into the group and risking being surrounded.

### Earth Tremor

Soma caused the ground to violently shake, dealing damage to multiple out-of-phase dwarves and knocking several of them prone. The spell also permanently transformed the affected area into difficult terrain, making it harder for creatures to move through.

One creature resisted and remained standing, while three others were knocked prone. Those creatures later had to spend movement standing up and then contend with the broken ground while approaching the party.

### Healing Word

After Soma’s paralyzed condition ended because she had been knocked unconscious, Valphine spoke words of divine healing that restored nine hit points and enabled Soma to continue fighting.

Valphine first selected the wrong spell level, then corrected the casting to first level. The ranged healing allowed her to restore Soma to consciousness without abandoning her position in the melee.

### Sacred Flame

Valphine called down a column of divine radiant fire upon an out-of-phase dwarf, dealing minor damage. The sacred light mixed with the rapidly shifting, distorted form of the creature but did not fully dispel the warping effect.
