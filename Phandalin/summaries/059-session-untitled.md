# Chapter 59

**Date:** 2026-10-20  
**Source recording/export:** Session #63

## Summary

After their concert in Neverwinter, the party gathered to weigh their next moves, torn between investigating a mysterious dead drop tied to Drow activity in the city and repairing a machine that had crippled trade across the region. They also debated whether repairing the machine was presently possible: Soma believed the necessary parts were still inaccessible, while the GM stressed that the problem was more involved than merely acquiring parts. Brewbarry, ever mindful of his bathrobe business and the funding he had secured from Aurelan Vance at the Counting House, was particularly eager to see the machine restored. Although the funding already allowed his bathrobes to be moved, Brewbarry had personally promised the manager of his business that the machine would be fixed and intended to keep his word. After some deliberation—and with Brewbarry casting the deciding vote—the group agreed to tackle the dead drop first, hoping to learn who the Drow operating in Neverwinter were before turning to Bimble and the machine.

Their destination was the Fallen Tower in the run-down Tower District. On the way, a nervous young worker stopped them and asked for directions to the Guild Registry. He was carrying a letter, had been sent on his first errand, and was terrified of getting lost or delivering it incorrectly. The party initially treated his approach with suspicion, but careful scrutiny confirmed that he was exactly what he appeared to be. Vukradin eventually pointed him in the right direction and, in a rare moment of generosity, pressed a sending stone into the young man's hand: “You take this, you call us if you get lost.” He made it equally clear that the stone was only a loan and that the party would return to collect it. Brewbarry encouraged the worker to buy a bathrobe, while Vukradin added an invitation to convert to Lathander.

The party arrived at the Fallen Tower, a tavern carved into the ruins of a wizard's tower destroyed during a dramatic confrontation with the Arcane Brotherhood. The tower had belonged to Llomnauvel Firehands, described as a powerful and thoroughly unhinged weather wizard. When the Brotherhood attacked to seize his magical collection, they encountered traps that included spells transforming intruders into sea creatures. Rather than surrender his tower, Llomnauvel triggered a final destructive spell and brought the structure down around everyone inside. Every night, at the exact moment of its destruction, silent phantasmic visions replayed at the ruined base: burning mages fell to their deaths, and the Brotherhood's overwizard plummeted while his limbs transformed into eels. The harmless spectacle had once been commercialized as a ticketed tavern attraction before the business declined into overpriced mediocrity and closed. An enterprising orc named Vagdru One-Ear eventually restored it as a functioning tavern and strictly enforced it as neutral ground, where nobody was permitted to break the peace.

Seated at a table with a clear view of the entrance was Tamsin, a human woman in her forties wearing a postal clerk's gray coat, ink staining the side of her right hand, and a brass parcel stamp hanging on a chain around her neck. An untouched drink sat before her as the agreed-upon signal, and she also had a book and stamp at hand. Vukradin approached with his characteristic charm, opening with, “What's a woman like you doing in a place like this?” Brewbarry joined him with, “Do you work for Google? Because you're everything I searched for,” prompting immediate groans from the others. Tamsin answered that she was waiting for a parcel and asked what they were doing there.

The conversation grew tense as Tamsin revealed that she had expected a particular courier, not the party. “She told me where it would be. She told me when I would have it. She has never been wrong about a when, and now she appears to have been wrong about a who.” She demanded to know who they were and whether they actually possessed the parcel. Tamsin explained that Aletra was never wrong—not about a route, a buyer, the weather, or the timing of a delivery—and that this was the first time in their working relationship that one of Aletra's predictions appeared to have failed. Her distress was professional as well as personal: everything Tamsin carried arrived, and that perfect record was the basis of her reputation.

After some confusion over which manuals the party possessed, they deliberately presented Tamsin with the wrong one as a test. She examined it and rejected it: “This is the wrong book. I need the other book.” The party then established that they also possessed the correct Model 1486 manual, previously obtained from Bimble. Before surrendering it, they pressed Tamsin about Aletra, the purpose of the delivery, and the party's inexplicable fame throughout Neverwinter.

Tamsin revealed her delivery method by opening a magical satchel gifted to her by Aletra. The satchel functioned as a one-way planar delivery gate. The party quickly realized that if a book could pass through it, so might a sufficiently small creature. Soma volunteered to transform into a rat and enter the satchel, despite Tamsin's alarm and the lack of any guaranteed return route. When warned of the danger, Soma replied, “I've had a good life.” She also observed that she likely had only a few years left anyway and was willing to take the chance. Tamsin found the idea of someone entering a device reserved for parcels deeply stressful, but the party calmed her enough to proceed.

Soma emerged in a hidden laboratory where a Drow woman stood among arcane machinery. The woman was Aletra, Valphine's sister. Finding a rat where the manual should have been, Aletra grumbled that one could not get good help, then stopped herself: the error should have been impossible because her arrangements were never wrong. She initially spoke in a language Soma could not understand and prepared to eliminate the apparent vermin. Soma fled for the nearest exit, and Aletra struck her with Magic Missile, expecting an ordinary rat to die. Soma survived, forcing Aletra to reassess the situation.

Aletra studied the rat and asked in Common, “Are you the druid?” When Soma paused and nodded, Aletra recognized her as the druid traveling with Valphine. She composed herself and dictated a message, insisting that the Model 1486 manual be delivered through a future dead drop. She refused an in-person meeting, accurately predicting that Valphine would want a deep conversation, the bard would try to persuade her that they could be friends, and the barbarian would want his own confrontation. Aletra offered three hundred gold to Vukradin's studio if he could convince Valphine to surrender the manual—a bribe aimed squarely at his musical ambitions and well-known interest in funding the studio.

To prove her message genuinely came from her, Aletra supplied a private detail only Valphine would know, identified at the table as the color of their mother's favorite cape.

Rather than immediately accept transport home, Soma tried to leave the laboratory and determine Aletra's location. She discovered that the laboratory existed in a pocket dimension. Aletra laughed at the idea that she would hide somewhere a rat could escape and guide Valphine back to, declaring, “This is a place that you can't… I cannot be found. You can only be expected here.” Strictly speaking, Aletra added, the book had been expected—not Soma.

Aletra eventually led Soma back to the laboratory and activated a machine resembling those the party had seen in Axeholm. She pressed buttons, cranked a lever, and set its steampunk mechanisms in motion. Before opening the portal, she declared that the plans already underway would restore the Sotorra family and urged Valphine to abandon her “stupid, pathetic, light god” and return to the family. Soma leapt through the resulting portal and emerged in a dusty warehouse in Neverwinter. The building appeared closed and had seemingly gone undisturbed for several weeks. Most importantly, Soma had arrived through the very transport machine everyone believed was not working. The machine had at least functioned as a receiving terminus, though whether it could operate normally—and how Aletra had activated the network—remained unresolved.

While Soma was away, the others questioned Tamsin. She explained that Aletra had initially approached her with a low-risk delivery job and offered well above the asking price. The first transaction unfolded exactly as Aletra predicted, as did the second. Once Tamsin realized how precise Aletra's information was, she agreed to remain on retainer. Aletra subsequently used her to collect and deliver various artifacts and curios of questionable provenance, including a distinctive wolf pelt the party recognized from their earlier adventures. Tamsin emphasized that she did not acquire such items herself; she was paid to transport them from one party to another.

Tamsin knew little about the rival Collector, including the Collector's identity or gender, but had encountered their agents and knew they had repeatedly outbid or preempted Aletra's people. She firmly denied any connection between the Collector and House Margaster, laughing at the suggestion that the Margasters were involved. She did reveal that Aletra claimed to work for an unnamed, powerful magic user with sweeping plans. Aletra also repeatedly insisted that she had discovered how to “get it all back,” though Tamsin could not explain what that meant.

Vukradin explored whether Tamsin might become the party's courier if her relationship with Aletra collapsed. He proposed a regular circuit in which Tamsin's people would carry cheese to the party's gold mine, collect the party's share of the gold, and return it to them. Tamsin initially said she did not normally transport that sort of cargo but reconsidered “for the right price.” She was willing to arrange one of her couriers for the work, but Vukradin lost interest when she declined to perform the route personally. Throughout the negotiation, Tamsin remained fixated on the fact that the party still held the book she had been hired to deliver and that her perfect professional record was now in jeopardy.

Meanwhile, Brewbarry, wearing his bathrobe, approached a citizen adopting what the citizen called the “Uthgardtian aesthetic.” Brewbarry was deeply offended and confused by what he saw as a cartoonish misappropriation of his culture—essentially a man in a fancy restaurant eating with a fork and knife while pretending to be a wild warrior. The citizen explained that dressing as an Uthgardt barbarian had become a subtle protest against Lord Neverember's taxes, controls, and financial mismanagement. Neverember had publicly presented the party as adventurers hired to protect the hinterlands and stabilize the region. Citizens subsequently turned Brewbarry's image into a symbol of resistance: a supposedly independent barbarian refusing to submit to established power.

As a barbarian, Brewbarry completely failed to understand these abstract social forces and complex political structures. Convinced the citizen was merely confused, Brewbarry viewed the situation through the simple lens of power and direct threat. He insisted that the public had chosen the wrong target: Lord Margaster, not Neverember, had sabotaged the machine, driven up prices, and profited from the city's misery. He ordered the man to spread that message. The citizen attempted an awkward approximation of a barbarian fist bump, which Brewbarry indulged to make him feel like a comrade. As Brewbarry walked away, he turned back and declared, “Until you've licked the blood of your enemy off your axe, you won't know what a barbarian feels like.”

Once Soma returned, the party compared what everyone had learned. A Sending exchange initially conveyed little beyond the fact that Soma had met Aletra, that Aletra wanted the manual, and that she lived on another plane; Soma forgot to include her location in the limited message, forcing her to find her own way back to the Fallen Tower. When she reunited with the group, she gave the full account. The party concluded that Aletra might be controlling or obstructing the machine network rather than dealing with genuinely broken machinery. Her ability to predict routes, deliveries, and outcomes suggested either supernatural foresight or the aid of a powerful diviner.

The party was unwilling to surrender the only useful copy of the Model 1486 manual, especially because it might still be needed to restore the transport machinery. They therefore arranged for the manual to be copied before any future dead drop, and the GM confirmed that the copy had been created. Aletra had not yet provided the time or coordinates for the exchange, and the original remained with the party.

The session ended with increasingly enthusiastic discussion of confronting Aletra. When Brewbarry asked how much Valphine loved her sister, Valphine answered, “Drive a knife into her heart.” Vukradin immediately offered to help. The GM warned that Aletra was part of a longer-term arc, appeared unusually powerful, and seemed able to know what would happen with unerring accuracy. He suggested that the party travel to Leilon and gain experience before attempting to kill her. Vukradin countered that the group had Hold Person, while Brewbarry argued that they simply needed to do something impossible to predict. No attack occurred, but the party ended the night already considering how to reach Aletra's pocket dimension and defeat someone who always appeared to know what was coming.

## NPCs

### Aurelan Vance

A representative of the Counting House who provided funding for Brewbarry's bathrobe business ventures. The funding already permits the transport of bathrobes, but Brewbarry separately promised his business manager that the larger machine problem would be repaired.

### Bimble

A gnome associated with the mysterious machine the party intends to fix. He possessed the Model 1486 manual before it came into the party's hands and claimed, after reading it, that the machine could not be repaired. The party now doubts that conclusion after Soma successfully arrived through the supposedly nonfunctional receiving machine. They intend to collect Bimble and bring him to inspect it.

### Nervous Young Worker

A frightened young laborer carrying a letter who stopped the party to ask for directions to the Guild Registry. He was afraid of getting his first errand wrong and repeatedly sought confirmation of the route. The party determined that his anxiety was genuine and that he posed no threat. Vukradin loaned him a sending stone, Brewbarry advertised the party's bathrobes, and Vukradin encouraged him to worship Lathander.

### Vagdru One-Ear

An enterprising orc who restored the Fallen Tower after its original ticketed attraction failed. He operates it as a functioning tavern and enforces its status as neutral ground, where no visitor is permitted to break the peace.

### Tamsin

A human courier in her forties wearing a postal clerk's gray coat, with ink staining the side of her right hand and a brass parcel stamp hanging from her neck. She is highly professional and slightly neurodivergent, finding the party's suggestion that Soma enter a parcel-only transportation device particularly stressful.

Tamsin has built her reputation on the principle that everything she carries arrives. Aletra initially won her loyalty by paying generously for low-risk jobs and predicting every logistical detail correctly. Tamsin eventually accepted a retainer and has since transported numerous artifacts and curios of questionable provenance, including a wolf pelt familiar to the party.

She is deeply distressed by her first failed delivery and by the prospect that Aletra may terminate their arrangement “thanks to that rat.” She knows little about the rival Collector beyond encounters with their agents. She denies any link between the Collector and House Margaster. She also knows that Aletra claims to serve a powerful unnamed magic user with sweeping plans and repeatedly says she has discovered how to “get it all back.”

Tamsin is open to new business if paid appropriately. She considered establishing a courier route between the party and its gold mine, carrying cheese outbound and gold on the return journey, but Vukradin lost interest when she proposed delegating the work.

### Aletra

Valphine's Drow sister, operating from a secret laboratory within a pocket dimension. Aletra once expected to become the new Matron Sotorra and remains obsessed with restoring the family's status and power. She describes Valphine as the naive sister and wants her to abandon her light god and return to the family.

Aletra has demonstrated an uncanny ability to predict routes, buyers, deliveries, weather, and timing. Tamsin had never known one of these predictions to fail before Soma emerged from the satchel in place of the expected manual. This flawless foresight is inconsistent with Valphine's memories of her sister, suggesting either a newly acquired ability or assistance from Aletra's powerful unnamed employer.

When Soma arrived in rat form, Aletra initially attacked with Magic Missile. Once Soma survived, Aletra recognized her as Valphine's druid and used her as a messenger. Aletra demanded the Model 1486 manual through a future dead drop, offered three hundred gold to Vukradin's studio, and supplied a private family detail to authenticate the message.

Aletra refuses to meet the party in person and accurately anticipates several of their likely reactions. She has access to functioning transport machinery resembling the Axeholm devices and can use it to send travelers from her pocket dimension to Neverwinter.

### Lord Neverember

The ruler of Neverwinter, currently unpopular due to tax hikes and controls imposed to cover the costs of wars, disasters, and the city's financial problems. He publicly presented the party as adventurers hired to secure the hinterlands and reassure the population. That effort inadvertently transformed Brewbarry into a symbol of independence and protest, contributing to the spread of an “Uthgardtian aesthetic” among disaffected citizens.

### Llomnauvel Firehands

The powerful and allegedly unhinged weather wizard who once owned the Fallen Tower. He defended his magical collection from the Arcane Brotherhood with elaborate traps, including magic that transformed intruders into sea creatures. When the Brotherhood breached his defenses, he destroyed the tower rather than allow them to claim it. Phantasmal echoes of that destruction still replay nightly.

### The Nervous Protester

A Neverwinter citizen dressed in barbarian-inspired clothing as a symbolic protest against Lord Neverember. He understood the costume as a statement of rebellion, independence, and refusal to submit to established power, but knew almost nothing about actual barbarian culture. Brewbarry redirected his anger toward Lord Margaster, accepted his awkward attempt at a barbarian fist bump, and sent him away to spread the new message.

### The Collector

An unidentified rival competing with Aletra to acquire artifacts and curios. Tamsin has encountered the Collector's agents but does not know the Collector's identity, gender, nature, or ultimate purpose. The Collector has repeatedly outbid Aletra's people or reached targets before them. Tamsin emphatically denies any connection between the Collector and House Margaster.

### Aletra's Employer

An unnamed and apparently powerful magic user with far-reaching plans. Tamsin knows only that Aletra claims to work for this figure. The party suspects the employer may be a diviner or the true source of Aletra's newfound ability to predict events.

## Locations

### Tower District

A run-down district in Neverwinter where the party travels to meet Tamsin at the Fallen Tower. It contains the city's only consistently respected neutral meeting ground.

### Guild Registry

The administrative office where all guilds in the city are registered. The nervous young worker had been sent there carrying a letter and feared failing his first errand. The party remembered seeing the Registry near the Counting House.

### The Fallen Tower

A former wizard's tower in the Tower District, destroyed during a conflict between Llomnauvel Firehands and the Arcane Brotherhood. Llomnauvel's magical defenses included traps that transformed attackers into sea creatures, and his final spell collapsed the tower rather than surrender its contents.

Silent phantasmic visions replay the destruction every night: burning mages fall through the ruins, while the Brotherhood's overwizard descends with his limbs becoming eels. The spectacle was once commercialized as a ticketed attraction, but the original tavern failed because of high prices, an excessive cover charge, and the district's decline.

Vagdru One-Ear later restored the site as a functioning tavern. It now serves as strictly enforced neutral ground where couriers, operatives, and other rivals can meet without violence.

### Aletra's Laboratory

A secret laboratory located within a pocket dimension and filled with arcane equipment and steampunk-style machinery. Soma reached it through Tamsin's magical delivery satchel. Aletra claimed that the laboratory could not be found normally: visitors could only be “expected” there.

The laboratory contains a transport machine resembling those found at Axeholm. Aletra used it to open a one-way portal that delivered Soma to a warehouse in Neverwinter.

### Docks Warehouse

A seemingly abandoned warehouse in the Docks containing a receiving machine similar to those found in Axeholm. It served as Soma's exit point from Aletra's pocket dimension and appeared not to have been entered for several weeks. The machine successfully received Soma despite the widespread belief that the transport network was broken.

### Neverwinter

The city where the party is currently operating. Neverwinter is gripped by economic tension, rising prices, and resentment over Lord Neverember's taxes and controls. Citizens blame the government for their suffering without knowing the full role House Margaster and the transport disruption may have played.

The party's fame has become entangled with these pressures. Neverember used them as symbols of his efforts to secure the hinterlands, while protesters adopted Brewbarry's appearance as an emblem of independence and resistance.

### Protector's Enclave

The district the party departed before entering the Tower District. The party had recently conducted business at its Counting House and remembered seeing the Guild Registry nearby.

### Axeholm

The location where the party previously encountered transport machinery resembling the device in Aletra's laboratory and the receiving machine in Neverwinter. Soma and Aletra had also met there before, allowing Soma to recognize her.

### Leilon

A potential future destination where the GM recommended that the party gain experience before attempting to confront Aletra. The party reacted by treating this warning as encouragement to confront her sooner.
