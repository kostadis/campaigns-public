# Chapter 14: Untitled

Date: 2025-09-02

## Summary

Before setting out, the party took a moment to reflect on their purpose. Vukradin, the ever-ambitious bard, made no secret of his desire to forge alliances with everyone they encountered—not purely out of altruism, but with an eye toward filling future concert halls. “I think I might be more for the fans, actually,” he admitted while debating whether pursuing popularity would make him a sellout. He still refused to accept money he considered unethical, though he remained eager to cultivate allies among Adabra, the Harpers, the Zhentarim, and anyone else willing to attend a performance. His companion Valphine, the drow musician whose art blended the soaring choral traditions of Lathander with the grinding intensity of drow metal, seemed unbothered by his mercenary motivations. Together with Soma the druid and the towering barbarian Brewbarry, the group set off on the one-hour hike from Phandalin toward the Whispering Grove, tasked by the midwife Adabra with rooting out the source of a mysterious magical blight that had taken hold of the forest.

When they arrived at the edge of the Whispering Grove, the party found it deceptively ordinary at first glance. There were no rotting trees and no obvious decay—only a vague, creeping sense of wrongness that settled over them like a cold fog. Soma, attuned to the natural world, felt that something was deeply off, though the forest wore its corruption like a mask, hiding it beneath a veneer of unsettling, too-vivid green. “Guys, something is off,” she warned, even though she could not initially identify any visible source. It was only as they pressed forward that the true strangeness of the wood began to reveal itself.

Their first encounter came in the form of a majestic stag standing in the path ahead—or rather, a stag that could not quite decide where it existed. Its fur shimmered and flickered, phasing in and out of reality, and its great antlers twisted through shapes that seemed to belong to more dimensions than the world possessed. The creature teleported about fifteen feet at a time, leaving an afterimage behind for several seconds after each jump. Soma called upon her gift for speaking with beasts and addressed the creature, which was initially territorial and irritable: “I was okay. Until you walked into my territory. Why are you in my territory? Get out of my territory.”

After Soma identified the rough boundary of its domain, the group respectfully backed away. The stag then became more willing to talk and reluctantly acknowledged that something was wrong. “I used to just be able to walk and prance and eat the vegetables. And now I can blink, and my horns are… Different. My antlers are different.” It complained that navigating trees had become nearly impossible with antlers that changed every time anyone looked at them, asking Soma to imagine having a shell that constantly changed shape. After accepting mushroom rations, it explained that the entire forest had become strange and pointed the party toward its center. It also mentioned other friendly people who could speak with animals and had promised to fix the problem, but had never returned with a solution.

Deeper in the wood, the party came upon a dense thicket of brambles whose leaves pulsed with unnatural colors, blooming and withering to black in the same breath. Their examination suggested planar leakage or bleeding rather than an ordinary plant disease. As Soma moved within ten feet to inspect it more closely, the thicket lashed out with thorny tendrils, piercing her flesh, inflicting necrotic harm, and flooding her veins with a creeping poison.

The creature seemed to slip between planes of existence, phasing just enough to let Valphine’s radiant fire pass harmlessly through it—twice. Brewbarry waded in with his halberd, its long reach allowing him to carve into the writhing mass of branches without standing directly beside it. Soma transformed into a great brown bear and tore into the thicket despite the lingering poison. Vukradin unsettled the hostile plant with bardic words and then hurled magical mockery at it, inflicting psychic harm and disrupting its attack. The thicket also regenerated while still active, confirming that simply battering it down might not permanently destroy it.

Brewbarry’s thunderous critical blow left the thicket nearly destroyed. Soma delivered the final blow with her claws, leaving the brambles temporarily inert. When it showed signs that it would regenerate again, the party hurried past. They considered setting it alight but rejected the idea after recognizing the obvious risk of burning down the entire grove. Vukradin dryly imagined Adabra’s reaction: “We rescued the Whispering Grove. It didn’t survive the rescuing, but we rescued it.”

The eerie quiet that followed was broken by howls—distant at first, then overlapping and out of sync with the world around them, as though the sound itself was caught between realities. A dire wolf and its pack emerged from the shadows, all bearing ominous red eyes. Their unified howl struck a primal terror into Vukradin’s heart. One wolf rushed him, bit him, and knocked him prone while he struggled against the supernatural fear.

Soma, still in brown bear form, called down a beam of pale moonlight that incinerated one wolf and scorched the dire wolf. The spell was a special gift of her circle and could be invoked even while she was transformed. Valphine outlined members of the pack in shimmering faerie fire, though most of the wolves escaped its glow. Brewbarry enlarged himself through Giant’s Havoc, entered a rage, and used his halberd’s extraordinary fifteen-foot reach to cleave through wolf after wolf. He killed two with a single sweeping assault and struck down another as it attempted to close with him. His enlarged stature also prevented the dire wolf from knocking him prone when it bit him.

As the lesser wolves fell, the pack’s supernatural hold over Vukradin broke. He recovered and delivered the final blow to the dire wolf. The last surviving wolf disengaged and fled into the trees, and the party allowed it to escape despite a brief suggestion that they tame it and make it a pet. They stripped five regular pelts and one dire wolf hide from the fallen, noting the strange, blighted shimmer that clung to the fur. Soma refused to carry the suspect hides in her own pack, while the others speculated about eventually crafting a wolfskin cloak of displacement.

As they ventured further into the heart of the wood, they stumbled upon a small, perfectly still pool. Rather than reflecting the canopy above, its surface showed twisted versions of their own faces, warped by extreme anguish or joy. For Valphine, the inversion prompted the suggestion that she might see a joy-ridden version of herself rather than one suffering in pain.

Vukradin, bolstering his nerve with bardic inspiration, leapt into the water. The pool erupted into a kaleidoscope of mirrors, faces, and fragmented scenes. His first attempt to understand the vision failed and inflicted a level of madness, but he remained in the pool and tried again. This time he glimpsed a distorted, pleading figure and received the message: “He saw beyond, but only what he was meant to see. The numbers. Always the numbers.” Deciding that he had tested his luck enough, Vukradin climbed out and immediately collapsed unconscious on the bank. His companions quickly revived him.

After hearing Vukradin’s account, Brewbarry unexpectedly proved the most knowledgeable about the phenomenon. Drawing on half-remembered legends of his people, he identified such pools as transient shadows or windows into other places. According to those stories, a person who looked into one might encounter another version of themselves and ask it a question.

Brewbarry decided to test that legend. The question he chose—which side of the bread to butter—was perhaps not the most dignified inquiry, but his reflection answered by buttering both sides simultaneously. The revelation drove Brewbarry into a screaming, wide-eyed panic that sent him sprinting into the dangerous forest.

Soma, still wearing the form of the brown bear, gave chase and brought Brewbarry down with a full-body tackle, pinning the terrified barbarian to the forest floor until the madness passed. “Stay down, Brewbarry, this is for your own good,” she told him. When he recovered, he remained fixated on his revelation: not one side, but both sides. Soma assured him that they would butter both sides and even cut off the crusts. Valphine considered asking the pool a more useful question about the source of the corruption, but the party decided they had watched enough companions lose their minds and continued toward the center instead.

Approaching from the northwest to skirt the worst of the blighted bramble patches, they finally reached the heart of the Whispering Grove. The area contained more strange wells or pool-like planar disturbances, along with additional masses of hostile brambles.

Caught within the grasping, pulsing vegetation were a druid and two rangers, their appearance marking them unmistakably as members of the Emerald Enclave. Soma recognized their affiliation, and the party inferred that they were the expedition the blink stag had spoken of—the people who had come before them with promises to heal the wood. They were alive but ensnared, and whatever had trapped them had clearly proven more than a match for trained wilderness defenders. Vukradin’s eyes lit up at the prospect of new allies: “Well, it’s a great time to get some more allies!” The party prepared to rescue them, joking that once freed, the Enclave members could be placed in front during the coming battle while the party collected the credit.

## Locations

### Phandalin

The town where the party received their quest from Adabra and from which they departed for the Whispering Grove. The grove is located about a one-hour hike from town.

### Whispering Grove

A dense wooded area located about a one-hour hike from Phandalin, nestled among valleys and currently suffering from a mysterious magical blight. Although the grove initially appears healthy, its corruption manifests as planar leakage: shimmering and teleporting animals, shifting antlers, unnaturally colored vegetation, hostile regenerating brambles, red-eyed wolves, displaced echoes, and reality-warping pools. The stag identified the center of the grove as the place where the changes began.

### The Distorted Pool

A small, perfectly still pool found within the Whispering Grove that acts as a planar mirror. Its surface shows warped reflections marked by extreme anguish or joy. Legends remembered by Brewbarry describe such pools as temporary shadows of other places where a person may encounter another version of themselves and ask a question. Entering or gazing too deeply into the pool can reveal cryptic truths but also causes madness, unconsciousness, or uncontrollable terror.

## Player Characters

### Valphine

A drow cleric and musician in the party who performs a unique mixture of Lathander choral music and drow metal. During the expedition she used sacred flame against the planar thicket and faerie fire against the wolf pack. Her companions continue to joke about her fondness for pain and her extensive piercings.

### Soma

A druid strongly attuned to natural environments and the central figure in Adabra’s Whispering Grove mission. She used speak with animals to negotiate with the blink stag, navigated the forest, and transformed into a brown bear to fight the thicket and wolves. Her Circle of the Moon training allowed her to invoke moonbeam while transformed. She later chased down and restrained Brewbarry when the distorted pool drove him mad.

### Vukradin

A bard whose overriding ambition is to gather allies and expand the audience for his music. He remains concerned about the ethics of his earnings but admits that he may care more about fans than artistic purity. During the expedition he mocked the blighted thicket, was frightened and knocked prone by the wolf pack, killed the dire wolf, and voluntarily entered the distorted pool in search of answers.

### Brewbarry

A towering barbarian who fights with a halberd and can enlarge himself through Giant’s Havoc. His combination of increased size and weapon reach allowed him to threaten enemies from fifteen feet away and dominate the battle with the wolves. He also recalled obscure lore about planar reflection pools, asked one which side of bread should be buttered, and temporarily fled in terror after witnessing a version of himself buttering both sides.

## NPCs

### Adabra

A midwife who previously aided the party and has now tasked them with saving the Whispering Grove from a magical blight that Adabra suspected was caused by interventionist activity. Vukradin regards helping her as another opportunity to gain allies—and potentially attract Adabra and the Emerald Enclave to future concerts.

### Ser Kaelen

A figure of suspicion for Vukradin, mentioned in connection with a letter that has raised concerns within the party. Vukradin said he was “still not so sure about Ser Kaelen” and that the letter had not helped, although his broader desire to make allies remains unchanged.

### The Blink Stag

A majestic stag caught between dimensions, possessing the ability to teleport short distances and leaving afterimages behind. Its shimmering fur phases in and out of reality, while its antlers constantly shift through higher-dimensional patterns. It is protective of its territory and initially demanded that the party leave, but became cooperative after they respected its boundary and gave it food. It revealed that the forest’s strangeness began at its center and that a previous group of friendly, animal-speaking visitors had promised to fix it but failed to return.

### Emerald Enclave Druid

A member of the Emerald Enclave found trapped in the blighted brambles at the center of the Whispering Grove, part of an earlier expedition sent to heal the wood. The druid’s distinct appearance allowed Soma to recognize the group’s affiliation immediately.

### Emerald Enclave Rangers

Two scouts accompanying the Emerald Enclave druid who have become ensnared by the unnatural, pulsing vegetation at the heart of the Whispering Grove. Their capture suggests that the corruption was capable of overwhelming a trained wilderness expedition.

## Items

A new staff acquired by Soma that requires attunement and occupies one of her attunement slots. It possesses a Discern Corruption capability intended to help detect unnatural blight, although the blink stag’s obvious planar instability soon made further confirmation unnecessary.

### Anonymous Letter to Vukradin

A letter signed by “a friend to True Justice” that did nothing to ease Vukradin’s suspicions of Ser Kaelen. Its author’s identity remains unknown.

### Gnomengarde Mushroom Rations

A type of travel food consisting of fermented Gnomengarde mushrooms. The party offered a portion to the blink stag after it asked whether they had anything to eat. The gift persuaded the stag to disclose that the corruption began near the center of the forest.

### Blighted Pelts

Five regular wolf pelts and one dire wolf pelt harvested from the unnatural wolves slain in the Whispering Grove, shimmering with a strange, sickly energy from the forest’s blight. Soma refused to carry them in her own backpack, while the others planned to bring them back to town for examination and considered using them to make a wolfskin cloak of displacement.

### Halberd

A polearm wielded by Brewbarry with devastating effect. Its natural reach, combined with the additional reach granted by Giant’s Havoc, allowed the enlarged Brewbarry to strike enemies from fifteen feet away. He used it to critically wound the blighted thicket, cleave through multiple wolves, and kill another wolf as it charged into range.

## Spells

### Speak with Animals

Cast by Soma to communicate with the blink stag. The spell allowed her to learn about its altered body, respect the boundaries of its territory, discover that the blight originated near the forest’s center, and hear about the previous group that had promised to fix the corruption. The spell had expired by the time the wolf pack appeared.

### Sacred Flame

Valphine called down radiant fire to strike the blighted thicket on two occasions, but the creature’s planar shifting allowed it to evade the flames both times. The thicket itself did not physically dodge; instead, the surrounding environment shifted just enough to carry it clear of the effect.

### Wild Shape

Soma transformed into a brown bear after the thicket poisoned and injured her. She used the form to finish the regenerating plant, fight the wolf pack, shield her companions, and ultimately chase down and restrain a maddened Brewbarry. The transformation could last for up to an hour.

### Vicious Mockery

Vukradin used his bardic magic to hurl distracting insults at the blighted thicket, causing it psychic distress and giving it disadvantage on its next attack. This followed his use of unsettling words to weaken the plant’s resistance to the spell.

### Moonbeam

A silvery beam of pale light called down by Soma while in her bear form. It incinerated one wolf outright and scorched the dire wolf. As a special Circle of the Moon spell, it was one of the few spells Soma could invoke while transformed, and she could reposition the beam on later turns if needed.

### Faerie Fire
