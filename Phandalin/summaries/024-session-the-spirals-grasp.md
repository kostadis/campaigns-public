# Chapter 24: The Spiral’s Grasp

Date: 2025-11-19

## Summary
The party gathered at the Loggers’ Camp to discuss their next move, with Vukradin learning that he had inadvertently built a reputation as a noble adventurer after helping repel Cryovain’s attack on Phandalin and advocating for fair wages. Despite his protests that he simply wanted to open his own studio, the public perception had taken hold. “I didn’t mean to be noble, I’m not a paladin,” he protested. His companions also remembered the church they had helped establish and suggested returning to check on it.

Before returning to Phandalin, the group recalled information about a missing Emerald Enclave patrol and its meeting location. Vukradin initially questioned whether Lyra, a low-ranking member they had previously saved, had enough influence to help them. A natural 20 Investigation check suggested a solution: rescue the missing agents and credit Lyra with the success, giving her the promotion she needed and the party an ally inside the faction. “She could be our political candidate,” Vukradin proposed. Valphine considered such maneuvering entirely normal. Brewbarry emphasized that they would also save lives and hoped to make amends for the devastation his people had caused. Soma had a separate motive: resentment toward Adabra, who she felt had tricked her.

The party took a long rest using the 2024 recovery rules. Shrimpy, tired of carrying their belongings for several days, asked to leave. Vukradin insisted that his earlier ambush warranted imprisonment: “So, no, you can’t leave Shrimpy, you’re sticking around until we can drag you to Phandalin.” Tibor, the loggers chief, refused to take him to Neverwinter, leaving the party responsible for transporting him.

Following their rest, the party consulted Joe’s notebook for directions to the Emerald Enclave meeting point and set off through the wilderness. Successful Survival checks kept them from getting lost, although Valphine observed that navigating without cavern walls was difficult. Having exhausted his wine, Brewbarry accepted fermentable mushrooms from Vukradin and entered an altered state of consciousness. The group discussed a Tankard of Sobriety, but its ability to prevent drunkenness was the opposite of what Brewbarry wanted.

They arrived at a riverbank where an unnatural, dense fog covered the eastern shore. The fog itself seemed ordinary to Soma; its confinement to a sudden patch of land was what made it strange. Standing rigidly by the water was Teega the Smith, with glassy, unfocused eyes. Brewbarry called across the river, “Do you know where we can find some good wine?” She answered in an eerie monotone about metal bending, minds being cleansed, and waves seeking uniformity. Asked about the missing patrol, she described them as trying to fix a leak with a wooden plug and said their brittle thoughts were being reformed by the current.

Teega drew a stylized spiral in the mud. Brewbarry’s religious knowledge established that it was not a symbol of Talos, but its actual significance remained unknown. Vukradin persuaded her to accompany them to Phandalin, briefly clearing her eyes. “why am I here? Where am I?” she asked before the trance returned and she wandered into a nearby glade. The party considered restorative magic and followed her.

Crossing the river, they felt a strange tingling in their minds, heard sounds echo oddly, and saw light flickering through the shadows in a way that recalled an earlier planar anomaly. The sensation filled the whole area rather than pointing toward a single source. In the glade, they discovered a grim-looking man wielding a black coral dagger, accompanied by three figures whose movements were unnaturally precise and robotic. The party recognized them as the missing Emerald Enclave patrol.

The man, a Cult Fanatic, coldly informed them that they were interfering with necessary adjustments and that the zealots now served a greater purpose. He spoke of a rising current and offered the party a chance to submit. Vukradin suggested that it might be flowing toward Neverwinter, but the fanatic refused to reveal its destination. Valphine read him as cold and detached rather than openly furious, while Vukradin’s attempt to intimidate him failed. The party refused submission and prepared to knock their opponents unconscious, hoping to rescue the controlled patrol.

Combat erupted as the Cult Fanatic compelled Brewbarry to flee, manipulating his mind to impose disadvantage on the saving throw. Soma transformed into a massive bear and attacked the cult leader. The warriors dealt heavy damage to Vukradin and Valphine. Vukradin used Lesser Restoration to release Brewbarry from the command before he lost his turn, allowing the barbarian to rage and focus on the leader. Brewbarry’s first swing was a natural 1, which he blamed on the mushrooms: “It’s just not my day, man. It’s those freaking mushrooms you gave me!”

Valphine used Balm of Peace to heal herself and Vukradin. Vukradin attempted to strike down the fanatic but struggled to land a blow. Soma continued battering the leader while avoiding the attacks directed at her, including a Spiritual Weapon. A warrior knocked Vukradin unconscious, but Brewbarry’s next attack delivered the crushing non-lethal blow that ended the fight. The controlled warriors immediately collapsed, screaming and clutching their heads. Valphine stabilized Vukradin and restored him to ten hit points.

The party dragged the captives across the river and secured them with Corbin’s help. The warriors wore green cloaks and gold badges bearing the spiral symbol, later identified as Kraken Society insignia. Soma tracked Teega through the wilderness in brown bear form, with Valphine accompanying her. After a short pursuit, she subdued Teega non-lethally and brought her back to be restrained.

When the Cult Fanatic awakened, he immediately reasserted control over his tied-up minions. Moving him away did not free them; the restrained warriors crawled toward him. The party decided to keep him unconscious for the journey, assigning Brewbarry responsibility for repeated sedation. Their six prisoners were Shrimpy, the three patrol members, the fanatic, and Teega. They discussed a four-day forest journey to Phandalin, alternative routes, the wine cart, and possible accommodation in the town’s prison or the church’s proposed dungeon.

After resting during the journey, they attempted Lesser Restoration on a controlled warrior, without discernible effect. Other possible targets were discussed. The Cult Fanatic resisted interrogation and offered only warnings: “You know, you people have no idea what you’ve gotten yourselves mixed up in.”

On the second day, a large raiding party of Talosian orcs ambushed them. Vukradin tried to establish common ground by pointing to Shrimpy, but Shrimpy belonged to an independent orc faction opposed to Talosian leadership, making him the wrong example. The orcs demanded the Cult Fanatic, identifying him as their enemy. Wary of the party’s strength and Valphine’s presence, they were willing to negotiate.

Given one final chance to explain himself, the fanatic answered, “the tide is coming, the waves are rising,” and refused to betray his trust. Vukradin sought assurance that killing him would allow everyone else to leave safely. The orcs agreed, and Valphine executed him. They took his head while the party retained the body to search it. The raiders declared the forest theirs and ordered the party to leave. A half-orc anchorite of Talos laughed over the severed head. Asked about the Carver, the orcs did not recognize the name, but the anchorite treated that figure as another obstacle for Talos to crush.

With the Cult Fanatic dead, the Emerald Enclave patrol finally awoke. One survivor belonged to Lyra’s old crew, and Vukradin immediately told him, “Well, she sent us to rescue you.” A disturbed warrior described the intrusion into his thoughts: “It wasn’t a monster, it was the emptiness. There was something in my mind. Orders that weren’t mine, thoughts that dripped like poisoned water into my skull.” He reported that someone had been stealing an inscription describing a drowned gate beneath Leilon. The party connected the captivity to the Kraken Society and wondered whether the gate lay in the Underdark.

Brewbarry, having drunk all of his ale, wanted the nearest tavern. A Survival check established that Falcon’s Lodge was only five miles away. Remembering its wine, he pressed for a detour. Vukradin agreed despite reservations about the lodge’s atmosphere, comparing it to Disneyland. The session ended with the party deciding to head toward the lodge, while their return to Phandalin, presentation of Lyra’s rescue, and investigation of the drowned gate remained ahead.

The loggers chief who refuses to take Shrimpy to Neverwinter’s jail, deeming it too risky.

### Teega the Smith
A woman encountered by the river, appearing rigid and glass-eyed, speaking in a monotone about ‘the metal bends’ and ‘the waves are coming’. She draws a mysterious spiral symbol in the mud and briefly regains lucidity before her eyes glaze over again. She appears possessed and wanders into the glade where the party finds the mind-controlled Emerald Enclave patrol. She subsequently disappears and is tracked, subdued, and returned by Soma.

- Her movement is unnaturally stiff, and her open eyes do not seem to focus on the people addressing her.
- She dismisses questions about wine as noise and describes the patrol’s minds as brittle thoughts undergoing reform.
- Vukradin’s invitation to return to Phandalin briefly elicits: “why am I here? Where am I?”
- Soma tracks Teega by scent in brown bear form, with Valphine accompanying her, and knocks Teega unconscious without killing her.
- She is restrained for transport. No separate recovery testimony from her is recorded at the session’s end.

### Cult Fanatic
The grim-looking leader of the mind-controlled Emerald Enclave patrol, wielding a black coral dagger. He is cold, detached, and speaks of ‘necessary adjustments’ and a ‘rising current’, attempting to recruit or subdue the party. Vukradin suggests the current is flowing toward Neverwinter, but the fanatic refuses to disclose its destination. He reasserts mind control when awakened; separating him from his minions does not free them. He is resistant to intimidation and refuses to reveal his true allegiances, only muttering about ‘the tide is coming’ and ‘petty squabbles’. He is eventually killed by Valphine and his head is taken by the orcs.

- He is the only person in the confrontation who does not visibly appear mind-controlled.
- He claims the zealots’ attempted purification amplified energies his cause needed.
- He casts Command and Spiritual Weapon, and can interfere with a target’s saving throw by manipulating the brain.
- Brewbarry knocks him unconscious, disrupting his control, but waking him restores it.
- The Talosian raiders identify him as their enemy rather than their ally.
- His death allows the Enclave survivors to awaken and speak. The party keeps his body for a proposed search, but no recovered belongings are reported.

### Emerald Enclave Warriors
Three individuals who were part of the Emerald Enclave patrol, now appearing robotic and precise in their movements, under the control of the Cult Fanatic. They wear green cloaks with gold badges featuring a spiral symbol. They are subdued non-lethally by the party and collapse in pain when their leader is defeated. After being rescued, they describe being mind-controlled with ‘orders that weren’t mine’; one survivor reports that someone was stealing an inscription about a ‘drowned gate beneath Leilon’.

- They become robotic again when he awakens and try to crawl toward him when he is moved away.

The leader of the Talosian orc raiding party who ambushes the party. He is a cleric of Talos who takes the Cult Fanatic’s head after Valphine kills him and calls the Carver ‘one more obstacle to be crushed by Talos’.
