# Chapter 5 To find a shapeshifter

## Scenes

### 005.01 Valphine 01-02-Tarsakh 1495

“The ogre is prone on the ground under the sun. Like Lathander obliterates the darkness with his light, I will smash his body,” Valphine thinks, her muscles tensing, a grim determination set on her face. The morning sun, painful yet spiritually compelling to her, now illuminates the brutish form of their downed foe, a testament to the battle that has raged through the early hours. She sees an opportunity, a chance to end this struggle decisively.

With a grunt of exertion, her mace swings through the air, a metallic arc against the backdrop of the rugged mountain peaks. The weighty weapon descends, intended to crush, to obliterate the ogre. But fate, or perhaps simply a moment of misjudgment, intervenes. The mace lands with a dull thud against the ogre’s hide, a glancing blow that carries no force, no impact. The creature, surprisingly resilient, remains unhurt.

Someone laughs at the failed swing. “Can’t hit the broadside of a barn or a giant ogre.” Valphine’s jaw tightens, a flush of annoyance rising in her cheeks.

The others debate whether she missed at all. One insists, “It’s not that she missed. She hit with not enough force.” Another suggests that the ogre’s padded armor absorbed the blow. Whatever the explanation, the result is the same: no wound.

Then, from her right, she hears the roar of Brewbarry, a sound that promises devastation. The Uthgardt barbarian, a force of nature in his own right, swings his formidable halberd down on the ogre. The massive weapon slices through the air with a menacing whisper, a testament to Brewbarry’s strength and fury. But just like Valphine’s strike, the blow misses its mark. The halberd, instead of finding purchase on the ogre’s flesh, strikes the rocky ground with a deafening clang that echoes through the tight mountain valley. The sound resonates, a stark reminder of their collective failure to land a decisive blow.

From behind them comes encouragement for the bard: “Keep the cheerleading up, Bard. Keep morale high. That’s your job.”

Just in front of her, Soma casts Poison Spray. A puff of acrid green gas surrounds the ogre, dealing nine poison damage but not felling it. She deliberately avoids using Ice Knife amid her clustered companions. “Ice Knife would just kill you guys. And I’m not doing that one again. I’ve learned.”

Valphine remembers the last blast differently. She had found something compelling in the brush with death, though Soma emphatically had not enjoyed causing it.

With a surprising burst of energy, the ogre stands, its beady eyes scanning the faces of its attackers. It looks at Valphine, then at Brewbarry, its monstrous mind weighing its options, deciding who to strike next. A moment of chilling deliberation, and then its gaze settles on the towering barbarian. It picks the Uthgardt.

Valphine watches in a moment of suspended horror as the ogre’s crude but massive weapon lands on Brewbarry. A sickening thud, and for a moment the barbarian buckles, his enormous frame swaying. His Rage blunts the critical blow, and Stone’s Endurance absorbs the remaining damage, leaving him uninjured.

The failure of such a mighty blow catches both the Ogre and Valphine by surprise. Valphine, her own heart pounding, watches the creature stare in disbelief, its brutish face contorted in a mix of confusion and fear. And then, a truly astonishing sight: the ogre turns and runs, its powerful legs carrying it away from the fight, its previous aggression replaced by a primal instinct for self-preservation.

“That hit was supposed to kill Brewbarry,” someone observes.

Its flight gives Valphine and Brewbarry an opportunity to strike. Brewbarry lands a vicious blow as the creature turns to flee, his Rage lending still more force to the attack.

Valphine, her mind still reeling from the unexpected turn of events, swiftly puts a bolt in her hand crossbow. She aims at the fleeing creature and shoots, but the bolt misses its mark.

“Just let him go, man. Don’t chase him down,” someone protests.

“What is this mercy you have for the ogre that tried to kill us?” comes the answer.

Before Brewbarry strikes the killing blow, Vukradin offers his theory of restitution: “He will be carrying treasure that he stole from someone else, and we should rightfully return that to ourselves.”

“Brewbarry does the killing blow,” Valphine thinks, as she watches the ogre breathe its last. A quiet satisfaction settles over her, a sense of accomplishment despite the messy end to the encounter.

From the rear comes an indignant protest: “Senseless. He was running away.”

Another voice dryly asks, “What kind of blood money do we find on this ogre?”

Valphine looks over the body and finds a whistle, twelve gold pieces, and a key. The key has no obvious lock and is set aside with the other unexplained items.

The party’s moral discussion continues. The gold will supposedly be given to the poor—or perhaps merely ensure that the adventurers themselves will no longer be poor. Either way, their expedition resumes with no injuries, few resources spent, and the ogre dead behind them.

### 005.02 Soma 01-02-Tarsakh 1495

The rest of the journey to Gnomengarde is uneventful. Soma finds the river that leads to the gnomish hold. At the end of the river is a waterfall whose erosion of the rock has created a natural concavity. The roaring waterfall creates a cloud of mist as it plunges into a shallow pool of water, within which rise two small islands covered with two-foot-tall red, green, and purple mushrooms. Several cave openings overlook the pool from rocky ledges twenty to thirty feet above. The mountain blocks any sunlight from reaching this place.

Their purpose here is to acquire a device that might help defeat the dragon. If they find something valuable but unsuitable, Townmaster Harbin Wester may still pay them for bringing it back.

“Where to?”

And before she can answer, Vukradin is wading through the water towards the mushrooms. She follows him closely.

The mushrooms are two feet tall and grow atop small islands in the middle of the 3-foot pool. The islands are perpetually shrouded in mist from the waterfall.

Soma watches Vukradin as he examines them.

“These red ones are oily. Not sure what they are useful for. These Green ones remind me of a mushroom that can be turned into flour. We should forage as many as we can. And these purple mushrooms can be fermented to make mushroom wine.”

The green mushrooms have a grainy taste, something like wheat or wheat germ. They are edible and can be ground into flour. Although Vukradin briefly considers taking all of them, the islands hold far too many; he settles for two or three days’ worth of green mushroom rations.

Soma has a delighted expression on her face as she imagines what it tastes like, “I want some of those.”

Vukradin doesn’t understand her expression and assumes she is jesting and says, “Mushroom wine tastes as bad as it sounds.”

Soma shrugs. Strictly speaking, they do not yet have wine—only purple mushrooms that have been magically altered and can be crushed and fermented to produce it.

With the mushrooms in his bag, Vukradin shouts, “Let’s go find the secret treasure room behind the waterfall.”

Soma stares at him. He announces, “There is always a secret treasure room behind the waterfall.”

She follows him and stands under the waterfall, getting soaked. And behind the waterfall is nothing but a rock wall. Vukradin taps at the rock, touches the rock, and pokes at the rock, but it is \- just that \- rock. It is a perfectly ordinary rocky cliff face: no secret door, passage, tunnel, or treasure chest.

“Can we get out of here?” she asks.

Vukradin nods. He looks at the cliff wall next to the waterfall, and notices that there is an entrance 20 feet above.

He starts to climb up.

Soma shrugs, and follows him.

Valphine and Brewbarry stand and watch.

The druid shouts, “You coming?”

“No,” answers the Drow.

Someone calls for a rope, but no one seems eager to climb back down and retrieve one. Soma briefly considers using Thorn Whip before deciding on a more practical solution.

Rather than retrieve Valphine’s rope, Soma reshapes the earth into a rough staircase or ramp for everyone else. “I’m going to mold Earth to make like a ramp for the handicap for people who don’t want to climb up.”

Once up the 20’ cliff, Soma walks up stairs that lead 15 feet deeper into the mountain and open up into a rough chamber with a hallway to the north, east, and a slick rock wall to the south, atop which is a guard post.

The party considers its marching order. Brewbarry takes the lead, despite the warning that the person in front will be the first to notice—or fall into—anything dangerous.

A gnome shouts in Gnomish. Soma stares at him. Then another female voice shouts, “Who goes there?”

Vukradin answers, “Vukradin, the Bard\!”

The guards announce their orders: “Our orders are to attack shape changers on site. Are you a shape changer?”

“No.”

“Can you prove it?”

Soma grouses, “Would we take these shapes if we were shapechangers?”

The female gnome talks to the male gnome and then says, “That’s what a shapechanger would say\!”

Soma rolls her shoulders, and mutters, “I sense more senseless violence coming.”

But Vukradin isn’t done.

“Your standing orders are only to attack shape changers; your interpretation would lead to attacking anyone. Is that the intent of the orders?”

He patiently points out that whoever issued those orders would presumably be upset if the guards attacked someone who was not a shapechanger.

The male gnome looks at Vukradin, and the female gnome shouts, “Perhaps, but that’s what a shapechanger would say.”

When the guards notice the harvested mushrooms, matters nearly become worse. “You took the purple mushrooms that we used to invent. They must be—you must be shape changers.”

Brewbarry has had enough. “I am Brewbarry, killer of Ogres\! I am no shapeshifter\!”

He orders them to stand down and points to his survival of the ogre’s devastating blow as evidence of his identity.

The male gnome and the female gnome talk some more. The female gnome announces, “We’ve decided that he’s a Uthgardt Barbarian and not a shapeshifter.”

Brewbarry growls, “I used to be one.”

At that, the female gnome smiles, “Oh, that’s so much better. There used to be a standing order to attack Uthgardt on sight as a result of the events of the last few months. But since you used to be one, it’s unclear whether that standing order stands. Furthermore, at the last meeting, it was unclear if the ‘attack Uthgardtian barbarians’ order was renewed and so given that we have decided to let you pass.”

The guards explain that a committee met but failed to decide whether the previous standing orders should be reviewed. Faced with this unresolved procedural question, they elect to let the party through.

Soma stares in disbelief, “Bureaucracy saved us.”

### 005.03 Vukradin 01-02-Tarsakh 1495

“I want to learn more about our new friends. Tell me, friends, who are you? Do you like music? I am a bard.”

The male gnome shouts, “Pog,” and the female gnome shouts, “I am Ulla\! And yea, we like music. Mostly gnomish music.”

Vukradin tries a few notes, and the gnomes seem pleased. He does not actually know any Gnomish music, but hopes he can fake a few bars.

Pog waves his hand, as if to tell Vukradin to stop, and asks, “Why are you here?”

Vukradin explains that the mayor is looking for some kind of magical device that can help fight against the threat of the dragon.

Pog recognizes the title rather than the office. “The mayor? Oh, you mean Townmaster Harbin?”

Vukradin praises the gnomes’ craftsmanship and says he would love to see their inventions.

Ulla scratches her head and says, “You should go to the inventor workshop.” She tells Vukradin that the best way to get there is to cross the hanging bridge from where they entered the caves.

Vukradin thanks them and marches towards the inventor’s workshop. After crossing the bridge, he enters a room that is lightly obscured by the mist of the waterfall. But he notices, just in time, that the room contains two rapidly spinning devices that look like turnstiles fitted with stacks of long, sharp blades spaced 1 foot apart. The northern turnstile spins counterclockwise, while the southern one spins clockwise.

He stares in disbelief and marches back to the where Pog and Ulla were sitting.

“You told us to go this way. Like, it’s like a death trap.”

Ulla and Pog look at one another before admitting, “Oh, yes, you’re right. That was the wrong way.”

“That was a death trap\!”

Ulla is surprised. “I thought you had a mage who could cast Mage Hand. Well if you don’t, then you’ll have to go the long way through the northern hallway. Go north past the first passageway going east, past a large room, and then you’ll see another passageway going east out of the mountain. Go out from there. Then cross the river, and climb up the cliff wall, where you will enter the sleeping chambers of most of the gnomes. From there, there is a passageway going south that leads to the inventors’ workshop.”

When someone offers to destroy the bladed contraptions instead, the guards object. They had simply assumed that a visitor would use Mage Hand to disable them.

Ulla draws out the alternate route for them and warns them not to take the bridge. The party reassures her that another cliff will be no obstacle: Soma can shape another earthen path.

Vukradin thanks her and heads north.

The first large room is not empty. It has a rotating platform equipped with four heavy crossbows that reload automatically. The inventor is a deranged gnome, as they learn later, named Facktoré, who decides to test her device on Vukradin and his companions.

Facktoré sits in the contraption’s chair, babbling incoherently and excitedly pressing its firing controls. She is not merely testing in their vicinity; she is deliberately targeting them.

Soma steps in front of Brewbarry and withdraws into her shell, presenting the machine with a heavily armored target while also giving her companions cover. Facktoré fires twice. Both bolts strike Soma’s shell or glance harmlessly away. Soma calls back, “Guys, I want you around.”

Brewbarry closes on the machine and begins smashing its crossbows. Each solid blow mangles part of the mechanism, and his first assault destroys one of the mounted weapons and damages another.

Valphine invokes divine authority and commands Facktoré to flee. The command takes hold.

Although Vukradin is tempted to kill her, he decides that perhaps disarming the device is sufficient. Valphine and Brewbarry continue battering the contraption while Soma emerges from her shell and tries to help with her quarterstaff. Soma’s blow misses.

Facktoré squeals and runs down the passage. No one strikes at her as she flees. The party remains focused on the weapon, methodically smashing the remaining machinery until it can no longer fire.

With the bow disabled and Facktoré having fled, the party continues through the passage, crosses the river, and follows the alternate route toward the gnome domiciles.

Once he’s climbed the walls, he enters a large cave that is strewn with the remnants of old campfires. Four side caves serve as sleeping areas, with five small wooden cots crammed into each one.

Eight gnomes are sleeping soundly when the party arrives. Vukradin considers breaking out his flute, but his companions insist that waking them would be both rude and unwise.

“Do you know how important sleep is?” someone demands.

Vukradin concedes that they have a mission to complete. The heavily armed party passes through as quietly as it can, accompanied by the occasional “Clunk, clunk, clunk, clunk.”

To the south is the entrance to the laboratory of the gnomes.

### 005.04 Valphine 01-02-Tarsakh 1495

The Drow cleric stares at the two gnomes arguing. She is familiar with these debates among inventors, arguing over details that don’t matter.

Their names are Fibblestib and Dabbledob, and they are already deep in an argument conducted in Gnomish when the party arrives. Vukradin interrupts with, “Hi. We’re here to fight the dragon.”

Vukradin explains that they are there to look for help to fight a dragon, and the inventors brush him off.

“That’s unimportant compared to the calamity facing our community. My name is Fibblestib, and this is my coworker, Dabbledob. We are Gnomengarde’s foremost inventors, and we are entirely focused on a cure for King Korboz’s madness.”

Dabbledob looks at him and grouses, “If you think your proposal will do anything you’re a fool. There is no evidence that any such ray would work.”

She turns to Valphine, “A sanity ray\!”

Fibblestib is offended and immediately starts shouting, “You want to build a straitjacket instead. A jacket with no holes for the hands\! That’s not a straitjacket, that’s a shirt with no holes for the hands.”

Someone points out, “Jackets are usually curved to shape the body.”

Before Valphine can interrupt them, Dabbledob and Fibblestib argue some more amongst themselves.

The party asks whether the crazed gnome they encountered was King Korboz. The inventors explain that she was Facktoré, who has always been “a bit crazy.” They are relieved that the party did not kill her, though Fibblestib seems unsurprised that her crossbow machine has finally been destroyed.

Soma points to the marks left by the bolts. “There’s some chinks on my shell. Look where it struck me twice.”

Valphine looks at them, “I am a cleric\!”

She suggests Lesser Restoration or Remove Curse.

Both gnomes stare at her aghast, “We are gnomes, we invent our way out of illness.”

They regard magical healing almost as an absurdity. “Next thing you’re going to do is propose some kind of medicine.”

Soma tries to explain the value of cleric spells, but the gnomes just keep shouting louder. Someone offers a compromise: medicine itself could be invented.

Valphine turns to Vukradin, “I don’t think I could cure him, and I also don’t think we have the money.”

Vukradin counts the money in his head and sighs, “Yes.”

Even combining the twelve pieces of “blood money” with their other funds would not easily secure the necessary magic.

Fibblestib turns to Valphine, “I think we should walk you through what happened. It will allow us to revisit all of the salient facts. And please interrupt us with any questions\! You may see something that we missed.”

And so the two inventors explain that two gnomes, Warryn and Orryn, went missing and that King Korboz claims it was a shape-changing creature. There were once twenty-two gnomes living in the caves; after the two disappearances, Korboz became convinced that a shapeshifter was responsible.

This explains the guards’ orders, although the inventors remain fixated on the supposedly more important question of how to cure the king.

Valphine hears what they have to say, and Brewbarry says, “We talk\! We cure\!”

Someone remarks, “It’s how we did it in the Uthgardt tribes.”

The exchange briefly makes Valphine and Brewbarry look as though they share an unusually harsh approach. Their companions can barely tell which one is proposing the solution; the Drow and the Uthgardt seem to be on the same wavelength.

Then Vukradin explains that he would like to try music.

Dabbledob sighs, “Music has helped in some circumstances.”

Vukradin offers to play for the king.

Vukradin asks if they have a piano, and Fibblestib regretfully says no, but offers to build one. “It won’t be a normal piano\! But it will play music.”

The inventors promise something full of gadgets and strange noises. Vukradin insists that they should build him a piano, and they agree to consider it if the party helps King Korboz.

Valphine remains focused on the original mission. “How’s this going to kill a dragon?”

### 005.05 Vukradin 01-02-Tarsakh 1495

“Let’s go talk to the king.”

Vukradin is not interested in arriving at any new death traps, and asks for the safest way to the throne room.

“How many death traps are there on the way to the king? Can you give us the death trap-free route to the king?”

Vukradin explains what happened with the rotating blades and the rotating crossbow assault system.

The inventors seem offended by the terminology. “What do you mean, death traps?”

“Well, we tried to come see you and almost got killed twice. There was the crossbows, there was the spinning blades.”

“Oh, you’re saying you’re referring to our experimental systems as death traps.”

Fibblestib laughs.

The gnome inventor adds, “My invention, the rotating blades, is the better option. And you have proven it.”

Asked whether the blades were effective, the party answers, “Deadly.”

Vukradin notices how Dabbledob rolls her eyes. The crossbow platform, they admit, was considered problematic because it was too stationary and did not sufficiently protect its operator. This sparks another argument over the merits of spinning blades, levers, and the inventors’ competing designs.

Eventually Fibblestib provides a route to the kings that avoids further experimental systems.

A few moments later, Vukradin is at the door to the King’s room. The door is locked, and Brewbarry tries the key taken from the ogre, but it does not fit.

The King is in a state of hysterics, screaming. Another voice, his co-King Gnerkli shouts, “Who is that?”

“Vukradin\!”

King Korboz shouts, “A shapeshifter\!”

Vukradin denies it, but Korboz answers, “How would I know that you’re not a shape changer? That’s exactly what a shape changer would say.”

King Gnerkli tries to calm his co-king, who refuses to leave the room while a shapeshifter remains loose in Gnomengarde.

The co-King sighs, “I am King Gnerkli. Are you adventurers?”

“We are on a diplomatic mission from Townmaster Harbin Wester to acquire a device to fight the dragon with.”

King Gnerkli sighs, “You sound like adventurers. How about this, kill the shapeshifter, and I will give you a device.”

Vukradin mutters, “I am a musician.”

And he laughs when Soma adds, “More of a tour guide.”

King Gnerkli sighs again, “Look, there is some kind of creature that is terrorizing our cave system. Can you deal with it or not?”

Gnerkli insists that dealing with mimics is precisely the sort of work adventurers perform. He cannot undertake the search himself because he is occupied with the increasingly hysterical King Korboz.

Valphine proposes a comprehensive solution: “If we kill everyone, then the shape changer will definitely be dead.”

Korboz responds by becoming still more hysterical.

Vukradin groans, “Where are we supposed to find this thing?”

“Well, it’s not bipedal. So if I had to guess, it would hide in a place with a lot of big things it could shape shift into.”

The King pauses, “The cellar. There are a lot of barrels.”

The party considers the possibilities. The creature might be a door, chest, bed, barrel, bridge, or almost any other apparently inanimate object. One proposal is to shoot every such object with an arrow until one reacts.

Vukradin asks, “And what treasure will you give us?”

King Gnerkli promises “a magical item from us that will be of excellent use against the dragon.”

Vukradin remains skeptical. “Well, what is it?”

“A hat of wizardry\!”

Vukradin shouts, “Not exactly going to terrorize a dragon with that.”

The hat can only be properly used by a wizard, and the party has none. Its principal appeal is that it looks stylish and can be surrendered to Townmaster Harbin for payment.

King Gnerkli shouts, “I don’t have much else.”

Soma then reminds Vukradin that they get the money for bringing something, not necessarily for bringing something helpful.

Vukradin ponders the distasteful nature of such a deal, counts the 50 gold in his head, and sighs, “deal.”

The bard and his friends go through the room with the spinning blades, this time disabling the blades, then cross the bridge, and arrive where Pog and Ulla are sitting.

They inspect the dormant machinery to ensure that none of the blades is the mimic. The examination confirms that they are, in fact, blades. As someone observes, “Whereas before you had a minor doubt that they were blades, now you have none.”

The bridge also permits them to cross without trying to eat anyone.

### 005.06 Valphine 01-02-Tarsakh 1495

Valphine, who knows about more dangerous shapeshifters in the Dark Realms, stares at the two gnomes, convinced they must be the shapeshifters.

The party studies Pog and Ulla carefully enough to make both guards uncomfortable.

Ulla looks disturbed and shouts, “We are real gnomes\!”

Valphine shouts, “That’s what a shapeshifter would say\!”

“How the turntables have turntables,” someone observes.

Ulla turns on Pog, “I noticed how he looked a little bit different the past few days\!”

Pog turns on Ulla, “Me? You’ve looked different.”

The two continue to argue in front of the Drow, who smiles. They begin citing obscure changes in one another’s habits and behavior as proof of possible replacement.

Their behavior is persuasive in its own way. They may be irritating and paranoid, but they are unmistakably gnomes.

From there, she follows the passage north, and instead of going to the room where they found the rotating crossbow, she takes a right into a room full of barrels.

This room contains twelve forty-gallon barrels set into wide alcoves. A wooden brace secures each barrel. The barrels in the south alcove have been tapped with wooden spigots. Two of those barrels are half full, and two are nearly empty.

The party discusses how to test the barrels without draining or destroying them. Each seems sturdy enough to survive an ordinary crossbow bolt, especially if they aim high. They decide to inspect the barrels by shooting them one at a time, starting on the right.

She steps into the room and points a crossbow at the first barrel.

The bolt strikes with exceptional accuracy, wounding the disguised creature before it can attack.

And the barrel immediately transforms into a mimic, its pseudo-pods reaching out to strike, and its massive mouth full of razor-sharp teeth opens.

“We found it\!” shouts Valphine.

“Found it,” someone confirms.

Brewbarry charges at the creature, striking with his Halberd. The beast is wounded, but the weapon is stuck in the creature’s body. Its adhesive flesh grips the halberd tightly, and Brewbarry cannot pull it free.

Valphine sees Soma prepare to cast Ice Knife and makes sure she is out of range of the clumsy druid’s spell. This time none of Soma’s companions are close enough to become collateral damage.

The shard strikes, then explodes. The mimic fails to evade the icy blast and is badly wounded.

The mimic advances on Brewbarry, attacks with a pseudopod that misses the barbarian entirely. With his halberd still stuck in the creature, Brewbarry cannot use it to exploit the mimic’s movement.

The bard unleashes invectives, and at first, Valphine is confused but sees the underlying enchantment that causes the mimic to flinch visibly. Vukradin’s mockery wounds it and leaves its next attack impaired.

She smiles and repeats a rhyme she remembers from her youth, “Sticks and stones may break my bones, and words can also hurt me.”

The mimic remains alive, Brewbarry’s halberd remains embedded in its adhesive body, and the battle is still underway when the moment ends.
