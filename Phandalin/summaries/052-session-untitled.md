# Chapter 52: Untitled

Date: 2026-08-18

## Summary
At the break of dawn, the party arrived at the Spire of the Morninglord in Neverwinter, finding the temple’s threshold already crowded with a diverse and fractious gathering. The stir had been building since Vukradin and Valphine’s impromptu performance at the Moonstone Mask, where the invitation to a dawn service was first extended - the band still had no name, and Vukradin meant to reveal it tonight; Valphine had been invited to deliver the morning sermon herself (“Sermon first. Followed by music and sacraments.” — “It’s a Unitarian service,” Soma observed). Skeptical Lathanderite clerics eyed the group with suspicion, agents of Loviatar lurked in the shadows, and Lord Neverember himself stood among the faithful and curious. Nobody, the party noticed, was armed. Adding to the spectacle, the armorsmith Ostren Vayle appeared at the doors, having worked through the entire night to finish Valphine’s gleaming white dragon scale armor. One of the devout, hoping for a blessing rather than payment, he fitted it onto her right there at the threshold, causing an immediate stir among the onlookers before the party had even stepped inside.

Valphine entered the temple with her eyes blazing with divine radiance and ominous phantom whispers trailing in her wake (Brewbarry, eyes lit by his own Thaumaturgy, touched her shoulder as they crossed the threshold “just for theatrics”), then raised her hand and unleashed a Daylight spell to announce the start of the morning service, causing half the congregation to squint and shield their eyes. “Hey, it wasn’t Sunbeam,” Vukradin noted. Before the sermon could begin, Brother Aldric pulled the party aside into a private receiving chamber — he had heard of the blessed temple in Phandalin and wanted to know what the “blessing” actually meant — where he and a scarred sun priest attempted to theologically trap Valphine by questioning her emphasis on pain and suffering. After a stumbling first answer, and with Brewbarry looming beside her, she deftly threaded the trap, arguing that healing is painful and that to embrace the fullness of Lathander’s light, one must experience its full intensity — “Otherwise, it’s just a pale shadow.” Aldric, ultimately persuaded by a natural 20, smiled, made the sign of the Morninglord, and welcomed her to the pulpit, though not without a warning: “I warn you to stay away from this path of pain. This is not the Lathander’s way.”

What followed was a sermon unlike anything the Spire had ever witnessed. Valphine declared Lathander not a god of quiet comfort, but a blinding, unyielding force that destroys darkness without negotiation. She introduced the sacred sacrament of blinding light, urging the faithful to cast off all illusions and stare unflinchingly into pure divinity until every shadow within the soul was utterly incinerated, and closed with “Through light alone we dominate. Through light alone we conquer. Now, behold, Lathander’s death.” When a Loviatarite agent rose to denounce her, accusing her of dressing their goddess’s sacrament of pain up as holy, Valphine responded by casting Blindness — the man failed his DC 15 Constitution save on a natural 1 — and calmly insisting she had simply given him the blessing. As the blinded man was cast out, a second Loviatarite warned that there would be consequences, but the congregation met them both with contempt, and Valphine’s message carried the room.

With the sermon concluded, Vukradin and Valphine took to performing for the congregation. Vukradin’s opening was a disaster (a natural 1 that left the crowd uncertain, with someone humming a different hymn), but Valphine’s 17, sung in Undercommon, gradually warmed the crowd into swaying and cheering. Brewbarry moved through the pews in his bathrobe, collecting donations with an enthusiasm that bordered on coercion, pausing to encourage similarly dressed attendees to purchase proper bathrobes (“it’s soft and comfy. That’s what true barbarians do”). Soma, meanwhile, was approached by a syncretic supplicant who called her the protector of the Glades and asked for a blessing on her garden, and Soma obliged with a druidic invocation using the Meril’s Staff, faking her way through the religious rites convincingly enough to satisfy the woman. When Cullen Sharpe of House Margaster appeared to compliment the band’s new name, Lathander’s Death, and hovered his coins over the basket only to smile instead of donating, Brewbarry used a magically amplified voice to ask why he wasn’t donating — Valphine sharpened it to “do you fear the light?” — then physically wrestled the man’s entire coin pouch away from him and dropped it into the donation basket. Cullen, surrounded by the congregation and unable to protest, was forced to smile and call it a generous donation, then assured Vukradin that “we want the same things.” Brewbarry went on to shake down a pair of gossiping congregants who had been whispering that Lathander himself had walked the surface to mark Valphine personally. Lord Neverember later approached Valphine privately, expressing deep admiration for her focus on eliminating enemies of the light and suggesting that the two of them could work wonders together, before walking away muttering about power and the need to exercise it. Aldus nervously confirmed that dinner would still happen.

The gathering also brought the long-awaited resolution of Perrin’s claim to the Alagondar family necklace. He arrived with three witnesses: a passionate revolutionary who was genuinely thrilled at the symbol of Neverwinter’s rightful rulers, a genealogist who had cross-referenced his research with Boney’s study of family corpses and would stake his professional reputation that Perrin was “the true and only claimant,” and a frightened notary clerk who revealed that many Alagondar records had been purged when Lord Neverember became the first Lord of Neverwinter. Brewbarry was convinced the party was being manipulated (“mock my words, this is gonna bite us in the ass”), Valphine suspected Perrin was controlled rather than evil and floated “an alternative protection racket,” and Soma’s investigation found the notary was not, in fact, afraid of Perrin. Vukradin chose to honor the principle of returning what was rightfully owned. He handed over the necklace in the Temple of Lathander and then refused to speak to Perrin further (“get out of my sight. I don’t want to talk to you”); Brewbarry supplied the solemn note, reminding Perrin where the handover had taken place and urging him to use it for good, and Soma called it the start of his redemption arc. “We did the right thing, right guys?” Soma asked. “No,” said Brewbarry. “There was no right thing,” said the DM. “It was just a thing.” By the end of the service, Valphine had established a small but devoted branch of Lathander’s Searing Light in Neverwinter, with eight new followers willing to stare into the sun in her name — “rookie numbers,” per Vukradin.

Afterward, Brewbarry sought out Cullen Sharpe — alone, since Vukradin wanted “nothing to do with Cullen” — to collect the name of the gnome technician connected to the shipping manifold failure. Cullen provided the name Bimble Nackle, describing him as a fastidious gnome who had worked on the machinery, but denied any knowledge of his current whereabouts or employer, insisting House Margaster had played no role in his hiring and had only heard the name after the machinery stopped working. Brewbarry, increasingly paranoid, began designing a surveillance package for Cullen’s returned coin pouch: a sending stone disguised as a coin paired with a Magic Mouth coin. The party then made their way to the Counting House, where a visibly nervous Aurelan Vance explained that the Commission — a secretive body with Lord Neverember as its majority holder — had publicly declared the manifold failure a maintenance issue and claimed their hired technician found nothing, without ever revealing his name (“For all we know, he never did exist”). Brewbarry quickly identified a troubling contradiction: House Margaster was not a member of the Commission, yet stood to benefit most from the trade shutdown, suggesting a hidden connection that had yet to be uncovered. When Valphine casually suggested consulting the Assassin’s Guild as the most natural way to locate a missing person, Aurelan Vance turned pale (“He jests,” Vukradin assured him. “As you know, she’s a cleric of Lathander.”), and Brewbarry smoothed things over by cementing a reciprocal favor-based relationship with the banker, confident that the threads of the conspiracy would eventually lead somewhere they could pull. Next session: Soma and her turtle soup.

### The Sermon of Searing Light
#### Valphine delivers a powerful and aggressive sermon to the gathered faithful of Lathander, introducing a radical new theology centered on the sacrament of blinding light.
- Before the sermon, Valphine spots Perrin — “the guy... claims that we owe him the thing” — sitting in the congregation; the party had told him to be there for the Alagondar handover.
- Valphine addresses the packed congregation, describing Lathander as a fierce, unyielding force of blinding radiance that destroys darkness rather than negotiating with it: “Lathander is not a soft god of quiet comfort or gentle reprieve. He is the blinding radiance that cuts through the stagnant dark and shatters the slumber of the world. Darkness does not yield to meekness, nor does the night retreat before trembling hands. Light is an absolute force, unyielding, fierce, and searing.”
- “When the first rays of morning pierce the sky, they do not negotiate with the shadows. They destroy them, exposing every hidden corruption and sweeping away the decay of the night with uncompromising glory.”
- The sermon introduces the ‘sacrament of blinding light,’ urging the faithful to stare into pure divinity until every shadow within the soul is utterly incinerated: “In this holy rite, the faithful cast off all comforting illusions and look unflinchingly into the incandescent glare of pure divinity, until the eye beholds only burning gold, and every shadow within the soul is utterly incinerated. It is in a baptism of fire and brilliance that hardens the spirit into a conduit of divine radiance.”
- “Through this sacrament, we are remade not as passive observers, but as living blazes. Forged to carry the intolerable heat of Lathander’s glory into every dark corner of this realm.”
- Valphine calls for the purging of the wicked and the destruction of those who harbor darkness, framing it as a holy duty: “We do not offer pity to those who cling to the dark, or quarter for the meek and weak, for to harbor shadow is to invite destruction.”
- The closing doubles as the band announcement: “Rise, warriors of the morning. Lift high your glowing crests. And let the untamed power of Lathander incinerate every enemy of the dawn. Through light alone we dominate. Through light alone we conquer. Now, behold, Lathander’s death.”
- Table reaction: “Well done.” (Vukradin) “That was extremely well done.” (DM) “I think I’m changing religions.” (Soma) Vukradin then takes the sermon’s last line as the band name in front of the whole congregation - “We are Lathander’s death” - and the DM accepts it on the spot: “Right. - Valphine responds by magically blinding the heckler with holy light, claiming it is a blessing rather than a curse: “DC-15 Constitution save... - The party litigates the theft charge: Vukradin — “I don’t think staring at the sun is a sacrament of Loviatar.” DM — “Inflicting pain, though, is…” Valphine — “The goal is the light, the pain is just a nice benefit, side benefit.” Brewbarry — “you call it blinding, I call it blessing. He blessed him with the holy light. You saw the light.” Soma — “It’s an alternate way of taking the sacrament.”

DM: “your speech was eloquent, which is why, as they leave, you know, people just sort of stare at them with, like, contempt, so they... didn’t make their point, but... ## Locations
### Spire of the Morninglord
A grand temple dedicated to Lathander in Neverwinter, featuring high windows that flood the interior with light and a large hall for congregations. It has a private receiving chamber where Brother Aldric questions visiting clerics before they preach, and clerics gather at its doors at dawn. It served as the stage for Valphine’s controversial sermon, the band Lathander’s Death’s performance, and the return of the Alagondar necklace. Outside, a colonnade gives cover for quiet conversation as the city wakes. Valphine’s Daylight spell “lights up the entire chamber” — the DM notes a Daylight inside the temple is not something the faithful see often.

### Counting House
The financial institution of Neverwinter where Aurelan Vance manages wealth and records. Its resources, by Vance’s own admission, are “more in the financial management rather than the investigatory.” Brewbarry visited to seek information on the Commission and the trade manifold failure, and established a favor-based relationship with the banker.

### Neverwinter
A major city where the party is currently operating, establishing religious influence through a new branch of Lathander’s Searing Light and navigating the complex financial and political landscape of its trade houses and commissions. By the end of the session the city is “waking to the news” of the new band and of Lord Neverember’s private conversation with Valphine. Per the notary clerk’s testimony, the Alagondars were removed from power here in a purge when Lord Neverember became the first Lord of Neverwinter (canon records the lineage killed in the Cataclysm of 1451 DR, and Neverember ruling as Lord Protector in the absence of an heir); the Commission manages trade and revenue on his behalf. The Moonstone Mask, site of Vukradin and Valphine’s impromptu performance, is where the stir that filled the Spire began.

## NPCs
### Brother Aldric
A high-ranking cleric of Lathander at the Spire who is concerned about Valphine’s unorthodox theological leanings regarding pain. He has heard of the temple in Phandalin and asks pointedly what the “blessing” means. He is unimpressed by her first answer, then accepts her theological points on a second attempt, warning her nonetheless to “stay away from this path of pain. This is not the Lathander’s way.” When Valphine threads the trap laid by his scarred colleague, he smiles and makes the sign of the Morninglord.

### The Scarred Sun Priest
A visibly scarred priest of Lathander in Aldric’s receiving chamber, who interrupts to ask “is pain the point or the healing?” and presses on the purpose of healing. The DM confirms afterward that he “was there to trap you.”

### Ostren Vayle
The owner of ‘Dragon Scales R Us’ who worked through the night to finish Valphine’s dragon scale armor and personally delivered and fitted it at the temple doors, seeking a blessing from Lathander as payment. Described as “one of the devout” and “very proud of himself.” Valphine visibly blessed him in front of the crowd after the service.

### Loviatarite Agents
Followers of the goddess of pain who lurked in the shadows of the Spire and attempted to denounce Valphine’s sermon as heretical theft of their own rituals — “The drow has dressed our sacramite in sacrament and called it Holy.” One was blinded by Valphine’s magic after failing a DC 15 Constitution save on a natural 1, and another warned “There will be consequences!” before they were cast out to looks of contempt from the congregation.

### Lord Neverember
The Lord Protector of Neverwinter who attended the morning service and privately approached Valphine to express admiration for her aggressive theological direction focused on eliminating enemies of the light, suggesting a future partnership. He was tight-lipped about the dinner invitation, appeared “particularly appalled with Cullen Sharpe,” and stipulated that any help must “keep in mind the need to circulate the wealth and not hoard it.” Aurelan Vance identifies him as the Commission’s majority holder. He walked off mumbling about power and the need to exercise it.

### Aldus
An associate of Lord Neverember who nervously accompanied him to the Spire and confirmed the party’s upcoming dinner invitation with the Lord: “have no worries, have no fears. Dinner... is going to happen. Lord Neverember is very busy and preoccupied.”

### Cullen Sharpe
A representative of House Margaster who attended the sermon wearing a green suit — the man Vukradin remembers calling off his own minder before it came to violence. He complimented the band’s name, remarked that “House Margaster always delivers the goods,” tried to pay his tithe with a smile, and was intimidated by Brewbarry into donating his entire coin pouch to the church. He insisted “we want the same things” as Vukradin, offering to help “return things to people.” Later he provided the name of the gnome technician Bimble Nackle and said he knew neither Bimble’s location nor who employed him — the DM confirmed he was not lying about that. He separately denied that House Margaster was involved in Bimble’s hiring: “We just heard of his name after the machinery stopped working.” He was disappointed Vukradin did not come to that meeting.

### Perrin
A claimant to the Alagondar lineage who brought three witnesses to the Spire to verify his claim. He squirmed whenever Brewbarry did “his terrifying thing” but sat waiting patiently with his witnesses. He successfully recovers the powerful Alagondar family necklace from the party after his lineage is confirmed, thanks them greatly, and is told by Vukradin to get out of his sight. Whether he is evil or merely controlled by evil people was left unresolved at the table.

### Perrin’s Witnesses
Three men vouching for Perrin: an enthusiastic revolutionary who sees the necklace as “a great symbol of the rightful rulers of Neverwinter”; a genealogist (self-corrected from “scientist” to “historian” under Brewbarry’s questioning) who had cross-referenced his research with Boney and would stake his professional reputation on Perrin being “the true and only claimant”; and an elderly, visibly frightened notary clerk who remembers the Alagondar family records from before the purge. Soma’s investigation established the clerk is not afraid of Perrin.

### Bimble Nackle
A fastidious gnome technician named by Cullen Sharpe as having worked on the machinery of the shipping manifold before its failure. His current location and employer are unknown, and the Commission never publicly identified him; Aurelan Vance allows that “for all we know, he never did exist.” Valphine joked that the short name marks him as low-class, then admitted she made that up.

### Boney
An associate of the party who provided genealogical insights by studying corpses, helping to verify Perrin’s Alagondar lineage claim in collaboration with the genealogist witness, who said Boney’s own interest in Alagondar genealogy was “almost as encyclopedic” as his. Soma: “Yeah, he had a lot of free time.”

### Aurelan Vance
A representative of the Counting House who provides financial and political context on the Commission and trade houses. His face registers “oh, shit” pain when Brewbarry walks in before he manages a professional smile. He is visibly intimidated by the party’s methods, turns pale at the idea of approaching the Commission, and is unsettled by Valphine’s suggestion of consulting the Assassin’s Guild, but agrees to a relationship based on mutual favors with Brewbarry. He mentions Linene as the party’s wealth management consultant.

### The Garden Supplicant
A syncretic worshipper at the Spire — “there’s plenty of gods, why worship only one?” — who recognized Soma as “the protector of the Glades” and a follower of Mielikki and asked for a blessing so her garden would grow “beautiful and lush.” Satisfied by Soma’s improvised rite.

### The Exile Aesthetic Merchant
A merchant who approached Soma after the service to “License your look. Your turtle look” as “the SOBA line,” prompting talk of merging production with Brewbarry’s bathrobes.

## Spells
### Thaumaturgy
Used at the Spire’s entrance to create ominous phantom whispers and to make eyes glow with divine light for theatrical effect — Brewbarry cast it on himself (“my eyes light up with light. As I touched Valphine’s shoulder”), sending “ominous Whispers” from the door “just for theatrics,” and reprised the effect in Aldric’s receiving chamber to back Valphine’s persuasion. Later used by Brewbarry to boom his voice across the temple hall to intimidate Cullen Sharpe into making a donation.

### Daylight
Cast by Valphine with her hand raised high to announce the start of the sermon — “it is time for the morning sermon” — filling the entire temple chamber with an intense, blinding radiance that far exceeded the standard Light spell used for the purpose and caused some in the crowd to squint. Brother Aldric noted she “weren’t supposed to do that”; Vukradin noted it wasn’t Sunbeam.

### Blindness
Cast by Valphine on a heckling Loviatarite agent who denounced her sermon, physically manifesting the ‘blessing’ of Lathander’s light by robbing the man of his sight for one minute. DC 15 Constitution save; the DM rolled a natural 1. Valphine framed it as a sacred blessing rather than an attack; Vukradin objected that she was “burning your spells.”

### Bless
Active on Valphine during the receiving-chamber exchange and the shakedown, adding a D4 to her persuasion rolls and to Brewbarry’s intimidation (“2 for my Bless”). A one-off table ruling, not a standing house rule - RAW Bless applies to attacks and saves, not checks.

### Magic Mouth
A spell the party plans to cast on a coin to trigger and repeat the last five minutes of recorded conversation, intended to work in tandem with a sending stone disguised as a coin for surveillance of Cullen Sharpe. Vukradin observed that the party lacks the levels for the disguise; Brewbarry intends to buy the enchantment.
