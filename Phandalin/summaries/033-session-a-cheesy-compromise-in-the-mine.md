# Chapter 33: A Cheesy Compromise in the Mine

Date: 2026-02-06

## Summary

The battle outside the mine continued as the party faced the surviving attackers. Orcs 8 and 1 maneuvered around Soma to attack Valphine, but both missed. Soma wounded Orc 8 with an attack of opportunity before Valphine’s spirit guardians overwhelmed both attackers. When they failed their Wisdom saves, the orcs were consumed by the guardians’ divine power. Their deaths brought only a brief respite: more orcs and ogres emerged from the other side of the hill, prompting Vukradin’s startled, “What? I thought we were gonna wrap up this battle.” The shifting battlefield labels did not establish which tracked ogre was the survivor from the previous fight.

The new attackers encountered a battlefield shaped by fire, divine radiance, and furious melee. Vukradin attempted to strike an ogre with starry wisp but rolled a natural one; he then attempted to apply Bardic Inspiration to his own attack, but the table stopped at its ruling that a natural one was an automatic failure and did not establish whether self-use was eligible. Orc 3 charged Brewbarry, surviving his halberd’s attack of opportunity with only two hit points before landing a critical blow. Brewbarry’s rage reduced the damage from 18 to 9, and he answered, “You just fueled my rage, you fool.” Soma then rolled her flaming sphere into the wounded orc, killing it even after it succeeded on its Dexterity save, and continued tearing into the remaining attackers in bear form.

Valphine dashed directly into the reinforcements, placing several orcs inside the deadly radius of her spirit guardians. Brewbarry followed with his helmeted eyes flashing red and used Thaumaturgy to make his voice boom as he charged. His halberd killed Orc 2, cleaved through Orc 5, and then struck another orc after he turned toward it and declared, “You’re next.” The spectacle prompted the prediction that Brewbarry would become a legendary terror among the local orcs and ogres: “Brewbarry of the Red Eyes,” a name they might use to frighten their children.

The ogres were tougher, but the party steadily wore them down. Brewbarry used Bardic Inspiration to turn a miss into a hit while joking that he reached simultaneously for the inspiration and the booze beside his foot: “Embrace the power of AND.” Vukradin repeatedly frustrated the ogres with Silvery Barbs, turning successful strikes into failures or lesser blows. Soma battered Ogre 1 with her flaming sphere, claws, and bite; Valphine shot it through foliage despite partial cover; and Brewbarry finished it with thrown hand axes. Vukradin killed Ogre 2 with a spell. With the last ogre dead, he celebrated, “I love it when a plan comes together.”

After securing the mine, the party searched the fallen. They recovered a lightweight black porcelain orb stoppered with cork, sealed with wax, and labeled “Flame,” which proved to be alchemist’s fire; a pouch of gems valued at eight gold pieces, eight silver pieces, and five copper pieces; and a symbol of Talos worth 15 gold pieces. Talos was identified as the evil god of storms and destruction. The ogres belonged to a tribe the party had previously encountered, but their allegiance had shifted. They had once served the Carver, yet after the disastrous Borg the Hammer incident weakened the Carver’s position, the ogres apparently changed sides—or, as the party speculated, accepted a larger meal from someone else.

Zeleen Varnaster, the female wererat leader of the Whiskered Gang, reemerged after the fighting was over and enthusiastically praised the party’s convenient timing. Zeleen claimed that the miners had been under siege and that Don-Jon Raskin had died in “an unfortunate incident with a carrion crawler.” Although ostensibly mourning him, Zeleen called Don-Jon “a very annoying person” and quickly shifted to concerns about employment, profit shares, and renegotiation. The scar offered as evidence of recent fighting appeared old under Valphine’s scrutiny. When Valphine calmly asked, “Did you kill Don-Jon?” Zeleen’s evasiveness suggested that “kill is such a strong word” and that Don-Jon had at least been allowed or lured into fatal danger.

The party jokingly added resurrecting Don-Jon to its quest tracker, despite being told that his remains had been chewed, digested, and excreted and would require true resurrection. More immediately, the carrion crawler was still blocking mining operations. Vukradin persuaded Zeleen and the gang to surrender Don-Jon’s share of the mine to the party in exchange for removing the creature. Zeleen consulted the crew and accepted. The promised payment of Petunia’s cheese was then produced. Zeleen cut a piece, exclaimed over its quality, and revealed that cheese from Petunia—the area’s famous prize cow—was a local delicacy.

After a short rest, the party approached the barricaded portion of the mine where the carrion crawler had last been seen. Valphine noticed signs of battle and investigated. The evidence showed this was where Don-Jon had died, but not from a carrion crawler attack. He had fallen during a fight, and the crawler dragged away and consumed his corpse afterward. Further examination uncovered dwarf footprints leading away from the scene, pointing toward the people involved in the fatal confrontation.

Soma cast Speak with Animals and opened negotiations with the carrion crawler. It communicated through rapid, wet clicks while its tentacles lashed the air, calling Soma “green life” and itself “the eater.” It described the “deep stonefolk” as smelling of iron and sweat and the “false rats” as smelling of musk and bad blood. The dwarves and wererats fought, bodies fell, and the crawler waited for silence before feeding. Its “belly is heavy” from the feast, but Soma warned that the conflict was about to end and that the mine would soon offer constant noise, no peace, and no easy corpses. With a natural 20 on Persuasion, Soma convinced it to seek better hunting elsewhere. The crawler climbed over the party and departed without a fight.

Searching its lair, the party found magical Goggles of Night and a chain belonging to Don-Jon Raskin. The goggles granted darkvision and were potentially useful to Soma, who lacked ordinary darkvision despite reporting that she had blindsight, though the source of that sense was not established. Vukradin insisted that Don-Jon’s chain should eventually be returned to his next of kin.

Following the dwarf tracks deeper into the mine, the party encountered five dwarves led by Horia. Vukradin announced, “I’m the legal owner of this mine,” and presented the party’s deed. Horia countered with a contract signed by Don-Jon authorizing the dwarven clan to work the mine, explaining that they had operated there long before the wererats displaced them.

The truth about Don-Jon’s death then emerged. Don-Jon had originally been expected to bring help against the wererats. Instead, he returned boasting that he had found “a sucker in some bard fellow” who convinced the wererats to work for cheese. Don-Jon intended to use this extraordinarily cheap labor and discard the dwarves as unnecessary. The resulting argument became deadly. Horia also warned that the wererats did not know how to mine and would wreck the operation through unsafe practices. Another dwarf launched into technical concerns involving mine props, water levels, and structural safety, though the details meant little to the party.

Faced with competing workers and conflicting promises, the party proposed a compromise: the dwarves would perform the skilled mining, while the wererats supplied menial labor, cooking, cleaning, and security. Horia was willing to consider the arrangement if the wererats apologized for dwarves killed during their initial takeover, though the party insisted that both sides might owe apologies for their dead.

Valphine scrutinized the dwarves’ contract for loopholes and concluded that the clan was not trying to cheat the party. Her response—“What is the point of a contract if it’s not to cheat someone?”—deeply offended Horia. When Horia recognized her as a drow, Valphine retorted, “Dwarves. We’re underground, aren’t we?” and stormed off. Vukradin calmed the dispute, but the party then opened a new front by insisting that Don-Jon’s death required a new contract and renegotiation of the dwarves’ share. Horia claimed the original contract awarded 20 percent to the entire clan, citing clan overhead. Brewbarry rejected that figure with, “Who are you, Michael Jordan of the gold mine?” The session ended before the new percentage was settled or the wererats were persuaded to accept the proposed joint operation.

#### Soma uses Speak with Animals to communicate with the carrion crawler and persuade it to leave the mine.

- Soma spends a 1st-level spell slot to cast Speak with Animals.
- Vukradin proposes acting as the persuasive “brains” while Soma translates “Bard to Carrion,” though the actual argument must pass through Soma.
- The DM grants Soma advantage on the Persuasion check to reflect Vukradin’s help.
- The carrion crawler communicates in “a series of rapid wet clicks” while its tentacles lash the air.
- Its first words are: “Smell! You smell of green life. Too much pulse. Too much heat. Why does the green life speak to the eater?”
- Soma explains that the party is trying to understand the situation and suggests that the crawler might leave.
- The creature initially refuses: “Out? Why would I go out? Food! Food! Much food!”
- When Soma suggests that better food exists elsewhere, the crawler describes the mine’s conflict:
  - “Deep stonefolk! They smell of iron and sweat.”
  - “And the false rats, they smell of musk and bad blood.”
  - “They make much noise. Clack! Clack. Metal hits bone. Blood spills.”
  - “When they stop moving, moving, the feast begins. Hi, I’m just waiting for silence.”
- The crawler clarifies that some of the dwarves remain alive and continue fighting the wererats.
- It taps its heavy belly while explaining that bodies fall and it eats the casualties.
- Soma warns that the battle will end shortly and that the mine will then become continuously active: “You won’t get a moment’s peace.”
- Soma argues that once mining resumes there will be constant noise, no easy bodies, and eventually no food.
- With advantage, Soma rolls a natural 20 on Persuasion.
- The crawler mournfully reflects, “The false rats were tasty. And the stone… the dwarves were tasty. It was so tasty. But there are fewer of them.”
- It finally concedes, “Maybe I should go.”
- Soma assures it that it will find better hunting elsewhere and is being warned before it starves.
- The crawler climbs over the party and leaves through the mine without combat.
- The party searches its lair for undigested equipment and remains from its meals.
- They find magical Goggles of Night and a chain belonging to Don-Jon Raskin.
- Vukradin says Don-Jon’s chain should be returned to his next of kin.
- The Goggles of Night grant darkvision. Most of the party already possesses darkvision, but Soma realizes she does not; she reports that she has blindsight, though its source is not established.

#### The party follows the dwarf tracks and encounters five dwarves led by Horia, leading to revelations about Don-Jon’s death and competing claims to the mine.

- The party follows the dwarf footprints farther into the mine rather than immediately returning to the wererats.
- The party debates whether the dwarves might replace the wererats as workers, but notes that the wererats accept cheese while dwarves presumably expect actual payment.
- They encounter five dwarves led by Horia.
- Although the party initially discusses entering cautiously and respectfully, Vukradin effectively walks in as if he owns the place.
- When Horia asks who they are, Vukradin answers, “I’m the legal owner of this mine.”
- Vukradin presents the party’s deed.

- Horia delivers a formal objection, insisting that the clan signs proper, honest contracts and that the wererats attacked them and took their workplace.
- Vukradin persuades Horia to remain open to the compromise.
- The party then insists that Don-Jon’s death means the dwarven contract must be rewritten and the clan’s percentage renegotiated.
- Horia protests that the original agreement should remain intact.
- The party openly decides to renegotiate anyway, creating another dispute before the wererat question has even been settled.
- Brewbarry asks the dwarves to state their percentage so the party can negotiate it downward.
- Horia claims the original share was 20 percent for the entire clan and begins explaining clan overhead.
- Brewbarry rejects the number with, “Who are you, Michael Jordan of the gold mine?” The exact adjustment he proposes afterward is unclear.
- The exact renegotiated share remains unresolved.
- The party still must convince the wererats to apologize and accept a joint dwarven-wererat operation.

Don-Jon had contracted the dwarven clan to work the mine and was expected to help them remove the wererats. Instead, he boasted that he had found “a sucker in some bard fellow” who induced the wererats to work for cheese. He intended to dismiss the more expensive dwarves, provoking the fatal argument. His body was consumed so completely that restoring him would require true resurrection. His chain remained in the carrion crawler’s lair.

### Carrion Crawler

A tentacled creature that communicates through rapid wet clicks. It calls itself “the eater,” Soma “green life,” dwarves “deep stonefolk,” and wererats “false rats.” It fed opportunistically on casualties from the conflict between dwarves and wererats, waiting for the sound of clashing metal to cease before beginning its feast. Soma convinced it that the conflict would end, leaving only constant mining noise and diminishing food. After her natural 20 Persuasion roll, it peacefully departed to seek better hunting.

### Horia

A dwarf who leads the five-dwarf group from a clan with a longstanding connection to the mine. Horia possesses a contract signed by Don-Jon Raskin and insists that the clan conducts honest business. Horia explains the fatal dispute with Don-Jon, warns that the wererats lack the expertise to mine safely, and is willing to consider employing them for unskilled labor and security. Horia asks that the wererats apologize for dwarven deaths, though the party presses for apologies from both factions. Valphine’s cynical view of contracts deeply offends Horia, who questions whether she is truly a cleric of Lathander. Horia claims the clan’s original contractual share was 20 percent.

### Wererats

The Whiskered Gang, a group of wererats who overran the mine and displaced its dwarven workers. They had fought the dwarves, suffered casualties, and apparently lured or abandoned Don-Jon Raskin to his death. The party previously arranged for them to work in exchange for Petunia’s cheese. According to Horia, they lack mining knowledge and will cause structural failures and safety violations if left in charge. The party now hopes to use them for menial labor and security under dwarven supervision.

## Locations

### The Mine

A contested mining operation occupied by the Whiskered Gang but historically worked by Horia’s dwarven clan. Its exterior was attacked by orcs and ogres, while its interior contains barricaded passages, evidence of fighting, unsafe or disputed workings, and a former carrion crawler lair. Don-Jon Raskin previously managed the operation and signed overlapping or conflicting arrangements with its workers. In Chapter 31, Vukradin believes the party’s deed establishes direct ownership. Retrospective note—revealed in Chapter 47: the Neverwinter Commission owns the mine, while the party holds shares. Its future operation now depends on whether the dwarves and wererats can be persuaded to cooperate.

### Carrion Crawler’s Lair

An area beyond the wererats’ barricades where the carrion crawler waited for casualties from battles between dwarves and wererats. It contained remnants of the creature’s meals, including magical Goggles of Night and Don-Jon Raskin’s chain. The crawler left peacefully after Soma persuaded it that the mine would soon become noisy and unprofitable hunting territory.

## Items

### Alchemist’s Fire

A lightweight black porcelain orb stoppered with cork, sealed with wax, and bearing a label reading “Flame.” Found on one of the defeated ogres.

### Pouch of Gems

A small pouch of gems valued at eight gold pieces, eight silver pieces, and five copper pieces. Found on one of the defeated ogres.

### Symbol of Talos

A religious symbol dedicated to Talos, identified as the evil god of storms and destruction. It is worth 15 gold pieces and was found on one of the defeated ogres, providing evidence of the tribe’s changed allegiance.

### Goggles of Night

Magical goggles that grant darkvision to a wearer who does not already possess it. Found in the carrion crawler’s lair among the remains of its meals. Soma may benefit from them because she lacks ordinary darkvision; she reports having blindsight, though its source is not established.

### Don-Jon Raskin’s Chain

A chain that belonged to the late Don-Jon Raskin. Found in the carrion crawler’s lair, confirming that the creature consumed his remains after his death. Vukradin believes it should be returned to Don-Jon’s next of kin.

### Petunia’s Cheese

Cheese from a prize cow named Petunia, famous and considered a delicacy in the area. It was promised to the wererats as compensation for their labor. Zeleen cut and tasted a piece, after which the entire gang became excited by its arrival.

### Mining Contract

A contract between Don-Jon Raskin and Horia’s dwarven clan for working the mine. Horia claims it grants the entire clan a 20 percent share, including clan overhead. Valphine’s scrutiny finds no apparent attempt to cheat the party, but Don-Jon’s death prompts the party to demand renegotiation.

### Mine Deed

A legal document that Vukradin believes proves the party’s ownership of the mine and presents to establish their authority before Horia. Retrospective note—revealed in Chapter 47: the Neverwinter Commission actually owns the mine, while the party holds shares. The deed does not erase the separate mining contract Horia produces.

## Spells

### Spirit Guardians

A powerful concentration spell cast by Valphine that creates spectral guardians around her. Affected creatures make a Wisdom saving throw, taking full damage on a failure and half damage on a success. It killed Orcs 8 and 1 when they approached Valphine, damaged several members of the second wave, killed additional wounded orcs, and injured Ogre 2. The party repeatedly treated Valphine as a mobile area-of-effect weapon and encouraged her to dash into groups of enemies.

### Starry Wisp

A spell attempted by Vukradin against an ogre. The attack failed completely when Vukradin rolled a natural one. He tried to apply Bardic Inspiration to his own attack after the failure, but the table ruled that a natural one remained an automatic miss before establishing whether self-use would otherwise be eligible.

### Flaming Sphere

A concentration spell used by Soma to create a rolling ball of fire that she maneuvered into enemies as a bonus action. Targets made Dexterity saving throws, taking full damage on a failure and half on a success. The sphere killed a badly wounded orc despite its successful save and repeatedly battered Ogre 1 while Soma attacked in bear form.

### Thaumaturgy

A cantrip used by Brewbarry to make his voice boom as he charged the orcs. Combined with his giant stature, helmet, and flashing red eyes, it reinforced the terrifying image of “Brewbarry of the Red Eyes.” Brewbarry considered using its tremor effect but decided the booming voice was more impressive.

### Bardic Inspiration

A magical ability that allows an ally to add a Bardic Inspiration die to a failed roll. Vukradin attempted to apply it to his own natural one, but the table stopped at its critical-failure ruling without establishing whether self-use was eligible. Brewbarry later used it to turn a missed opportunity attack against an ogre into a hit, and Valphine used it to overcome partial cover while firing her crossbow.

### Stone’s Endurance

A reaction ability belonging to Brewbarry. He considered using it against Orc 3’s critical hit, but he had already spent his reaction on an opportunity attack. His rage instead reduced the damage from 18 to 9.

### Silvery Barbs

A spell used by Vukradin as a reaction to force an enemy to reroll a successful attack and use the lower result. It turned Ogre 2’s hit against Valphine into a miss and later converted Ogre 1’s critical hit against Soma into a regular hit for 15 damage. Vukradin’s repeated interference led the DM to declare growing irritation with bards.

### Speak with Animals

A 1st-level spell cast by Soma to communicate with the carrion crawler. It translated the creature’s rapid wet clicks and allowed Soma to learn about the ongoing dwarf-wererat conflict. With Vukradin’s persuasive assistance represented by advantage, Soma rolled a natural 20 and convinced the crawler to leave the mine peacefully.
