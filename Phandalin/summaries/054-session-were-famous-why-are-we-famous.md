# Chapter 54: We’re Famous. Why Are We Famous?

Date: 2026-09-01

## Summary

Earlier that night, while waiting for the third-hour stakeout, the party remained at the Common Chord, once the dive that had thrown Vukradin out unpaid and now reputedly the hardest ticket in Neverwinter. A nearby patron complained—without realizing Vukradin was within earshot—that the venue had become unaffordable because of him. Vukradin responded by pitching his vision for an independent, low-cost music studio where people could regularly hear Lathander’s Death. He invoked every Faerûnian equivalent of crowdfunding he could think of, from sending stones to “Portal Pay,” and promised contributors recognition through annual program credits and tiered patron status.

The first drunk patron handed over ten gold and was delighted to learn that this placed him at the “Plus” level, though not yet “Premier.” Vukradin then worked the rest of the room, finding a handful of willing patrons: Wick, an enthusiastic supporter of the arts who did not much care whether Vukradin was really Vukradin; Sabbatha, who spent much of the conversation admiring Brewbarry’s imposing physique and dedication to his look; Old Hesp, a grizzled regular who remembered when Vukradin played to empty rooms; and Ondrelle Kass. One fan recognized the real Vukradin but had already completed his charitable giving for the year, assuring him, “biggest fan, biggest fan,” until Brewbarry’s physical size complicated the claim. Old Hesp lamented that the Common Chord had become too polished, too expensive, and too commercial under Petra, recalling when Brewbarry’s table had neither shiny leather nor a plaque and when the venue did not serve fancy food. Each contributor’s name was dutifully set aside for the studio’s program credits. The night raised 20 gp for the studio; Petra’s separate 500 gp likeness payment remained pending until the performance two days later.

While listening to the tavern crowd, the party also learned that the sea’s tides run through Neverwinter’s sewers. The city experiences two high tides and two low tides per day, giving the group only a few relatively comfortable hours underground around the 3:00 a.m. low tide. Soma noted that she could remain underwater indefinitely, but the same was not true for most of the party.

By the third hour of the night, the party had positioned themselves near a sewer grill at the edge of the city. Two carriers emerged and deposited nine crates, eight of them lacking an official Commission Harbor mark and therefore apparently smuggled into Neverwinter. Those eight were stacked for Lim’s collection, while the ninth was placed apart on a ledge. The carrier who set it there paused halfway through his work, looked down into the darkness as though expecting something, and then departed. What emerged was not a person: it was small, quick, cat-sized, built from brass and leather, and moved “with the unhurried competence of a thing following instructions.” It took the ninth crate and disappeared below.

The two carriers separated and deliberately behaved as if they did not know each other. Valphine used her intuition to identify the man who had watched the ledge as the more knowledgeable of the pair. The party then split: Vukradin, protected by his exceptional stealth and Boots of Elvenkind, followed the construct into the sewers, while Valphine, Soma, and Brewbarry followed the suspicious carrier. Vukradin and the others retained a pair of Sending Stones so the two groups could exchange updates.

Deep in the tunnels, Vukradin failed a Wisdom save against an area effect. He was not stripped of his memories; instead, the route he had been following suddenly seemed utterly boring and unworthy of further attention. After abandoning it, he wandered into an unfamiliar section of the sewer and encountered a group of hard-eyed men. Their leader demanded, “How the hell did you get over here?” and informed Vukradin that he had entered his territory. Vukradin answered, “Because I’m not over there,” then successfully presented himself as a hopelessly lost traveler trying to reach Lim’s Cafe. Mentioning Lim initially made the men suspicious, but one of them recognized him as the famous singer Vukradin, transforming the encounter into an impromptu autograph session.

Rsolk’s demeanor shifted completely after that recognition. He identified himself as the manager of the local tunnels and revealed that he knew Zeleen through the Mountain’s Toe Mine. He had doubted Zeleen’s claim to have a business relationship with Vukradin, but the bard confirmed it—while dryly observing that the mine had yet to produce very much. Rsolk then explained that trouble had begun about two months earlier along a floodline tunnel. His people had stopped using the area after several men were lost to an unfamiliar creature resembling a spider “but it had all the wrong colors.” Its webs were also wrong in a way that evoked the party’s previous encounter with such creatures. Vukradin offered the party’s services, seeing both a useful job and a source of “Fair Trade Gold” for the studio. Rsolk eagerly accepted and asked whether Vukradin could obtain tickets to the Common Chord. Vukradin promised only to ask, noting the party’s arm’s-length relationship with Petra. After autographs, the tunnel dwellers offered to escort him out so that “Mr. Vukradin” would not become lost again.

Meanwhile, the others followed the carrier to one of the few cook shops open around the fourth hour. He calmly ate breakfast alone, and Valphine’s intuition suggested that he was exactly what he appeared to be: a delivery man, albeit one who might know who hired him. Rather than risk spooking Lim’s connections, the party continued the surveillance. After eating, the man went to sleep in a high-efficiency, single-room-occupancy-style building in the Bluelake District.

Soma’s familiar rat searched the man’s room. Although it found no insignia or obvious affiliation, the room, clothing, and resident’s ability to eat at a cook shop at four in the morning suggested an income higher than an ordinary crate carrier should command. More importantly, the familiar discovered seven to ten days of nearly identical delivery chits. Each read, “9 crates, 3rd hour, 8 up, 1 down,” carried a client reference and a house mark but no name, and showed a weekly settlement. Every prior chit appeared settled, while the newest—the delivery the party had just witnessed—remained unpaid.

The building’s landlord, a man called Uncle Tom, greeted the armed and unusual group with a series of bewildered observations about Tortles and Drow. He told Soma, “I thought turtles carried their house on their shoulders, so they didn’t need a place to stay,” prompting Soma to explain that her shell merely protected her and was not a residence. He then asked Valphine whether Drow disliked sunlight; she replied, “Oh, I love the sunlight.” Despite insisting he ran an equal-opportunity establishment because “money’s money,” he grew nervous after properly noticing Valphine’s white armor and glowing eyes, Brewbarry’s halberd and physique, and Soma’s Staff of Meril. He offered the party a discount before they admitted that they only wanted information about his tenant.

From the landlord, the party learned the carrier’s name: Denvar. He was quiet, bothered no one, and was generally considered a good customer, but he owed rent that day. That made the unsettled delivery chit especially significant: Denvar needed to collect his latest payment before the end of the day. Before leaving, the landlord earnestly asked Soma what made a good Tortle room. Soma, declining the opportunity to mislead him, recommended a nice hearth, plenty of water, and proximity to a body of water.

After several hours, as Denvar woke, Soma recast *Find Familiar* and spent another slot to restore her rat. Vukradin rejoined the group and shared news of Rsolk and the strange spiders. Denvar then approached the stone exterior wall of a building, looked around to ensure discretion, pulled loose a stone, retrieved a bag, replaced the stone, and walked away smiling. The party concluded that it was a two-way dead drop. Following the building around revealed that it belonged to an open tavern called the House of a Thousand Faces.

Rather than enter completely blind, the group opened their side of the dead drop and used *Knock* on its inner closure. Soma’s rat passed through and found a windowless basement office containing a desk, papers, supplies, and the unmistakable symbol of the Harpers. The discovery established that the dead drop and office were Harper-run, although it did not establish who arranged or ultimately benefited from the ninth crate. The party reminded themselves that the Harpers in this campaign were not necessarily benevolent; the DM characterized the Order of the Gauntlet as the only genuinely good faction, now reformed after rejecting the political masters who had manipulated it.

The House of a Thousand Faces was strikingly luxurious for the otherwise lower-end Bluelake District. It held mirrors, mannequins dressed in antiquated fashions, lush couches, and a properly drawing fireplace, giving the entire establishment an elegant but subtly incongruous atmosphere. Brewbarry had heard of it during his poorer years in Neverwinter but had never been able to afford entry. Now, having become a man of means and reputation, he walked through the front door. The session ended with the party at the Harper establishment, carrying two promising leads: the mystery of the ninth crate and the spider-infested floodline tunnels controlled by Rsolk.

## NPCs

### Petra

The half-elf owner of the Common Chord who negotiated a 500-gold-piece, one-time likeness-rights deal with the party, covering past and future use and still tied to an upcoming performance two days away. She had previously booked another man to perform Vukradin’s songs under the band’s name while disclosing that he was not Vukradin. Old Hesp acknowledges Petra’s business skill but blames her for turning the former dive into an expensive, polished, highly commercial venue.

### Lord Neverember

The Lord of Neverwinter, who reportedly has a new Drow advisor. He has an upcoming dinner engagement with the party, although Aldus has not confirmed the exact time. Valphine and the others suspect the advisor could be one of her siblings.

### Wick

A Common Chord patron who enthusiastically supports Vukradin’s music studio project. He does not know Lathander’s Death and does not particularly believe that the fundraiser is the real Vukradin, but he does not care: he loves the idea of patronizing the arts and assumes that the studio will train musicians like a school.

### Sabbatha

A Common Chord patron who contributes to Vukradin’s studio fund. During the pitch, she repeatedly looks Brewbarry over and comments on how completely he has committed to the Brewbarry look, inspiring descriptions of him as “bulging” and “rippling.”

### Old Hesp

A long-time local who recognizes Vukradin and remembers when he performed for empty rooms, promising refunds to anyone who did not rate the show ten out of ten. He dislikes the expensive, fashionable, commercial atmosphere of the modern Common Chord and remembers when Brewbarry’s table lacked leather, polish, and a plaque. He supports Vukradin’s promise to recover the venue’s old gritty spirit through a new studio.

### Ondrelle Kass

One of the named locals at the Common Chord persuaded to support Vukradin’s music studio fund. Their name is added to the future program credits alongside Wick, Sabbatha, and Old Hesp.

### Lim

A Tortle chef who runs the Board Laid Bare restaurant and is the intended recipient of eight of the nine smuggled crates. She is an old friend of Soma and reputedly cooks better than anyone Brewbarry has eaten from. Her prices remained stable while costs elsewhere in the city doubled because her supplier was never on the manifold. Lim asked the party not to frighten or disrupt her connections while investigating the anomalous ninth crate.

### Denvar

A delivery man involved in a recurring smuggling route through the sewers. He and another carrier deposit nine crates during the third hour, eight observed without official Commission Harbor marks, with Denvar placing the ninth separately and looking down into the darkness for its collector. He behaves like an ordinary night-shift laborer but earns enough to eat at a cook shop at four in the morning and afford a cash-up-front room in the Bluelake District. His delivery chits repeat the same instruction—“9 crates, 3rd hour, 8 up, 1 down”—and are settled weekly. After the latest chit remains unpaid, he retrieves a bag of payment from a dead drop connected to the House of a Thousand Faces.

### Rsolk

A man who manages a specific section of the Neverwinter sewers and leads several tunnel dwellers. He initially confronts Vukradin as an intruder but becomes enthusiastic and accommodating once he recognizes the famous singer. He knows Zeleen and had doubted Zeleen’s claimed relationship with Vukradin and the Mountain’s Toe Mine. For roughly two months, his men have been disappearing near a floodline tunnel because of creatures resembling spiders with all the wrong colors and strange webs. He accepts Vukradin’s offer of help and asks the bard to seek Common Chord tickets for him. His people receive Vukradin’s autographs and escort him out safely.

### Uncle Tom

The landlord of the flats where Denvar resides. He requires substantial cash up front, cares primarily about being paid, does not scry on his tenants, and describes Denvar as quiet and dependable. He holds several misconceptions about Tortles and Drow, including the belief that Tortles live inside their shells, while insisting that his establishment offers equal-opportunity housing because money is money. After recognizing that his visitors are heavily armed, he becomes markedly more conciliatory and offers them a discount. He later asks Soma for sincere advice about designing rooms suitable for Tortles.

## Locations

### The Common Chord

A performance venue in Neverwinter that once operated as a gritty dive and even threw Vukradin out unpaid. It is now reputedly the hardest ticket in the city, with fashionable patrons, expensive food, polished furnishings, a leather-covered Brewbarry table, and a commemorative plaque. Petra has transformed it into a profitable commercial operation. The party negotiated a 500-gold-piece likeness-rights deal there and must perform again in two days. Vukradin uses the remaining hours before the stakeout to raise money for an independent, lower-cost music studio.

### Neverwinter Sewers

A damp network of tunnels beneath the coastal city, affected by two high and two low tides every day. The party times its investigation for the third hour and low tide, expecting only a few relatively safe hours before the water rises. The sewers support an organized smuggling route, including a grill where nine crates are deposited, eight observed without official Commission Harbor marks. Different territorial factions manage individual sections. Rsolk reported that one floodline area had been abandoned after several tunnel workers disappeared around strangely colored spider-like creatures and unnatural webbing.

### Bluelake District

A generally lower-end district of Neverwinter where Denvar rents a room and the House of a Thousand Faces operates. The tavern’s luxurious interior is noticeably out of place against the district’s more modest surroundings.

### Uncle Tom’s Flats

A cash-up-front, high-efficiency apartment building with many small rooms and a live-in landlord. It resembles a single-room-occupancy establishment and houses a collection of unrelated tenants. Denvar’s ability to afford a room there helps reveal that he is better paid than an ordinary delivery laborer.

### House of a Thousand Faces

A high-end tavern in the Bluelake District decorated with mirrors, mannequins in antiquated fashions, lush couches, and a well-drawing fireplace. A movable stone in its exterior wall serves as a two-way dead drop used to pay Denvar. Behind that mechanism lies a windowless basement office containing desks, papers, supplies, and a Harper symbol, identifying the tavern as a local Harper headquarters. Brewbarry had long heard of the establishment but could not afford to visit it during his poorer years.

## Items

### Boots of Elvenkind

Magical footwear owned by Vukradin that grants advantage when he moves silently; his separate Stealth bonus is +9. Their effectiveness gives him enough confidence to follow the brass-and-leather construct into the sewers alone.

### Sending Stones

A pair of magical stones used as “walkie-talkies” while the party splits during the stakeout. Vukradin carries one, while Soma’s group retains the other, allowing both branches of the investigation to exchange updates before reuniting.

### Smuggled Crates

Nine crates brought out through a sewer grill during the third hour. Eight lack official Commission Harbor marks, indicating that those crates entered Neverwinter outside the legal harbor process. They are stacked for Lim, while the ninth is set separately on a ledge and retrieved by a construct. Denvar’s chits show that this precise pattern has repeated for at least seven to ten days.

### Brass and Leather Construct

A small, quick, cat-sized mechanical creature that moves “with the unhurried competence of a thing following instructions.” It emerges from the darkness beneath the ledge, retrieves the ninth crate in the delivery, and carries it back into the sewers. Its destination remains unknown because the magical area effect diverts Vukradin from the trail.

### Delivery Chits

Seven to ten days of papers found among Denvar’s belongings, each documenting the same recurring job: “9 crates, 3rd hour, 8 up, 1 down.” They bear a client reference and a house mark but no name and are reconciled through a weekly settlement. All but the newest chit have been settled, prompting Denvar to retrieve his latest payment from the House of a Thousand Faces dead drop.

## Spells

### Find Familiar

Cast by Soma to summon a rat familiar. Soma can see and hear through the rat’s senses while it remains within 100 feet, even when walls and corners block direct sight. After the several-hour wait for Denvar, Soma recasts the spell and spends another slot to restore the rat. The familiar helps scout Denvar’s room, where it finds his delivery chits, and later passes through the dead drop into the windowless basement office of the House of a Thousand Faces, where it identifies the Harper symbol.

### Knock

Used on the inner closure of the House of a Thousand Faces’ two-way dead drop after the party opens the street-facing side. The spell successfully releases the inaccessible mechanism, allowing Soma’s familiar rat to enter and scout the Harper office beyond.
