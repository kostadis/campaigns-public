# Chapter 56: The Fall of the Rift Weaver Prime

Date: 2026-09-15

## Summary

The party clashed fiercely with a nest of Rift Weavers deep within the stinking sewers of Neverwinter, the air thick with rot and the crackling heat of spreading fire. Soma, having taken the form of a giant spider so she could ignore the webs, skittered across the ceiling and sank her fangs into the Rift Weaver Prime. She repeatedly drove her flaming sphere into the lesser creatures while the flames spread through roughly five feet of webbing each round. Valphine unleashed bursts of radiant light that scorched the creatures where they were most vulnerable. Brewbarry, towering in his giant form, cut down a phase-shifting weaver as it tried to slip past him toward Vukradin, his reach proving inescapable. One by one, the creatures fizzled or burst apart in showers of sparkling energy.

The Rift Weaver Prime proved a far more dangerous foe. After Brewbarry wounded it, the Prime generated fifteen temporary hit points and released a defensive burst that forced nearby combatants to dodge. It repeatedly drove its venomous jaws toward Vukradin. On one attack, the jaws snapped around him and sprayed poison everywhere, yet the venom “artistically” missed him; later bites were less forgiving. Even Silvery Barbs could not save the bard—the spell forced the Prime to reroll one attack, only for it to roll even higher. The Prime finally overwhelmed Vukradin, leaving him unconscious on the wet stone floor.

In a final act of spite, the Prime carefully plucked the Carver’s Obsidian sword from the unconscious bard and teleported away. It intended to put distance between itself and the party, but materialized within the vast reach of Brewbarry’s giant form. Brewbarry refused to let it escape and landed a blow that dealt exactly enough damage to kill it. The creature exploded in a spectacular flash of light, sending the stolen sword splashing into the murky, rising water below.

With the battle won, the party stopped the water by pulling the chamber’s control lever, dismissed the flaming sphere, and tended to the aftermath. Brewbarry returned to his normal size and retrieved the Carver’s Obsidian sword from the foul water, while Valphine stabilized the unconscious Vukradin. The party jokingly considered looting the bard before waking him and telling him they had found nothing—particularly tempting because Vukradin would have had strong opinions about the treasure.

Searching the bodies scattered across the chamber, they found fallen members of the Rat Pack and two operatives bearing Harper pins. Their presence confirmed that both the Harpers and the wererats had been trying to force a path through the Rift Weaver territory. Most curiously, the dead included a male drow bearing a concealed spiderweb tattoo with a crest worked into its design. He appeared to have been staff rather than an important figure and carried no helpful mission notes. Valphine carefully cut the tattoo free so the body could not be readily identified through it, while preserving the corpse as possible evidence that a drow had been present.

The party gathered treasure worth 700 copper pieces, 5,000 silver pieces, and 1,700 gold pieces, represented largely by assorted gems and other monetary instruments rather than loose coin. They also recovered two Harper pins and a peculiar suit of hide armor of blasting from one of the dead wererats. The armor could produce a fire bolt and replenish that power after a long rest.

Attention then turned to the shimmering anomaly at the chamber’s edge: a vertical plane approximately ten feet wide and fifteen feet high, with water running sideways along its boundary. Meril’s Staff pulsed urgently in response. Valphine’s natural 20 on Arcana identified it as a crease or rift in reality rather than a true portal—it could not simply be passed through—and connected it to the similar anomaly encountered at the Treant scene.

Soma pressed Meril’s Staff directly against the rift to see what would happen. The staff shimmered, but Soma failed to overcome the rift’s power, suffered a violent magical backlash, and was displaced across the chamber into the stinking sewer water. Amphibious and undeterred, she climbed out with the insight that the staff could overwhelm and close the tear. On her second attempt, made with advantage, she succeeded. The rift collapsed, and a single leaf peeled away from Meril’s Staff and fell to the ground, leaving only three. The loss confirmed that the staff expends its leaves when its power is used to heal the natural order.

Meanwhile, Soma’s rat familiar watched giant rats approaching from the west. Their clothing and appearance identified them as members of the Rat Pack rather than ordinary sewer vermin. With the Rift Weavers dead and the rift sealed, the route the Rat Pack had wanted was finally open.

Moving northeast through the rubble-strewn passageway, the party discovered a gnome crouched behind barrels with a large fire cannon aimed down the corridor—the perfect weapon for creatures vulnerable to flame. A tiny homunculus, likened to a gnomish Iron Giant, perched near him. The gnome was wary at first, demanding to know whether the newcomers were from the Temple of Lathander and whether they had truly cleared the Rift Weavers.

When Brewbarry confirmed their victory and asked whether any of the barrels contained ale, the gnome initially demanded assurance that the party would protect him. Brewbarry invoked the disappointing state of “gnome hospitality,” prompting immediate contrition. The gnome then recognized Brewbarry’s tattoos and accused him of being one of the fools dressing like the famous barbarian. When Brewbarry answered, “I am Brewbarry,” the gnome realized he was speaking to a living symbol of the resistance. He rushed to pour a gnomish-sized beer, reconsidered, and returned with an entire bucket, insisting that his mother must never hear that he had demonstrated poor hospitality. Brewbarry drained it rapidly, wiped his mouth, and burped loudly.

The gnome introduced himself as Bimble, operator of the planar manifold. He had tuned the machine for nine years and, every ten days, performed a routine that kept the Rift Weavers below a safe threshold. During the previous two years, that routine had held for progressively shorter intervals while Commission management maintained fixed throughput targets and demanded ever greater trade capacity. Bimble insisted that the machine had not simply failed: “The machine did not fail. It was finished.”

An unknown person had received sustained, unsupervised access to the machinery and rewritten its routing outside every register. Bimble grudgingly admitted that the new work was better than his own and increased capacity without producing additional tears, but eventually the manifold stopped entirely. He attempted the restart procedure in an operator’s manual, without success, and concluded that the illicit efficiency improvements had burned out the machinery. He knew how to tune the manifold, but even after studying it for more than a decade, he had never determined how it was originally assembled or where replacement components could be obtained.

Shortly before the collapse, a drow woman asked Bimble what he had removed from the machine’s housing. Her question told him that he needed to disappear. Although Bimble did not know her name, his description matched Sridar, the drow woman the party had met at Falcon’s Lodge and to whom they had previously sold planar artifacts.

Bimble feared the Commission would make him the convenient scapegoat. The manifold had, as Bimble put it, allowed merchants to teleport goods everywhere in Faerûn in exchange for a percentage paid to the Commission, with the Lionshield Coster among its principal beneficiaries. Lord Neverember had also been a major investor. Now, as Bimble told it, the machine was dead, the Commission’s revenue had collapsed, and the port trade was in House Margaster’s hands. Bimble did not believe the sabotage had been designed specifically to frame him, but he was certain the Commission would need someone to blame and that the undocumented stranger would be dismissed as a desperate technician’s fiction.

Bimble produced an operator’s manual for the Model 1486 Spatial Rectification Calliope, an older model distinct from the Model 1489 at Axeholm, and the party compared its restart sequence with the guide already in their possession. Vukradin—revived after the looting and told little about the valuables recovered while he was unconscious—used his photographic memory to absorb its contents. Bimble had retained the manual as evidence that he had followed the proper procedures and had not broken the manifold himself.

Although Bimble could not explain how to repair the machine, he suggested inspecting it for damage or clues he might have missed. Brewbarry placed a reassuring hand on the gnome’s shoulder and declared him under the party’s protection. The homunculus with Bimble was also revealed to be the creature that had previously delivered his food, provisions supplied through the Harpers.

Boney, ever the dutiful record keeper—and emphatically “a proud member of the livery staff,” not a butler—reminded everyone that their dinner engagement with Lord Neverember was waiting and likely took priority over immediately inspecting the manifold. The machinery was not going anywhere. The party still needed to decide what to do with Bimble, whether to entrust him to the Harpers, and how to handle the Rat Pack’s newly opened sewer route. With the Harpers likely to investigate, House Margaster benefiting from the manifold’s destruction, and Sridar interested in both the machine’s housing and its manual, the threads of the conspiracy were tightening.

## NPCs

### Rift Weavers

Phase-shifting spider-like creatures that inhabit or are attracted to rifts in reality. They can teleport up to thirty feet as a bonus action, move through webbed environments, cling to ceilings, restrain prey, and attack with venomous jaws. They are particularly vulnerable to fire and radiant energy. When slain, they tend to fizzle, sparkle, radiate, or explode into planar light. Several were encountered and destroyed in the flooding sewer chamber.

### Rift Weaver Prime

The formidable leader of the Rift Weavers. It could teleport, attack with venomous jaws, use legendary actions, and trigger a defensive burst that granted fifteen temporary hit points while forcing nearby creatures to dodge. It relentlessly targeted Vukradin, eventually knocking him unconscious despite Silvery Barbs. It then stole the Carver’s Obsidian sword with its teeth and tried to escape, but Brewbarry killed it with a reaction as it moved within his giant reach.

### The Rat Pack

A gang of wererats operating within the Neverwinter sewers. Several members were found dead after attempting to penetrate the Rift Weaver-controlled chamber. Others approached in giant-rat form after the battle, identifiable by the clothing they retained. They had been eager to seize the blocked hallway and were pleased that the party finally opened the route.

### Dead Drow

A deceased male drow found among the sewer casualties. The party did not recognize him, and he appeared to have been staff rather than an important member of his organization. He carried no explanatory notes but bore a concealed spiderweb tattoo incorporating a crest. Valphine recognizes the crest as her own family’s seal, House Sotorra, though she does not recognize the dead drow himself. Valphine cut away the tattoo to prevent its use in identifying the body while preserving the corpse as evidence of drow involvement.

### Bimble

A gnome artificer and technician hiding in the sewers behind barrels and a fire cannon. He is the operator of the planar manifold and tuned it for nine years, performing a ten-day maintenance routine to suppress Rift Weaver activity. He warned the Commission that its throughput demands were unsustainable, but an unknown person was allowed to rewrite the machine’s routing before its catastrophic failure.

Bimble is verbose, technical, defensive about his competence, and determined to explain events in their proper order. He does not know how to reconstruct the machine and fears the Commission will execute him as a scapegoat. He recognizes Brewbarry as a symbol of the resistance and responds strongly to suggestions that he has shown poor hospitality.

### Sridar

A drow woman previously encountered at Falcon’s Lodge who was interested in purchasing planar artifacts. The party sold such artifacts to her there. Bimble’s description identifies her as the likely woman who asked what had been removed from the manifold’s housing shortly before its collapse. Her question prompted him to flee into hiding.

### The Homunculus

A small, robot-like familiar constructed by Bimble, visually compared to a gnomish Iron Giant. It remains with him at his defensive position and was the creature that previously delivered food supplied by the Harpers.

### Boney

A skeletal horse who serves as the party’s meticulous record keeper. Although others call him a butler horse, Boney insists, “I’m not a butler horse. I’m a proud member of the livery staff.” He is particularly concerned with teeth, bone health, bathing before dinner, and proper social obligations. He reminded the party that its dinner with Lord Neverember should take priority over inspecting the manifold.

### Lord Neverember

The ruler of Neverwinter, whom Bimble described as a major investor in the Commission. The party has an important upcoming dinner engagement with him. Because the planar manifold was a significant source of Commission revenue, its loss may affect his political and financial interests.

## Locations

### Sewer Chamber

A stinking, flooded chamber in the Neverwinter sewers filled with Rift Weaver webbing. It contains machinery and a lever controlling the rising water. A vertical planar rift approximately ten feet wide and fifteen feet high stood between two piers, with water flowing sideways along its edge.

The chamber held the remains of Rat Pack members, Harper operatives, and a male drow, suggesting that several factions had tried to force their way through. It became the site of the party’s battle with the Rift Weaver Prime, its subsequent looting, and Soma’s successful closure of the planar rift.

### Sewer Passageway (Northeast)

A rubble-strewn route leading northeast from the Rift Weaver chamber. Although the debris initially appeared to mark a wall, it could be crossed. Farther along, the passage turns toward an opening defended by Bimble’s fire cannon. Barrels, supplies, and Bimble’s homunculus occupy the gnome’s makeshift refuge.

Two pins recovered from dead Harper operatives in the sewer chamber. Their presence confirms that the Harpers had been actively attempting to pass through the Rift Weaver territory. The party briefly joked that possessing the pins might make them Harpers, but the pins alone confer no such status.

### Hide Armor of Blasting

A magical suit of hide armor recovered from a fallen wererat. It grants the ability to cast Fire Bolt, with the power replenishing after a long rest. Its presence on an otherwise ordinary Rat Pack casualty surprised the party.

### Operator’s Guide to the Model 1486 Spatial Rectification Calliope

A technical manual for an older version of the planar manifold machinery, retained by Bimble as evidence that he followed the correct restart procedure and did not cause the failure. Its restart sequence differs from that of the Calliope model already known to the party, but using it did not revive the damaged manifold.

Vukradin used his photographic memory to retain the guide’s contents without spending the time required to make a complete written copy. As a planar artifact connected to the manifold, the manual may interest Sridar and other drow operatives.

### Fire Cannon

A large gnomish defensive weapon aimed down the northeastern sewer passage. Bimble positioned it to defend his hiding place, and its flames make it particularly effective against Rift Weavers. Its existence underscores that Bimble considered the creatures a dangerous but manageable perimeter defense.

## Spells

### Flaming Sphere

A rolling ball of fire controlled by Soma throughout the battle. She used it to strike individual Rift Weavers and burn away their webs at roughly five feet per round. The creatures’ vulnerability to fire ensured that even successful saves left them badly injured; one lesser Rift Weaver died when the sphere dealt enough damage that halving it could not save the creature.

### Guiding Bolt

A bolt of radiant energy cast by Valphine against a lesser Rift Weaver. The spell dealt twelve radiant damage, doubled to twenty-four by the creature’s vulnerability, leaving it with only eight hit points.

### Bless

A divine enchantment active during the battle. It added a d4 to affected attack rolls and saving throws, turning at least one of Brewbarry’s near misses into a successful hit and helping Vukradin survive an earlier saving throw against the Prime’s poison.

### Starry Wisp

A radiant cantrip cast by Vukradin against Rift Weaver Prime. The attack struck with a sixteen and inflicted twelve radiant damage, doubled because of the Prime’s vulnerability. It burned through the Prime’s temporary hit points and carried damage into its remaining health.

### Silvery Barbs

A reaction spell used by Vukradin to force the Rift Weaver Prime to reroll a successful attack. On the decisive use, the magic backfired in practice: the Prime “rolled even harder,” struck Vukradin anyway, and knocked him unconscious.
