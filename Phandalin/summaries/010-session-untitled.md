# Chapter 10: Untitled

Date: 2025-08-05

## Summary

The battle against the orc ambushers raged on with brutal intensity. An orc warrior landed a punishing blow on Valphine, shattering her concentration and extinguishing the Faerie Fire outlining their enemies. Vukradin attempted to bend the orc shaman’s will with Command, strengthening the spell with his bardic inspiration, but the shaman resisted with uncanny fortitude. Soma, still in beast form, pressed the attack relentlessly, biting and clawing at the shaman until he was forced out of his boar transformation and reverted to his true form, battered but still dangerous.

With the shaman exposed, the party tried to press its advantage, but poor strikes and missed opportunities gave him time to retaliate. Cornered and furious, the shaman unleashed a searing lightning bolt through Brewbarry and Valphine. Both managed to partially evade the blast, but each still suffered sixteen points of damage before Brewbarry used Stone’s Endurance to reduce his share to six. Vukradin then successfully commanded the shaman to halt, robbing him of an entire turn. Even with their enemy rendered helpless for a round, however, the party struggled to capitalize, prompting Vukradin to observe, “Guys, I gave you a whole round to, like, kill this guy, and you did nothing,” followed by Valphine’s dry, “Exactly zero.”

Valphine struck the shaman with Guiding Bolt, badly wounding him and leaving him vulnerable to the next attack. Before Soma could use that opening, Brewbarry was felled by an orc’s weapon and began making death saves. Soma then tore the shaman down and redirected her second attack toward one of the badly injured orcs. Moments later, Orc 1 shouted something about Talos and landed a critical blow on Valphine, sending her unconscious as well. With both companions bleeding on the ground, the party’s overriding concern became killing the remaining attackers before the death saves ran out.

One badly injured orc disengaged and attempted to flee, but the party cut him down with a critical hit. Soma objected to killing an enemy who was running away, prompting an argument over whether a surviving assassin would remain a threat. Her protest—“You shot him in the back”—was answered with the claim that the orc would simply return and try to kill them again. The debate continued even as the remaining enemies attacked, until Wade reminded everyone out of character, “Talking may be a free action, but it wastes everybody’s time.”

Vukradin faced the final orc with both daggers, striking twice and landing a critical hit, but the warrior remained barely alive. Soma followed with two attacks and delivered the killing blow. The battlefield finally fell silent, with Brewbarry and Valphine surviving their death saves long enough for the victorious companions to tend to them. Reflecting on the narrow victory, the group agreed that it had been an exceptionally intense fight.

Once the dust settled, Vukradin acted on a suspicion he had been harboring. Convinced that the pin given to the party by Ser Kaelen’s associates had guided the assassins to them, he broke it in two and planted the pieces among the debris so its destruction would look accidental—something that could plausibly have happened when an orc struck him during the battle.

The party searched the fallen and recovered six platinum pieces, six gold pieces, and a book concerning Talos. Valphine’s familiarity with Faerûn’s evil gods allowed her to identify Talos as the deity of storms and destruction. His dogma demanded absolute obedience from his priests and commanded them to spread destruction wherever possible; some traditions even regarded him as an aspect of the orcish god Gruumsh.

More troubling evidence appeared among the supplies: a carefully sealed packet of unusually good rations, far better made and packaged than anything expected among orcs. Corbin, the former bandit now traveling with the group, initially went pale when examining it. He eventually admitted that it looked as though someone had supplied the orcs. When the packaging was opened and examined more closely, the party found a distinctive letter “C.” Corbin recognized it as the Carver’s monogram.

The discovery suggested that the Talos-worshipping orcs had been supplied or paid by the local bandit chief. The party recalled that the ambushers had known they were coming and had reportedly been told they would be easy prey. Only the town master and Ser Kaelen were known to have been informed of their plans, immediately placing both under suspicion. The party entertained increasingly alarming possibilities: Ser Kaelen might be working with the Carver, or might even be the Carver himself. Corbin could not settle the question, because he had never personally seen the Carver; during his time as a bandit, he had worked through the leader of his own band and other intermediaries. He did, however, warn the party that the Carver had agents in the village, though he did not know their identities.

Badly depleted, the party decided that they needed a long rest before approaching the lighthouse, though they chose a more secure nearby location rather than sleeping directly on the battlefield. Once recovered, they resumed their journey toward the Tower of Storms. Despite considering a change of destination to throw off anyone tracking them, they decided to honor their commitment. They would still investigate the lighthouse, but approached circuitously and stealthily from the south instead of following the most obvious route.

The Tower of Storms stood atop a rocky outcropping nearly surrounded by water and connected to the beach by a natural causeway. A stone building supported the lighthouse tower, from which an eerie green beacon pulsed westward over the sea. Each pulse was accompanied by the thump of a slow-beating heart. Several wrecked ships lay scattered among the rocks below.

On the beach, the party encountered a crab of monstrous size. Soma approached intending to use Speak with Animals, only for the crab to greet her first in perfectly fluent Common: “Well met.” The crab spoke of evil harpies nesting atop the outcropping, though it admitted that it did not truly know why so many vessels had wrecked beneath the lighthouse. Five shipwrecks were scattered around the rocks, and the crab offered to retrieve their treasures—including a magical weapon from the wreck of the *Star-Crossed Lover*—if the party would first perform a good deed.

The crab explained that its intelligence had been bestowed by a sea elf named Miraal. Once, it had been “but a dumb mute crab,” but Miraal’s gift had awakened its mind. Miraal had since died and become a tormented ghost haunting a nearby cave. The crab wanted its former mistress laid to rest but was too large to enter the narrow passage and, eventually, confessed to another reason: “Well, I’m scared.” It did not want to see Miraal in her ghostly form, preferring to remember her as she had been. The confession reduced the crab to tears, and the party agreed to help. The crab warned that Miraal demanded a quest from those entering her cave. Those who accepted and made an honest attempt were allowed to pass; those who refused had met with screams and death.

The party entered the damp, lichen-encrusted cave cut into a thirty-foot natural pillar of rock. Miraal materialized before them as a ghostly sea elf with gills, webbed hands and feet, and spectral garments that drifted as though underwater. She explained that Moesko, a half-orc anchorite of Talos, had invaded the tower and killed her. He had taken her opalescent conch—her spellcasting focus—as a trophy, and she was cursed to remain until it was returned.

The party asked whether the Talos cultists might be connected to the Carver. Miraal could not identify the hidden organizer, but she observed that the attack upon the tower displayed more sophisticated planning than she expected from orcs, suggesting that some larger organization or more powerful cultist might be directing them. She had initially come to investigate the lighthouse because its pulsing green beacon was not its normal color. There she encountered Moesko, and during their fatal battle she saw the most disturbing thing about him: there was a hole in his chest where his heart should have been. The party suspected that he might be undead or afflicted by some unnatural ritual, though neither Valphine’s religious knowledge nor Soma’s medical observations revealed a clear explanation beyond the obvious conclusion that hearts were normally necessary for life.

The group accepted Miraal’s demand to retrieve the conch. She released them to continue toward the lighthouse, wishing that the blessings of the sea gods would accompany them. They climbed the narrow switchback path toward the tower while the green beacon throbbed overhead like a heartbeat.

At the last turn in the path, three hungry harpies flew out from the cliffs. Two began singing, their supernatural melody winding into the minds of the climbers. Valphine and Brewbarry succumbed to the Luring Song and became charmed, while Vukradin and Soma resisted. With the harpies closing in and half the party enthralled, the session ended just as initiative was rolled. Vukradin declared that he would begin the coming fight with *Silence*.

## Locations

### Ambush Site

The wilderness location where the party was attacked by Talos-worshipping orcs and their shaman. The fight left Brewbarry and Valphine unconscious and the remaining combatants badly depleted. Afterward, the party recovered money, religious material, and a ration packet bearing the Carver’s monogram. Vukradin also planted the broken remains of his suspected tracking pin among the battlefield debris. The group moved to a more secure nearby position before taking a long rest.

### Tower of Storms

A stone building surmounted by a lighthouse tower, situated atop a rocky outcropping nearly surrounded by water and connected to the beach by a natural causeway. High coastal cliffs and rocky shelves surround it. An eerie green light pulses from the beacon westward out to sea, accompanied by a sound like a slow-beating heart. The green color is an unnatural change that originally drew Miraal to investigate. Five wrecked ships are scattered among the surrounding rocks, including the *Star-Crossed Lover*. Harpies nest on the heights, and a shark patrols the waters below.

### Miraal’s Cave

A damp, eight-foot-high cave encrusted with lichen, connected to a seven-foot-high tunnel passing through a thirty-foot natural pillar of rock beside the causeway. The cave remains above sea level even at high tide. It is haunted by the ghost of the sea elf Miraal, who demands that travelers recover her stolen conch from Moesko. Those who accept and genuinely attempt the quest may pass; those who refuse risk being killed.

## NPCs

### Orc Shaman

A powerful Talos-worshipping spellcaster leading the orc ambush. He transformed into a boar to survive the party’s initial assault. Soma’s attacks depleted the boar form and forced him back into his natural body. He resisted Vukradin’s first enhanced Command with a natural 20, then unleashed a lightning bolt through Brewbarry, Valphine, and one of his own allies. Vukradin subsequently halted him twice, and Soma finally killed him after Valphine’s Guiding Bolt left him vulnerable.

### Corbin

A former bandit now traveling with the party as a camp companion and caretaker. He hid during the battle while protecting the party’s equipment, then emerged once the fighting ended. Corbin went pale while examining an unusually well-packaged ration packet among the orc supplies. He recognized the “C” on its wrapper as the Carver’s monogram and agreed that someone appeared to have supplied the orcs.

Corbin has never seen the Carver personally. During his bandit career, he worked for the leader of his own band and dealt through intermediaries. He warns that the Carver has agents in the village but does not know their identities. He also explains that the bandits have no shared insignia, tattoo, pin, or secret code by which they can be readily identified.

### Ser Kaelen

A figure suspected by the party of being in league with the orcs or the Carver. The party notes that only Ser Kaelen and the town master knew of their travel plans, making both possible sources of the information used to prepare the ambush. The ration packet marked with the Carver’s monogram leads the party to speculate that Ser Kaelen may be working with the Carver—or might even secretly be the Carver himself. Vukradin also suspects that a pin connected to Ser Kaelen was used to track the party.

### The Carver

A local bandit chief whose distinctive “C” monogram was found on high-quality rations carried by the orc ambushers, suggesting that he or his organization supplied the Talos cultists. Corbin confirms that the symbol resembles the Carver’s monogram, but he has never personally seen the bandit chief. The Carver operates through intermediaries and maintains unidentified agents within the village.

### Crabby

A monstrously sized, sentient crab living on the beach near the Tower of Storms. It speaks fluent Common and greets Soma before she can cast Speak with Animals. Miraal gifted the crab its intelligence when it was “but a dumb mute crab,” and it remains devoted to her memory.

The crab is cheerful, literal-minded, and surprisingly well-informed about the coastline. It knows about the five shipwrecks, the harpies, and the shark in the surrounding water, though it admits that it does not know what truly causes the wrecks. It offers to salvage treasure, including a magical weapon from the *Star-Crossed Lover*, if the party lays Miraal’s spirit to rest. It cannot enter Miraal’s cave because of its size and because it is frightened to see her as a ghost, preferring to remember her as she was.

### Miraal

The ghost of a sea elf with gills, webbed hands, and webbed feet, haunting a cave near the Tower of Storms. Her spectral garments drift as though she were still underwater. She bestowed intelligence upon the giant crab and regarded it as a good friend.

Miraal came to the Tower of Storms after noticing that its beacon had begun pulsing with an unnatural green light. Moesko killed her during her investigation and took her opalescent conch spellcasting focus as a trophy. Her curse compels her to demand its return from everyone passing through her cave and to kill those who refuse. She suspects that the Talos cultists’ unusually sophisticated operation may be guided by a larger organization or more powerful evil figure, but she cannot identify the Carver or confirm any connection.

### Moesko

A half-orc anchorite of Talos who invaded the Tower of Storms and killed Miraal. He keeps her opalescent conch as a trophy. Miraal remembers that his chest contained a gaping hole where his heart should have been, yet he remained alive and capable of defeating her. The party does not yet understand how he survives without his heart and cannot identify any standard Talos ritual that would explain it. He appears to be connected to the lighthouse’s unnatural green beacon and the larger Talos operation.

### Harpies

A trio of hungry winged monstrosities nesting on the rocky outcroppings near the lighthouse. They intercept the party at the final turn of the narrow switchback path. Two immediately use their enchanting Luring Song while the third prepares to join them. The song charms Valphine and Brewbarry, while Vukradin and Soma resist it.

### The Anthropology of Talos

A book recovered from the orc ambushers that details the dogma of Talos, the deity of storms and destruction. Valphine uses her familiarity with evil Faerûnian gods to interpret its significance. Talos demands complete obedience from his priests and commands them to spread destruction wherever possible. He attracts those who fear nature’s destructive power and is sometimes associated with the orcish god Gruumsh.

### Monogrammed Ration Pack

A carefully wrapped and sealed packet of high-quality rations found among the orc shaman’s supplies. Its craftsmanship, packaging, and contents are markedly better than normal orc provisions, suggesting that it was supplied by an outside party. Corbin finds the food tasty and remarks that it appears someone gave it to the orcs. The discarded wrapper bears a distinctive “C,” which he identifies as the Carver’s monogram. The party suspects the rations may have formed part of the payment for the ambush.

### Opalescent Conch

A beautiful, shimmering shell that served as Miraal’s spellcasting focus. Moesko stole it after killing her and keeps it as a trophy inside the Tower of Storms. Its loss binds Miraal’s ghost to the cave and compels her to demand that passing travelers retrieve it. Returning the conch should allow her spirit to find peace.

## Spells

### Faerie Fire

A concentration spell cast by Valphine to outline enemies in glowing light and grant advantage on attacks against them. An orc strikes Valphine for five points of damage, and she fails the resulting concentration save, ending the spell. The party immediately feels the loss as several attacks miss afterward.

### Command

Cast repeatedly by Vukradin against the orc shaman. Vukradin enhances his first attempt with bardic inspiration, increasing the effective difficulty of the shaman’s save, but the transformed shaman resists with a natural 20. A later casting succeeds, and Vukradin orders the shaman to halt, depriving him of all actions for a round. The party fails to exploit that first opening effectively. Vukradin subsequently halts him again rather than commanding him to flee, preventing further spellcasting until Soma kills him.

### Lightning Bolt

A powerful electrical discharge unleashed by the orc shaman through Brewbarry and Valphine, also catching one of the shaman’s allies in its path. The blast would have dealt thirty-two points of damage on a failed save. Both heroes succeed and take sixteen damage. Brewbarry then uses Stone’s Endurance to reduce his damage from sixteen to six; his rage does not grant resistance against lightning.

### Guiding Bolt

Cast by Valphine at the orc shaman after Vukradin halts him. The radiant attack reduces the shaman to ten hit points and grants advantage to the next attack against him. Soma uses that advantage to land the killing blow before redirecting her remaining attack toward another orc.

### Speak with Animals

A spell Soma prepares to cast when she sees the enormous crab on the beach. Before she completes it, the crab greets the party with “Well met” in fluent Common, making the spell unnecessary.

### Harpy Luring Song

A supernatural melody used by the harpies as the party rounds the final switchback beneath the lighthouse. Two harpies begin singing, forcing the climbers to resist the enchantment. Valphine and Brewbarry fail and become charmed, while Soma and Vukradin resist. The charm remains a threat for as long as its victims can hear the song, leaving the party in a precarious position when the session ends.
