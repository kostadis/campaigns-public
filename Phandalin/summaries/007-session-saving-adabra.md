# Chapter 7: Saving Adabra

Date: 03-02 of Tarsakh 1495

## Summary
The chapter opens not on the road but on an interrogation. Corbin — a gaunt man in his late twenties with the hollowed-out look of someone who has stared into the abyss and found it staring back, matted dirt-brown hair, a patchy beard, and pale blue eyes that go distant until they sharpen with feral intensity — stands before Vukradin in ill-fitting leather armour scavenged from a dead man. Vukradin notices it immediately and is displeased: “Is that stolen armor?” Corbin explains that the armour was supplied during his bandit training and had belonged to someone who no longer needed it. The party points out that he will be tending camp and suggests he may not need it there. Corbin appeals over his head — “Mr Brewbarry, can I please keep my armor?” — which backfires, since the goliath wears none at all and says so. But the despair in the bandit’s eyes gets through, and Vukradin relents with a promise rather than a permission: “We need to get you some proper fair trade armor.” The scene ends in farce when a spider squeaks, waves its arms frantically, and points at Corbin as though trying to ask a question. Corbin panics. Vukradin laughs: “That’s Soma, our tortle.”

Still in spider form, Soma points and makes strange skittering or hissing noises because she cannot speak Common. The exchange turns into a joke about Corbin being a “squishy human” among an aasimar, a drow, a goliath, and Soma’s spider form.

They travelled on to a hill topped by a windmill, a set of cairns, and a ruined building. Atop the windmill, quite obviously, sat a manticore, and from the building came a woman’s voice: “Help me!” Soma was working out how to advance when Valphine began asking questions instead — what does one do, in the overbright, when there are people being attacked by monsters? The answer was that one usually tries to save the sentient life. Valphine turned to Vukradin: they had been told to bring the woman back. Soma saw exactly where this was going and cut it off — yes, but it was implied alive. Valphine drew the conclusion (“So we must kill the manticore?”), and Vukradin promptly found an angle: what if they killed the manticore, returned to Phandalin, demanded more money, and only then came back for her? Soma rolled her eyes and proposed the alternative of simply killing the manticore and saving her.

They started up the hill quietly. Behind them Corbin suggested Vukradin leave his instrument behind so it wouldn’t get damaged, and got a flat “No” and a look of terror for his trouble — he had only wanted to make sure. Soma offered to mend the instrument if it were damaged. After a discussion about whether magical repair had the care and sensitivity of restringing by hand, Vukradin accepted the offer. He then said, “Let’s go quietly,” and pointed at the manticore in the distance. Soma nodded. And although Vukradin was silent, Soma was not. The manticore’s head came up. It saw all of them, and Soma’s chance to talk to it was gone.

The creature roared and the fight began, though it kept trying to be a negotiation. Valphine loaded her hand crossbow and shot; Vukradin chose that moment to object that they were supposed to be returning those magical items, being a fair trade group. The bolt wounded it — “That hurt,” it howled, revealing it could speak — and it explained itself: “I am just trying to get a tasty meal!” Vukradin, on brand, asked whether they could pay it. It eventually named its price: “A small payment would do fine.” The manticore’s answer was a volley of tail spikes into Valphine and a suggestion that this might motivate them to find more money. Vukradin’s shouted command spell failed outright. Then the creature spotted Corbin and made its real offer: “I’ll take him in exchange.” It added, “If you’re willing to exchange the human, I’m willing to forgive.” Valphine noted, drily, that in a different life she would have called that a fair trade. Soma shouted “What?”

Soma’s counter-offer was to conjure a familiar and order it to fly into the manticore’s mouth. The jaws snapped shut. The intent was a tasty morsel; the effect was rage — “That was not satisfying! Where’s the meat!!! The blood? The tasty crunch!” Soma charged with the look of a mother whose clan has been touched, and the rest advanced with her. Brewbarry got close enough to throw hand axes, one of which struck the manticore’s wing, earning him the creature’s attention and the observation that he might be tastier. It flew in and rent him with its claws while he struck it with his halberd; both came away injured, but it was the manticore that retreated, howling, to the windmill’s second floor. From the first floor the woman was still shrieking to be saved. Soma tried reassurance — “Stay calm!” — and then, with characteristic confusion, suggested she could run out while it was distracted, which drew the obvious objection that it could attack her.

Vukradin, wearing the look of a gambler, asked whether he should try the spell again. Soma is not a gambler, but she said the encouraging thing anyway: “He’s unlikely to avoid falling to your spell twice! I believe in you.” It failed. Vukradin’s response established a pattern the party would live with for a long time — “Well, I didn’t study maths or chance! I studied the arts! I trusted you, Soma!” — and when the visibly hurt tortle asked how she could make it up to him, he told her he didn’t know if he could ever trust her again. Valphine reloaded and landed a second bolt for eight damage. Brewbarry climbed to the second floor and hit the creature once with his halberd, adding to the opportunity attack he had landed earlier; it turned on him, and his rage absorbed the worst of the claws. Then it broke off and flew — and Valphine, tracking it, saw Corbin in the distance and understood that the beast had decided to eat him instead. Vukradin and Soma struck it with spells. The killing blow was Valphine’s to take, but her final bolt missed, leaving her staring at her crossbow in disbelief while Brewbarry screamed “YES!!!” over the body.

Brewbarry cut off the creature’s head and carried it to the windmill, calling that the occupant could come out now. Soma layered on her soothing tone — “It’s safe now” — the tone of a mother to a small child. The woman who opened the door was Adabra Gwynn, the human midwife and apothecary who lived and worked at the Umbrage Hill windmill. Vukradin straightened up and asked whether she was Adabra. Valphine, trying to help, offered to teach her about Lathander. Adabra’s only question was whether it was dead, which Brewbarry answered by holding up the severed head. She explained her caution afterwards: she had heard the thump of a body hitting the ground but could not be certain whether it was one of them the creature had picked up and dropped or the beast itself, and she was very determined not to tempt fate. The siege, they learned, had been short.

Then she refused to leave. She darted back inside and returned with a vial — “This is my special potion of healing with extra scent and taste that makes it go down sweeter!” — which Soma, who had lived near a city and seen her share of rich people scenting their baths, dismissed under her breath as sounding like a bath water potion. Adabra was immovable: she must stay to craft her potions. Vukradin came away certain there was a deeper agenda at work that he did not understand.

The agenda surfaced when Adabra turned to Soma. “You are a tortle?” she asked, and then something much stranger: “In the great schism, are you on the naturalist or interventionist side?” Soma did not know what the question meant, but sensed her own heart lay with preserving rather than intervening, and answered “Naturalist.” Adabra’s whole aura and bearing shifted. “Very good.” She explained that the naturalists regard all creatures as part of the natural order, including demons, devils, and powerful gods who intervene in the world. Soma agreed, reading it as a simple wish to keep things as they should be. Vukradin pressed the practical case, that it would be safer for the villagers not to hike into the open with a white dragon about, and was told that the natural order requires that they come to her. Then Adabra placed them: were these the ones Townmaster Harbin had been looking for, after he sent a note to his Lordship asking for help and was promised it? Told yes, she sighed and settled the matter — “In that case I will stay here, safely protected by you” — in a tone Soma could not read as either mockery or earnestness.

Vukradin’s last instrument was his music, and Soma asked him to try. He played a good tune, and Brewbarry was delighted, which prompted Soma to remark on how much the goliath loves his music — and drew out the story that would define Vukradin’s finances: he used to survey his customers for a score out of ten and accept money only from those who rated him 10/10, and Brewbarry was in fact the only customer who ever paid him. Soma stared at him, aghast, and concluded he was a naive child in need of protecting. Adabra was simply delighted — “How wonderful! This is truly blessed music! Aasimars are truly touched by the Celestial planes.” It changed nothing about her determination to stay. As Soma prepared to leave, Adabra called her back and produced a note, which Vukradin read aloud with permission: “Thank you, Townmaster Harbin, but I will not be leaving my home. Thank you for your help. Please reward these heroes.” Attached to it was a long-winded description of how to take some medicine, which Vukradin queried — was she healing him? Adabra said yes. Then Brewbarry, of all people, asked whether they could spend the night, and Adabra squealed in delight and volunteered a fantastic meal, provided she got help.

Corbin arrived at the windmill looking even worse than before, and now smelling. Vukradin resumed the interrogation, asking whether he had robbed Adabra, and persisted even after Adabra insisted no one had ever robbed her — an inquisition Valphine found exquisite. Corbin broke: he hadn’t wanted any of this, had woken with nothing but the clothes on his back after his family was killed, had come to town where nobody wanted another broken man, had gone into the forest, and had joined the crew when they told him to. Vukradin then turned the same suspicion on his host, asking whether she had stolen the old dwarven house outside — “We’ve encountered a lot of people taking things, thinking they are entitled.” Adabra was hurt. The house was destroyed before she came; she holds a deed to the land; she has tended the dwarf cairns for the dead. “I am quite human, not a grave robber. That’s why the manticore wanted me!” Soma, younger but wiser, heard the casual speciesism in that and muttered that it thought she was just meat. Adabra then proved her own point — manticores really do prefer human flesh — and delivered the correction as a lesson: Soma had not been adequately trained by her druidic masters, and Adabra would help. Soma, deflated, said “Whatever.” The room ate in silence: hearty fresh-baked bread and a vegetable and herb stew of local fungi, foraged vegetables, berries and wild greens. Valphine credited the quality to Lathander, whose glory fuels the ground and the food and makes it *just* tastier.

The jab about her training landed harder than Soma let on. Meril had been her druidic mentor, and the gaps in her training still stung. After dinner she sat and listened to Corbin’s own history: a farmer from a small, forgettable village called Greenmeadow in the plains east of the High Forest, working the land his father had worked, married to a woman named Elarah, with a young daughter named Lily who loved the wildflowers at the edge of the fields. Then the Ordning broke, chaos swept the North, the giants grew emboldened, and one smoke-filled afternoon the Uthgardt barbarians of the Black Lion tribe came down on Greenmeadow — not raiding for supplies but “a storm of pure destruction, a wildfire in human form.” He fought with a wood-axe and a farmer’s grit, watched his fields burn and his home collapse into embers and his family fall, and survived only because a charging barbarian’s shield knocked him unconscious and left him for dead in a ditch beneath his neighbour’s body. He woke to silence and ash and walked south for months, “a ghost haunting the roads,” taking a half-eaten apple, a discarded blanket, a few coins from a sleeping traveller’s purse — each petty crime chipping away at the man he had been, and each one necessary to keep living. Soma noted the symmetry: his life had been changed by visitors, and hers by meeting Meril.

Adabra woke her in the morning with bread on the table and marching orders — she had work to do, and they had a reward to collect. Soma’s reply that Adabra had been the real reward collided with Vukradin’s muttered conviction that they would not, in fact, be paid, which left Adabra flabbergasted and offering to give Harbin a piece of her mind. Soma explained the problem: the bard will only take money from very few sources, and he is desperate. Vukradin laid it out himself — everyone is pressuring him to kill monsters and take their money, and he will not, because that is stolen property, whereas rescuing her is “organic, conflict-free, fair-trade gold.” If she doesn’t come to town, he doesn’t get paid. He feigned a tear, which Soma noted he acted well, and mourned the studio he was never going to get. And that, not any of the arguments before it, moved her — because it was the same shift Soma had triggered by saying *naturalist*. “That’s such a wonderful change from all the slaughtering of the interventionist faction of the Emerald Enclave. I’m going to come and support you. Why didn’t you say that was why you wanted me? I did not know you were one of us.” She took a breath and escalated: “This barbaric slaughtering of monsters to seize their treasures is an act of barbarism and vandalism! It’s a CRIME!” Brewbarry, hurt, said “I am a barbarian.” Soma saw an opening to take her down a peg — “We don’t use the B word in front of Brewbarry” — and it did not work in the slightest. Adabra simply overruled the goliath about his own identity: he is not a barbarian, he is a nomad, and she would not permit such language in her house. Her voice went shrill with real passion as she explained why the word matters — that “barbarian” implies civilized and uncivilized, and that the uncivilized stand outside the natural order; that the interventionists want to draw lines, and when you draw lines, those who fall outside become prey and can be destroyed; that Brewbarry is part of the natural world and not opposed to it. Vukradin, wearing the look of a child promised his favourite food and not quite daring to believe it, asked whether he had heard right that she was coming with them. “Well, yes. You are fellow naturalists!”

## Locations
### Umbrage Hill
A hill topped by a windmill, a set of cairns, and a ruined building, and the site of Adabra’s home and workshop. The manticore held the windmill’s roof and later its second floor. Outside stands an old dwarven house, destroyed before Adabra arrived, and dwarf cairns for the dead that she tends herself. The winds here are hard enough to have weathered her face. She holds a deed to the land and initially refuses to leave it, because “the natural order requires that they come here.” She later agrees to return to Phandalin with the party.

### Phandalin
The town the party was sent from, and the destination Adabra initially declines to visit before agreeing to return with the party. Vukradin’s plan to kill the manticore, return to Phandalin to demand more money, and only then collect her is floated and rejected. Townmaster Harbin sent a note to his Lordship asking for help, and his Lordship promised it; the party is that help.

### Greenmeadow
Corbin’s home before he became a bandit — “a small, forgettable village called Greenmeadow, nestled in the plains east of the High Forest.” He worked the land his father had worked. It was destroyed by the Uthgardt barbarians of the Black Lion tribe after the Ordning broke, and he survived by being left for dead in a ditch.

## NPCs
### Corbin
A former bandit, now the party’s camp hand, and the chapter’s most interrogated man. Late twenties, gaunt, hollowed-out, in leather armour scavenged from a dead man — the fact that opens the chapter and nearly costs him the armour. His story is that of a farmer from Greenmeadow with a wife named Elarah and a daughter named Lily, who lost both when the Black Lion tribe came, survived under a neighbour’s body, and drifted south for months taking what he could. He is terrified of spiders, deferential to Brewbarry (“Mr Brewbarry”), and nearly eaten by the manticore, which singles him out as the only human in the party’s group.

### Adabra
The woman the party was sent to retrieve, found calling for rescue while under siege in her own windmill but initially unwilling to relocate to Phandalin. A human midwife and apothecary who lives and works at the Umbrage Hill windmill and is devoted to Chauntea. She is warm to guests and initially immovable on substance: she will not go to town, sends Harbin a note declining, and treats her staying as a requirement of the natural order. Her real interest emerges when she asks Soma which side of “the great schism” she is on — naturalist or interventionist — and her entire manner changes at the answer. She regards the killing of monsters for treasure as “a CRIME,” corrects Soma’s druidic education uninvited, and refuses to let the word *barbarian* be used of Brewbarry, renaming him a nomad on the spot. She agrees to return to Phandalin with the party once she believes they are fellow naturalists.

### The Manticore
A talking manticore holding Umbrage Hill, and a negotiator before it is a monster. It states its motive plainly — “I am just trying to get a tasty meal!” — entertains Vukradin’s offer of payment, and proposes a trade: it will take the one human in the party’s group and leave everyone else, including Adabra, alone. Its stated preference for human flesh over other humanoids is confirmed afterwards by Adabra as a real trait of its kind. Valphine wounds it twice with crossbow bolts while it resists two of Vukradin’s spells, wounds Valphine with tail spikes and Brewbarry with its claws, retreats wounded to the windmill’s second floor, and dies attempting to break off and eat Corbin.

### Townmaster Harbin
Not present, but the reason the party is here. He sent a note to his Lordship asking for help and was promised it; Adabra recognises the party as that help. Her written reply is addressed to him, and declines to leave while asking him to reward the party — a reward Vukradin is convinced will not materialise.

### Meril
Soma’s druidic mentor and the source of her sentient staff. She is defensive about the gaps in her training.
