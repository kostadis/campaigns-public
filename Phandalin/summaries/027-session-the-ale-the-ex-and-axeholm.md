# Chapter 27: The Ale, the Ex, and Axeholm

Date: 2025-12-18

## Summary

The party returned to Phandalin after successfully negotiating a peace agreement at the gold mine, where the wererats would work in exchange for cheese. The arrangement transformed Vukradin into a partial owner of a functioning source of “Organic Fair Trade Gold,” although the party still needed to secure the cheese supply that would make the agreement practical. Harbin Wester, the Lord Mayor, greeted them warmly and expressed satisfaction with the recent changes in the community. However, his mood turned serious when Brewbarry inquired about the upcoming ale supply. Harbin warned that the dragon occupying the mountains and the resulting orc incursions were preventing farmers from tending their crops, threatening the town’s precious ale production for the season. Brewbarry immediately declared, “Whatever is gonna get in my way to get some more ale, and I’ll take care of it.”

Harbin reviewed the party’s available objectives: new quests to investigate Dragon Barrow and secure Axeholm fortress, and a return to the Woodland Manse after their earlier retreat. Dragon Barrow was said to contain the magical sword of a warrior who helped slay a green dragon, while Axeholm could potentially be made safe as a refuge if a dragon attack became imminent. The Shrine of Savras also remained unexplored. These missions, Harbin explained, were funded by Neverwinter’s tax system and offered rewards that could help Vukradin establish his music studio. When Vukradin questioned the safety of transporting gold from Neverwinter, Harbin explained that Lord Neverember periodically sent troops and heavily armored caravans carrying supplies, mail, and funds.

Vukradin was appalled that these soldiers protected the gold without dealing with the dragon, the orcs, or the threatened mine. Harbin then revealed Neverwinter’s troubled history—decades of catastrophes, corruption, crime, rebellion, and violence that Lord Neverember was attempting to remedy through progressive reforms and efforts to make the city modern and exciting. Vukradin realized with some embarrassment that he had been so devoted to his musical studies that he had completely missed these major events unfolding around him. Harbin suggested that Lord Neverember might be delighted to meet him after the dragon was dealt with, though he warned that the lord was a very busy man. Vukradin also firmly reasserted his status as head of Phandalin’s Privy Council.

The party then turned their attention to ensuring a steady cheese supply for the wererats at the gold mine. Soma magically communicated with Petunia, Big Al’s prized cow. Petunia confirmed that Big Al treated her well, but said, “I miss the taste of the alfalfa there,” referring to Butterskull Ranch. She also missed the other cows and wanted a quiet place with bovine companionship and good alfalfa. Big Al explained that Petunia had not been the same since the ranch burned down. He mentioned that Qelline Alderleaf, a local farmer, had offered to let Petunia graze on her land for one gold piece per week, though he lacked the funds to pay.

When the party approached Qelline to negotiate, they immediately sensed tension. She recognized that Big Al had sent them instead of coming himself and demanded, “The little shit is too cowardly to come here and negotiate with me, and he sends his pretty-faced bard to talk to me.” Qelline revealed her deep resentment toward him. During his prosperous days at Butterskull Ranch, Big Al’s goods had flooded the local market and damaged her farm’s income. The one-gold-piece fee, she clarified, was not a refusal to host Petunia but an “I-want-it-to-hurt-for-him price.” After some discussion, she offered to reduce the fee to five silver pieces per week, but only if Big Al came to apologize to her personally.

Returning to Big Al with this condition, the party first established the business case for reconciliation. Don-Jon Raskin would become a consistent, large-scale cheese buyer, giving Big Al predictable revenue with which to rebuild Butterskull Ranch. When Big Al asked why Don-Jon needed so much cheese, the party assured him that the reason did not matter as long as his money was good. With a buyer secured, Big Al agreed that an apology was “merited and appropriate.”

The party then pressed him about the actual nature of his relationship with Qelline. Under Vukradin’s extraordinarily persuasive questioning, the large man broke down in tears and confessed the painful truth. He and Qelline had once been engaged to be married, but Big Al had abandoned her the night before their wedding. Though he had always loved her, he hadn’t been ready to settle down at the time. The party insisted he tell Qelline the full truth rather than merely offer a perfunctory apology. Big Al, still emotional, took Petunia and headed to her farm to make amends. When the party considered intervening, Big Al and Qelline glared at them and shut the door, making it clear that the reconciliation was private. Petunia, meanwhile, happily grazed and began producing milk.

The party also learned that Adabra had gone missing and that Ser Kaelen had no knowledge of her whereabouts. Lyra then appeared in town and reunited with the two Emerald Enclave members the party had rescued. Despite this success, she was deeply discouraged about her leadership abilities, citing the treant incident and her men’s capture as evidence of her failures. One of the rescued men reminded her that the party had explicitly said their rescue was Lyra’s plan. Vukradin assured her, “Cheer up, Lyra. They’ll believe you.”

Heartened by this support, Lyra agreed that Vukradin’s persuasive help at the Emerald Enclave might be useful, though they would need to wait until the next day when the organization was meeting. Her confidence quickly began to collapse again as she described widespread planar anomalies and wondered whether she was truly suited to leadership. The party’s proposed visit therefore became both an effort to report on recent events and a mission to convince the Enclave that Lyra was competent.

Ser Kaelen approached the party with important information about the statue they had recovered from the lighthouse. His Order of the Gauntlet contacts had identified it as belonging to Elara Seasong Meliamne, a wealthy noblewoman and art collector currently attending an extravagant, weeks-long gala in Neverwinter hosted by her eccentric cousin, Lord Cassian Meliamne — the two called each other brother and sister. However, he warned that agents of House Margaster—a family notorious for unsavory business and alliances with demonic powers—intended to intercept the statue to embarrass the Meliamnes.

During the conversation, Ser Kaelen also questioned Valphine about her unusual worship of Lathander, including a ritual in which worshippers stared into the sun until their eyes were damaged and then received magical healing. Valphine defended the practice with, “No harm, no foul, Ser Kaelen,” and assured him, “It is painful. But it is safe.” Ser Kaelen declined an invitation to undergo the ritual himself.

Ser Kaelen requested that the party escort the statue to Neverwinter and present it to Elara personally, making sure to offer the regards of the Order of the Gauntlet. He clarified that the party was still expected to receive Elara’s generous reward; the Order merely wanted credit for identifying the statue so it could later seek her financial support. The Order had strong support among the downtrodden but little influence among Neverwinter’s upper classes, who tended to favor the Lords’ Alliance.

Jenna Roscoe overheard and described Lord Cassian’s parties as wonderful, extravagant, and debauched. She also suggested that he might be associated with the Kraken Society, though she dismissed that organization as people who gathered for orgies and shared strange experiences through a collective mind meld. She remained skeptical of claims that the society was a powerful cult connected to interplanar events or mind flayers. Vukradin, ever the opportunist, considered thanking multiple organizations—or whichever organization made the best offer—for assisting in the statue’s recovery.

That evening, the party performed at the local tavern. While Valphine struggled with her performance and disappointed the “Valphiners,” Vukradin’s music energized his fanboys. Brewbarry joined the show with impressive physical displays and dance moves that the audience began imitating. The performance earned applause, cheering, coin, and free drinks. Brewbarry’s attempt to leverage the successful show into still more free alcohol eventually met a firm refusal from the tavernkeeper.

After resting, the party chose Axeholm as its next destination. The Dragon Slayer Sword remained attractive, but Axeholm was nearby, and completing one more quest would advance the party to fifth level. Their intended visit to Lyra and the Emerald Enclave remained pending, with the exact meaning of going there “tomorrow” left unresolved.

The following day, they embarked on a day-long journey to Axeholm, traveling across grassy lowlands that skirted the rocky foothills of the Sword Mountains. Cool winds and rain from the Sword Coast made the trek unpleasant, but it passed without incident. Upon arrival, they found the entrance to the ancient dwarven fortress—a dark, fifteen-foot-wide passageway carved into the base of a gray mountain, flanked by forty-foot-high bulwarks of smooth, seamless stone. A rusted iron portcullis blocked their way, and the arrow slits carved into the walls showed no signs of occupation.

Brewbarry attempted to force the portcullis open with an incredible display of strength. Even his mighty effort could not overcome the massive stone block jamming the mechanism, though he did manage to move the gate and emerged feeling appropriately powerful. The party realized they would need an alternative approach. Soma considered using Wild Shape but had no suitable form prepared, so she instead summoned a rat familiar to scout through the narrow arrow slits. Establishing a telepathic link that would allow her to see and hear through the familiar, Soma sent the creature into Axeholm. The session ended just as the rat began exploring the darkness beyond the fortress walls.

## NPCs

### Harbin Wester

The Lord Mayor and townmaster of Phandalin who is pleased with the party’s recent accomplishments. As Lord Mayor, he provides quests funded by Neverwinter’s tax system; Vukradin chairs the Privy Council. Harbin is concerned about the dragon and orc threats impacting the town’s farming and ale supply, and has knowledge of Neverwinter’s political landscape and Lord Neverember’s efforts to rebuild the city. He knows Brewbarry’s priorities well enough to use the threatened ale supply as motivation. Under Vukradin’s questioning, he became increasingly deflated while defending Neverwinter’s caravans and political system.

### Petunia

A cow owned by Big Al, capable of producing high-quality milk for cheese. Through magic, Petunia can communicate and expresses happiness with Big Al’s care while missing the alfalfa from her former home at Butterskull Ranch. She also misses the other cows and prefers a quiet environment without annoying intruders. She is central to the party’s plan to supply cheese to the wererats at the gold mine. Once taken to Qelline’s farm, she happily grazed and began producing milk while the humans addressed their complicated history.

### Big Al

The owner of Petunia whose Butterskull Ranch was burned down, leaving him financially struggling and unable to pay even Petunia’s grazing expenses. He has a complicated romantic history with Qelline Alderleaf, having left her the night before their wedding. He insists their old business dispute was actually a personal matter. Despite his past mistakes, he is willing to apologize and make amends when motivated by the prospect of Don-Jon becoming a reliable bulk cheese customer and providing predictable income for rebuilding his ranch.

### Qelline Alderleaf

A local farm lady in Phandalin who holds deep resentment toward Big Al due to both business competition and personal betrayal. She was abandoned by Big Al the night before their wedding, and her farm suffered when his ranch was successful. Her original one-gold-piece grazing fee was an intentionally punitive “I-want-it-to-hurt-for-him price.” She is willing to provide grazing land and alfalfa for Petunia at a reduced rate of five silver pieces per week if Big Al personally apologizes to her.

### Lyra

A member of the Emerald Enclave experiencing a crisis of confidence in her leadership abilities. She has suffered setbacks including the treant incident and the capture of two of her men, whom the party rescued. She is investigating planar anomalies that many others appear to be ignoring. Despite her self-doubt, she is willing to believe in herself when given encouragement and plans to address her standing with the Emerald Enclave. Her renewed confidence is fragile, and she quickly returns to questioning whether she should remain a leader.

### Ser Kaelen

A member of the Order of the Gauntlet who uses his organization’s contacts to gather information. He traced the provenance of the lighthouse statue and is grateful for the party’s work. He seeks to leverage their return of the valuable object to gain future donations and greater upper-class influence for his order without depriving the party of its expected reward. He is curious and somewhat alarmed by Valphine’s unusual Lathander worship practices, particularly the ritual involving staring into the sun until healing is required.

### Elara Seasong Meliamne

A wealthy noblewoman and art collector currently in Neverwinter attending a weeks-long gala hosted by her eccentric cousin. She is the rightful owner of a statue recovered by the party from the lighthouse and is expected to be generous to those who return it. Both the party and the Order of the Gauntlet hope to gain rewards or influence through the statue’s safe delivery.

### Lord Cassian Meliamne

Elara Seasong Meliamne’s eccentric cousin who hosts extravagant and debauched parties in Neverwinter. Jenna Roscoe speaks highly of the extravagance of his events while implying that his private conduct is scandalous. He is rumored to be involved with the Kraken Society.

### Jenna Roscoe

An individual who overhears conversations and provides commentary on social matters. She is familiar with Lord Cassian’s reputation and Neverwinter’s upper-class social scene. She is dismissive of the Kraken Society as a serious threat, viewing its members as people who gather for orgies and collective mind-meld experiences rather than as a dangerous cult manipulating interplanar events. She similarly dismisses claims connecting it to mind flayers.

### Toblen Stonehill

The owner of the tavern in Phandalin where the party performs. He is pleased with their performances and provides free drinks as rewards, though he expresses concern about excessive drinking and recognizes that even Brewbarry’s supply of complimentary alcohol must have limits.

### Lord Neverember

The lord of Neverwinter who is attempting to rebuild and modernize the city after decades of catastrophes. He promotes the idea of a progressive, modern, exciting “new Neverwinter,” although the city still struggles with corruption, crime, rebellion, and violence. His government oversees the tax system that funds quests in Phandalin and sends troops with well-armored caravans carrying money, supplies, and mail.

### Don-Jon Raskin

The mine representative and prospective large-scale buyer of cheese from Big Al. His consistent purchases would provide Big Al with a stable revenue stream to rebuild his ranch operations while supplying the wererats who now work at the gold mine. The party declined to explain his enormous need for cheese, arguing that his reasons did not matter as long as he paid reliably.

### Adabra

Adabra has gone missing, with Ser Kaelen having no information about her whereabouts. She carried no sending stone that could be used to contact or trace her. Jenna Roscoe regards Adabra’s warnings about cults, planar events, and mind flayers as unreliable alarmism.

## Locations

### Phandalin

A small frontier town serving as the party’s base of operations. The town features a tavern where performances are held, and is home to various NPCs including Harbin Wester, Big Al, Qelline Alderleaf, and Spider-Man. The community is currently concerned about ale supply because dragon and orc activity is preventing farmers from safely tending crops. It also serves as the center of the party’s increasingly complicated cheese-based supply network.

### Butterskull Ranch

Big Al’s former ranch that was burned to the ground. Petunia the cow misses the alfalfa fields, quiet environment, and other cows from this location. It was once a successful operation whose goods competed heavily with Qelline Alderleaf’s farm. Big Al cannot safely return while the dragon and orcs remain active, but he hopes the income from Don-Jon’s cheese purchases will help him rebuild it.

### Neverwinter

A major city that has suffered catastrophes over the past fifty years. Under Lord Neverember’s leadership, the city is attempting to become modern and progressive, though it struggles with corruption, crime, rebellion, and violence. The city sends protected caravans with gold, mail, and supplies to Phandalin. It is home to wealthy art collectors like Elara Seasong Meliamne and hosts the extravagant gala at which the party hopes to return her statue.

### Qelline Alderleaf’s Farm

A local farm in Phandalin owned by Qelline Alderleaf. The farm offers alfalfa and grazing land for Petunia and has a history of business rivalry with Big Al’s former Butterskull Ranch. It also becomes the site of Big Al and Qelline’s private confrontation and possible reconciliation while Petunia grazes happily outside.

### Emerald Enclave

A faction headquarters located somewhere in the Neverwinter Wood. Lyra is a member of this nature-focused organization, and the party plans to visit during its next meeting to help restore her standing and explain the rescue of her two subordinates. The exact timing of that visit remained unresolved when the party departed for Axeholm.

### Axeholm

An ancient dwarven fortress carved into the base of a dark gray mountain in the foothills of the Sword Mountains. The entrance features a dark, fifteen-foot-wide passageway flanked by forty-foot-high bulwarks of smooth, seamless stone. A rusted iron portcullis blocks the main entrance and is jammed by a massive stone block. Arrow slits line the bulwarks but show no signs of current occupation. A rat familiar entered through one of those slits, but the interior had not yet been described when the session ended.

### Sword Mountains

A mountain range whose rocky foothills the party traversed on their journey to Axeholm. Grassy lowlands skirt the foothills, and cool winds and rain from the Sword Coast made the party’s day-long journey unpleasant.

### Dragon Barrow

A barrow mound of a warrior whose magical Dragon Slayer Sword is said to have helped fell a green dragon. This location is north of Phandalin and lies along the route toward Neverwinter. Brewbarry is the party member most likely to wield the sword, making its recovery an attractive step before confronting the dragon.

### Woodland Manse

A quest location that forms a triangle with Axeholm and Dragon Barrow, representing another available quest for the party. The party had previously retreated from dangers there, and the location remains an unresolved objective associated with Falcon the Hunter.

### Shrine of Savras

An unexplored area that the party has not yet visited, representing another potential quest location. The completion-minded party explicitly added exploring it to their quest tracking.

## Items

### Statue from Lighthouse

A valuable art piece recovered by the party from a wreck near the lighthouse, identified through the Order of the Gauntlet’s contacts as belonging to Elara Seasong Meliamne, a wealthy art collector in Neverwinter. A magical sword located in the Dragon Barrow, belonging to a warrior whose weapon helped fell a green dragon. The party considers retrieving this weapon as preparation for confronting the dragon threatening Phandalin. Brewbarry is the party’s apparent candidate to wield it and said he would feel more comfortable facing the dragon with the proper tool in hand.

A heavy, rusted iron gate blocking the main entrance to Axeholm. It is jammed by a massive stone block, preventing Brewbarry from lifting it open even with an exceptional Athletics result of 25. Brewbarry did manage to move it slightly, but forcing a complete opening could require months of work, requiring the party to find an alternative entry method.
