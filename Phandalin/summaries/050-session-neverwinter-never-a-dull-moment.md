# Chapter 50: Neverwinter, Never a Dull Moment

Date: 2026-07-29

## Summary
The party arrived in Neverwinter with a long-overdue task weighing on their minds — the return of an ancient, inscribed statue belonging to House Meliamne, recovered from a sunken shipwreck near a lighthouse what felt like a lifetime ago. Before setting out, they took stock of the city around them. The political landscape of the North was a tangled web of competing factions: the Lords’ Alliance, a coalition of powerful city-states committed to firm borders and centralized governance, currently led by Open Lord Lady Laeral Silverhand of Waterdeep — with Lord Dagult Neverember still on the council after losing the leading position during the War of the Dragons, alongside figures such as Grand Duke Ulder Ravengard of Baldur’s Gate and Taern “Thunderspell” Hornblade; the Zhentarim, an amoral organization that viewed unregulated markets as a virtue — not criminals, merely of the view “that market dynamics should not be constrained by regulatory systems”; the Harpers, a secretive group of anarcho-syndicalists devoted to decentralized power, whose “idea of a perfect world is everybody lives in a nice village, and nobody bothers each other”; the Emerald Enclave, still fractured in the wake of the War of the Dragon and conflicted over what the natural order even is; and the Order of the Gauntlet, the only faction considered unambiguously good-hearted, though their co-optation by the Lords’ Alliance after the War of the Giants had left many questioning their independence — and in a world where every faction has an angle, nobody trusts the good guys. Vukradin, the celebrated bard, had risen to considerable fame in the city — though for his earlier work rather than his more recent exploits — while Brewbarry’s distinctive Uthgardtian look had become so fashionable in Neverwinter that people on the street were dressing in imitation of him. The party set out long-rested and, in the DM’s words, “complete with, like, all violent spells,” with Boney the skeletal horse permitted in the streets on the grounds that “he doesn’t poop.” Prutha, no longer with the party, was off on a crusade to convert the orcs — a departure that, the DM noted ominously, “will have consequences.”

The group made their way through the Protector’s Enclave, a wealthy district bristling with signs of power and privilege, before crossing into the grungier dock area beyond. As they passed, Vukradin and Brewbarry caught sight of the Common Chord — their old haunt, once a desperate, half-empty tavern where Vukradin had been allowed to play and then promptly thrown out without pay, his satisfaction-guaranteed refund policy having “created a problem.” It was now, inexplicably, the hardest venue in all of Neverwinter to get a ticket to — a city guard had already begged Vukradin for tickets on their arrival. The party pressed on without stopping, their destination the Moonstone Mask, Neverwinter’s most luxurious inn, perched atop one of the city’s three great earthmotes — massive chunks of landthat had been torn skyward by the Spellplague of 1385 and tethered above the city by enormous chains, lest they drift off over the Sword Coast. The other two motes — the Pirates’ Skyhold and Fisher’s Float — hung nearby, and the party mused, only half in jest, that three floating fortresses were perfectly positioned for assaulting the town: “when the drow invade,” Vukradin observed, “they’re gonna be welcomed as liberators.” The DM noted dryly that “never has Castle Never been conquered” — to which Vukradin replied, “Challenge accepted.” The floating island was connected to the docks by a bridge, and as the party crossed it, the grandeur of the establishment came into full view.

Inside the Moonstone Mask, the staff moved with practiced elegance, each dressed in sheer black and wearing the inn’s namesake moonstone-lined half-masks alongside delicate amulets. A staff member approached the group and immediately recognized Vukradin — “Oh my. Vukradin, you’ve come to our establishment” — offering refreshments with a warm smile. Her expression cooled, however, when the party asked for Lady Elara Meliamne — the woman they had come to see. “Didn’t you hear?” she said. Elara had been killed eight days prior, during what was supposed to be a grand festival that “ended in a catastrophic, catastrophic, spectacular and bloody disaster.” The details were murky and contradictory: some said it was a love affair gone wrong, others a raid, still others a magical accident. What was clear was that “Lord Neverember has declared that the death of Elara was a tragic accident, and that we were all to mourn her passing and move on.” The staff member noted, with a careful pause, that Elara’s death had been so severe that even resurrection was beyond reach — and that Cassian had not seemed eager to pursue it regardless. She directed the party to Lord Cassian, Elara’s cousin — the two called each other brother and sister — who was seated at the finest table in the house. Along the way she took Brewbarry for a daring fashion statement rather than the man himself, poured him “a very large pouring of something that tastes amazing,” and greeted the golden-eyed Valphine with “Oh, you’re a blessing upon me.”

Lord Cassian, a sea elf nobleman, rose to greet them as they approached — and got straight to the point: “You have the statue. Good. My cousin told me someone was bringing it. She was killed. Eight days ago. House Margaster. I thought you should know before you handed it to anyone.” When Vukradin presented the statue, Cassian’s composure cracked ever so slightly — “She was 12 when she last saw this,” he said, setting it on the table — and he spoke of his cousin in a deeply personal way, describing how she had last held the artifact as a child, hundreds of years ago, and how ecstatic she had been when word reached her that it had resurfaced. She had been eagerly awaiting the party’s arrival at her festival, he said, but they had been otherwise occupied — saving the world from a dragon. Vukradin insisted that no payment was necessary — “Money? You were gonna pay me for the statue? … This is your statue, and I’m returning it to you, because it’s the right thing to do” — but Cassian would not hear of it. He pressed fifty gold into the hands of each of the party’s companions as a gesture of gratitude, and made a separate donation to Vukradin’s music studio so as not to offend the bard’s sense of honor, noting “it would be insulting to you, Vukradin, to give you money for this.” Drinks arrived at the click of his fingers, and the party settled in — Brewbarry pleading, “Let’s stay just for a little while, please.”

Sensing an opportunity, Vukradin and the group launched into an impromptu musical performance for the assembled elite of Neverwinter. The rolls were not kind, but the crowd responded with enthusiastic applause, though Valphine — her eyes now glowing with the golden light of Lathander — could see through the polished veneer. Some patrons were quietly over-intellectualizing the missed notes, and others were hiding their distaste behind practiced smiles, unwilling to appear uncultured — or gauche — in such company. Brewbarry finished his drink, stood, applauded thunderously, and scanned the house for anyone not clapping. Vukradin, undeterred, pivoted immediately from the performance to a fundraising pitch (a natural-feeling 20 on Persuasion), extolling the importance of supporting the arts and encouraging the wealthy patrons — “Please get out your checkbooks,” Brewbarry added — to open their purses for his upcoming music studio, the funds to be held in a properly restricted, earmarked trust. The appeal was remarkably successful, and a considerable sum was raised before the drinks had even been refilled; the studio fund stood at 550 gold by evening’s end. The party also extended an open invitation to the crowd to attend a performance and sermon at the Spire of the Morninglord at dawn.

When the music faded and the applause died down, Lord Cassian leaned in and shared something that had clearly been weighing on him. Before her death, Elara had been investigating the malfunction of the Planar Manifold — the “displacement manifold,” an interplanar shipping device at the docks operated as a commercial service by the Commission, which had gone dark about seven weeks prior, cutting off regional distribution for the Lionshield Coster, their primary client. “Nobody official will say why. She believes the timing wasn’t coincidental.” She had met the device’s regular technician — a gnome — twice at the docks: “Smaller, older, very precise, the kind of person who asks questions that go past the job,” a man who “seemed more interested in why the planar routing worked than whether it was functioning, like he was mapping something.” He had not been seen since the manifold stopped, and Cassian had no name for him, only his cousin’s description and her unease. Cassian himself preferred that “when it comes to the Commission, one should keep one’s distance,” but he asked the party, quietly and with evident gratitude for what they had already done, to look into whether there was any connection between the shipping disruption and Elara’s death. When Vukradin mentioned they had come under the auspices of the Order of the Gauntlet, Cassian smiled: “They are truly one of the few heroes of the realms.”

Before the party could fully absorb this new charge, a man in a well-tailored dark green suit arrived at their table. He introduced himself as Cullen Sharpe — spelling it out, C-U-L-L-E-N, S-H-A-R-P-E — an associate of House Margaster, and presented what he claimed was a Harbor Authority writ asserting a prior salvage claim on the very statue the party had just returned: “No need for this to become complicated.” (“Oh, you’re the ones who slaughtered Elara,” Vukradin said flatly; “No, we had nothing to do with that,” Cullen replied.) The party examined the documents — Valphine with a 16 on Perception, Vukradin with a natural 20 — and found them deeply suspicious: the hallmarks of a racket rather than a legitimate legal claim. Lord Cassian, visibly rattled, clearly recognized the power House Margaster wielded in the city. Despite the party’s silent disapproval, he paid Cullen two hundred gold pieces to make the matter go away, and Cullen transferred the writ with a satisfied smile. Cassian insisted, looking very nervous, “I was not swindled… It’s totally legitimate. This is exactly how things happen here.” The party, recognizing this was no place for a private conversation, arranged through Valphine to call at his house for “a cup of tea” — “I think that would be an excellent idea,” he said. Before departing, Cullen turned to the party with a business proposition — House Margaster, he explained, “has developed considerable expertise in regional distribution since the unfortunate disruption at the docks,” and would welcome a conversation about handling goods recovered in the Phandalin hinterlands: “You find things that are missing… Our organization would be happy to help.” Brewbarry let out a very large burp and demanded who could attest to this expertise; “All you have to do is ask around,” Cullen replied, “and everyone will say House Margaster always delivers the goods.” The party listened politely and said they would think about it. Cullen, invited to donate to the music studio, smiled politely, made no donation, and walked away.

As the group rose to leave, a steward in the livery of the Lord Protector intercepted them near the door, reading their names and honorifics from a notice — Vukradin, Brewbarry, “Valphine of the Blessed,” and “Soma, the protector of the Glades.” He introduced himself as Aldus and delivered a formal summons on behalf of Lord Dagult Neverember, Lord Protector of Neverwinter. Neverember, Aldus explained, had been following the party’s activities with considerable interest (“It’s about frickin’ time,” said Vukradin) — particularly their economic impact and the questions raised by the Candlekeep Report regarding adventuring parties and the circulation of wealth, a matter with obvious bearing on the party’s unofficial rearrangement of Phandalin’s government. There were, Aldus hinted delicately, “comparisons some sages have drawn between his financial philosophy and that of a large reptile” — a dragon, hoarding gold — before hastily apologizing: “I was just conveying his lordship’s concerns.” The party resisted the suggestion of a morning meeting (“Are you arresting me? … Am I free to go?” — “Of course you are”), citing sacred rites at the Spire of the Morninglord and a full schedule of obligations. Vukradin, ever the showman, proposed instead (with a 22 on Persuasion) that the Lord Protector host a charitable gala to benefit the music studio — a soiree where business could be discussed over fine food and generous donations. Aldus, clearly charmed — “I must say, I am quite the fan” — agreed to carry the proposal back to his lordship and suggested that it would work best if Vukradin and the group attended a private dinner the following evening where Vukradin could pitch to Lord Dagult directly, promising to be very supportive of the idea and get his Lordship on board. In a moment of inspired goodwill, Vukradin offered Aldus an autograph, but the steward — looking as if he were about to cry — was too embarrassed to accept it for himself and claimed it was for his daughter: “I don’t, but my daughter would.” When Vukradin asked for her name, Aldus stammered out “Alducia” — a name suspiciously close to his own, which Vukradin immediately realized was entirely invented on the spot. Vukradin produced the personalized autograph anyway — signed, at Brewbarry’s suggestion, “with love” — and the steward departed visibly moved, having taken the party’s dietary requirements (“Meat, lots of it,” said Brewbarry; “not too many greens”; alcohol required, and a vintage Aldus did not recognize) and promising “a meal that you will never forget.”

With the evening winding down and their obligations mounting, the party took stock of what lay ahead. They had a dinner with the Lord Protector to prepare for, a dawn performance at the Spire of the Morninglord to deliver — “We’re just gonna bust in through the door, and then take the service,” Valphine declared — a private meeting with Lord Cassian at his residence to arrange, and a growing mystery surrounding a dead noblewoman, a missing gnome, and a broken interplanar shipping device. Their next stop would be the counting house, where Brewbarry’s letter of recommendation waited to open doors into the disrupted supply chain of the Lionshield Coster. Neverwinter, it seemed, was not going to let them rest.

## Scenes
### Political Landscape and Party Status
#### The party gathers in Neverwinter and the Dungeon Master recaps the current political factions and the party’s recent rise in fame.
- The Dungeon Master explains the current state of the major political factions: the Lords’ Alliance, the Zhentarim, the Harpers, the Emerald Enclave, and the Order of the Gauntlet.
- The Lords’ Alliance is a council of the city-states of the North — Waterdeep, Neverwinter, Silverymoon, Baldur’s Gate and others — “the plutocrats,” committed to “firm borders, policing, proper governance, and control.” Its Open Lord is Lady Laeral Silverhand of Waterdeep; Lord Dagult Neverember remains on the council but lost the leading position during the War of the Dragons. Other named members include Grand Duke Ulder Ravengard of Baldur’s Gate and Taern “Thunderspell” Hornblade.

- The Emerald Enclave is conflicted over what the natural order is, torn apart by the War of the Dragon.
- The Order of the Gauntlet is “the only group that qualifies as unalloyed good guys,” but was co-opted by the Lords’ Alliance after the War of the Giants — and in a world of angles, “Nobody trusts the good guys. - The party discusses their current reputation in Neverwinter, noting that Brewbarry, a male goliath, has become a known cultural figure and Vukradin has acquired fame for his early musical work — “not for his current stuff.”
- The group identifies their most urgent task: returning a recovered statue to the Meliamne family.
- A brief discussion occurs regarding local dining, specifically a tavern known for Tortle home cooking — prompting Soma to clarify the naming convention: “It’s like Girl Scout cookies aren’t made of Girl Scouts, come on.”

## Locations
### Neverwinter
A major city-state on the Sword Coast featuring three floating earthmotes created by the Spellplague of 1385, tethered above the city by chains. Home to the Lords’ Alliance and currently governed by Lord Dagult Neverember. Landmarks noted this session include the Counting House, Dragonscales, the Orc’s Plaque, and the Spire of the Morninglord. Castle Never’s name carries a boast: never has Castle Never been conquered — a fact that, per the DM, “creates conviction among those who should know better.”

### The Moonstone Mask
Neverwinter’s finest and most luxurious inn, situated on a floating earthmote tethered to the docks of the Protector’s Enclave and reached by bridge and teleportation circles. Famous for its staff — uniformly beautiful, of mixed genders and species, dressed in sheer black — who wear moonstone-lined half-masks and amulets, and known as a gathering place for the rich and powerful to chill out and relax. The first floor holds a warm dining room with an impressive hearth and kitchens dedicated to haute cuisine, with a grand spiraling staircase up to the guest rooms. In his heyday, the Mask was one of the very few establishments that snubbed Vukradin. The site of the party’s meeting with Lord Cassian.

### The Common Chord
A tavern that was once a desperate, low-end establishment where Vukradin used to perform for little to no pay — they would periodically let him play, then throw him out without paying, a situation aggravated by his money-back satisfaction guarantee. It has since become the hardest place in the city to get a ticket, now one of Neverwinter’s most exclusive venues; a city guard has already asked Vukradin to get him tickets. Also the place where Brewbarry canonically discovered, by contrast, that ale elsewhere could be good — the Common Chord’s ale was “piss.”

### Protector’s Enclave
A wealthy district in Neverwinter characterized by signs of power and riches, contrasting sharply with the grungier dock area just beyond it — the transition as abrupt as finding the tracks and crossing them.

### Neverwinter Docks
The site of the ‘displacement manifold,’ an interplanar shipping device that stopped functioning about seven weeks ago, causing regional supply chain disruptions for the Lionshield Coster. The Commission operated the manifold as a commercial service: goods arrive in Neverwinter by sea, and the manifold distributes them to stores across the region. Elara Meliamne met the manifold’s gnome technician here twice while tracking the shipping gap.

### Spire of the Morninglord
A temple dedicated to Lathander in Neverwinter where the party plans to perform sacred rites and a musical sermon at dawn — on their own terms, busting in through the door to take the service. The party invited both the Moonstone Mask crowd and Aldus’s daughter to attend.

### The Counting House
A financial institution in Neverwinter where the party intends to secure capital and investigate trade disruptions related to the Lionshield Coster. Brewbarry carries a letter of recommendation for the visit; the quest tracker lists it under “Capital Gang: secure capital for Brewbarry’s bathrobe business.”

## NPCs
### Lord Dagult Neverember
The Lord Protector of Neverwinter and a member of the Lords’ Alliance council — formerly the Alliance’s leading lord until he lost that position during the War of the Dragons. He officially declared Lady Elara’s death a tragic accident and ordered the city to mourn and move on. Though not appearing in person, he sends a formal summons to the party to discuss their wealth, cultural influence, and the economic situation in Phandalin — including the party’s unofficial change of Phandalin’s government. He is reportedly concerned that the party is hoarding gold like a dragon, citing the Candlekeep Report on adventuring parties and the circulation of wealth.

### Lady Elara Meliamne
The deceased sea elf noblewoman of House Meliamne and cousin of Lord Cassian. She was killed eight days before the party’s arrival in a violent incident during a festival, officially ruled a tragic accident by Lord Neverember — though Cassian flatly names House Margaster as responsible. Her death was beyond even resurrection. She last held the family statue when she was 12 — several hundred years ago — and had been ecstatic and eagerly awaiting the party’s arrival at her festival when word came it had resurfaced. Before her death, she had been actively investigating the malfunction of the Planar Manifold, meeting its gnome technician twice at the docks and recording her unease about him in writing.

### Lord Cassian Meliamne
A sea elf nobleman and the grieving cousin of the late Lady Elara, holding the best table at the Moonstone Mask. He is deeply moved by the return of the family statue, which his cousin had last held as a child hundreds of years ago, and speaks of it in intensely personal terms — the party’s insight confirms every word. He rewards the party generously (50 gold each, plus a studio donation framed so as not to insult Vukradin) and asks them to investigate a possible connection between the shipping disruptions at the docks and his cousin’s death, though he personally keeps his distance from the Commission. He regards the Order of the Gauntlet as “truly one of the few heroes of the realms.” He appears easily intimidated by the political power of House Margaster — paying their 200-gold shakedown while insisting, very nervously, that “this is exactly how things happen here” — and accepts Valphine’s invitation to continue the conversation privately at his residence. Not much of a music fan, as it turns out.

### Moonstone Mask Staff Member
A polite and observant woman dressed in sheer black with a moonstone mask. She recognizes Vukradin’s fame — “for you, Mr. Vukradin, anything you would like” — informs the party of Lady Elara’s passing (her eyes growing suddenly cold at the name), relays the competing rumors and the official line, and directs them to Lord Cassian. She mistakes Brewbarry for someone dressed in the popular ‘Brewbarry aesthetic’ rather than the actual person — praising it as “a counterstatement to the statement” — and treats the golden-eyed Valphine as a living blessing: “Oh, you’re a blessing upon me.”

### Cullen Sharpe
A well-tailored associate of House Margaster in a dark green suit who arrives at the Moonstone Mask to assert a legal salvage claim on the recovered statue, addressing each party member by name and denying any Margaster involvement in Elara’s death. The party suspects his documents are fraudulent — a natural 20 confirms “this is a racket.” After successfully extorting 200 gold from Lord Cassian and transferring the writ, he attempts to recruit the party into a regional distribution partnership for goods found in the Phandalin hinterlands (“House Margaster always delivers the goods”), hinting there “might even be an opportunity in the city.” Invited to donate to the music studio, he smiles politely, makes no donation, and walks away.

### Aldus
A steward in the livery of the Lord Protector of Neverwinter. He is polite, professional, and a secret fan of Vukradin’s music, acting as the intermediary for Lord Neverember’s formal summons to the party — and enduring Brewbarry’s heckling with strained composure. Charmed by Vukradin’s 22 Persuasion, he pledges to champion the charitable-gala idea with his lordship (“I must say, I am quite the fan”) and promises “a meal that you will never forget.” He is a secret fan of Vukradin’s music, though he embarrassedly claimed his autograph request was for an invented daughter named Alducia — looking as if he were about to cry when he thought he’d missed his chance.

### Alducia
An apparently fictional daughter invented on the spot by Aldus out of embarrassment when receiving an autograph from Vukradin — her name suspiciously the feminine form of his own. The autograph was signed to her “with love” regardless.

### Orsik
A former adventurer mentioned as having retired to Parnast, currently living in peace and apparently being shielded from the Lords’ Alliance by a friend, which conveniently allows the Lords’ Alliance to benefit from his ignorance of his past accomplishments — the Alliance “thinks it’s very convenient that he’s become ignorant of what he’s accomplished.”

### Prutha
A former party member who has departed on a crusade to convert orcs, no longer traveling with the group. The Dungeon Master notes this will have consequences.

### Boney
The party’s skeletal horse companion, permitted to wander Neverwinter with the party on the grounds that “he doesn’t poop.” As Soma insists: “Boney’s just a normal horse. We don’t take him inside, is all.”

## Items
### Meliamne Family Statue
An ancient, inscribed family heirloom belonging to House Meliamne, recovered by the party from a sunken shipwreck near a lighthouse long ago — with Soma doing the diving. It holds immense sentimental value, as Lady Elara last held it when she was a child — she was 12, hundreds of years before it was recovered — and was ecstatic when word came it had resurfaced. The party returned it to Lord Cassian at the Moonstone Mask, refusing payment on principle (“this is your statue, and I’m returning it to you, because it’s the right thing to do”), though it briefly became the subject of a fraudulent legal dispute when House Margaster claimed salvage rights over it.

### Harbor Authority Writ
A legal document presented by Cullen Sharpe of House Margaster asserting a prior salvage claim on the Meliamne family statue, offered for review with a promise of a fair market rate and “no need for this to become complicated.” The party suspected it was part of a fraudulent shakedown — Vukradin’s natural 20 on Perception revealed the documents as a racket — and Lord Cassian ultimately paid 200 gold to have it transferred to him, settling the dispute and placing him in full, documented possession.

### Moonstone Masks
Namesake half-masks worn by all staff of the Moonstone Mask inn, lined with glowing moonstones. Each staff member also wears an accompanying amulet — inspected by the party and confirmed not to be the mark of any dark god or faction.

### Vukradin’s Autograph
A personalized note written by the famous bard Vukradin for Alducia, the (likely invented) daughter of Aldus, the Lord Protector’s steward — signed “with love” at Brewbarry’s suggestion. It was given as a gesture of goodwill during negotiations over the dinner meeting with Lord Neverember, and left the steward visibly moved.

### Letter of Recommendation
A document carried by the party — specifically Brewbarry — intended to help them investigate disruptions within the Lionshield Coster’s supply chain at the counting house, where Brewbarry also hopes to secure capital for his bathrobe business and, if needed, “whack a few heads.”
