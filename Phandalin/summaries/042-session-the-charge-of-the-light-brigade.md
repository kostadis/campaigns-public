# Chapter 42: The Charge of the Light Brigade

Date: Apr 29th, 2026

## Summary

In the aftermath of the tense and deeply personal confrontation at the Druid Council, Vukradin returned to Phandalin with something weighing on his mind. Adabra had demanded repayment of the five gold pieces she had previously paid him for the report she now disputed. Unwilling to let the matter rest, Vukradin carefully counted out six gold pieces—the disputed five-gold fee plus one more for interest—sealed them in an envelope addressed to Adabra, and sent the whole thing off through the local postal system. The gold, naturally, was organic fair trade gold. “Now that she’s lost, she can have her money back,” Vukradin explained. The gesture was deliberately petty, and everyone agreed it was also extremely funny.

Vukradin admitted that Adabra’s assault had left him feeling betrayed. He had expected something closer to an evidentiary hearing and had anticipated opposition, but not an attack so viciously personal. His repayment therefore settled more than a financial dispute: it closed the account on his terms, with dignity, interest, and impeccably sourced currency.

Back at the Stonehill Tavern, Lyra was practically glowing with admiration. She had accompanied the party back from the Glade, announced that Vukradin was now her hero, and seemed to have developed a crush on him. What moved her most was his refusal to allow his honor to be sullied. She interpreted his walkout not as a retreat but as a brilliant rhetorical maneuver: he had absorbed Adabra’s attack, deprived her of her intended target, and left Soma free to take control of the proceedings. “It was brilliant!” she insisted, calling for Spider-Man to bring Vukradin at least two beers. Brewbarry received two as well, while Soma requested tea and Valphine maintained her customary aloofness.

Vukradin modestly protested that Soma had done the hard work and that he had merely “walked out at the right time,” but Lyra would not be dissuaded. The evening continued with a musical performance from Vukradin and Valphine. It was not spectacular, but it was solid and well received, and Lyra remarked that the other druids had been disappointed not to hear Vukradin perform before he left the Council.

The party also found time to report to Ser Kaelen, informing him of Sister Kaella’s escape and the tragic death of Corbin. The mood was somber on that front, and Soma in particular carried the weight of it. Although some of the party wanted Corbin avenged, the Druids had regarded his death as a preventable tragedy while upholding Soma’s broader decision to prioritize the regional crisis. Sister Kaella had nevertheless vanished, and the group agreed that tracking her down was a task better suited to organizations with broader reach.

That suggestion opened the door for Jenna Roscoe, a composed and usually unflappable representative of the Lord’s Alliance, to offer its assistance as a “courtesy” carrying “no obligations.” She spoke about the power and prestige the organization could bring to someone like Vukradin, including what it might do for his music, career, and planned studio. Vukradin cheerfully redirected the conversation toward donations for Valphine’s church. The suggestion that the Alliance fund what the group readily admitted was “kind of a torture cult”—or, more precisely, “a masochism cult”—briefly cracked Jenna’s composure. She recovered, clarified that the Alliance’s real interest was in Vukradin, and resumed recruiting.

When Jenna turned her attention to Brewbarry and began musing about the symbolic value of an Uthgardtian barbarian accepting the Alliance’s “civilizing” influence, Brewbarry replied, “Despite my looks, I’m not an idiot.” He then delivered a long, resonant burp. Jenna Roscoe got the point and walked away.

With the evening’s business concluded, the party reviewed the threats still facing the region: the ruined, Talosian-occupied Woodland Manse, Falcon’s overrun hunting lodge, the possibly still-occupied Shrine of Savras, and the Circle of Thunder—the lair of the evil half-orc spellcasters serving Talos. Vukradin favored following Xanth’s lead, in part because Falcon still had not personally apologized to him. The Circle of Thunder was unquestionably dangerous; the party remembered being badly beaten by Talosian spellcasters before. Nevertheless, they chose to confront them.

Vukradin used Sending to contact Xanth, delighting in his magical equivalent of a new cell phone. When Lyra reacted with horror to the prospect of him sending messages to everyone all day, he celebrated its virtues: “Limited range! Any plane of existence!” Xanth directed the party to meet him where the Triboar Trail turned toward Phandalin.

Before departing, the party reviewed its supplies. They had one regular potion of healing, one Potion of Greater Healing, one Potion of Superior Healing, and one “scented” potion of healing. They considered obtaining protection against lightning, having repeatedly suffered lightning attacks from Talosians, but lacked the money. Valphine could prepare Protection from Energy, though it would protect only one creature and require concentration. Ultimately, the party set out with what they had, accompanied by Boney.

They met Xanth at the agreed-upon spot, and together the group hiked thirty miles into the forest before making camp for the night. Xanth praised the party’s recent accomplishments and Soma’s performance before nearly launching into a discussion of Meril, whose personal story was firmly declared “flavor” rather than the focus of the campaign. Xanth then guided the group to a safe campsite and regaled them with stories of birds, flowers, heroes, Neverwinter’s depredations, and the neglect suffered by the forest.

Around the fire, Xanth explained that the woods had once been far safer. The white dragon had shattered the peace, displaced the orcs, and frightened even the animals. The new orcs were not ordinary raiders but something fundamentally darker and more dangerous: converts to the Cult of Talos.

Talos, Xanth explained, was the deity of storms and destruction. He was not confined to one homeland or temple; he lived “in the breast of all those who seek the power to destroy.” His cult was small, outlawed by civilized societies, and inherently unstable. It behaved like a disease: it erupted, destroyed everything around it, and eventually destroyed itself. Adabra might have described it as a necessary force of destruction in the cosmic balance, but Xanth dismissed that as nonsense. To him, Talos was a lunatic god who found foolish people with fantasies of destruction and empowered them to enact those fantasies.

Vukradin asked the crucial question: Talos had existed for centuries, so what had changed recently? Xanth reasoned that the cult had probably acquired a new Chosen—a cleric blessed by Talos and convinced that he had received divine inspiration. The Chosen was not in this forest, Xanth believed; if he were, Xanth would know and would take the party to him. “You’d probably die, but at least we’d learn something about him,” he added, before assuring them it was a joke.

The next morning, the journey continued, and the party stumbled upon a heated argument between a group of young, inexperienced druids and a band of wildcat loggers working for Tibor Wester, Harbin Wester’s half-brother. The druids immediately recognized Soma’s staff and looked to her as an authority. They explained that Neverwinter and its druids permitted controlled logging, but these workers had no permits and no regard for the forest’s balance. Soma agreed that limited logging could be necessary to remove dead growth and protect a healthy woodland. The problem was not logging itself but Tibor’s illegal operation.

Rather than driving the loggers away, Soma proposed turning the confrontation into a lesson: “Why don’t you show them the ropes? Show them how to properly log.” Vukradin supported her with an extraordinary speech about the sanctity of nature, sustainable forestry, honest labor, fair trade, and the need to balance economic and natural interests. It was no dry lecture; it was alive with quips, warmth, and eloquence. By the time he finished, the loggers had put down their axes, apologized through tears, promised to seek honest logging work, and agreed to report Tibor Wester’s illegal camp to Neverwinter. Even Brewbarry, remembering all the trees he had torn from the ground, was moved to guilt.

It was not long before the party came upon something far more unsettling. In the middle of a sixty-foot-wide forest clearing stood a forty-foot pine tree, charred black and stripped of its limbs long ago, its dead form rising like a needle against the sky. Tied near its base were ten ghastly dolls, each fashioned from twigs and bound with black hair. Comparisons to the Blair Witch and Burning Man did little to improve the atmosphere.

Xanth confirmed that the dolls were not typical of anything he recognized, although he conceded that he knew more about the Cult of Talos than the party did. Brewbarry studied the figures and remembered an old Uthgardtian tale: such objects were left by people who wished to torment and hurt their enemies. He had been taught only one response to them: avoidance. His verdict was concise: “Black magic, voodoo, no touch. That’s what Brewbarry remembers.”

The party debated whether the figures belonged to the half-orc spellcasters or perhaps to their enemies. Vukradin concluded that they were evil and could not be left standing. He destroyed one doll with radiant power.

A still-beating pig’s heart tumbled to the ground.

Vukradin destroyed the heart as well. The dead tree’s roots immediately erupted from the earth, thrashing and coiling across the clearing. Boney was caught fast and battered by the roots, while the others scrambled clear. Soma rushed back to free Boney, only to be caught in turn. Vukradin, undeterred, announced that the dolls were hurting his friend and therefore had to die. He continued methodically destroying doll after doll and heart after heart while Brewbarry reminded him, “We do no touch. That’s what Brewbarry said, you’re not listening.”

The trap did not grow worse with each heart, but it continued to lash the clearing. Soma used Wild Shape to become a giant elk, gaining enough strength to tear free with Valphine’s magical aid. The experience cost Soma ten hit points and one use of Wild Shape, while Boney served as the unfortunate test subject who revealed what the trap did. Once Vukradin had destroyed all ten dolls and hearts, the cursed tree was purged. Vukradin declared that they had saved the ten people the effigies had been intended to torment. The party then took a short rest so Soma could recover, Vukradin could renew Bardic Inspiration, and Valphine could reapply Emboldening Bond.

They had not gone much farther when the ridge ahead came alive with movement. Across a sixty-foot-wide, ten-foot-deep gully, ten hulking orcs crested the opposite ridge with terrible battle cries. They were accompanied by Yargath, an Anchorite of Talos with elongated claws.

Vukradin acted first, using Universal Speech to make himself understood by Yargath and the three nearest orcs. Rather than attack, he told them about the wonders of Lathander. The appeal only enraged Yargath, but the other three orcs hesitated and slowed their advance, rushing forward without committing to an all-out charge. Vukradin insisted that the party was there to proselytize—at least until the orcs attacked.

Valphine then stepped forward and cast Daylight upon her holy symbol. Brilliant magical light spread sixty feet around her, framing the party in the glory of Lathander. The intended miracle did not inspire Yargath. He screamed across the gully, “Kill the false cleric of Lathander!” and denounced the drow cleric as an abomination. Valphine’s display provoked Yargath and the still-hostile orcs into attacking, while the three orcs influenced by Vukradin remained neutral and hesitant. The battle on the approach to the Circle of Thunder was about to begin.

#### Xanth the Centaur shares the history and dangers of the Cult of Talos as the party travels through the forest.

- Xanth distinguishes the new Talosian orcs from ordinary orcs, calling them fundamentally more evil and dangerous.

- He identifies Talos as the god of storms and destruction, present wherever people seek destructive power and revel in ruin.

- Talos’s worship is not tied to a single homeland. Xanth says, “He is in the breast of all those who seek the power to destroy.”

- The cult is relatively small and outlawed by most civil and polite societies.

- Xanth compares the Cult of Talos to a disease: it suddenly explodes, destroys everything around it, and ultimately destroys itself.

- Adabra would interpret destruction as a necessary component of cosmic balance, but Xanth dismisses this position as nonsense.

- Vukradin asks why Talos’s influence has increased now when the god has been corrupting people for centuries.

- Xanth praises the question and concludes that someone has probably acquired power and received Talos’s blessing.

- He explains that the cult almost always gathers around a Chosen, generally a cleric convinced that Talos has given him divine inspiration.

- Xanth does not know the Chosen’s identity, but he is confident that the figure is not currently within this forest.

- Xanth jokes that if the Chosen were nearby, he would take the party directly to him: “You’d probably die, but at least we’d learn something about him.”

- Valphine’s Emboldening Bond provides the final assistance Soma needs to escape.

- Soma loses ten hit points and expends one Wild Shape during the encounter.

- Once all the dolls and hearts have been destroyed, the roots fall still and the tree is considered purged.

- Vukradin declares that the party has saved the ten people whom the effigies were intended to harm.

- The party takes a short rest. Soma recovers the lost health, Vukradin renews Bardic Inspiration, and Valphine reapplies Emboldening Bond before the next confrontation.

- The party plans to concentrate its attacks on the Talosian leader while attempting to convert the remaining orcs.

- The session ends with the confrontation poised to become a battle.

## Locations

### Phandalin

The frontier town where the party returns to rest, regroup, and report its findings after the Druid Council meeting. Vukradin uses its postal system to send Adabra six gold pieces, and the party considers purchasing healing or lightning-resistant supplies before leaving for the forest.

### Stonehill Tavern

A local inn in Phandalin where the party meets Ser Kaelen and Lyra, receives drinks bought by Lyra, and performs a musical show for the patrons. It is also the site of Jenna Roscoe’s Lord’s Alliance recruitment pitch and Brewbarry’s emphatic, burped refusal.

### Triboar Trail

The main road where the party meets Xanth at the junction with the path to Phandalin. Xanth selected the meeting place in response to Vukradin’s Sending spell, and the junction marks the beginning of their trek toward the Circle of Thunder.

### The Forest

A vast wilderness where the party hikes thirty miles on its first day and camps overnight under Xanth’s guidance. Once peaceful, the forest has been destabilized by the white dragon, the displacement of orcs, the fear of local animals, Talosian activity, and Tibor Wester’s illegal logging operation.

### The Cursed Clearing

A sixty-foot-wide forest clearing centered on a charred, needle-like, forty-foot pine tree. Ten twig dolls bound with black hair were tied near its base, each concealing a still-beating pig’s heart. Destroying a heart activated roots that erupted throughout the clearing, battered victims, and held them fast.

### Circle of Thunder

The hilltop henge toward which the party is traveling, described as the lair of evil half-orc spellcasters devoted to Talos. Before reaching it, the party encounters Yargath and ten orcs on the far side of a sixty-foot-wide, ten-foot-deep gully.

## Items

### Organic Fair Trade Gold (Six Gold Pieces)

Six gold pieces placed in an envelope by Vukradin—the disputed five-gold information fee plus one for interest—and sent through Phandalin’s postal system. Vukradin removed the amount from the party’s funds and described the gesture as allowing Adabra to have her money back now that she had lost.

### Meril’s Staff

Meril’s evolving staff, carried by Soma and noted by Ser Kaelen after the Druid Council proceedings. It is distinct from Soma’s Staff of Birdcalls. Its presence also causes the novice druids encountered in the forest to regard Soma as an experienced authority.

### Potion of Greater Healing

A magical healing draught held in the party’s inventory for emergencies. It is one of only a few restorative consumables the party identifies before leaving Phandalin.

### Potion of Superior Healing

A potent restorative tonic held by the party. Vukradin jokes that he does not want it used in front of him because he would prefer not to know what it contains; the others agree they can administer it while he is unconscious.

### Twig Dolls

Ten ghastly effigies made of twigs bound with black hair, found tied near the base of a charred pine tree. Brewbarry remembers them as old symbols used by those wishing to torment their enemies. Each contains a still-beating pig’s heart, and destroying a heart activates the tree’s constricting roots.

### Valphine’s Holy Symbol

A religious icon through which Valphine channels the power of Lathander. She casts Daylight upon it during Vukradin’s attempt to convert the orcs, causing it to flood the ridge and gully with brilliant magical light and making Valphine the principal target of Yargath’s fury.

## Spells

### Sending

A third-level spell available to bards, used by Vukradin to contact Xanth across a great distance and arrange a meeting at the junction of the Triboar Trail. Vukradin compares it enthusiastically to owning a new cell phone, while Lyra worries that he will spend the entire day sending messages to everyone he knows.

### Wild Shape

Used by Soma to transform into a giant elk after being restrained by the cursed tree’s roots. The form’s immense size and strength, combined with Valphine’s magical assistance, allows Soma to tear free. Soma expends one use of Wild Shape in the process.

### Bardic Inspiration

Used by Vukradin to bolster the party’s resolve and physical coordination. Brewbarry expends his inspiration to avoid the cursed roots, while Soma retains hers when it cannot improve an earlier failed attempt. Vukradin restores the party’s inspiration after a short rest.

### Emboldening Bond

Valphine’s Peace Domain feature, which the table informally called “Bless.” She bonds up to three willing creatures for 10 minutes without concentration. While a bonded creature is within 30 feet of another, it can add a d4 once per turn to an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw.

### Universal Speech

Used by Vukradin at the start of the ridge encounter to make himself understood by Yargath and the three nearest orcs. He uses that shared understanding to preach the wonders of Lathander. Yargath becomes more enraged, but the other three targets hesitate and advance more slowly.

### Daylight

Cast by Valphine on her holy symbol during the ridge encounter, conjuring a sixty-foot radius of brilliant magical light. The spell is presented as a miraculous demonstration of Lathander’s glory, but Yargath interprets it as a direct religious challenge and orders the “false cleric” destroyed.

## NPCs

### Adabra

A woman who attacked Vukradin’s character and honor during a trial before the Druid Council, demanding that he return the five gold pieces she had paid for a report she now disputed. Vukradin had expected hostility but was surprised by how personal and vicious her attack became. He sends her six gold pieces by mail as a petty but pointed repayment with interest.

### Lyra

A woman who accompanies the party from the Druid Council and has developed a crush on Vukradin. She deeply admires his principles and refusal to allow his honor to be sullied. Lyra interprets his walkout as a brilliant rhetorical maneuver that neutralized Adabra and allowed Soma to prevail. She buys the party drinks and orders at least two beers each for Vukradin and Brewbarry.

### Ser Kaelen

A contact in Phandalin who meets the party at the Stonehill Tavern. He notices that Soma retained Meril’s Staff and receives the party’s report about Sister Kaella’s escape and Corbin’s death. The party wants him and organizations such as the Order of the Gauntlet or Lord’s Alliance to help locate Kaella.

### Jenna Roscoe

A composed and normally unflappable representative of the Lord’s Alliance. She offers the Alliance’s help in finding Sister Kaella as a courtesy designed to demonstrate its resources and prestige to Vukradin. She suggests that the Alliance could advance his music and studio, briefly loses her composure when asked to donate to Valphine’s church, and offensively pitches the “civilizing” value of recruiting Brewbarry. His loud burp ends the conversation.

### Sister Kaella

An agent who murdered Corbin and has since fled. The party wants the fugitive located because of Corbin’s murder and the critical intelligence she possesses; their motives include both anger and the need for information. She has “gone to the wind,” prompting the party to seek help from larger organizations with the reach to track her.

### Xanth

A centaur ally who directs the party to a meeting point at the Triboar Trail and guides them through the forest toward the Circle of Thunder. He praises Soma’s recent performance, provides a safe campsite, and shares lore about the white dragon, Talos, and the cult’s probable Chosen. He cautions that he is not himself a Talosian expert and narrowly avoids being ensnared by the cursed tree.

### Boney

Vukradin’s sentient skeletal-horse companion and sixth-level sidekick, who travels with the party rather than remaining stabled in Phandalin. Boney becomes the only creature initially caught by the cursed clearing’s animated roots, takes a battering, and is freed by Soma before escaping across the clearing.

### Tibor Wester

Harbin Wester’s half-brother and the operator of an illegal wildcat logging camp in the forest. He has hired new workers to cut timber without permits or respect for the ecological agreements between Neverwinter and the local druids. Vukradin’s speech persuades his loggers to abandon the operation and report him to Neverwinter.

### Yargath

An Anchorite of Talos who leads the ten-orc patrol encountered on the approach to the Circle of Thunder. His elongated claws reveal his supernatural transformation. Vukradin’s preaching about Lathander enrages him, and Valphine’s Daylight spell provokes him to denounce her as a false cleric and abomination. He orders the still-hostile orcs to attack while several others remain hesitant.
