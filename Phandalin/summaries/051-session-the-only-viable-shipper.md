# Chapter 51: The Only Viable Shipper

Date: 2026-08-11

## Summary
The party made their way to the Counting House, Neverwinter’s premier banking establishment, with Brewbarry leading the charge. He had spent the previous evening rehearsing a grand speech in his bathrobe, and he delivered it with full barbarian conviction — only to find himself addressing an empty room (“Damn! I had prepared that speech in my bathrobe last night”). Vukradin offered tips on persuasion from his silver tongue; Brewbarry countered that his axe *was* his tongue. When the banker Aurelan Vance finally appeared and mistook Brewbarry’s authentic Uthgardtian hides for an elaborate costume — “I think your costume is wonderful, and I really admire the cosplaying of it” — the barbarian’s patience was immediately tested: “Now, slippery slope here, Aurelan. Use your next words very carefully.” Brewbarry presented Linene’s letter of introduction and made his intentions clear: he needed funding and shipping arrangements for his bathrobe business, and he was not interested in lengthy financial deliberations. Vukradin, meanwhile, introduced the group as their newly named band, Lathander’s Death, and repeatedly demanded to know what, exactly, the Counting House counted — a question that would not be answered to his satisfaction until the very end of the visit (“We count money.” “Oh, you could have just said you’re a bank.”).

Aurelan attempted to steer the conversation toward profit margins and alternative distributors, suggesting that House Margaster offered far cheaper shipping rates than the Lionshield Coster. Brewbarry was having none of it — “You’re not gonna help me, I’m gonna help you, old man” — and cut off every tangent with a pointed tap of his halberd and a glare that made the banker’s calculations feel suddenly less important. Valphine noted the banker’s projections failed to tally the temple markup and the church’s bulk demand, prompting Aurelan to recognize her as one of the blessed of Lathander — “and you’re a drow.” Vukradin smoothed the edges with a well-placed appeal to an acceptable interest rate for the added risk. By the end of the exchange, Aurelan had signed the loan paperwork, securing the capital needed to launch Brewbarry’s bathrobe venture with Linene as the shipping agent, and promised to make inquiries into House Margaster by the next day: “You can rely on the Counting House.” A wealthy client in the bank, dressed in silk imitations of Uthgardtian barbarian clothing, attempted to compliment Brewbarry on his “commitment to the bit,” awkwardly mimicked his stance, and was thoroughly ignored — though not before being warned, “Are you touching my clothes? Be careful.”

Aurelan then directed Vukradin to a separate department to collect a letter of credit from the Neverwinter Commission — “I do loans, they do fulfillment,” he explained, a division of labor Brewbarry found unimpressive (“You see my halberd, you see my axe, there are two different departments as well”) — and the party found themselves face to face with a familiar comedy duo: Rimardo and Corrin, now operating as “divine accountants and occasional entertainers” behind a small folding table with a velvet rope and a queue of zero. The party, delighted, produced the letter they had been carrying for the pair from the Falcon. Rimardo solemnly declared that he was there to audit the party’s moral economy, while Corrin produced receipts from every establishment the party had ever visited — he literally brought receipts. After a pointed accusation that Vukradin had merely laundered dirty money through better penmanship — his gaze lingering on the UBT ledger, over Vukradin’s protest that “it was drunk money” — Corrin stamped the party’s subcommittee charter as “comedically compliant,” then stamped the credit receipt as “approved,” and the two promptly got up and left. The receipt turned out to be worth two hundred gold pieces — a dividend from the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine, paid by the Neverwinter Commission, which, to Vukradin’s evident surprise, actually owns the mine; the party merely holds shares.

As the party debated the moral provenance of mine gold on their way out — Soma cutting through the ethics seminar with “if you eat a potato, is that an ill-gotten potato because it came out of the ground?” — Valphine recorded the entry as fair trade gold: the employees were paid, the work produced goods, and the party had contributed management labor, “the hardest and most valuable type.” They then turned their attention to the growing shadow of House Margaster. They had already witnessed the house’s agents engage in extortion and forgery — Vukradin recalled that Ser Kaelen himself had warned him to stay away — and now the same organization had been pointed to as the cheapest shipping network in the city — conveniently so, given that the Neverwinter Commission’s interplanar displacement manifold — on which the Lionshield Coster’s cheap distribution depended — had recently and mysteriously stopped working. When Vukradin carefully spelled out the sabotage hypothesis, Brewbarry replied, “No shit, Sherlock. The question is, what are we gonna do about it?” The party warned of impossible odds — city guards in Margaster pockets, Lord Neverember likely on their side, expulsion from the city, seizure of assets — but Brewbarry, loyal to his tribe and to Linene, had no interest in switching distributors and none whatsoever in working from the shadows: “I’m a barbarian, I don’t stab in the back. You know, I cut your head while looking at you.” He had every interest in paying House Margaster a visit and making his feelings known in the most direct way possible.

Before the party could set off, a man in his thirties wearing unfashionable clothes stepped forward and introduced himself as Perrin Alagondar. He explained, with barely contained anxiety, that his family had lost a precious heirloom to grave robbers a generation ago, and that word had reached him that the party was in possession of it — the Necklace of Fireballs. He had been waiting for them for hours, and had read the recovery office’s letter “about 40 times.” He spoke of the necklace as more than jewelry; at the word “lineage” a fire lit in his eye, and he launched into how the Alagondars had once been the first family of Neverwinter before “that imposter Neverember” — the necklace a symbol of the true heirs of Neverwinter, a rallying point for those who opposed Lord Neverember’s dictatorial, high-taxation rule. Brewbarry yawned, very loudly. Soma pointed out the obvious problem: “if this necklace is the symbol that you say it is, I’d imagine a lot of people would lie to claim it’s theirs.” Almost immediately, a shadowy figure emerged and produced notarized paperwork sealed by House Margaster, vouching for Perrin’s lineage and suggesting the necklace be handed over at once. The party marveled at how many people kept stepping into their path; the GM noted that a Goliath, a tortle bearing the staff of Meril, and a drow cleric whose eyes glow with Lathander’s blessing are not exactly inconspicuous, and Brewbarry proposed office hours with a ticket system.

Boney, the skeletal horse who had once served Lady Alagondar herself, was deeply skeptical. He noted that the necklace had only recently been returned to the world by Vukradin — recovered from Lady Alagondar’s own barrow — meaning it lay in her tomb, not in any family’s hands, when Perrin’s family supposedly lost it to grave robbers. Perrin’s account of how it had been lost contained troubling inconsistencies. Boney, it emerged, is obsessed with noble lineages and rights of succession — “if you’re stuck in a tomb for a few hundred years, you start to, like, do research,” conducted from the gravestones themselves — and he prefers the term “recently unalived” to “dead.” The party refused to surrender the necklace on the spot, and Brewbarry settled the matter by grabbing the House Margaster fixer firmly by the scruff and announcing that they would all be heading to House Margaster’s offices together to sort this out properly — “It’s not a kidnapping!” Vukradin clarified; “We don’t want them to fall down.” The fixer, after a brief and unsuccessful attempt to wriggle free, accepted his fate. Before setting off, Valphine handed Brewbarry a suit of elven chainmail from her own stores — she has had no use for spare body armor since the White Dragon Plate — and he donned it in place of his scale mail, at her suggestion that combat might be in the offing.

On the way to House Margaster, the party passed a building near the docks bearing a civic notice nailed to its door. It declared, by order of Lord Neverember, that the establishment’s services for instantaneous shipping had been closed until further notice. The party recognized it immediately as the former home of the interplanar displacement device. Perrin launched into a tirade suggesting the Neverwinter Commission had deliberately shut it down to tighten their grip on trade, while the party quietly noted how conveniently the closure had benefited House Margaster’s traditional caravan business. Brewbarry, remembering that the same Lord had invited them to dinner and the same Commission had just paid their dividend, allowed that he was “starting to see your point.” Valphine argued that undermining power structures is best done behind the scenes; Brewbarry countered that a threat only has leverage if the target knows where it’s coming from — “then they’re forced to sit at the table.”

The party arrived at the House Margaster Notary, a clean and well-organized establishment that served as the legal face of the house’s operations — no forgeries on the premises, the party concluded; those happen off-site and merely get notarized here. Vukradin raised the possibility that Perrin could be anyone — “He could be a polymorph, for all we know.” The notary presented a thick tome titled the Genealogy of the Alagondars, claiming it had been verified against forgeries, doppelgangers and miscellaneous magic through the “threefold rule” — coordinating between three different sources of conflicting evidence with different provenance — and that Perrin was indeed a rightful descendant of Lady Alagondar. House Margaster’s agent then offered to hold the necklace in their warehouses for safekeeping — a form of “title insurance,” as Vukradin dubbed it: “We can’t really guarantee anything, but if it’s wrong, we’ll take care of you.” The notary, visibly filing the concept away as a scam worth running in the future, warmed to the idea; Soma inquired whether an extended warranty on the necklace could also be arranged. The party saw through the scheme immediately and flatly refused. Valphine’s careful examination of the paperwork itself found it authentic — which, as Vukradin observed, made things worse, not better: Perrin was no cheap fraud, but an Alagondar genuinely allied with Margaster, and “could all be totally legitimate, and they’re still evil.”

The party then made their way to the Margaster Logistics Operations at the Eastern Quay, a bustling and impressively well-run shipping hub near the docks. Soma noticed mainly that it was very clean; Valphine — whose family had experience in the “trade” of goods — quickly observed that the facility was stocked with Lionshield Coster goods, moved the old-fashioned way by caravans with armed guards. House Margaster had effectively become the only game in town for moving cargo, charging whatever rates they pleased now that the displacement device was gone: not stealing goods, just legitimately moving them — how they got all the business being the less legitimate part. Cullen Sharpe, the smooth-talking house representative in the green suit the party had last seen extorting Lord Cassian, greeted them warmly and with practiced charm — and let slip, in passing, that Ser Kaelen had reached out to House Margaster through one of their agents about connecting the party’s finds with their “real proprietaires.” He denied any involvement in the device’s failure — “perhaps if we had been, it would not have gotten damaged” — but let slip that a gnome had been hired to fix, or was it improve, the device — and that the gnome’s tinkering had preceded the device’s final breakdown.

Vukradin pressed Cullen for the gnome’s name, and after some persuasion, Cullen agreed to look into it and share the information the following day. Brewbarry, for his part, attempted to make his displeasure known in no uncertain terms — a firm grip on the shoulder, “I’ve been told that you’re quite sharp, no pun intended” — but Cullen Sharpe remained thoroughly unintimidated, treating the barbarian’s barely veiled threats as the charming eccentricities of a colorful new business contact. (The bathrobe, Soma suggested, was not helping the intimidation.) Brewbarry even floated the repaired device as a future business opportunity for Margaster — a deception so thoroughly botched that Cullen simply took him for a barbarian who didn’t know what he was talking about, which Brewbarry declared “perfect cover.” The party extended an invitation to the benefit concert at the Spire of the Morninglord — a chance to announce the band’s new name to the whole town — which Cullen graciously accepted as a representative of his house, though he could not promise the whole house would attend.

It was here, with Perrin and the fixer marched along in tow, that the necklace question finally came to a head. Brewbarry made his position crystal clear by crumpling the notarized paperwork into a ball and tossing it over his shoulder — “Is that making it crystal clear for you, Perry?” The fixer’s hand moved briefly toward his weapon before a look from Cullen Sharpe made him think better of it. The party restated that Perrin would need to produce three independent witnesses — none of them affiliated with House Margaster — before any necklace changed hands. Vukradin accepted the uncomfortable corollary: if the identity is established, the necklace must be handed over, “and if he then takes the necklace, puts it on, and shoots a fireball at us, well, that sucks, but that’s what we have to do anyway.” Perrin agreed, and a meeting was arranged for after the party’s upcoming benefit concert at the Spire of the Morninglord, a venue that Valphine noted with some satisfaction would require Perrin to set foot inside a temple. Perrin professed himself, if not an exclusive worshipper, a big believer in the Morninglord as a god of good; Valphine received this with practiced blaséness.

As the evening drew to a close, the party found themselves with a growing list of appointments, a pocketful of mine dividends, and the unsettling realization that in Neverwinter, House Margaster seemed to have its fingers in nearly everything — and, as Brewbarry resolved, that finding out who did *not* answer to them, and aligning with those people, might be the most important task of all.

### The Heir of Alagondar

- The party demands verification — Soma: “Can you prove its lineage?” Valphine: “Or at least start with your passport.”
- At the word “lineage,” a fire lights in Perrin’s eye (the table calls for a dramatic camera zoom — “and it adds 15 pounds”) and he reveals his belief that the necklace is a symbol of those who reject the current rule of Lord Neverember and could rally political opposition — the Alagondars were “the first family of Neverwinter” before “that imposter Neverember.” Brewbarry yawns very loudly.
- Perrin hastily walks the revolution back — “I’m not going to start a revolution, no, no, this is a symbol” — and Soma lands the obvious objection: “If this necklace is the symbol that you say it is, I’d imagine a lot of people would lie to claim it’s theirs.”

## NPCs
### Aurelan Vance
A banker at the Counting House who handles loans. He is meticulous with calculations and profit margins, initially mistakes Brewbarry’s authentic barbarian attire for a costume (“I really admire the cosplaying of it”), and is easily cowed by Brewbarry’s aggressive negotiation style. He suggests House Margaster as a cheaper shipping alternative but ultimately signs the loan paperwork for Brewbarry’s business. He recognizes Valphine on sight as “one of the blessed of Lathander” — and, after a pause, “a drow.” He explains the bank’s strict departmental divide (“I do loans, they do fulfillment”), personally escorts Vukradin to the fulfillment table, and commits to investigating House Margaster’s operation by the next day: “You can rely on the Counting House.”

### Linene Graywind
Brewbarry’s wealth management consultant and Lionshield Coster franchise representative, whose letter of introduction to the Counting House helped secure funding for Brewbarry’s bathrobe business. Her shipping operation has been severely disrupted by the failure of the interplanar displacement device — she had no traditional transport arm of her own, and her goods now move through House Margaster’s caravans at steep rates. Brewbarry’s loyalty to her is absolute: when Aurelan suggests a cheaper distributor, he treats it as a near-insult — “You tell me to go and do some business with somebody else? ### Rimardo
A self-styled ‘divine accountant’ and occasional entertainer who audits the party’s moral economy at the Counting House, reviewing their financial receipts with a critical eye and accusing them of laundering dirty money through better penmanship. He had been expecting the party — “Yes, we’ve been waiting for you for some time” — and accepts the letter they carried for him from the Falcon before departing without ceremony.

### Corrin
Rimardo’s partner, an accountant and occasional entertainer who produces receipts from every location the party has visited and stamps official documents, including stamping the party’s subcommittee charter as ‘comedically compliant.’ He never speaks — communicating in shrugs, receipts, and stamps — and produces the party’s own subcommittee charter from apparently nowhere before stamping the credit receipt ‘approved’ and leaving with Rimardo.

### Perrin Alagondar
A man in his 30s wearing decent but unfashionable clothes who claims to be a descendant of Lady Alagondar. He had waited hours for the party and read the recovery office’s letter “about 40 times.” He views the Necklace of Fireballs as a political symbol to rally opposition against Lord Neverember’s rule — the Alagondars, he says, were the first family of Neverwinter before “that imposter Neverember” — though he hastily insists he is not starting a revolution. He is closely assisted by House Margaster agents, blames the Neverwinter Commission for the displacement device’s shutdown, and professes belief in the Morninglord when a temple meeting is proposed. Boney notes holes in his story about how the necklace was lost — Perrin visibly does not know all the facts of its history.

### The Fixer
An agent of House Margaster who emerges from the shadows with notarized paperwork to vouch for Perrin’s lineage and attempts to steer the party toward surrendering the necklace to House Margaster for safekeeping — even suggesting, when the hour grows late, that they simply settle it on the spot with his official piece of paper. After a brief, futile struggle against Brewbarry’s grip, he resigns himself to being marched across the city. He briefly reaches for his weapon when Brewbarry crumples his paperwork but thinks better of it — a look from Cullen Sharpe settling the matter.

### Boney
A skeletal horse formerly associated with Lady Alagondar who travels with the party. He is obsessed with royal lineages and successions, having studied them from gravestones while interred in a tomb — “if you’re stuck in a tomb for a few hundred years, you start to, like, do research.” He bristles (“huffing and puffing, as much as a skeletal horse can”) at Perrin’s claim and cross-examines him directly, but is scrupulous about the limits of his knowledge: the necklace belonged to Lady Alagondar, and if Perrin truly is of the clan, “it is entirely possible that he has a rightful claim to it.” He expresses skepticism about Perrin’s account, noting that the necklace was only recently returned to the world — recovered by Vukradin from Lady Alagondar’s own barrow, not from any family’s keeping — and that Perrin’s story therefore has holes. He prefers the term “recently unalived” to dead.

### The Notary
An official at the House Margaster-controlled notary house who uses the ‘Genealogy of the Alagondars’ and the ‘threefold rule’ to validate Perrin’s lineage claim. He is very pleased to see the party, quick to concede that questions of identity are “utterly a reasonable concern,” and enthusiastic about the concept of ‘title insurance’ as a potential future product — his eyes visibly clicking through the possibilities of the scam as Vukradin explains it.

### Cullen Sharpe

## Locations
### The Counting House
A high-end banking establishment in Neverwinter filled with tables and clerks, serving as the financial hub for the city’s industrialists — “a typical medieval bank for high-end industrialists.” It is divided into various departments, including loans and fulfillment, with strict walls between them. Its clientele includes wealthy patrons in silk imitations of barbarian hides, and — at a small folding table with a velvet rope and a queue of zero — the divine accountants Rimardo and Corrin.

### Neverwinter Streets
The bustling thoroughfares of the city where the party is approached by Perrin Alagondar and a shadowy House Margaster fixer while en route to their next destination. The party has become enough of a local legend — a Goliath in genuine Uthgardtian hides, a tortle carrying the staff of Meril, and a drow cleric with glowing eyes — that strangers now lie in wait for them for hours; Brewbarry proposes instituting office hours with tickets.

### House Margaster Notary
A clean and well-organized establishment in Neverwinter that serves as a front for House Margaster, used to notarize documents and legitimize their business ventures through official-looking paperwork. The party’s investigation finds it to be exactly what it appears — just a notary, with none of the usual Margaster trappings; the forgeries, they conclude, happen off-site and are merely notarized here. Home of the ‘Genealogy of the Alagondars.’

### Shuttered Shipping Hub
A building near the Neverwinter docks bearing a civic notice, nailed to the door, declaring it closed by order of Lord Neverember and its services for instantaneous shipping suspended until further notice. It formerly housed the interplanar displacement device used for near-instantaneous shipping of goods — the manifold on which the Lionshield Coster’s cheap distribution depended.

### Margaster Logistics Operations, Eastern Quay
A highly organized and efficient shipping hub near the Neverwinter docks — impressively clean, in Soma’s assessment; an extremely well-run operation, in Valphine’s more expert one. It serves as the primary base for House Margaster’s caravan-based distribution network — the usual caravans with armed guards — and is stocked with Lionshield Coster goods. Margaster is in the business of moving goods, not stealing them; with the displacement device gone, they are the only game in town and can charge whatever they want.

### Spire of the Morninglord
The grand temple of Lathander in Neverwinter where the party plans to perform their benefit concert — and announce the band’s new name, Lathander’s Death, to the whole town — and subsequently meet with Perrin Alagondar to settle the necklace dispute. Valphine specifically chose the venue to see Perrin set foot inside the temple; Cullen Sharpe has also accepted an invitation to attend as House Margaster’s representative.
