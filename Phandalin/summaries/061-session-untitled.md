# Chapter 61

Date: 2026-11-03

## Summary
The party stood in the shadow of Aletra's lifeless body, the damaged machine humming faintly with residual energy beside them. Aldus Hern pressed them urgently to stop wasting time and make for Leilon, while a frantic Bimble explained that a particular resonator was needed for the machine, which had broken when Aletra died. Soma suggested a soul bond; the GM described the cause as magic without confirming that mechanism. Whether the machine could ultimately be repaired remained open.

Bimble's agreement with House Margaster became the subject of prolonged scrutiny. Soma argued that even a well-intentioned engineer could unwittingly serve as Margaster's eyes and ears, particularly when magical confidentiality prevented the party from examining every clause of his contract. Vukradin defended Bimble as someone who had been duped. Valphine's insight confirmed that Bimble genuinely cared about repairing the machine: he had operated it for years, possessed a repair manual, and was excited to know where to find the replacement part. The GM clarified that the contract required repairs to happen under House Margaster's paperwork; it did not explicitly make Bimble their agent. Soma remained concerned about what obligations the undisclosed clauses might impose.

Vukradin initially advocated a full frontal assault on House Margaster. He feared that leaving Neverwinter for weeks would give the devils time to consolidate their influence, and argued that killing two of their lawyers had already made retaliation likely. Valphine favored undermining their power and allies so they could not simply return. The GM warned that further delay could bring consequences in Leilon. The party settled on one day of investigation after a long rest: stealthily search Margaster's premises for evidence and consider an attack only if a suitable opportunity arose. Soma offered to scout in animal form, while Valphine and Brewbarry discussed creating a religious distraction involving fiend detection and bathrobe sales.

They also considered relocating the damaged machine to make interference more obvious. It was large, heavy, and vulnerable to further damage; Bimble would be distressed by anyone handling it. Moving it within the building was possible, but removing it would mean stealing Commission property. The discussion prompted a distinction between suspicion and evidence: the party suspected Lord Neverember was a puppet, but the GM confirmed they had no proof that Margaster—or anyone else—controlled him. What they did know was that he governed a city recovering from two major wars and was paying adventurers to handle problems his armies would normally address. Vukradin emphasized the moral duty to restore commerce for Neverwinter's suffering people; Valphine also saw an opportunity to gain political influence by solving a recognized public problem.

Before resting, the party set out to retrieve their sending stone from the Guild Registry. A young boy repeatedly ran ahead, stopped at corners, and checked which way they turned. Vukradin offered him an autograph and signed his hat. The flustered boy admitted that Lynette, a dockside baker, had promised him six coppers if he could steer the party past her shop. The party accepted the detour, and Lynette paid him when they arrived.

Cinderwick Hearth and Loaf was a modest, salt-scented bakery stocked with bread, peppery pies, and sweet buns. Its sharp-eyed proprietor had attended Valphine's sermon and created Lathander's Death Pie in response: a dramatic confection with a black cocoa crust, a hidden eclipse layer, golden lemon filling, meringue, raspberries, and pomegranates. Lynette wanted a celebrity endorsement. Vukradin proposed baptism into their church as the condition, but Lynette already belonged to Lathander's church and hesitated over the new emphasis on sunburn and pain. Valphine tasted the pie and found it extraordinary, then wrote an endorsement on her own paper and sealed it as Bishop: “I approve of this pie.” Lynette immediately displayed it behind her counter.

The bakery visit grew into a theological and commercial negotiation. Valphine assigned religious meaning to the pie's colors and scorched edges, while Lynette suggested a special cayenne blend whose burn came and went unpredictably. Brewbarry secured a promise of a free pie for the poor and needy, and the party arranged for Lynette to sell clean bathrobes on consignment. Her observation that the House of a Thousand Faces already had very nice bathrobes renewed the rivalry over whose robes were softest.

As they continued toward the Guild Registry, the party reviewed unfinished business, including their Harper report, Lim's outstanding eight crates, and arrangements for Aletra's remains. Valphine wanted her sister's body stored at the church with protection against resurrection. Aldus Hern and Lord Neverember were handling the aftermath, and Aldus could arrange the transport. True Resurrection remained a possible means of bringing Aletra back, rather than a guaranteed one.

A sweating Aurelan Vance intercepted them near the Registry and Counting House. He had already sought them at the Spire of the Morninglord and now brought a request to meet the Commission. Inside, the Factor waited with five cups of tea, one already in use. He assured the party that the manifold's failure would not jeopardize shareholder returns: arrangements with House Margaster would restore the machinery and keep goods moving in the meantime, albeit more slowly.

Vukradin called his shares a conflict of interest and asked to sell them, preferring proceeds for the studio fund. The Factor promptly produced paperwork, explained that the other three party members could buy his portion before an open-market sale, and warned that a non-shareholder would have to leave the meeting. Valphine's suggestion of converting blood money into trade money prompted jokes about laundering, which Vukradin rejected. He maintained his wish to sell on the open market, though no sale was completed. The party again noticed that the Factor never used the word “I.”

After retrieving the sending stone, the party arranged to notify Ilsbeth Marrow by ordinary post that they had secured her temporary harbor-fee relief. They wanted her to know who had obtained the concession. On the way to the House of a Thousand Faces, they encountered a pig nosing through someone's washing while three neighbors tried to herd it with baskets. Soma spoke with the animal and discovered it had followed an exciting street noise, then mistaken the ensuing chase for a delightful game. Once Soma explained that the people were trying to keep it safe, the pig returned to its yard. Its grateful owner rewarded the party with a basket of fruit.

At the House of a Thousand Faces, Brewbarry disparaged the displayed bathrobes and nearly turned the greeting into an argument over furniture. The elderly Harper known as the Seat stopped any table-smashing by pointing out that they would have nowhere to put the beer. A tray of five beers arrived for Brewbarry alone, with more available for his companions.

The party reported their success against the Rift Weavers at the sewer junction. The Seat honored the earlier bargain: reopening the route allowed the Harpers to bring in fine fabric without paying House Margaster's extortionate shipping prices, and Brewbarry would receive the discounted rates. After another tactile inspection, he accepted the fabric's quality and sealed the deal with a spit-in-the-hand handshake. Vukradin maintained that Brewbarry would never do anything illegal when the GM pointed out the arrangement involved smuggling.

Questions about the Margaster family estate revealed a more fundamental misunderstanding. The Seat said the warehouse was their base because House Margaster was an infernal institution rather than an ordinary family. In exchange for a future favor, which Vukradin accepted after raising moral objections to an unrestricted obligation, she described them as the reputed lawyers of a powerful entity. People who became entangled with them invariably found contracts, NDAs, and offers for something they needed. The Harpers therefore kept their distance.

The Seat warned that destroying individual establishments had never destroyed the institution. She also knew about the party's two killings: factions watched the building containing the coveted machine, and people entering without returning made events easy to infer. Her more disturbing revelation was that special partner contracts continued through death. Agents killed in the field returned as infernal beings; the party had effectively promoted the two they slew. She distinguished these arrangements from Bimble's ordinary contract. The party discussed defeating such beings on their own plane, but identifying that plane would require a diabolist.

After a long rest at their usual inn, where they enjoyed free lodging, the party found lunch boxes packed, horses saddled and provisioned, and Bimble's equipment cleaned and sharpened. A small card read: “With courtesy, House Margaster.” Bimble admired the perfect packing, while the party understood the preparations as evidence of Margaster's access and knowledge of their plans. Shaken by their warnings, Bimble resolved not to sign further contracts. He did possess a copy of his agreement, but its restricted portions remained covered by the magical NDA.

The party still intended to scout the Margaster warehouse before leaving for Leilon. The GM explicitly warned that an assault was beyond seventh-level adventurers and that reckless use of their two portable holes could create other disasters. Soma reiterated that the purpose was scouting. The session ended before that reconnaissance began.

## NPCs
### Aldus Hern
An associate who insists the party must move on to Leilon and stop wasting time in Neverwinter. He and Lord Neverember are handling the aftermath of the deaths at the machine's warehouse. He is also noted as being able to arrange transport of Aletra's body to the church.

### Aletra
Valphine's sister, whose death coincided with the machine breaking down. The party seeks to have her body stored in the church with protections against resurrection. True Resurrection is discussed as a possible means of restoring her, with no guarantee that it would succeed.

### Bimble
A socially awkward engineer and longtime machine operator whose central motivation is repairing the damaged device. He has a manual, knows a particular resonator is needed, and treats the repair as an absorbing puzzle.

His magical contract and NDA with House Margaster make the party suspicious despite Valphine's confirmation that his interest in the repair is sincere. The contract requires repairs under Margaster's paperwork; its full obligations remain inaccessible. The Seat distinguishes his agreement from the special perpetual contracts held by Margaster partners.

He is delighted by the overnight cleaning and packing of his equipment, but the party's warnings leave him unwilling to sign further Margaster agreements.

### Lord Neverember
The ruler of Neverwinter, struggling to maintain order after two major wars. Some party members suspect he is under House Margaster's influence, though this is not confirmed.

The GM emphasizes that the party has evidence of an overburdened ruler relying on adventurers and negotiating powerful constituencies, rather than proof of a puppet master. The Commission is closely tied to him, and he is involved with Aldus Hern in handling the aftermath at the machine's warehouse.

### Lynette
The sharp-eyed owner of Cinderwick Hearth and Loaf, described as baking with the precision of a quartermaster. She pays a boy six coppers to bring the famous party past her shop.

She secures Valphine's sealed endorsement, agrees to a charitable pie offering, and enters a bathrobe consignment arrangement with the party. ### Aurelan Vance
A representative of the Counting House who tracks down the party to summon them to the Commission. He first seeks them at the Spire of the Morninglord, then intercepts them near the Guild Registry. His sweating and breathlessness suggest that the pursuit is more physical exertion than he usually undertakes.

### The Factor
A formal and calculated official who manages the Commission's interests. He speaks in terms of shareholders and returns, notably avoiding “I” throughout the meeting.

He has tea ready before the party arrives and paperwork immediately available when Vukradin proposes selling his shares. He presents Margaster's involvement as reassurance: machinery repairs, continued trade, and profitable investments are all being arranged.

### The Seat
An elderly human woman and high-ranking Harper agent operating from the House of a Thousand Faces. She uses hospitality, flattery, information, and commercial favors to cultivate the party.

She honors the fabric bargain after the sewer route is cleared, disarms Brewbarry's aggression with beer, and obtains a future favor from Vukradin in exchange for intelligence. She describes Margaster's institutional nature, perpetual partner contracts, and repeated survival after attacks on its establishments, while acknowledging that identifying the infernal agents' home plane requires a specialist.

## Locations
### Neverwinter
A city recovering from two major wars where the party navigates political intrigue, disrupted commerce, and magical anomalies. House Margaster holds significant influence, but the extent of its control over Neverember remains unproven. Surveillance by competing factions makes the party's conspicuous actions difficult to conceal.

### The Warehouse
The location housing the damaged magical machine and the scene of Aletra's death. The Commission knows its location, and multiple factions watch it because of the device's importance. Removing the Commission-owned machine from the building would constitute theft; repositioning it inside is permitted.

### Cinderwick Hearth and Loaf
A modest, salt-scented bakery in a narrow lane on the Neverwinter Docks, run by Lynette. It sells bread, peppery pies, and sweet buns.

Valphine's sealed pie endorsement is mounted behind the counter. The shop is also the agreed venue for bathrobe sales on consignment and a charitable pie offering.

### Guild Registry
A building near the Counting House where the party retrieves its sending stone. Its proximity to the Commission House makes Aurelan Vance's interception and the ensuing meeting convenient.

### Commission House
The Commission's administrative center near the Counting House. The Factor receives the party with five prepared cups of tea and treats the meeting as shareholder business, from which someone who sells their stake would be excluded.

### House of a Thousand Faces
A Harper-affiliated establishment where the party trades reports and future obligations for supplies and intelligence. Its bathrobes become a recurring point of contention for Brewbarry. The Seat uses generous beer service to smooth the meeting and honors the agreement for cheaper fabric shipments.

### House Margaster Warehouse
The primary residence and base of operations for the Margaster family, serving as both a place of business and a fortified home, as revealed by the Harpers.

The Seat further explains that House Margaster is an infernal institution rather than an ordinary noble family. The warehouse is the party's intended scouting target; no reconnaissance occurs during this session.
