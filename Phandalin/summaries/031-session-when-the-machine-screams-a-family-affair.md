# Chapter 31: When the Machine Screams: A Family Affair

Date: 2026-01-21

## Summary

The party found themselves in a tense standoff with Valphine’s sister, Aletra, who was working to extract a mysterious package from a broken Model 1489 Spatial Rectification Calliope. The room was covered in interplanar spider webs, and six Rift Weavers—spider-like creatures bound to the ten-foot-high ceiling—stood guard over the malfunctioning device. The webs blanketed the floor as well, allowing the creatures to sense movement throughout the room. Aletra associated the machine with her boss but paused over whether it had been a “gift” or a “purchase,” explaining that such distinctions were complicated. She claimed everyone was caught in a “spatial equilibrium” and warned that if she could not retrieve her package, she had no reason to fix—or more likely destroy—the machine. If it blew up, she did not particularly care.

The package appeared to be connected to House Sotorra’s illegal drug trade and contained vials of interdimensional poison. Aletra needed the party to hold off the Rift Weavers while she “worked the package,” prompting an extended round of double entendres before initiative began. When no one immediately advanced, Aletra invoked a lesson from House Sotorra: “I learned in House Sotorra that you only make your move when you’re ready to make the move, not before you’re ready to make the move.” She then impatiently asked whether the party intended to act or remain in “this Menzoberranzan standoff until the end of time.”

As the party engaged the Rift Weavers, Vukradin cast Slow, catching five of the six creatures. Brewbarry entered behind him and struck two Weavers with sweeping halberd attacks. The slowed creatures soon demonstrated an unusual countermeasure: they could weave shimmering cosmic filaments that ended a spell of third level or lower on a target, allowing each affected Weaver to spend an action removing Slow from itself.

Aletra then revealed the true purpose of the supposed equilibrium. She pointed a special wand at Brewbarry, causing him to glow red. The machine announced, “spatial anomaly detected,” and every Rift Weaver turned toward him with a singular urge to destroy or repair the anomaly. Aletra immediately began working on the machine while the party served as her cover. Valphine recognized the family tactic: the equilibrium story had been a lie, and Aletra merely needed another target. As the GM clarified, anyone entering with a magic item might have attracted some attention, but Aletra’s wand ensured that Brewbarry attracted all of it.

The battle intensified as Rift Weaver 3 crawled across the ceiling and restrained Brewbarry with webbing. Soma summoned a Flaming Sphere amid three of the creatures, scorching them and burning away nearby webs before transforming into a bear. Valphine cast Heroism on Brewbarry, restoring four temporary hit points each round and helping him endure the concentrated assault. Aletra thanked Brewbarry for his “contributions” and remarked that the boss would be pleased.

When Brewbarry was restrained a second time, Aletra called out to Valphine with a reminder of an old family tactic: “Remember that whole trap that we did, where we put the stuffed creature, and they kept attacking it, and all you had to do was keep healing it?” Her concern was practical rather than compassionate. If Brewbarry died, the Rift Weavers would come after her and interrupt the extraction. She instructed Valphine to keep the decoy alive while she cursed the machinery, blamed dwarves for trying to wring more efficiency from it, rummaged through tools, pulled components, and produced streams of frustrated technobabble.

Soma’s Flaming Sphere proved crucial. The Rift Weavers were deeply unsettled by magical fire, while their webs were explicitly vulnerable to it. The sphere burned through the strands restraining Brewbarry and, under the GM’s ruling, continued to incinerate any new webbing crossing its flames. One Weaver tried to restrain Soma through the sphere, only for its filaments to burn away; it chittered in frustration and lifted two of its eight arms in anger. Keeping Brewbarry close to the flames singed him repeatedly, but Heroism absorbed much of that damage and the party agreed that it was preferable to being restrained.

Valphine then struck Rift Weaver 5 with Guiding Bolt. The radiant blast revealed that the creatures themselves—not merely their webs—were vulnerable to radiant damage. The doubled damage destroyed the Weaver. Aletra greeted this discovery with familial contempt: “Mother always said you weren’t the sharpest tool in the shed,” before adding that their mother had remarked upon Valphine’s fondness for being the target of pointy tools. When challenged on her knowledge of radiant damage, Aletra returned to the machine and muttered, “Damn it, the hydrospanic cabling foo isn’t working with the…” before further technobabble swallowed the rest.

The party steadily turned the fight. Vukradin used Bardic Inspiration to turn a miss into a killing radiant strike against Rift Weaver 6, then later protected Brewbarry with Silvery Barbs, forcing dangerous attacks—including a critical hit—to be rerolled into misses. Brewbarry broke free of the webbing, carved through multiple Weavers with his halberd, and used Cleave to transfer attacks between adjacent enemies. Soma mauled the weakened creatures in bear form while rolling the Flaming Sphere around the room. The slain Rift Weavers did not leave ordinary corpses; at least one “turns into a fine, crackling series of sparkles and disappears.”

After three rounds of work, Aletra triumphantly pulled a box from the machine. She asked Valphine whether she had the shutdown code and advised her to use it, adding, “I’d hate to see… I’d hate to have to collect your body parts and bring them back to mom.” She then explained that she unfortunately could not leave evidence behind, pressed a fob, and opened a portal directly in front of herself.

Valphine had been waiting for Aletra to make her escape and cast Blindness in an effort to stop her. Aletra failed the Constitution save and was blinded, but because she knew precisely where she had opened the portal, she could still step through it. The spell nevertheless achieved a partial victory: Aletra dropped several vials from the box and muttered that the boss would not be happy. She escaped angry but grudgingly pleased that Valphine had at least attempted to betray her rather than “simper and cry like the last time.” Aletra then wondered whether Valphine had actually been crying or moaning with pleasure during that earlier incident.

With Aletra gone, the machine began shouting, “catastrophic failure, imminent.” The Rift Weavers became confused by competing imperatives: Brewbarry was still marked as a spatial anomaly, but the rapidly failing machine now demanded their attention as well. The party continued cutting them down while Valphine moved to the keypad. By the time she reached it, the warning had become an uncertain countdown—perhaps ten seconds remained, perhaps only four.

Valphine entered the shutdown code. The machine stopped, folded in upon itself, shrank, and vanished back into whatever dimension it had come from. An Arcana check convinced Valphine that this was a safe shutdown sequence rather than another impending disaster. All that remained of the machine was a small paper bill from “Interplanar Trade Consultants Incorporated,” listing payments due for services rendered and thanking the customer for their business. The company was described as a research firm connected to interplanar trade, complete with the limited liability implied by “Incorporated.”

The last surviving Rift Weaver, having no planar rift or damaged device left to repair, simply winked out of existence. The party was left in the suddenly quiet room with Aletra’s dropped poison vials, the interplanar invoice, and unanswered questions about the Model 1489 Spatial Rectification Calliope, House Sotorra’s trade, and the identity of Aletra’s unseen boss.

## NPCs

### Aletra

Valphine’s older sister and the fourth daughter in their Drow family; Valphine is the fifth daughter. Described as chaotic evil. Aletra was attempting to extract a package of vials related to House Sotorra’s illegal drug trade—specifically interdimensional poison—from a broken planar machine.

Aletra linked the machine to her boss, hesitating between “gift” and “purchase” because “these things are complicated” and leaving the transaction’s direction unclear. She claimed that the chamber was caught in a spatial equilibrium, but this was a deception intended to make the party enter first and become the Rift Weavers’ targets.

Her special wand marked Brewbarry as a spatial anomaly and concentrated every Rift Weaver’s attention upon him. Once he glowed red and the machine detected him, Aletra treated him as a disposable decoy and began working in peace. She even thanked him for his contributions and suggested the boss would be happy.

Aletra’s practical concern for Brewbarry arose only because his death would deprive her of protection. She reminded Valphine of an earlier trap in which the sisters used a stuffed creature as bait and kept healing it while enemies attacked. Her instructions were essentially to repeat that tactic with Brewbarry.

While working, Aletra cursed the machine’s creators, blamed dwarven attempts to improve its efficiency, manipulated tools and components, and muttered fragments of technobabble such as, “Damn it, the hydrospanic cabling foo isn’t working with the…”

She successfully extracted the box and opened an escape portal with a fob. Valphine’s Blindness spell did not stop her from stepping through, but it caused her to drop several valuable vials. Aletra left angry about disappointing her boss but pleased that Valphine had made a genuine attempt to betray her.

Aletra’s relationship with Valphine combines cruel familial intimacy, drow hierarchy, shared memories of old traps, and sexualized mockery of Valphine’s masochism. Her concern about returning Valphine’s body parts to their mother was delivered as both warning and joke.

### Rift Weavers

Six spider-like creatures with 45 hit points each. The players joked that the GM’s custom Rift Weavers were mechanically ghouls, but their demonstrated abilities stood on their own. They moved across the ten-foot ceiling and had spun webs across the entire floor, allowing them to detect movement through the room.

Their cosmic filament ability could end one spell of third level or lower on a creature, object, or area. Each slowed Weaver used an action to remove Slow from itself, eventually ending Vukradin’s control spell without needing to break his concentration.

Their ranged webs restrained targets and could be attacked directly. The webbing had AC 10, 15 hit points, and vulnerability to fire. A restrained creature could instead use an action and a DC 13 Strength check to break free.

The creatures attacked with bites but dealt relatively modest damage. Their strength came from restraint, ceiling mobility, and concentrating attacks against detected spatial anomalies.

They were vulnerable to radiant damage, taking double damage from Guiding Bolt and other radiant attacks. Magical fire did not inflict extra damage upon the creatures themselves, but it visibly disturbed them and destroyed their webbing. Web attacks projected through Soma’s Flaming Sphere burned away before reaching their targets.

The Weavers expressed frustration by chittering; one lifted two of its eight arms in anger after its webbing burned. When killed, they could dissolve into fine, crackling sparkles rather than leaving bodies.

Five were killed in battle. Rift Weaver 4 survived until the Model 1489 disappeared, then winked out because no planar damage remained for it to repair.

## Locations

### The Machine Room

A chamber containing the broken Model 1489 Spatial Rectification Calliope. The ceiling is ten feet high, allowing the Rift Weavers to cling overhead while remaining within reach of Brewbarry’s halberd and other melee attacks.

Interplanar webs cover the entire floor. Dense strands form visibly webbed areas, but even the apparently open ground contains enough webbing for the Rift Weavers to sense movement throughout the chamber.

The machine occupies the focal point of the room and includes a keypad for its shutdown code. Its spatial malfunction drew the Rift Weavers and contributed to the wider area’s distorted state.

During the battle, Soma’s Flaming Sphere burned clear portions of webbing and created a protected zone around Brewbarry where restraint filaments could not survive.

After Aletra removed the package, the machine began warning of imminent catastrophic failure. Once Valphine entered the code, it folded in upon itself and vanished, leaving the room quiet except for the paper invoice and Aletra’s dropped vials.

## Items

### Planar Machine

The Model 1489 Spatial Rectification Calliope, a broken planar device associated with Aletra’s boss. Aletra ambiguously described it as a “gift” or “purchase,” indicating that the transaction was complicated.

The device held a package that Aletra needed to retrieve before completing her assignment. Its malfunction appeared responsible for the area’s spatial distortions and attracted Rift Weavers tasked with repairing planar anomalies.

The machine could detect marked anomalies. When Aletra used her wand on Brewbarry, it announced, “spatial anomaly detected,” causing all six Rift Weavers to redirect their attention toward him.

A keypad allowed the party to enter a previously obtained shutdown code. After Aletra extracted the package, the machine began shouting, “catastrophic failure, imminent.”

Entering the code halted the warning. The machine folded inward, collapsed, shrank, and returned to another dimension. Its disappearance also removed the last planar damage in the room, causing the surviving Rift Weaver to vanish.

### Aletra’s Wand

A special wand designed to redirect the Rift Weavers’ attention. Aletra pointed it at Brewbarry and successfully marked him, making him glow red.

The wand caused the machine to identify Brewbarry as a spatial anomaly. Although magical items entering the room might naturally attract some of the Rift Weavers, this effect ensured that all of them prioritized Brewbarry over Aletra.

The table nicknamed it a “wand of taunting.” Its specialized function suggested that whoever equipped Aletra had anticipated the need to manipulate creatures guarding or repairing the planar machine.

### Package Vials

Vials of interdimensional poison tied to House Sotorra’s illegal drug trade. They were stored in a box trapped inside the malfunctioning Calliope.

Aletra spent three combat rounds extracting the box. Valphine’s Blindness spell caused Aletra to drop several vials as she escaped, leaving them available for the party to collect.

The loss prompted Aletra to mutter that her boss would not be happy. The exact properties, number, and value of the recovered vials were left for later treasure discussion.

### Fob

A small handheld device used by Aletra to open an escape portal. She pressed it after extracting the box, causing a portal to appear directly in front of her.

Because Aletra knew where she had created the portal, Valphine’s Blindness spell did not prevent her from stepping through it, though it did cause her to drop part of the shipment.

### Bill

A small piece of paper left behind after the planar machine vanished.

The bill came from “Interplanar Trade Consultants Incorporated,” a research firm related to interplanar trade. It listed payments that would have to be rendered for services and included stationery thanking the recipient for doing business.

Its mundane corporate form contrasted sharply with the machine’s catastrophic warnings and extradimensional disappearance. The “Incorporated” designation prompted the observation that even an interplanar concern needs limited liability.

### Spider Webs

Interplanar webs spun throughout the Machine Room by the Rift Weavers. The entire ground was threaded with them, allowing the creatures to sense movement even outside the visibly dense webbed areas.

The Weavers could project strands at range to restrain targets. Their restraint webbing had AC 10 and 15 hit points and could also be escaped with a DC 13 Strength check using an action.

The webs were vulnerable to fire. Soma’s Flaming Sphere burned away existing strands and destroyed new webbing projected through its flames. While Brewbarry remained close to the sphere, the fire continuously burned through the strands before they could restrain him.

## Spells

### Slow

A concentration spell cast by Vukradin against all six Rift Weavers at the start of combat, using a DC 15 Wisdom save. Only Rift Weaver 3 succeeded, leaving five initially slowed.

The spell halved movement, imposed a −2 penalty to AC and Dexterity saving throws, prevented reactions, restricted affected creatures to either an action or bonus action, and allowed only one attack when using the Attack action. It also carried a chance to delay spellcasting.

The Rift Weavers countered it with shimmering cosmic filaments capable of ending a spell of third level or lower on a target. Each affected Weaver spent an action removing Slow from itself, ultimately neutralizing the spell but sacrificing much of the opening round to do so.

### Flaming Sphere

A concentration spell cast by Soma, creating a five-foot-diameter sphere of roiling flame with a damaging area around it.

Under the encounter’s initial area ruling, the sphere damaged Rift Weavers 3, 5, and 6 when cast. Soma rolled 12 damage; successful Dexterity saves reduced the damage to 6 for Rift Weavers 3 and 5, while Rift Weaver 6 took the full amount.

On later turns, the spell used its normal collision and end-of-turn triggers. Soma moved it as a bonus action, repeatedly placing it beneath ceiling-bound Weavers and around Brewbarry.

Its most important contribution was controlling the webbing. The sphere burned away existing restraints and incinerated new filaments projected through its area. The GM ruled that placing it close to Brewbarry burned through the anchoring webs without requiring the sphere to damage him directly.

Brewbarry nevertheless ended several turns inside its damaging area. His successful saves and Heroism’s temporary hit points made the friendly fire manageable and preferable to repeated restraint.

### Heroism

A concentration spell cast by Valphine on Brewbarry.

It granted four temporary hit points that refreshed each round. These points did not stack with other temporary hit points, could not revive Brewbarry from zero hit points, and would disappear when the spell ended.

Heroism repeatedly absorbed damage from Rift Weaver bites and Soma’s Flaming Sphere. In one exchange, a 9-point attack was reduced by resistance and then entirely consumed by the refreshed temporary hit points, leaving Brewbarry’s actual health untouched.

### Guiding Bolt

Cast by Valphine against Rift Weaver 5.

The spell initially dealt 12 radiant damage. When the party discovered the Rift Weavers’ radiant vulnerability, that damage doubled and killed the creature.

The attack established that radiant magic was particularly effective against the Rift Weavers and provoked Aletra’s sarcastic remarks about Valphine finally figuring it out.

### Silvery Barbs

A first-level reaction spell cast by Vukradin to force successful Rift Weaver attacks against Brewbarry to be rerolled.

The first use turned a hit into a miss and also granted advantage on an ally’s next attack. A later use forced Rift Weaver 4 to reroll a critical hit, again producing a miss.

The party concluded that spending first-level spell slots to negate critical hits was especially effective.

### Blindness

Cast by Valphine on Aletra during her escape, requiring a DC 15 Constitution save.

Aletra failed and became blinded. The spell did not prevent her from escaping because she had already opened the portal directly in front of herself and knew where to step.

Blindness nevertheless caused Aletra to fumble and drop several vials from the extracted box. Valphine therefore failed to capture her sister but left part of the illicit shipment available for the party to recover. Aletra expected her boss, whose identity remained unknown to the party, to be displeased.
