# Chapter 41: The Intervention

Date: Apr 22nd, 2026

## Summary

Before setting out for the council, Vukradin completed his delayed level-six advancement. The table reviewed Unfailing Inspiration and ruled that Universal Speech allowed two-way conversation with Soma in beast form for one hour once per long rest.

On the road to the Emerald Enclave council, the party was intercepted by Thomas, one of Lyra’s trusted agents, who warned them of the political trap being laid. Adabra lacked formal authority to ban Lyra, but she could ostracize her and persuade the Enclave to treat her with indifference. Thomas explained that Reidoth, the nominal head of the local Enclave chapter, was away—and that Adabra had called the meeting specifically during his absence to push her agenda without opposition. Reidoth favored open communication and consensus, but the Enclave had no meaningful top-down hierarchy through which he could simply halt the proceedings. The party briefly considered reaching out to him and kept the option in reserve, though Thomas warned that by the time they fetched Reidoth, Adabra would already have made her case.

The council was already assembled when the party arrived. Adabra refused even to look directly at Soma or Vukradin, addressing the room as though the two did not exist. With cold precision, she described personally walking to the Whispering Grove and finding the treant dead: “Not wounded. Not sleeping. Dead. Roots collapsed. Crown black.” She had pressed Lyra’s people for answers, spoken with two others who had been present, and concluded that the party had deliberately concealed what happened. Adabra admitted that when Vukradin first described the planar creature gathering material near the grove, she had been pleased, calling it “a planar magpie” and the world working as it should. She had paid him five gold pieces for that information. Now, having seen the grove herself, she believed she had rewarded the man who falsified the report.

Vukradin challenged Adabra’s chronology, pointing to the two-week gap between hearing his account and seeing the dead treant herself: “Two weeks. Lot can happen in two weeks.” Adabra called Thomas and Lyra to testify. Both openly acknowledged that Lyra’s chapter had attempted to handle the metastasized treant, failed, and then watched the party kill it. Neither considered this an admission of wrongdoing. Lyra maintained that the grove had been bleeding from a planar infestation and that the party had done what was necessary. Soma’s knowledge of druidic thinking made the situation clear: Lyra and Thomas were not confessing to a crime; they were proudly stating facts about an action they believed had been right.

The party briefly debated whether to pursue Vukradin’s legalistic strategy or openly defend intervention. Soma concluded that the land itself was suffering and that intervention was sometimes the only way to protect it. She quietly used Meril’s staff’s Resonance to test whether Adabra was being mentally controlled, magically manipulated, or consciously lying. The answer to all three possibilities was no. Adabra was a true believer, utterly sincere in her naturalist philosophy. With no conspiracy to expose, Soma embraced the ideological fight: “The land is hurting, and intervention is the only way out. We are here to protect the land at all costs, and if that makes us interventionists, then so be it.” When prompted, she added, “We’re loud, we’re proud.”

The mood began to shift, but Falcon rose in Adabra’s defense. He declared, “I came because someone finally sat long enough to listen. You can keep cheering your victories, I’ve seen what comes after them,” and pointed at Vukradin while blaming men like him for dead trees. Vukradin pulled Falcon aside and demanded to know why a former adventurer—whose lodge, fame, wealth, and life had all been built through intervention—was now publicly condemning him. He threatened to expose Falcon’s own history, only for Falcon to accept the challenge and confess before the council. Falcon admitted that he had once sought to fight dragons, had failed, and had watched intervention repeatedly bring him loss. He claimed that his lodge had burned despite his appeal to heroes for protection. Adabra had given that pain a philosophical explanation.

Falcon’s public confession included another personal attack on Vukradin, whom he called a “silky-tongued liar.” Vukradin had had enough. Quietly rather than theatrically, he announced that he was no longer interested in helping Lyra, turned away, and walked back toward Phandalin. Brewbarry asked whether he should kill Falcon, received no answer, and then asked the remaining party members whether they should kill everyone or leave. Soma rejected the idea: whatever their political differences, the druids were still needed to protect the land.

Without Vukradin’s silver tongue, Soma turned to Aldric Stone Path, a weathered human who had spent decades mapping the mountains. Aldric supported Adabra because she had been the first person willing to ask what he had actually seen: orc incursions, disturbances in nature, and changes everyone else dismissed as normal. Soma forced him to confront the unanswered question at the center of his position—what did Adabra propose doing about any of it? When Soma pointed out that Adabra’s philosophy required allowing those threats to proceed unchecked, Aldric faltered. Adabra insisted, “The displacement is downstream of the intervention. The answer is to stop the cascade by withdrawing. The ecosystem finds equilibrium without help.” Soma answered, “Equilibrium comes in many forms. One of these equilibriums could be the end of everything, of all life. That’s not the equilibrium I’m here to protect.”

Then Xanthopoulos the centaur arrived. He immediately noticed that Soma still carried Meril’s increasingly leafy staff and made its significance plain to the room. Meril was no wandering nobody; he had been deeply respected by the gathered druids. Xanthopoulos declared, “I know whose staff that is. I know what it means that Meril gave it to her. He did not give it for sentiment. He gave it because he picked her.” The revelation lent Soma authority that Adabra could not safely attack without also attacking Meril’s judgment.

Xanthopoulos then delivered grim news: Corbin was dead, and Sister Kaella had escaped “to the wind.” The party had left a low-level fighter guarding a highly dangerous assassin at Dragon Barrow, and Kaella had killed him. Adabra immediately called this another body produced by careless intervention. Xanthopoulos stopped her: “I am not on your side. I’m not on theirs. I’m telling you what my people saw and what happened.” Soma acknowledged the mistake without attempting to sugarcoat it, while also noting that Corbin had been entangled with the Carver and that the party’s previous interventions had prevented other dangers from spreading.

When the council returned to Lyra for a fuller explanation, she explained that a planar rift had been damaging the forest and that the treant was already corrupted when the party killed it. The subsequent attempt to seal the rift failed, leaving the rift unresolved. Soma supported her account with the party’s own experiences: the twisted dwarves at Axeholm, other unnatural distortions, and Talos’s spreading blight. She deliberately withheld discussion of the drow-related incidents but emphasized that these anomalies did not belong in the natural world. She also pointed out Falcon’s hypocrisy: he had once asked the party to intervene against the Talosians who seized his lodge, but now condemned intervention while remaining furious that they had failed to save it.

During a recess, Valphine approached Falcon with a more sympathetic understanding of what had driven him toward Adabra. Falcon was a man who kept losing things; every time he tried to act, something else was taken from him. Valphine apologized that the party had not yet completed the tasks he had entrusted to them and invoked the shared financial interest connecting Falcon to the party through his and Brewbarry’s mine shares. The party’s intervention against the wererats was already helping restore that investment. The reminder cracked Falcon’s certainty, leaving him silent and staring at the floor while Adabra raged against the party’s “colonial” and interventionist mindset.

The council’s private deliberation lasted scarcely thirty seconds. Its verdict was decisive: Adabra’s case did not hold. Corbin’s death was regrettable, but he had been a criminal, and Soma’s choice to prioritize saving the forest from the dragon had been correct. Aldric had effectively admitted that Adabra’s philosophy offered no protection against the threats he cared about, while Falcon depended financially upon interventionists and still needed their help. As one druid summarized, “All you managed to do was offend the bard.” The Emerald Enclave existed to preserve the forest and the region—not to sit idle while everything died in service of some supposed cosmic equilibrium.

Lyra was restored to full good standing. Adabra left in barely contained rage while Soma gave her a smug look and punctuated the victory by dropping Meril’s staff like a performer dropping a microphone. The remaining druids condemned Adabra’s personal attacks on Vukradin as disgraceful and asked whether Soma could persuade the bard to return because they had heard he played very good music. Soma suggested they visit him in Phandalin—or wait until his studio opened. Lyra, overwhelmed and elated, offered to buy the party drinks at the Stonehill Tavern. The moderates had sided with Soma, rejected Adabra’s extreme naturalism as contrary to the druidic calling, and made Lyra a grateful ally.

Afterward, Valphine continued counseling Falcon. She described her journey from Menzoberranzan after demons ravaged the city and her emergence into the Overbright, where “the vividness of the sun beating down on us” became a glorious revelation. The story reached Falcon. He admitted he no longer knew why he had supported Adabra, resolved that the orcs threatening the region needed to be dealt with, and acknowledged, “I probably owe the bard an apology.” Valphine and Soma suggested useful forms that apology might take: a rare instrument, a performance hall or studio room at his rebuilt lodge, and perhaps a small shrine to Lathander. Falcon was not yet converted, but Valphine had opened a path.

As Xanthopoulos departed, he told Soma, “Perhaps Meril made a good choice.” The party elected not to press the other druids for more information about Meril, preferring to leave the mystery intact for the moment. Their next major destination remained Neverwinter, though the Talosian orcs threatened the Phandalin economy and lay close enough to their route that the party could deal with them along the way. Vukradin remained deeply wounded by the evening, tired of saving people only to be vilified by those he had helped, but the others had secured a decisive political victory for Lyra, Soma, and active defense of the forest.

- The DM notes that Vukradin’s silver-tongued reputation has begun to cause resentment among those who feel they have been manipulated by him.
- Adabra and Falcon both reacted so strongly because they believed Vukradin had previously persuaded or “bamboozled” them and wanted to reassert their own power.
- Falcon’s naturalism was not his deepest conviction; it was a despairing response to repeated loss that Adabra exploited during a moment of weakness.

## Locations

### Phandalin

A frontier town containing the Stonehill Tavern and Vukradin’s studio. Vukradin returned there after walking out of the druid council, and the party’s regional obligations shaped the debate over its next destination.

### Whispering Grove

A forest containing a sacred grove where the party killed an already-corrupted treant and then failed to seal the planar rift that had been bleeding the forest. Adabra personally visited and found the treant dead—“Not wounded. Not sleeping. Dead. Roots collapsed. Crown black.” Lyra maintains that destroying the corrupted treant was necessary and that the subsequent ritual left the rift unresolved.

### Emerald Enclave Council Site

A gathering place in the woods where members of the Emerald Enclave met to debate naturalism versus interventionism and decide the fate of Lyra’s standing within the order. The proceedings were relatively informal, with side conversations and druids occasionally speaking at length on rambling subjects, reflecting the Enclave’s lack of top-down hierarchy.

### Axeholm

A location mentioned during the council debate where the party previously encountered disturbing planar anomalies and dwarves twisted by otherworldly forces. Soma uses these transformations as evidence that planar contamination cannot safely be left to seek its own “equilibrium.”

### Stonehill Tavern

A local establishment in Phandalin where Lyra offers to buy the party drinks to celebrate her restored status within the Emerald Enclave.

### Falcon’s Lodge

The hunting lodge belonging to Falcon was overrun by Talosians. Falcon claimed at the council that the lodge had burned down; later campaign records establish that it remained structurally intact but damaged. Its loss is central to Falcon’s despair: he asked heroes to intervene, believes they failed him, and now interprets the occupation and damage as proof that intervention only multiplies suffering. Valphine and Soma suggest Falcon repair it with a shrine to Lathander and a performance hall or studio space for Vukradin as a gesture of goodwill.

### Neverwinter

A major city that the party intends to travel toward as its next destination. The party plans to head north while potentially dealing with the regional orc threat along the way.

## Items

### Five Gold Pieces

The payment Adabra gave to Vukradin for what she now considers a falsified report regarding the state of the sacred Whispering Grove. At the time, she believed his description of a “planar magpie” indicated the world was healing itself. She cited the payment at the council as evidence of how thoroughly she was deceived.

### Falcon’s Ring

A brass ring inscribed with the word “lightning” in both Elvish and Sylvan, which the party discovered inside Falcon’s mechanical dragon-head box while snooping through his lodge. Falcon later showed it to them in daylight and explained that he had recovered it from Iymrith’s already-plundered hoard along with the coins that helped fund his lodge. His companions died during that disastrous expedition; years later, Falcon heard reports of Iymrith’s death but found no body when he reached the hoard. The party returned the ring to Falcon, but Vukradin recalls it during their confrontation as evidence of Falcon’s concealed and embellished past.

### Meril’s Staff

A druidic staff now carried by Soma, noted for its growing green leaves. It formerly belonged to the highly respected druid Meril. Xanthopoulos recognized it immediately and said that Meril gave it to Soma because he personally chose her, marking her as Meril’s chosen successor without settling the staff’s formal status within the Emerald Enclave. The gathered druids’ reaction reveals that Meril was an important and widely respected figure, not merely the wandering drifter Soma imagined. Soma used the staff to determine Adabra was not under magical influence and dramatically mic dropped it at the conclusion of the council meeting.

## Spells

### Resonance

A magical ability used by Soma via Meril’s staff to discern whether Adabra was being mentally controlled, magically manipulated, or consciously lying. The result confirmed she was none of these—she is a genuine true believer in her naturalist philosophy. That discovery caused the party to abandon the possibility of exposing hidden coercion and instead challenge Adabra’s beliefs directly.

### Sending

A standard bard spell cast by Vukradin to contact Reidoth and inform him of the ideological conflict and council taking place in his absence. The message framed the gathering as an unfolding schism or attempted political maneuver within the Enclave.

### Bardic Inspiration

Used by Vukradin to bolster Soma’s confidence and insight during the tense political debate at the Druid Council. The table used a d10 for his inspiration die and treated Unfailing Inspiration—the ability to retain the die when it fails to turn a roll into a success—as available. This records the table’s handling rather than asserting that sixth-level Bardic Inspiration is mechanically a d10.

### Universal Speech

Vukradin reviewed this new level-six feature before the party left for the council. The table ruled that it permitted two-way conversation with beast-form Soma for one hour once per long rest, extending the standard feature beyond merely allowing selected creatures to understand him.

## NPCs

*PCs (Vukradin, Soma, Valphine, Brewbarry) are tracked in `docs/party.md` and not duplicated here.*

### Thomas

An agent and ally of Lyra who intercepts the party on the road to the council, warning them about Adabra’s plans and Reidoth’s absence. He explains that Adabra cannot formally ban Lyra but can orchestrate her ostracism. He also testifies at the council that Lyra’s chapter tried and failed to deal with the metastasized treant before the party intervened. Thomas is openly proud of that intervention and becomes increasingly exasperated by Vukradin’s questioning about Enclave hierarchy.

### Reidoth

The nominal head of the local Emerald Enclave chapter, currently absent from the council. He is known for favoring consensus, discussion, and open communication. Adabra called the meeting specifically during his absence to minimize opposition, though Reidoth lacks the hierarchical authority to prevent her from speaking.

### Adabra

A staunch naturalist druid who believes nature should take its course without outside interference. She called the Emerald Enclave council to ostracize Lyra and sanction Soma, presenting evidence of the dead sacred treant and accusing Vukradin of lying to her. Resonance confirms that she is neither controlled nor consciously deceptive; she sincerely believes withdrawal will allow ecosystems to find equilibrium. Her case was ultimately rejected by the moderate druids after Soma demonstrated that Adabra’s prescription could permit the destruction of everything the Enclave exists to protect. She left the meeting in a rage.

### Falcon

A former adventurer and high-level ranger who owns a hunting lodge. His companions died during his failed attempt to confront Iymrith, and his later efforts to defend his lodge also ended in loss. Adabra persuaded the despondent Falcon that intervention itself was the cause of his suffering. He publicly denounced Vukradin at the council, calling him a silky-tongued liar, but Valphine later broke through his nihilism by invoking the party’s shared financial interest with Falcon through his and Brewbarry’s mine shares, describing her own discovery of Lathander, and offering the possibility of purposeful action. Falcon resolved to address the orc threat and admitted that he owed Vukradin an apology.

### Lyra

A druid whose chapter was assigned to protect a sacred grove. She faced ostracization from the Emerald Enclave after a planar rift forced the party to kill an already-corrupted treant. Lyra testified honestly that the party killed the treant to stop the immediate threat and that the subsequent ritual failed to seal the rift. She never regarded that decision as shameful. The council restored her to full good standing, making her a grateful ally who can be called upon in the future.

### Aldric Stone Path

A weathered human whom the GM describes as being in his early forties. Aldric claims to have spent fifty years mapping the mountains and to know how every valley once appeared, an apparent table contradiction preserved in the account. He initially supported Adabra because she was the first person to listen to his observations about orcs, disturbances, and the mountains’ decline. Soma forced him to acknowledge that Adabra’s policy of withdrawal offered no remedy for any of those dangers, causing his support to collapse.

### Xanthopoulos

A centaur who arrived at the council as a voice of moderation, holding that both pure interventionism and pure naturalism are follies. He brought news of Corbin’s death at the hands of Sister Kaella, refused to let Adabra use his report as an ideological weapon, and recognized the significance of Soma’s staff as belonging to the respected druid Meril. His public declaration that Meril had chosen Soma materially strengthened her position. Before leaving, he told her, “Perhaps Meril made a good choice.”

### Sister Kaella

An assassin and agent of the Carver who was previously in the party’s custody at Dragon Barrow. She escaped and killed Corbin, who had been left to guard her. Her flight creates yet another renewed pursuit of a recurring enemy the party has already had to track repeatedly.

### Corbin

A former bandit and party retainer who supplied information about the Carver. Sister Kaella killed him after the party left them both behind at Dragon Barrow under inadequate guard. The council regarded his death as unfortunate but not sufficient evidence that Soma’s broader interventionist choices had been wrong.
