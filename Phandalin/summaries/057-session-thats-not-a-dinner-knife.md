# Chapter 57: That’s Not a Dinner Knife!

Date: 2026-09-22

## Summary

The session resumed in Bimble’s sewer hideout with the anxious gnome asking where he was supposed to go now. Vukradin immediately tried to persuade him that his name ought to be “Bimbo.” Even a strong persuasion attempt could only make the gnome consider the suggestion; it could not make him surrender his own name. “I see your point, but my name is still Bimble,” he replied with calm, unshakeable firmness.

The party debated several possible refuges. Vukradin proposed their camp, which he described as perfectly safe despite Corbin’s death after the party had left him behind; bringing Bimble openly to dinner with Lord Neverember; leaving him with Lim and the Harpers; or even entrusting him to the local criminal element. Bimble was horrified by the suggestion of appearing before Neverember, whom he expected to blame for the manifold’s collapse and who did not strike him as particularly forgiving. The party finally settled on the Spire of the Morninglord, reasoning that the temple could offer both literal and political sanctuary.

Before leaving the sewers, Valphine knelt beside the body of a drow warrior slain in the earlier battle and communed with the dead. Through the corpse’s eerie, hollow answers, she learned that the drow had been sent by “the sage’s lieutenant” to recover the book—the planar manifold’s instruction manual. The lieutenant had provided a drop-off location, and the book had to be collected before a figure known as “the collector” obtained it. Asked who the collector was, the corpse answered only, “Another drow.” The party debated leaving the body behind, disguising it for a grotesque “Weekend at Bernie’s” procession, or carrying it on Boney’s back. Valphine insisted there was value in preserving evidence of hostile drow activity, so Brewbarry carried the corpse with them.

Vukradin rendered Bimble invisible for the journey. He broadly warned the gnome not to do anything that might break the spell; under the spell’s ordinary operation, only attacking or casting a spell would end it. The party chose to head directly to the temple rather than detour to the manifold while the spell remained active. Valphine, resplendent in her gleaming white armor, drew attention as they passed through the streets: some citizens stared approvingly, others negatively, and everyone seemed to be waiting to see what she would do next.

When Brewbarry unceremoniously dropped the drow corpse outside the temple, Valphine proclaimed, “Behold the power of Lathander. We have discovered Drow operating in the city.” The announcement immediately became complicated by the fact that Valphine was herself a drow. One onlooker decided she planned to raise the corpse as a miracle; when Valphine protested and insisted that this particular drow should remain dead, another cried, “All hail the great Valphine, who wishes to keep the drow dead.” A citizen then approached with a convoluted theological question, but Valphine redirected the exchange toward the practical need to destroy evil in Neverwinter. The resulting applause convinced the party that an even larger crowd would soon form, so they announced that it was time for private prayer and hurried inside despite several followers attempting to accompany them.

Inside, they were met by Brother Aldric, who gave Valphine a particularly keen look when she announced that they had discovered drow operating in the city. Vukradin explained that Bimble had been pursued by the slain drow and needed the temple’s protection because more enemies might soon come for him. Aldric agreed to shelter the gnome and oversee the corpse, although he considered it deeply unorthodox not to attempt last rites even for an enemy. The party ultimately left those rites in his hands, while carefully distinguishing between Valphine and “the wrong kind of drow”—a follower of a weak god rather than Lathander.

More pressing to Aldric was Valphine’s increasingly unorthodox theology. He had summoned Father Jerome, who was expected in roughly a week, to discuss the “searing pain doctrine” and other teachings widely considered heretical. When Aldric suggested that Father Jerome might help her arrive at a more acceptable doctrine, Valphine challenged him for questioning Lathander’s chosen. Her forceful response made him decide that the conversation should not continue in front of nonbelievers. Vukradin promptly declared himself a follower of Lathander who was “all in with Valphine,” while Brewbarry was identified as the entrepreneurial source of the church’s increasingly popular merchandise and possible future “bathrobes of Lathander.” Valphine’s formal title was finally recalled: Bishop of Searing Light, charged by Lathander to found a militant holy order to combat aberrations and evil.

During the temple conversation, Aldus contacted the party by *sending*. He reminded them that Lord Neverember had been waiting and, in his frustration, characterized this as the second dinner engagement after they had already blown Neverember off once. Vukradin promised they would arrive in thirty minutes. A second message later reached Valphine: “His lordship has been seated for an hour. I have told him you are delayed by a matter of civic importance. Please let it be one.” She assured Aldus that they were already walking there.

As the party left the temple, a woman approached Valphine with the confidence of someone who believed she was addressing Lord Neverember’s influential advisor. She introduced herself as Ilsbeth Marrow, proprietor and matron of Marrow and Sons, a small independent shipping company she ran with her four sons. House Margaster’s control of harbor shipping since the manifold’s failure had caused her fees to quadruple. She asked Valphine to advocate for her firm and to persuade Neverember to treat the manifold’s repair with “royal urgency.” Ilsbeth said the machine had appeared about ten years earlier through an arrangement made by the Commission, but she did not know where it had come from and assumed Lord Neverember might. The party’s scrutiny found her transparent and genuinely horrified by the suggestion that she might have any connection to House Margaster.

Boney, the party’s skeletal record keeper, then made his displeasure about their sewer-caked appearance abundantly clear in the tones of a pompous butler. Since they had chosen not to return Bimble to the Harpers, he asked whether they had at least considered obtaining clean clothes. Vukradin discovered that *mending* could not clean fabric, but *prestidigitation* could clean one cubic foot at a time. He used the cantrip to remove the filth from the party’s clothing—a small miracle of practicality that the group marked as perhaps the first useful application of the spell in forty years of Dungeons & Dragons.

They arrived at Lord Neverember’s estate and were welcomed by Aldus, who said his daughter had been delighted by Vukradin’s signature and personal message. The dinner itself was lavish, formal, and European in style, with Neverember initially avoiding business. Valphine treated the meal with traditional drow caution and checked it for poison before anyone ate. Nothing dangerous was detected, and the hungry party dug in.

Lord Neverember proved to be a man who liked to hear that the numbers agreed with what he had already decided to do. He opened the formal conversation by expressing support for Vukradin’s proposal for Universal Basic Treasure. Aldus, he explained, had confirmed that the numbers supported what Neverember already intended to decide. Vukradin objected that UBT was “more of a political philosophy than a spreadsheet” and “transcends mathematics,” but Neverember remained interested in ledgers, circulation, and the danger posed when adventurers hoarded wealth like dragons. Vukradin countered that his party returned treasure to its rightful owners, unlike ordinary adventurers who accumulated houses and retainers like oligarchs. Neverember agreed that distributing treasure would encourage spending and taxation, but was not yet prepared to transform Neverwinter’s entire financial system.

Neverember then produced a written decree declaring Vukradin the mayor of Phandalin in place of Harbin Wester. Waterdeep had opinions about who should govern unclaimed towns near Neverember’s border, and he preferred Vukradin to any alternative. Vukradin tried to clarify whether the appointment required him to remain in town, but Neverember envisioned him as a “big picture” leader while Harbin handled daily administration. Protecting Phandalin now meant traveling to Leilon and assisting with the work there. Vukradin attempted to make Neverwinter’s adoption of UBT a condition of cooperation, but even a persuasive appeal could not move Neverember into changing the city’s entire financial system over dinner. Neverember instead suggested that success in Leilon would create a better opening for that discussion.

The party also raised the planar manifold. Neverember knew it only as the Commission’s machine and assumed it had originally been some magical object brought in by adventurers and purchased by the organization. He admitted that he did not know its source and suggested speaking to the Commission or its current Factor. He promised to ask around about possible replacement parts or another machine. The revelation that the manifold might be repairable surprised him because the Commission had insisted there was nothing to be done. Valphine framed the investigation as patriotic service to Neverwinter and added that she did not want the manifold’s failure to become a profit center for evil parties. Neverember answered approvingly, “A patriot. A patriot,” while Vukradin observed, “The bishop cares for the people.”

The dinner came to an abrupt and fragrant turn when the magical cleaning effects on the party’s clothing wore off, filling the elegant room with the full, unambiguous aroma of the Neverwinter sewers. Vukradin considered cleaning everyone again, but Neverember instead offered fresh clothes. When the party demurred, he clarified that the offer was “not so much for you, it’s for me.”

Brewbarry, meanwhile, had caused his own brand of chaos by refusing to surrender his enormous combat blades at the door. He insisted that they were his personal cutting knives and demonstrated by using them on the food and bread, smashing part of the cutlery and several plates in the process. Neverember attempted to explain that simpler utensils existed, but Brewbarry refused to abandon his method. Valphine declared her gleaming white armor a holy artifact of Lathander, and the evening drew to a close with the party holding a mayoral appointment, a mission in Leilon, a growing list of enemies and political entanglements, and the lingering smell of the sewers clinging to their dignity.

## Scenes

### Bimble Needs a Safe House

#### The party resumes with Bimble’s immediate lodging problem, tests the limits of persuasion, and chooses the Spire of the Morninglord as his sanctuary.

- Bimble asks where he is supposed to go now that his sewer refuge is no longer safe.

- Vukradin jokingly attempts to persuade Bimble to change his name to “Bimbo,” but the gnome steadfastly refuses. Even after the persuasion attempt, Bimble concedes only, “I see your point, but my name is still Bimble.”

- The table establishes that persuasion is not mind control and cannot force someone to accept something they are fundamentally unwilling to do. Vukradin decides that changing the gnome’s mind may simply require persistent daily effort.

- Vukradin proposes the party’s camp, insisting, “Camp’s very safe. Nothing ever bad has happened at our camp.” Soma answers, “Yeah, we only lost what’s-her-face,” and the DM supplies Corbin’s forgotten name before clarifying that the party technically lost him after leaving him behind, rather than in camp itself.

- Bimble strongly opposes meeting Neverember. He expects the Lord Protector to blame him for the manifold’s failure, which has cost Neverwinter an enormous amount of money.

- Valphine weighs whether presenting Bimble would demonstrate the party’s capabilities or merely reveal that they possess something valuable. Her overriding concern is that “Lathander’s power is demonstrated.”

- The party rules out the Harpers and the Dead Rats, settling on the Spire of the Morninglord because it offers both literal sanctuary and political protection.

- When Vukradin presses him about Neverember, Bimble patiently explains the danger again and then apologizes for raising his voice: “You’re absolutely right. My mother would have been very disappointed in me.”

- Vukradin warns Bimble, “If you do anything, you will no longer be invisible, so don’t do anything.”

- The party remains visible; only Bimble is concealed.

- The party discusses whether to visit the planar manifold before heading to the temple. Bimble explains that it lies near the docks and that he knows a sewer route to it.

- Since the docks are not on the route to the temple and Vukradin does not want to risk combat while concentrating on the spell, the party chooses to take Bimble directly to sanctuary.

- The group agrees that it can return to inspect the manifold later even if it cannot yet repair it.

## NPCs

### Bimble

A gnome technician and former operator of the planar manifold who has been hiding in the sewers with a small homunculus on his shoulder and a bucket that recently held ale. He spent approximately nine years tuning the manifold and performed its ten-day maintenance routines, but could not persuade the Commission to reduce its increasingly dangerous throughput targets.

Bimble can tune and operate the manifold but does not understand how its original parts were constructed or where replacements might be found. He believes unauthorized routing changes burned out the machine and fears being scapegoated by Lord Neverember for the resulting loss of revenue. Vukradin, not Bimble, used his photographic memory to retain the manual’s contents.

He is precise about language, calm under Vukradin’s efforts to rename him, and capable of dry patience: “I see your point, but my name is still Bimble.” With the Rift Weavers destroyed and the Dead Rats able to reach his refuge, he accepts the party’s protection and is sheltered at the Spire of the Morninglord.

### Sridar

A drow woman previously encountered by the party at Falcon’s Lodge, where she purchased planar artifacts—including artifacts sold to her by the party for fair-trade gold. She approached Bimble roughly a week to ten days before this session and asked what had been removed from the manifold’s housing. Her knowledge that something had been taken, and her interest in planar technology, prompted Bimble to retreat further into hiding.

### Boney

A skeletal companion and record keeper for the party who acts with the demeanor of a pompous British butler. He reminds the group of its dinner appointment and criticizes its sewer-caked appearance, pointedly asking whether the party has made any arrangement for clean clothes. When someone calls him a pompous ass, the party observes that there is not much of an actual ass left for him to be pompous with.

### Lyra

A druid involved in local politics whom the party mocks as more interested in politicking than rift-closing. The party’s earlier attempt to seal the Whispering Grove rift failed; separately, Soma used Meril’s Staff to close the sewer rift in Chapter 53, prompting the joke that she merely had to “bonk it with a stick”—albeit a very special stick.

### Dead Drow

The corpse of a drow agent slain in the sewers. Through magical interrogation, he reveals that a female lieutenant serving a mysterious sage sent his group to recover “the book,” apparently the planar manifold’s instruction manual. The lieutenant supplied a drop-off location and insisted the book be collected before “the collector” obtained it. The corpse identifies the collector only as “another drow.”

The party carries the body to the Spire of the Morninglord as evidence of hostile drow activity. Brother Aldric undertakes to provide last rites despite the dead agent’s hostile faith.

### Brother Aldric

A keen-eyed brother of the Spire of the Morninglord who agrees to shelter Bimble and oversee the proper handling of the dead drow. He is deeply concerned by Valphine’s controversial “searing pain doctrine” and other teachings considered heretical by much of the church.

Aldric has summoned Father Jerome to meet with Valphine in roughly a week. When Valphine frames his concern as questioning Lathander’s chosen, he becomes visibly uncomfortable and suggests continuing the discussion away from nonbelievers. He also worries that Brewbarry’s influence may cause the faithful to begin wearing bathrobes.

### Aldus

Lord Neverember’s steward and scheduler, who contacts the party through *sending* after they keep his lordship waiting for over an hour. He tells Valphine that he has characterized their delay as a matter of civic importance and adds, “Please let it be one.”

Aldus thanks Vukradin for an autograph and personal message ostensibly meant for his daughter Alducia—a cover for his own fandom. He has reviewed the financial implications of Universal Basic Treasure and reassured Neverember that the numbers support what the Lord Protector already wished to decide.

### Ilsbeth Marrow

The proprietor and matron of Marrow and Sons, a small independent shipping company operated with her four sons. She is being squeezed out by House Margaster’s control of much of the harbor’s regional-distribution business; since the manifold failed, her shipping fees have quadrupled.

Ilsbeth approaches Valphine because she believes the Bishop of Searing Light is an advisor to Lord Neverember. She asks the party to protect her company and press for the manifold’s repair as a matter of royal urgency. She says the machine appeared about ten years earlier through an arrangement made by the Commission but does not know its original source. She reacts with transparent horror when asked whether she has ties to House Margaster.

### Lord Dagult Neverember

The Lord Protector of Neverwinter, a major investor in the Commission, and host of the party’s formal dinner. The wars of the Giants and Dragons have depleted his finances, making the manifold’s failure a significant economic and political problem.

Neverember is intrigued by Universal Basic Treasure but insists that even a political philosophy requires financial ledgers. He worries about adventurers behaving like dragons by removing treasure from circulation, while recognizing that distributing gold to ordinary people can stimulate spending and taxation.

He strategically appoints Vukradin mayor of Phandalin to keep Waterdeep from installing a more convenient authority near Neverwinter’s border. He expects Vukradin to provide vision while Harbin manages mundane administration and sends the party to assist Leilon.

Neverember admits that he does not know the manifold’s origins and is surprised to hear that it might be recoverable after the Commission insisted otherwise. His patience is tested by the party’s returning sewer odor and Brewbarry’s destructive dining technique, but he responds by offering clean clothes and trying to explain conventional cutlery.

## Locations

### Bimble’s Sewer Hideout

A secluded chamber within the Neverwinter sewers where Bimble has been hiding with his homunculus and ale bucket. It was formerly protected by a barrier of Rift Weavers and lies near the site of a recently closed planar rift and several dead bodies.

Once the party destroys the Rift Weavers, the hideout is no longer secure. The Dead Rats can reach it, and the Harpers who supplied Bimble with food are expected to investigate. Bimble knows a route from the sewer network toward the planar manifold near the docks.

### Spire of the Morninglord

A prominent house of worship in Neverwinter where Brother Aldric resides. It offers both religious and political sanctuary, making it the party’s preferred hiding place for Bimble.

Public prayer areas stand near secure catacombs or mortuary spaces where the party leaves the dead drow. A crowd gathers outside after Valphine displays the corpse and announces that hostile drow are operating in the city. The temple is also the center of the growing controversy surrounding Valphine’s doctrines and title as Bishop of Searing Light.

### Lord Neverember’s Dining Hall

A lavish and formal dining room within the Lord Protector’s estate in Neverwinter. The dinner is attended by the party, Neverember, Aldus, waitstaff, household servants, and guards—roughly twelve to fifteen people.

Weapons are ordinarily surrendered before entry, although Brewbarry persuades the household that his enormous blades are necessary cutting knives. The hall’s dignified atmosphere is disrupted by smashed plates, broken cutlery, and the sudden return of the party’s sewer stench.

### Phandalin

A town near Neverwinter’s border that Lord Neverember officially designates as Vukradin’s mayoral responsibility. It has publicly endorsed Universal Basic Treasure and is still developing its rules, making it a potential testing ground for Vukradin’s political philosophy.

Neverember sees Phandalin as a strategic buffer against Waterdeep’s influence. Harbin Wester may continue handling daily administration, while Vukradin is expected to provide higher-level governance and protect the town by assisting nearby Leilon.

## Items

### Bimble’s Bucket

A simple bucket that recently held ale, carried by Bimble in his sewer hideout. Along with the homunculus on his shoulder, it contributes to the improvised, isolated character of his living arrangements.

### Model 1486 Spatial Rectification Calliope Manual

An operator’s manual for the planar manifold produced by Bimble during the party’s meeting in the sewers. Its 1486 designation refers to the year 1486 DR (Dalereckoning), and its restart procedure differs substantially from that of the Model 1489 Calliope at Axeholm.

Vukradin has retained the manual’s contents through his photographic memory. The mysterious sage wants the physical manual recovered, while the sage’s lieutenant ordered the dead drow to retrieve it before “the collector” could obtain it.

### Meril’s Staff

Soma’s sentient staff, used by the party to close a planar rift at the cost of one leaf; three leaves remain. Its ability to seal the rift leads the party to joke that Soma proved more capable than Lyra simply by “bonking” the planar disturbance with the correct magical stick.

### Planar Manifold

A powerful magical machine near Neverwinter’s docks, used by the Commission to teleport goods throughout the region. Merchants paid the Commission a percentage of each shipment, the Lionshield Coster was its primary client, and Lord Neverember invested heavily in it.

The machine required maintenance every ten days to suppress Rift Weaver activity. After approximately two years of increasingly unsustainable operation, an unknown person received prolonged access and installed technically superior but unauthorized routing modifications outside the official register.

The manifold has been non-functional for roughly seven weeks after those modifications apparently burned it out, though the true cause remains unresolved. Its collapse has disrupted trade, quadrupled costs for businesses such as Marrow and Sons, and allowed House Margaster to profit from its control of conventional shipping. Bimble can operate and tune it but does not know how to obtain replacement parts.

### Mayoral Decree of Phandalin

An official written document produced by Lord Neverember during dinner that legally instates Vukradin as the new mayor of Phandalin in place of Harbin Wester. Neverember had prepared it before the party arrived, making the appointment effectively decided in advance.

### Brewbarry’s Combat Blades

Oversized weapons that Brewbarry insists are personal cutting knives. He refuses to surrender them at Lord Neverember’s dinner and uses them on his food and bread, smashing fine cutlery and plates while demonstrating their supposed necessity.

### Valphine’s White Armor

A set of gleaming white armor worn by Valphine. It draws both reverent and hostile attention on the streets of Neverwinter and is permitted at the formal dinner as part of her religious image. Valphine declares it to be a holy artifact of Lathander.

## Spells

### Invisibility

Cast by Vukradin upon Bimble to conceal him from sight, allowing the party to smuggle him through the city streets to the Spire of the Morninglord without attracting unwanted attention.

The spell lasts one hour and requires concentration. Vukradin warns Bimble not to do anything that might break the effect and rejects a detour to the manifold because he does not want to risk combat while maintaining it.

### Speak with Dead

Cast by Valphine to question a slain drow about his mission in the sewers. The corpse reveals that the sage’s female lieutenant sent the drow to retrieve “the book” before “the collector” could acquire it, provides a drop-off location, and identifies the collector only as another drow.

### Sending

A telepathic communication spell used by Aldus to deliver direct mental messages to the party on two occasions, urging them to attend their dinner with Lord Neverember. The recipient recognizes Aldus as the sender and can answer immediately.

Aldus first reminds the party that his lordship is waiting. His second message tells Valphine that Neverember has been seated for an hour and that Aldus has attributed their delay to a matter of civic importance.

### Prestidigitation

Used by Vukradin to instantaneously clean the sewer filth from the party’s clothing before their meeting with Lord Neverember. Each casting cleans an object no larger than one cubic foot, requiring multiple uses to make everyone presentable.

The table celebrates this as an exceptionally rare practical use of the cantrip. The effects wear off during dinner, causing the party’s sewer odor to return and prompting Neverember to offer fresh clothes for his own comfort.

### Detect Poison and Disease

Cast by Valphine upon the food served at Lord Neverember’s dinner table. The precaution is treated as normal drow etiquette rather than an accusation against the host. No poison is detected, and the party eats safely.
