# Chapter 13: Untitled

Date: 2025-08-27

## Summary

The party made their way back toward Phandalin, their minds turning to unfinished business and forgotten obligations. Along the road, talk turned to a quest they had nearly let slip through their fingers — a plea from Adabra Gwynn, the midwife and herbalist they had once rescued from Umbrage Hill. The quest had never made it onto their tracker, prompting jokes about a “critical failure” on bookkeeping. Adabra had spoken of a grove near Phandalin that was once “a testament to Chauntea’s blessing, teeming with life,” but now suffered from a strange magical blight. Its trees twisted in unnatural ways, while its leaves grew too bright or sickly pale, “as if the colors are wrong.” She believed the corruption was not part of the natural cycle but the consequence of interventionists, zealous rituals, and forceful cleansing around Phandalin and Leilon.

Vukradin immediately asked how much money saving the grove would pay, explaining that “the forest is not gonna build my music studio.” Adabra had offered no monetary reward, making the task a difficult sell for him. The party initially judged the grove to be out of their way, only for the GM later to correct its marked location: it was actually only a few hours outside Phandalin, practically on the town’s doorstep. They continued into town before deciding whether to investigate.

They had barely crossed into Phandalin before Adabra herself stepped out to meet them, her eyes finding Soma with quiet urgency. When she asked whether they had visited the Whispering Grove, Soma admitted that they had not yet reached it during their travels because they had been dealing with other blights. Adabra answered, “This is important,” warning that if the changes continued, “the balance, the natural balance, will be disturbed.” She pleaded with Soma to address the problem and began to walk away.

Vukradin stopped her to ask about the Order of the Gauntlet. Adabra immediately noticed that his Order pin was gone; he first said it had been broken and then corrected himself, claiming it had been lost in battle. She called the Order “quite transparent” in its desire to make the world better, but condemned that goal as dangerously naïve. In her view, every forceful push toward good provoked an increasingly powerful reaction from evil, threatening the symbiosis of the multiverse. She mocked the Order for chasing dragons, killing orcs, clearing land for civilization, and even donating recovered treasure to charity rather than behaving like ordinary adventurers.

Adabra clarified that she did not deny the existence of good or evil acts; she believed that forcibly remaking the world in either direction destroyed the balance she regarded as intrinsically good. She reminded Vukradin that she had never wanted to leave her house at Umbrage Hill, but had nevertheless intervened with Harbin on the party’s compensation because she recognized the balance of Vukradin’s cause. She had believed his devotion to music — “part of the music of the universe” — would help him see the world as she and Soma did. Instead, his alliance with the Order had disappointed her. Vukradin, increasingly irritated by what he considered her patronizing tone, ended the discussion. Adabra renewed her warning about the Whispering Grove and offered to sell the party healing potions, which they declined.

The party then turned their attention to their accumulated loot, including octopus hide armor and goods recovered along the coast. They visited the Lionshield Coster and asked whether markings might reveal where the objects originated. The merchant told them that such questions called for identification magic. No Identify scrolls were currently available, but the Coster might be able to locate some — or find a suitable magic user — given a few days.

The party paid a visit to Harbin Wester, the Town Master of Phandalin, to report their success at the Tower of Storms. Harbin reacted to the report of Talos worshippers with alarm: “We have dragons, and now we have Talos?” He was relieved to hear that the altar had been sanctified, though the party cautioned that Talos worship might remain active in Leilon. The green heart beacon had already been destroyed. They warned that the lighthouse lacked a keeper to maintain a sustainable replacement; no operating replacement was confirmed. Harbin promised to inform Lord Neverwinter that they had cleansed the lighthouse and to put in a good word for them.

When the party considered carrying the report to Neverwinter personally, Harbin warned that Lord Neverwinter was a busy man and might question why they had left while Phandalin remained in disrepair. The GM put the disparity more bluntly: Lord Neverwinter ruled a large city, while they were third-level adventurers. The party conceded that they “didn’t rate” an audience and moved on.

Their next stop was the tavern, where Ser Kaelen and Toblen Stonehill — nicknamed Spider-Man — welcomed them warmly. Their previous visit had been good for business: Vukradin’s music had drawn a crowd, while Spider-Man had enjoyed recounting the tale behind his nickname. Ser Kaelen eagerly asked whether the party had secured the Tower of Storms. When they described the Talos cleric’s hole in his chest and explained that he had illuminated his own heart to power the lighthouse, Kaelen grew grave. No ordinary shaman of Talos could accomplish such a thing, suggesting that some deeper force had been operating there. He connected this danger to the disorder that had followed the wars of giants and dragons, during which many of his brethren had died.

When the party raised the ambush they had suffered on the road to the tower, Ser Kaelen denied betraying them and, according to the GM, was telling the truth. Asked who else had known their destination, Kaelen simply addressed the tavern. Patrons admitted they had known and had even taken bets on whether all four adventurers would return. The party’s mission had never been secret: they had argued about it publicly and spoken to Harbin in the town square, including a memorable demand about how to destroy a tower. Spider-Man had bet that Vukradin’s musical inspiration would rally everyone and bring the whole party home. Having won the pool, he pressed ten gold pieces into Vukradin’s hand. He added that the only people who had not known where the party was going were “the truly dead, not the partially dead, or the undead, or the coming back.”

Ser Kaelen also confirmed that Adabra belonged to the Emerald Enclave. He described the Enclave and the Order of the Gauntlet as friends who had allied against existential threats, notably when Tiamat appeared and an Enclave faction helped defeat the armies outside the Well of Dragons. Nevertheless, the two organizations disagreed sharply. The Enclave sought a mythical balance; the Order believed that refusing to strive for good would allow that balance to crush everything beautiful. Kaelen launched into an extended defense of universal good, heroism, and the intrinsic value of fighting for what was right.

He then shared rumors of a new bandit leader operating around Phandalin: a figure called the Carver. Unlike most bandits, who boasted and made themselves easy to locate with divination, the Carver concealed his identity behind a name and the organized violence of his crew. Ser Kaelen admitted that discovering the Carver’s location was one reason he had remained in Phandalin. He hoped the party might gather intelligence in the field so the Order could “call in the cavalry.” The party noted that they knew a former member of the Carver’s organization, though by their estimation he was “the lowest lackey imaginable” and might still have “trainee” written on his armor.

Asked about the Whispering Grove, Ser Kaelen described it as a formerly pleasant, shaded place where townspeople once relaxed. Something had changed over the preceding months, and people no longer visited. He had not investigated because no one had been hurt, dragons and bandits seemed more urgent, and Adabra’s warnings sounded like an internal Emerald Enclave dispute “about a bunch of trees.” He was particularly dismissive of her claim that the grove suffered from interplanar interference, calling it one of the most ridiculous things he had heard. Even so, he acknowledged that Phandalin needed help and said the townspeople would be grateful if the party restored the grove.

The discussion returned to the recovered treasure. Vukradin realized that Identify was already among his spells. The party then discovered that the six 100-gp pearls recovered from the Golden Gull shipwreck were still listed on their tracker. The GM ruled that an ordinary casting could reveal an item’s normal magical information, while an augmented historical reading would consume a 100-gp pearl. Vukradin was morally willing to spend “blood money” but hesitated to sacrifice party treasure — especially treasure that might otherwise fund his music studio. No casting or expenditure was confirmed.

The party nevertheless told Ser Kaelen about the recovered goods, the boats, and their names. He promised to make inquiries and asked them to return in a few days. He commended their apparent desire to return stolen goods to their rightful owners rather than robbing the dead. Valphine improvised a supposed authentication test involving a secret code hidden inside one chest, but the invention was immediately obvious to everyone present.

That evening, Vukradin took to the stage at the tavern. Valphine danced beside him, balancing his subtle and complex music with a more broadly accessible performance. The combination pleased more patrons than Vukradin’s music alone and earned the party ten silver pieces in total. Because they no longer had to pay for their room, Vukradin celebrated that they were “really moving up in the ranks.”

The party retired to their shared room. In the morning, Vukradin found a letter that had been slid beneath their door. It was addressed to him and signed as coming from “a friend to True Justice.” Its contents caused immediate alarm and recognition around the table, including references to House Margaster, and led the party to suspect Harper involvement. Vukradin observed that he had distrusted Ser Kaelen only minutes before speaking with him and declared a new interest in contacting the Harpers.

When questioned, Spider-Man revealed that he himself had put the letter beneath the door. It had arrived by post from Neverwinter, and he had simply forgotten to deliver it the previous evening. He insisted that he did not pry into guests’ correspondence because he was “a gentleman.” Vukradin thanked him for respecting the letter’s privacy and tipped him a silver piece.

With the mysterious letter in hand and the corrected location of the Whispering Grove only a few hours outside town, Soma made clear that she wanted to investigate the blight next. Vukradin agreed to support his friend despite expecting no monetary reward, speculating — without much confidence — that the Carver might be there. He also reasoned that gaining allies among several organizations could benefit the party. The GM cautioned that every major organization contained internal factions, and that Ser Kaelen’s beliefs did not necessarily represent every member of the Order of the Gauntlet.

- Soma expresses a strong desire to investigate the Whispering Grove next, having been unable to sleep comfortably with Adabra’s warning hanging over her.
- Vukradin agrees to back Soma because they are friends, even though he doubts the grove will yield any money.
- He speculates that the Carver might be hiding there; Soma doubts it.
- The party observes that resolving the blight may at least produce “a happier guide,” and that a happy guide is a safer guide.
- Vukradin reasons that gaining allies among several organizations could be useful, though Soma warns that those organizations may object when they learn the party also works with their rivals.
- The GM clarifies that the Order of the Gauntlet, Emerald Enclave, Harpers, Zhentarim, and Lord’s Alliance all contain internal factions; no single representative speaks for an entire organization.

## Locations

### Phandalin

A small frontier town that serves as a central hub for the party’s activities. The party returns here to rest, report their findings, gather information, perform at the tavern, and consult local contacts. Their supposedly private plans have a habit of becoming common knowledge because discussions with Harbin and others occur openly in the town square.

### Whispering Grove

A grove only a few hours outside Phandalin, once vibrant and described as “a testament to Chauntea’s blessing, teeming with life.” It formerly offered a pleasant patch of trees and shade where townspeople could relax. It is now suffering from a strange magical blight that twists the trees unnaturally and causes leaves to become too bright or sickly pale, as though their colors are wrong. People have avoided it for months. Adabra attributes the disturbance to interventionist meddling and possible planar interference, while Ser Kaelen dismisses it as a low-priority Emerald Enclave concern.

### Leilon

A nearby town mentioned as a site where Talos worship may still be active, despite the party having cleansed the lighthouse. Adabra also includes the region around Leilon in her criticism of interventionists, zealous rituals, and forceful cleansing.

### Lionshield Coster

A merchant outpost in Phandalin where the party inquires about purchasing magical identification scrolls, identifying the origins of recovered goods, and potentially selling their octopus hide armor. No Identify scrolls are presently available, but the merchant may locate scrolls or a capable magic user within a few days.

### Tower of Storms

A coastal lighthouse recently cleared by the party of harpies and a strange cleric of Talos. The cleric had a hole in his chest and illuminated his own heart to power the lighthouse beacon, a feat Ser Kaelen says lies beyond an ordinary Talos shaman. Its altar has been sanctified and the green heart beacon destroyed. Temporary illumination and fuel had been considered; the party warned that a sustainable replacement needed a keeper, but neither an operating replacement nor an appointment was confirmed.

### Neverwinter

A large city to the north and the seat of Lord Neverwinter. Harbin plans to send word there about the party’s success at the Tower of Storms. He discourages the party from seeking a personal audience while Phandalin remains in disrepair. The mysterious letter addressed to Vukradin also arrived by post from Neverwinter.

## NPCs

### Adabra Gwynn

A midwife, herbalist, and member of the Emerald Enclave whom the party previously rescued from Umbrage Hill despite her desire to remain in her home. She recalled intervening with Harbin on the party’s compensation, believing Vukradin’s devotion to music represented a balanced and worthy cause.

Adabra is a firm believer in natural balance. She does not deny that good and evil acts exist, but she believes attempts to force the multiverse toward either extreme provoke an opposing reaction and threaten its essential symbiosis. She regards the Order of the Gauntlet’s zealous pursuit of universal good as “the foolish path of imbalance.”

She stopped engaging closely with Vukradin after he began wearing the Order’s pin and instead entrusted the Whispering Grove matter privately to Soma. She warns that the grove’s magical blight may be connected to interventionist meddling and interplanar interference. Before departing, she offered to sell the party potions of healing.

### Harbin Wester

The cautious Town Master of Phandalin, known for receiving visitors from behind a locked door. He is alarmed that the region must contend with both dragons and Talos worshippers. He is relieved the lighthouse altar has been sanctified and promises to send Lord Neverwinter a favorable report about the party.

Harbin also recognizes the need for a new lighthouse keeper. He discourages the party from traveling to Neverwinter personally while Phandalin remains in disrepair, warning that Lord Neverwinter may question why they abandoned the work they agreed to perform locally.

### Ser Kaelen

A member of the Order of the Gauntlet who values heroism, universal good, and the intrinsic meaning of fighting for what is right. He is troubled by the unusual power displayed by the Talos cleric at the lighthouse, stating that no typical shaman of Talos could illuminate his own heart in that fashion.

Kaelen confirms that Adabra belongs to the Emerald Enclave. Although the Enclave and Order have allied against existential threats such as Tiamat, he rejects the Enclave’s faith in balance and argues that failing to strive for good allows ugliness and evil to triumph.

He remains in Phandalin partly because of the Carver’s organized bandit raids and hopes to use the party as reconnaissance agents before calling in Order forces. He is skeptical of Adabra’s claims of interplanar interference in the Whispering Grove and has treated the blight as a lower priority than dragons, bandits, and other direct threats. He also agrees to investigate the party’s recovered treasure and the names of the associated boats.

### Toblen Stonehill

The tavern keeper in Phandalin, nicknamed Spider-Man after a story he enjoys recounting to customers. Vukradin’s performances and the party’s adventures have been good for his business.

He participated in a tavern betting pool concerning the party’s expedition to the Tower of Storms and won by wagering that Vukradin’s musical inspiration would bring all four adventurers home. He gave Vukradin ten gold pieces from the winnings.

Spider-Man also acted as the middleman for a private letter sent to Vukradin from Neverwinter. He forgot to deliver it the evening it arrived and slipped it beneath the party’s door overnight. When questioned, he insisted that he never pries into guests’ correspondence because he is “a gentleman.”

### The Carver

A mysterious and secretive new bandit leader rumored to be operating around Phandalin. The Carver’s forces have carried out organized raids and may be influencing local humanoid groups to act with unusual coordination.

Unlike typical bandits, who boast and expose themselves to investigation or divination, the Carver hides behind a name and the violent actions of his crew. Ser Kaelen does not know the Carver’s location or true identity and hopes the party can gather enough intelligence for the Order of the Gauntlet to intervene.

### Valphine

A drow cleric and player character in the party. She danced during Vukradin’s musical set at the tavern, balancing his subtle and complex music with a more broadly appealing performance and increasing the group’s earnings.

During the discussion of recovered treasure, she improvised a false claim that a secret code had been hidden inside one chest as a test for anyone claiming ownership. The ruse was immediately recognized as an invention.

## Items

### Octopus Hide Armor

Ordinary hide armor made from giant-octopus hide, recovered along the coast. The party discusses its provenance and possible rightful owner before deciding whether to sell it.

### Order of the Gauntlet Pin

A symbol of alliance previously worn by Vukradin. Its absence was immediately noted by Adabra, who had withdrawn from him after he began displaying it. Vukradin first claimed the pin had been broken and then corrected himself, saying it had been lost in battle.

### Pearls

Six pearls worth one hundred gold pieces each, recovered from the Golden Gull shipwreck near the Tower of Storms. Their continued presence surprised both the party and the GM because treasure is often sold immediately upon returning to town.

The party discusses using a pearl to fuel an augmented historical reading of one recovered item. Vukradin is morally willing to spend wealth he regards as “blood money” but worries that consuming shared treasure would betray his companions and impede construction of his music studio. No pearl was confirmed expended.

### Mysterious Letter

A private note addressed to Vukradin, sent by post from Neverwinter and delivered beneath the party’s door by Spider-Man. It is signed as coming from “a friend to True Justice,” contains a reference that recalls House Margaster, and leads the party to suspect contact from the Harpers. Its actual author remains unconfirmed.

## Spells

### Identify

A spell Vukradin realizes he possesses after the party has already asked merchants where to find identification magic. Its standard use can reveal an item’s magical properties, but not necessarily its complete provenance.

The GM distinguishes ordinary identification from an augmented reading of an item’s history, which would cost a 100-gp pearl. Because the party possesses six such pearls, this is possible, but Vukradin hesitates over consuming shared treasure. No casting or pearl expenditure is confirmed.

Vukradin also briefly considers casting Identify on the mysterious letter, but the note is not magical.

### Mending

A spell Soma mentions possessing, capable of repairing damaged objects.

### Locate Object

A spell Valphine mentions possessing, though she jokes about its effectiveness working in reverse.
