# Chapter 26: From Retreat to Redemption (and Cheese)

Date: 2025-12-10

## Summary

At the Woodland Manse, Valphine called upon her divine powers to heal her battered companions, restoring 16 hit points to everyone with her Channel Divinity. The respite was brief: another half-orc Anchorite, disguised as a wild boar, moved into position and struck Soma and Vukradin with a crackling bolt of electricity. Both made their Dexterity saves and suffered reduced damage, but even that was enough to wipe away much of Valphine’s healing. As more cultists remained visible to the north and at least one more waited inside the house, the party realized they were dangerously outnumbered.

Faced with overwhelming odds and the prospect of still more lightning-wielding enemies, the adventurers made the tactical decision to retreat. The warning signs were difficult to ignore—especially once someone observed, “It’s never a good sign when the GM says flee.” Vukradin, mindful of how close he had come to acquiring a share in a gold mine, strongly supported withdrawal rather than a glorious but pointless death. Soma, whose bear form gave her a speed of 40 feet, grabbed Valphine and carried her to safety, while giant-sized Brewbarry tucked Vukradin under his arm, taking care not to damage the bard’s musical instrument. The party successfully disengaged from the cultists and escaped into the forest.

As they withdrew, one of the talking boars muttered, “Typical. All these strangers, they come in with promises and then they don’t deliver.” The boars remained offended that the Anchorites were masquerading as boars without observing proper boar culture and etiquette. The party pointed out that greeting strangers with lightning bolts violated their own standards of etiquette, and that disliking lightning was hardly unique to boar culture.

At the clearing where Corbin and Shrimpy were waiting with the cart, the party found Shrimpy arguing that everyone must clearly be dead and that Corbin therefore had no reason to keep holding him. Shrimpy insisted that he had heard the lightning from a distance—“They make a big boom, a flash”—rather than possessing prior knowledge of the danger. Brewbarry and Valphine remained suspicious and displeased. The party gathered the Emerald Enclave fighters from Falcon’s Hunting Lodge, traveled south to the forest’s edge west of Conyberry, and then followed the road toward Phandalin. The journey was too long to finish in one day without exhausting Brewbarry, so they covered nearly thirty miles, camped, and took a much-needed long rest before continuing.

Upon arriving in Phandalin, Vukradin called a council meeting. The party delivered Shrimpy to Town Master Harbin Wester and reported the Cult of Talos presence at the Woodland Manse, including shapeshifting half-orc Anchorites who could hurl lightning. Ser Kaelen was relieved to see them after several days away and asked what they had learned. The party’s immediate conclusion was succinct: “Lightning bolts hurt.”

The party also disclosed their encounter with the Cult of the Kraken and asked whether anyone knew of the organization. Most of the council knew nothing. Ser Kaelen of the Order of the Gauntlet described it as one of many cults found along the Sword Coast, promising unity of mind and purpose. He acknowledged suspicions that powerful psionic creatures might be behind the society, though he was skeptical of Valphine’s immediate insistence that mind flayers were responsible.

Jenna Roscoe of the Lords’ Alliance dismissed the Kraken Society as one of the “posh cults” joined by rich and powerful people who danced around in the woods. Under further questioning, she described ceremonies involving a communal mind meld accompanied by drinking, debauchery, and shared sensation. She maintained that the society was not a threat to the Lords’ Alliance, arguing that the Alliance would deal with it if it were. Nevertheless, Valphine sensed that Jenna knew more than she initially admitted. Jenna eventually acknowledged that several members of Neverwinter high society were associated with the cult and supplied names. She rejected the mind-flayer theory on the grounds that illithid control did not ordinarily work over the distances attributed to the Kraken Society.

The meeting took a dramatic turn when Adabra learned of the party’s intervention against the Kraken cult. Seething with fury, she identified what they had witnessed as a temporal violation and planar distortion. In her view, the party had no business interfering: “You should have just let it grow and expand.” When the party protested that the cultists had been stabbing and trying to kill them, she maintained that intervention only caused the forces behind planar incursions to push harder.

Adabra used the involvement of mind-controlled Emerald Enclave members as proof that the Enclave’s interventionists were wrong. She regarded multiversal and planar distortions as part of nature and believed the attempt to prevent Tiamat from entering the world had triggered later cataclysms. Her preferred response to smaller incursions was to accept them as part of the natural order. Soma concluded that Adabra was entitled to her opinion just as Soma was entitled to tell her to “fuck off,” while others suggested Adabra was also entitled to a bonk on the head. Adabra stormed out in righteous fury. The party concluded that her views were dangerously extreme and suspected this would not be their last conflict with her.

Ser Kaelen then provided welcome news. Through a chain of contacts extending to a specialist at Candlekeep, the Order of the Gauntlet had identified the original owners of magical items the party had found aboard the ships. He stressed that the research had cost the Order time and money—not to demand reimbursement, but to demonstrate how much the Order valued its relationship with Vukradin. He supplied a list connected to the items and assured the party there would be “conflict-free gold” for returning them, while warning that the matter would not be simple.

Valphine returned to the partially reconstructed church, where cleanup and construction had progressed during the party’s absence. She delivered a powerful sermon that sent the congregation into religious ecstasy. Unfortunately, the worshippers concluded that proving themselves required staring directly at the sun until they went blind. Valphine clarified that they were meant to appreciate “the pain of the light,” not permanently blind themselves, but the discovery that Lesser Restoration could cure blindness transformed the misunderstanding into a new sacrament: aspirants would endure Lathander’s light and then be restored. Valphine specified that it should occur only on high holy days, though the congregation understood it as an initiation required to become a true church member.

Valphine also mounted the severed head of a Kraken Society cultist on a pike outside the church. Brin and Giles were ecstatic, having found renewed purpose in their work. Teega the Smith, recently freed from the Kraken Society, was visibly unsettled by the theatrical sermon, deliberate blinding, miraculous restoration, and severed head. Even so, Valphine’s message forced her to reflect. The Kraken Society had offered peaceful bliss; Valphine offered a way to endure pain and redeem it. Teega remained conflicted before departing for her own home.

The party then encountered Don-Jon Raskin, a verbose and boastful man holding court in the tavern. He regaled listeners with stories from his careers as a gold prospector, miner, fur trader, privateer, and whaler. He claimed to have discovered the “mother load of all mother loads” and boasted of possessing a natural nose for gold. He drew the line at ivory trading, declaring, “Ivory trade is immoral,” and protested that he was “not a monster” when seal clubbing was suggested.

Don-Jon was the newly appointed overseer of the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine, hired by a consortium in Neverwinter. At the party’s prompting, it emerged that one member of that consortium overlapped with Jenna’s list of Kraken Society associates, though Don-Jon himself appeared to be a hired doer rather than a conspirator. His employers had ordered him to wait for an escort and threatened consequences to his profit margin if he traveled alone.

Before departing, Don-Jon insisted on telling Brewbarry about attending a barbarian tribal celebration. He fondly remembered food, drink, and fires and believed the tribe had embraced him as one of their own. Brewbarry immediately understood that the tribe had regarded Don-Jon as a fool and humored him as an outsider, but maintained a stoic expression, placed a large hand on his shoulder, and accepted another ale in the spirit of “brotherhood.” Don-Jon remained oblivious.

Don-Jon was equally fascinated by Valphine. Upon learning that the drow cleric worshipped Lathander despite suffering in sunlight, he declared her devotion a welcome variation from the faith’s usual “straight and narrow” emphasis on sunrises and sunsets. He praised the new temple and its importance as a religious center for the community, then continued talking for what felt like five hours.

As the party escorted Don-Jon toward the mine, they discovered six dead orcs scattered across rocky ground within five miles of their destination. The corpses wore hide armor, and their greataxes and javelins lay around them. Examination revealed that extreme cold—not lightning—had killed them, providing clear evidence of the white dragon’s activity. The orcs bore no Talos markings and were ordinary mountain orcs. Once Don-Jon learned they were not Talosians, his bravado returned immediately.

At the foot of a soaring, snow-capped mountain, the party found a tunnel concealed among bushes. A wooden plank above its mouth bore the words “Mountain’s Toe” in Common. Before they could simply leave Don-Jon there, two wererats in hybrid form appeared and announced, “Excuse us, but this mine belongs to us.”

Don-Jon admitted that he knew nothing about the mine, its layout, its personnel, or its current occupants because he had assured his employers that “Don-Jon don’t need no stinking preparatory maps or information.” The wererats explained that they had seized the mine after losing their former lair. They offered to bring the party before their leadership and guaranteed their safety. The creatures initially became alarmed when they noticed Valphine was a drow, but Don-Jon’s indiscriminate backslapping and the party’s otherwise friendly conduct calmed them.

Deeper in the mine, the party met Zeleen Varnaster, leader of the Whiskered Gang. She explained that the gang’s former home was the Shrine of Savras, a ruin north of the rocky foothills of the Sword Mountains. Roughly sixteen orcs and several ogres had driven them out after the white dragon displaced those creatures from the mountains. Some of the ogres belonged to a tribe the party had already antagonized through earlier killings and the incident with Borg.

Zeleen proposed that the party clear the Shrine of Savras, after which the Whiskered Gang would supposedly leave the mine and return home. She initially refused to risk any of her people, then grudgingly offered two wererats after the party challenged her unwillingness to fight for or reclaim her own lair. Zeleen excused herself by declaring, “I certainly can’t go. I’m too valuable to go,” prompting the two nominated wererats to react uneasily when she spoke of possibly “losing” them.

The party questioned how the wererats were feeding themselves inside a gold mine. Zeleen explained that some gang members remained available to hunt. When the discussion turned to cheese, she delivered a lengthy objection to the offensive stereotype that wererats must love cheese—before finally asking whether the party had any. Attention then turned to Petunia, the exceptional cow rescued at Butterskull Ranch, whose milk was said to produce spectacular cheese.

Valphine proposed exchanging regular cheese shipments for the wererats’ labor. Don-Jon objected that he had no budget for cheese and had not been given money to recruit miners. The party pointed out that the mine produced no gold while idle and that a ready-made workforce was standing in front of him. An insight check also revealed that the wererats were concealing several things, most importantly that they liked the mine and had no intention of surrendering it regardless of what happened at the shrine.

Don-Jon initially insisted that no one could live in an operating mine because it was unsafe. When reminded that the proposed residents were lycanthropic wererats, he conceded that he had never considered the issue from that angle. Vukradin’s persuasion finally convinced him that the gang could nest in one section and mine another. The party struck an initial accommodation: the Whiskered Gang would live in and work the mine in exchange for regular shipments of cheese made from Petunia’s milk. The dangerous orcs and ogres at the Shrine of Savras were left undisturbed, and the party returned toward Phandalin with the cheese supply still to be arranged.

- The party strikes an initial deal: the Whiskered Gang will live in part of the mine and work another section in exchange for regular shipments of cheese made from Petunia’s milk.
- The party leaves the orcs and ogres at the Shrine of Savras alone, avoiding a dangerous and unnecessary confrontation.
- The cheese arrangement remains incomplete because the party must arrange production with Big Al and Petunia in Phandalin; Qelline’s grazing agreement remains a prerequisite for regular deliveries.
- With participants beginning to depart for the evening, the group agrees to continue from Phandalin after treating the mining arrangement as provisionally settled.

## NPCs

### Anchorites

Shapeshifting half-orcs and members of the Cult of Talos who can transform into wild boars. They wield powerful lightning magic and attacked the party at the Woodland Manse, forcing them to retreat. At least two expended lightning attacks during the confrontation, while additional Anchorites remained visible north of the party and inside the manor.

### Shrimpy

A prisoner being transported by the party who seemed to anticipate danger at the Woodland Manse, claiming everyone would die. While guarded near the cart, he tried to convince Corbin that the party’s apparent death meant he should be released. Shrimpy claimed he knew about the battle only because he heard and saw the explosive lightning from a distance. He was delivered to Town Master Harbin Wester in Phandalin.

### Corbin

An individual who accompanied the party and guarded Shrimpy while the adventurers approached the Woodland Manse. Corbin refused to release the prisoner despite Shrimpy’s insistence that the party was dead. He remained with the group afterward.

### Harbin Wester

The Town Master of Phandalin to whom the party delivers prisoners and reports completed quests. Shrimpy was turned over to him after the party returned from the forest.

### Ser Kaelen

A member of the Order of the Gauntlet in Phandalin who is concerned about evil cults. He was relieved to see the party after their several-day absence. He described the Kraken Society as a cult promising unity of mind and purpose and noted suspicions that powerful psionic creatures were involved, though he remained skeptical of Valphine’s automatic attribution to mind flayers.

Ser Kaelen also arranged research through contacts at Candlekeep to identify the original owners of the party’s shipboard magic items. He emphasized the Order’s investment of time and money as evidence that it valued a better relationship with Vukradin, not as a demand for repayment.

### Adabra

A council member in Phandalin who holds extreme views about non-intervention in planar events. She believes the multiverse and planar distortions are part of nature and that resisting incursions causes the responsible forces to push harder. In her view, attempts to stop Tiamat began a cascade of later catastrophes.

Adabra became furiously enraged when learning of the party’s actions against the Kraken cult, identifying the incident as a temporal violation and planar distortion. She used the involvement of mind-controlled Emerald Enclave members as evidence against interventionist philosophy and stormed out of the council meeting in righteous fury.

### Jenna Roscoe

A member of the Lords’ Alliance who initially dismisses the Kraken Society as a harmless “posh cult” for the wealthy. She describes its ceremonies as involving mind-melding, debauchery, drinking, and the communal experience of sensation.

Jenna believes—or claims—that the cult poses no threat to the Lords’ Alliance. She rejects the mind-flayer theory because illithid control should not operate over the reported distances and because the kraken imagery makes the connection too obvious. She knows more than she first reveals and eventually identifies members of Neverwinter high society associated with the organization.

### Brin

One of two individuals working on reconstructing the church in Phandalin. Brin has found new purpose and religious ecstasy in Valphine’s teachings and embraces the church’s new sacrament of enduring Lathander’s light and being restored afterward.

### Giles Slipper-Shine

One of two individuals working on reconstructing the church in Phandalin. Like Brin, Giles is ecstatic about the church’s progress, Valphine’s sermon, and the congregation’s increasingly unconventional practices.

### Teega the Smith

Teega is the blacksmith of the Wayside Inn. Recently freed from Kraken Society control, she observes the church’s activities and is visibly disturbed by the new sacrament of intentional blinding and healing, as well as the severed cultist head displayed outside.

Valphine’s sermon nevertheless gives Teega pause. The Kraken Society offered her peaceful bliss, while Valphine presents pain as something that can be endured and redeemed. Teega remains conflicted rather than converted and returns to her home.

### Don-Jon Raskin

The verbose and oblivious new overseer of the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine, hired by a consortium in Neverwinter. He has worked as a prospector, miner, fur trader, privateer, and whaler and possesses an apparently inexhaustible supply of self-aggrandizing stories.

Don-Jon believes an Uthgardt barbarian tribe once accepted him as a brother, although Brewbarry recognizes that they regarded him as a fool. He is proud of his ethics regarding ivory, considers himself capable of traveling without protection, and agreed to an escort only because his employers threatened his profit margin.

Despite being the mine’s overseer, he deliberately sought no preparatory information about it. He is sociable, easily swayed, and fascinated by Valphine’s painful devotion to Lathander. He eventually accepts that the Whiskered Gang can serve as both residents and workers if supplied with cheese.

### Zeleen Varnaster

The female leader of the Whiskered Gang, a group of wererats occupying the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine after being displaced from the Shrine of Savras. She is pragmatic, self-important, protective of the gang as a whole, and willing to volunteer two subordinates for risks she considers herself too valuable to face.

Zeleen objects strongly to stereotypes connecting wererats with cheese, though she becomes interested once actual cheese is offered. She claims the gang will return to the shrine if the party clears it, but the party determines that she likes the mine and does not truly intend to surrender it.

### Whiskered Gang

A group of approximately seven wererats led by Zeleen Varnaster who have established a warren in the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine. They are resilient creatures who prefer darkness and were displaced from their previous home by orcs and ogres.

Some members guard the mine while others hunt for food. The gang initially proposes that the party reclaim the Shrine of Savras for them but ultimately accepts a provisional arrangement to inhabit and work the mine in exchange for regular cheese deliveries.

### Talking Boars

Wild boars encountered near the Woodland Manse who were upset that the Anchorites were masquerading as boars without following proper boar culture, pumpkin-patch patrol, or etiquette. One condemned the fleeing party for arriving with promises and failing to deliver. The party countered that being struck by lightning violated their etiquette as well.

## Locations

### Woodland Manse

A crumbling manor occupied by the Cult of Talos, including half-orc Anchorites disguised as boars who wield powerful lightning magic. The party was ambushed here and forced to retreat after sustaining heavy injuries. Multiple enemies remained outside and within the house, leaving the site unresolved for a future expedition.

### Triboar Trail

A road leading from the forest toward Phandalin. The party traveled nearly thirty miles before camping along the route for a much-needed long rest rather than forcing Brewbarry to push on to the point of exhaustion.

### Phandalin

A small trading post where the party delivers prisoners, reports to the town council, receives new quests, and where Valphine’s church is being reconstructed. It serves as the party’s operational hub. Don-Jon Raskin waited here for escorts to the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine.

### Church in Phandalin

A religious center dedicated to Lathander undergoing reconstruction. The building has been cleaned out and construction is progressing under Brin and Giles.

Valphine delivered a powerful sermon here that created an unusual sacrament involving intentional blindness through exposure to sunlight followed by magical healing. A Kraken Society cultist’s severed head is displayed on a pike outside.

### Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine

A gold mine located at the foot of a soaring, snow-capped mountain in the Sword Mountains. Its tunnel entrance is hidden among bushes and marked by a wooden plank bearing the mine’s name in Common.

The Whiskered Gang has established a comfortable warren within. Don-Jon initially regards residence and mining as incompatible but agrees to let the wererats nest in one section while working another in exchange for cheese.

### Shrine of Savras

A ruin in a vast field north of the rocky foothills of the Sword Mountains, formerly home to the Whiskered Gang. It is now occupied by approximately sixteen orcs and several ogres displaced by the white dragon, including members of an ogre tribe previously antagonized by the party.

Zeleen claims she would return there if the party cleared it, but her sincerity is doubtful. The party leaves the shrine’s occupiers undisturbed.

### Neverwinter

A distant city where the consortium that hired Don-Jon Raskin is based. Members of its high society are associated with the Kraken Society, and at least one name connected to Don-Jon’s employers overlaps with Jenna Roscoe’s list of known associates.

### Butterskull Ranch

The former home of Petunia, the special cow whose milk is meant to supply cheese for the party’s arrangement with the wererats. Don-Jon has heard that Petunia’s milk produces spectacular cheese. The party still needs to negotiate with the ranch’s proprietor and arrange regular shipments.

## Items

### Magic Items from Ships

Magical artifacts previously found by the party aboard ships. Through a chain of contacts culminating in specialized research at Candlekeep, Ser Kaelen has identified their original owners. Returning them forms a new quest expected to provide monetary rewards, though Ser Kaelen warns that the process will not be simple.

### Kraken Cultist’s Head

The severed head of a member of the Kraken Society. The party retained it after surrendering the rest of the cultist’s body. Valphine places it on a pike outside her church in Phandalin as a warning and public display of the church’s triumph over the cult.

## Spells

### Channel Divinity

Used by Valphine through Balm of Peace as a powerful healing ability that restores 16 hit points to each party member after the earlier lightning attack at the Woodland Manse. Valphine still has movement available after invoking it, but the recovered health is soon threatened by another Anchorite’s lightning.

### Lightning Bolt

A devastating spell wielded by the Anchorites of Talos, capable of striking multiple targets in a line. One bolt catches Soma and Vukradin; both make their Dexterity saves and take reduced damage. Previous bolts had badly injured Brewbarry and the front line. The prospect of additional Anchorites still able to cast it is the decisive factor in the party’s retreat.

### Lesser Restoration

A spell Valphine can use to cure blindness. Its availability turns the congregation’s dangerous misreading of her sermon into an unintended sacrament: worshippers deliberately stare into the sun, experience the pain of Lathander’s light, and are then restored as proof of their devotion.
