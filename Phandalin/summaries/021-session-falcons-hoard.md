# Chapter 21: Falcon’s Hoard

Date: 2025-10-24

## Summary

While his companions slept, Vukradin slipped out of the guest house under the cover of night and made his way into the main lodge. Soma slept in her shell, Brewbarry snored, and Valphine remained bemused by Vukradin’s plans. Although Brewbarry would have followed him, Vukradin decided that his decidedly unsubtle companion needed a full night’s rest—and all his rages—for the next day. Vukradin wanted identifying information and provenance that could help trace the treasure’s rightful owners, not to take anything. As he insisted when Falcon’s hidden possessions were later discussed, “I didn’t steal anything!”

Vukradin moved through the empty dining room—a small second hall and trophy gallery opening above an oak dining table surrounded by tall-backed chairs, with two iron chandeliers hanging from the high rafters—and crept up to the upper floor. He followed the hallway, glanced through its windows, and arrived at the door to Falcon the Hunter’s private quarters. The bedroom was cozy and warm but untidy, the unmistakable bachelor pad of someone who had lived alone for many years. It contained a chest, shelving, and a walk-in closet.

Wrapping the room in a sphere of absolute silence, Vukradin stepped inside and began his search. The spell’s 20-foot radius covered Falcon’s room, Sridar’s adjacent quarters, and even part of the hallway. On a curio beside the bedstand, Vukradin found a pair of wooden elven ears strung on a golden chain, later noticing words along the chain resembling “my dearly beloved.” Beside them was a delicate wooden square carved with a labyrinth maze. A strong recollection of old stories allowed him to associate the design with legends about the undercity of Waterdeep, though he did not establish that it mapped the entire undercity.

A locked drawer proved more tempting. Lacking thieves’ tools—and objecting once again that he was “Not a thief”—Vukradin used his dagger to pry it open within the magical silence. Inside he discovered a small iron mechanical box crafted in the remarkably realistic shape of a blue dragon’s head. After winding its key, he watched its jaw open and reveal a brass ring inscribed with the word “lightning” in Elvish and Sylvan script. The dragon head was so anatomically precise that it looked less like an ordinary toy and more like a severed head somehow shrunk into a vindictive trophy. Examination using his knowledge of arcana established only that it was not literally the dragon itself; neither the identity of the modeled dragon nor any connection between the box and Iymrith was confirmed.

Vukradin returned everything as best he could, declined to take the ring, and left no payment for the damaged drawer because, as he explained, “I don’t really like him.” He retreated to the guest house, pulled out quill and paper, and wrote what he jokingly called “a poem about the wonders of the dragon hoard,” accompanied by notes, pictures, and sketches of everything he could remember. His first drawings were imperfect but recognizable enough that someone familiar with the objects might identify them, albeit with difficulty.

The discoveries suggested several possible connections. Vukradin suspected that the ring came from a dragon’s hoard and entertained the theory that whoever owned the elven ears had been harmed by the dragon, prompting Falcon to miniaturize its head and imprison a piece of its treasure in its mouth as a gesture of revenge. The lightning motif also recalled the half-orc shamans associated with Talos, the lighthouse dedicated to Talos, and Falcon’s request that the party remove half-orcs from the Woodland Manse. This left Vukradin wondering whether Falcon merely opposed Talos or carried a far more personal grudge against the god’s servants.

The lightning motif also prompted Vukradin to recall Orsik and a blue dragon. When he described Orsik as having killed it, the GM corrected the account: Orsik had forced a blue dragon into an infernal pact. The discussion did not establish that this explained Iymrith’s fate.

Come morning, Falcon discovered both the broken drawer and the obvious signs that its contents had been moved. The fact that someone had opened the hiding place without taking its treasure only deepened his confusion. At breakfast, he looked over Vukradin, Brewbarry, Valphine, and Soma and announced, “The cult of Talos. Came into my house. to steal… To steal the artifact. Yet they did not steal it.” Falcon was convinced that Talos’s clerics had broken into his room during the night and failed to steal the ring only because they could not figure out the mechanism of the dragon-head box.

According to Falcon, the ring had come from the hoard of Iymrith, a fearsome blue dragon known as the Doom of the Desert. He said Talos’s clerics regarded the dragon as one of their god’s favorites and wanted a piece of her gold as a sacrifice or offering. Vukradin listened with a perfectly composed expression as Falcon’s accusation left him unsuspected. He feigned wide-eyed wonder—“Wow. Can… can we see it?”—and claimed he had never seen an artifact from a dragon’s hoard before. His deception was flawless, and Falcon believed him completely.

Falcon passed the ring around the table, permitting Vukradin to examine it openly in daylight. Vukradin noted the imperfections that had escaped him during the nighttime search and improved his earlier sketch into a faithful reproduction. Then, with the ring back in his hand, Falcon grew quiet and gave his account. He had once been an adventurer whose party faced Iymrith, and he had been the only one to escape. Later, after hearing reports that she had died, he returned to her hoard as a survivor seeking closure. Her body was not found, most of the hoard had already been plundered, and dragon bodies and other corpses littered the surrounding floors and fields. Falcon recovered the ring and enough overlooked coins to purchase remote land and begin building his lodge.

Pressed about whether that hoard had funded the entire establishment, Falcon described the money as only the modest beginning, and Vukradin called it “seed capital.” Falcon claimed to have spent twenty years building the business from that start—an exaggeration, not a reliable duration. The party is not established to have recognized that exaggeration. At Falcon’s first request for secrecy, Vukradin reassured him, “Oh, your secret’s safe with me,” and his attempt to mend their relationship after the previous day succeeded. Falcon came to appreciate Vukradin’s integrity and willingness to stand by his principles, even where the two still disagreed.

He admitted, “I was a bit of a scavenger,” and confessed that he had embellished his history because the truth made poor advertising. “Falcon the Hunter who stole what was left over from the Dragon Hoard” did not have the appeal of Falcon the Hunter, killer of dragons. His clientele paid for the fantasy of dining with a celebrated dragon slayer, hearing his hunting stories, and feeling safe in his presence.

Falcon acknowledged that he lacked Vukradin’s “silvery tongue,” but understood that guests who had paid to hear his legend wanted to believe it. Soma saw no reason to expose him, while Valphine was impressed—and disappointed that she had not immediately recognized the obvious implausibility of a lone ranger possessing an intact dragon hoard. The party agreed to keep Falcon’s secret.

Vukradin left the encounter with a head full of threads: a lightning ring Falcon said came from a blue dragon’s hoard, a labyrinth tied to Waterdeep, a pair of ears belonging to someone dearly beloved, and a growing suspicion that Falcon’s grudge against the Cult of Talos ran far deeper and far more personally than he had let on.
