# Chapter 34: Brewbarry’s Bloody Axe and the Beer Blight

Date: 2026-02-18

## Summary

When Vukradin questioned the oxymoronic name “Stonetallow,” Horia launched into an etymological explanation. She revealed that the name came from “stone to allow,” referring to allowing an arch to pass. Over time, the phrase collapsed into the clan name Stonetallow, reflecting its founder’s architectural expertise and the clan’s fixation on doing things properly. Horia happily continued into a broader discussion of dwarven surnames while Vukradin admitted that the explanation made him feel better.

The party then approached the wererats to present their proposal. Vukradin had gained insight that the wererats never truly wanted to mine and preferred acting as muscle rather than engaging in hard labor. They met with Zeleen Varnaster, the wererat leader, who immediately asked whether they had killed the dwarves. Instead, the party explained that the dwarves would do all the menial labor and mining while the wererats acted as muscle, treated the mine as their territory, and scared away intruders. They also emphasized that the wererats could remain in the caves and continue receiving Petunia’s excellent cheese.

Zeleen initially seemed delighted by a deal under which the wererats only had to prevent anyone else from entering. After Vukradin successfully closed the deal, however, Zeleen admitted the wererats had planned to skim ten percent from the mine and proposed an alternative upfront payment of one thousand gold pieces for the first six months. Brewbarry countered by reminding Zeleen that the wererats had been driven from their previous home, had not been expelled from the mine, and were now being offered shelter, cheese, and an opportunity to do nothing but look intimidating. With his weapon still covered in orc and ogre blood, Brewbarry handled it conspicuously while asking why the wererats would demand money on top of such a favorable arrangement.

Impressed by Brewbarry’s argument and its implied threat, Zeleen reframed the arrangement as the wererats protecting their own home and family. She renounced both the thousand-gold payment and the planned ten-percent theft, promising that nothing untoward would happen at the mine. Zeleen then offered Vukradin a piece of Petunia’s cheese. It proved as delicious as advertised, with a distinctive texture that Vukradin compared to Gouda, “but better.”

During the conversation, Zeleen warned that orcs, ogres, Talosian clerics, and a dragon were still causing chaos in the area. The wererats could defend the mine but hoped the party might use its “sword thing” to address the larger threat. Zeleen suggested that the Shrine of Savras might hold information about the dragon’s location, explaining that this rumor was why the orcs and ogres had gone after it.

The cave’s narrow entrances made Zeleen feel safe from the dragon. Vukradin attempted to frighten her by claiming dragons could polymorph into smaller creatures and enter caves, but Zeleen saw through the deception completely. She treated it as a joke and cheerfully told the other wererats that Vukradin was a kidder and the best boss they had ever had.

Brewbarry then introduced himself on this occasion as “Brewbarry Root Smasher Ogavacano,” a spoken variant of the established reference form Ogalakadu, explaining that, like every Goliath, he had a family name, nickname, and clan name. When Zeleen struggled to pronounce it and tried “Big Guy” instead, Brewbarry questioned whether that referred to his weight or height and finally instructed her to call him “Your Highness.” More alarmingly, Zeleen warned that the continuing raids were disrupting agriculture so severely that there might be no brewing of beer that year. Brewbarry was literally struck speechless when his audio cut out at that exact moment. Once restored, he declared that they had to do something and suggested making “dragon barbecue.”

The party reviewed their outstanding quests and debated their next destination. Their open concerns included helping Lyra and the Emerald Enclave, visiting Elara “Seasong” Meliamne in Neverwinter, returning the mermaid statue from the lighthouse before House Margaster attacked, crediting the Order of the Gauntlet, dealing with the still-at-large Carver, waiting for Sister Kaella to make contact, finding the dragon, and recovering the Dragonblade. The GM warned that the world would continue moving and that NPCs would not remain in eternal pause while quests went unattended.

Vukradin favored Neverwinter, where he also had personal business, while Brewbarry considered the dragon the only challenge worthy of them. Because Dragon Barrow lay along the route to Neverwinter, the party decided to stop there first and obtain the dragon slayer sword. They took a long rest in the mine, now protected by the wererats and worked by the dwarves. Horia and the wererats shook hands and exchanged contracts, although the recording did not state whether the wererats apologized for the slain dwarves or what final percentage replaced the dwarves’ earlier twenty-percent share. The dwarves rolled their eyes at the wererats’ recent modifications while the wererats regarded the dwarves as suckers, leaving everyone satisfied—especially Petunia.

Vukradin asked how much gold he should expect from the mine each week. He was reminded that the mine belonged to a committee in Neverwinter and that overhead and other details still needed to be calculated. Vukradin nevertheless proposed one hundred gold pieces per week, while the GM noted that the eventual return might be greater with further investment and a proper business plan.

While traveling cross-country toward Dragon Barrow, the party crossed the Triboar Trail and encountered The Falcon. He was distraught because the party had never cleared the Woodland Manse, where fireballs and lightning had previously driven them away. In the meantime, orcs and ogres had seized Falcon’s Hunting Lodge and were attempting to muscle everyone out of Neverwinter Wood. The Falcon was heading to Phandalin to settle debts and plan his next move, declaring that the lodge now carried too much “bad juju” for him to return without help.

When Vukradin asked whether clearing the lodge would make it free for the taking, The Falcon clarified that he still owned it. He instead offered to provide a particular kind of wood useful for Vukradin’s music studio if the party solved his problem. He also proposed a business arrangement in which the famous Vukradin performed at the lodge once a week, perhaps alongside Valphine. Drawing on the popularity of the musical story about the Aasimar and the drow cleric of Lathander, The Falcon believed the performances could attract visitors and justify higher rents. Ale would, naturally, be served. Brewbarry insisted that everything tasted better with ale, while Vukradin observed that music sounded better if the audience drank before hearing it.

The party continued to Dragon Barrow, joking about Brewbarry’s drinking “talent.” Near the burial mound, a centaur named Xanth approached peacefully. Vukradin, who had never seen a centaur, panicked at the apparent fusion of man and horse and prepared to protect the party. Soma reassured him that Xanth was simply a woodland creature and advised him to ignore the horse half. Still shaken by earlier attempts at peaceful contact that ended in his brutal defeat, Vukradin sat on the ground and rocked while Brewbarry assured him that there was nothing to fear.

Xanth warned the party about strange witch-lights seen over Dragon Barrow at night, saying the hill was haunted by the restless spirits of the dead. Soma distinguished these lights from the strange phenomenon previously witnessed near the illegal logging operation run by Harbin Wester’s brother. Drawing on lessons from the druid Meril, Soma concluded that Xanth’s description likely referred to will-o’-wisps: evil, intelligent lights associated with natural places.

Xanth recognized Valphine as the famous cleric of Lathander whose doctrine of painful, purifying light had begun to spread. Soma offered him the sacrament immediately, but Xanth hastily clarified that he was not yet a believer. Valphine then assured him that he could become one. Xanth warned that Neverwinter Wood was overrun by orcs allied with half-orc spellcasters. These Anchorites of Talos performed dark rites at a circle of standing stones atop a cave-ridden hill, and Xanth offered to guide the party there after they finished at Dragon Barrow. Brewbarry initially asked why such enemies deserved his attention, but then connected the orcs to displaced farmers and the consequent threat to beer production. The Anchorites immediately became the party’s second priority after recovering the sword.

The party arrived at Dragon Barrow itself, a thirty-foot-high hill with a top too flat to be natural and a row of ten-foot-tall, bone-white rocks arcing toward the stormy sky like outstretched talons. Brewbarry deduced that the place was called Dragon Barrow because a dragon had been buried there, proudly announcing that he had put “two and two together.”

Valphine’s keen perception revealed an entrance at the base of the hill: a rock acting as a stone plug embedded in the earth. She was disappointed because the entrance was so obvious that calling it secret seemed overly generous. The party considered using magic to move it, but Brewbarry stepped forward to apply his strength. Soma quietly used Mold Earth while letting Brewbarry believe he had moved the stone unaided, but Brewbarry proved strong enough that he did not need the help. The scene left him feeling especially powerful, and the GM awarded him inspiration after describing the confidence boost. He also explained the origin of his nickname: he had once uprooted a tree and used it as a club.

Inside the tomb, the party saw yellow lights coming from several directions. They were eager to obtain the sword before nightfall, although Brewbarry argued that night was when the fun would begin and that great songs required great deeds. Soma summoned a rat familiar and used their telepathic connection to scout the western passage through its senses. The rat found four sarcophagi. The northwest sarcophagus held a lute, the southeast was sealed too tightly for the rat to enter, and the remaining two contained the moldy bones of adventurers.

As the rat investigated, one of the mysterious lights went out and then reappeared when the rat moved past. Soma made the rat move back and forth to test the effect, causing the light to blink on and off like a macabre dance. The experiment provoked the light, which struck the familiar for nine points of lightning damage and killed it instantly. The party decided that passages containing the lights were best avoided, despite Brewbarry dismissing nine points of damage as “just a scratch.”

The party proceeded east and found two sealed sarcophagi in alcoves beside a two-foot-wide passage. Brewbarry prepared to open one immediately, but his friends persuaded him to permit a trap inspection first. Valphine found no evidence of a mechanical trap on either the exterior or base. When Brewbarry opened the sarcophagus, however, it released a cloud of corrosive dust filling the surrounding ten-foot area. The dust was an environmental hazard rather than a mechanical trap. Brewbarry suffered fourteen points of acid damage but invoked his Stone’s Endurance, reducing the harm by eleven and taking only a minor burn. The sarcophagus proved completely empty.

Valphine attempted to recall relevant drow knowledge about tombs but became distracted by memories of people being buried alive. Brewbarry approached the second sarcophagus more cautiously, using his halberd to open it from ten feet away. Another cloud of corrosive dust emerged but settled harmlessly without reaching him. The second sarcophagus was also empty. The session ended with two empty hazardous sarcophagi explored, Soma’s familiar destroyed, the intelligent will-o’-wisps still lurking elsewhere in the barrow, and the Dragonblade yet to be found.

## NPCs

### Horia Stonetallow

A proud shield dwarf from the Stonetallow clan, an ancient lineage that traces its ancestry to a disgraced engineer from Gauntlgrym who was supposedly the only one who knew how to build a proper archway. Horia believes that only dwarves understand mining and that only the Stonetallows truly understand it among dwarves.

She values exact extraction rates, proper arches, and carefully constructed supports that future dwarven generations will admire. Guarding the mine distracts from this higher calling, so she quickly recognized the value in employing the wererats as muscle.

When Vukradin questioned her clan’s name, Horia asked whether he had a problem with the proud Stonetallow lineage. She then enthusiastically explained that “Stonetallow” was a contraction of “stone to allow,” referring to allowing an arch to pass, and continued into a wider lecture on dwarven surname etymology. She helped formalize the mine agreement with the wererats, although she and her fellow dwarves openly disapproved of the wererats’ modifications.

Horia discusses the mine’s potential earnings but notes that overhead, ownership, investment, and operating details must be resolved before a reliable weekly figure can be given.

### Don-Jon

A character who was killed by dwarves and subsequently eaten by a carrion crawler. The party’s prior investigation established that the crawler consumed his remains but was not responsible for his original death.

### Zeleen Varnaster

The leader of the wererats, who gives off a gangster-like air and prefers acting as muscle to performing hard labor. She initially hoped the party had killed the Stonetallow dwarves, but quickly became enthusiastic about making them handle the mining.

Zeleen was especially attracted to a deal in which her people could remain in the caves, eat Petunia’s cheese, regard the mine as their territory, and do little beyond preventing unwanted visitors from entering. After Vukradin closed the basic arrangement, she admitted that the wererats had planned to skim ten percent from the mine and demanded an upfront payment of 1,000 gold pieces for six months.

Brewbarry’s forceful counterargument—and his conspicuously bloodied weapon—persuaded Zeleen to abandon both demands. She reframed the work as protecting the wererats’ own home and family, promising that the planned ten-percent theft would not happen because they liked Vukradin.

Zeleen offered Vukradin a piece of Petunia’s cheese as a gesture of goodwill. She warned the party about orcs, ogres, Talosian clerics, and the dragon, and shared the rumor that the Shrine of Savras could reveal the dragon’s location. She also explained that the ongoing raids threatened farming and could prevent beer from being brewed that year.

Zeleen dismissed the concern about dragon breath entering the cave, believing the wererats could retreat deeper into the mine. She also completely saw through Vukradin’s false claim that the dragon could polymorph into a smaller creature, treated the attempt as a joke, and declared Vukradin the best boss the wererats had ever had.

Zeleen struggled to pronounce Brewbarry’s full Goliath name, tried calling him “Big Guy,” and ultimately addressed him as “Your Highness.” She later shook hands and exchanged contracts with Horia to formalize the mine arrangement.

### The Falcon

Owner of Falcon’s Hunting Lodge, encountered on the Triboar Trail. He is distraught because his lodge has been taken over by Talosian forces, including orcs and ogres allied with the half-orc Anchorites of Talos, and he is unwilling to return to it.

The Falcon blames the worsening situation in part on the party’s failure to clear the Woodland Manse, where fireballs and lightning previously forced them to retreat. He says the occupying forces are trying to muscle everyone out of Neverwinter Wood.

He previously bragged about being able to handle any danger but was revealed to have stolen valor by arriving after someone else fought a dragon and taking some of the treasure. Brewbarry directly criticizes his habit of finding others to solve his problems.

The Falcon is returning to Phandalin to settle debts and plan his next move. If the party reclaims his lodge, he offers Vukradin a particular kind of wood useful for his music studio. He also proposes a commercial partnership featuring regular musical performances by Vukradin and Valphine, using their growing fame to draw visitors, raise rents, and establish the lodge as a destination for music and ale.

### Xanth

A centaur encountered near Dragon Barrow who approaches the party peacefully. His unfamiliar appearance terrifies Vukradin, whom Soma must reassure that Xanth is merely a woodland creature.

Xanth warns the party about strange witch-lights haunting Dragon Barrow at night and describes them as restless spirits of the dead. His account allows Soma to identify the creatures as possible will-o’-wisps. Xanth distinguishes these lights from the stranger phenomenon previously reported near Harbin Wester’s brother’s illegal logging operation.

He recognizes Valphine as the famous cleric of Lathander and praises the “painful and glorious light,” along with her doctrine that pain can be purifying. Despite his enthusiasm, he declines an immediate sacrament and clarifies that he is not currently a believer.

Xanth also informs the party about half-orc spellcasters—the Anchorites of Talos—who perform dark rites at a circle of standing stones atop a cave-ridden hill in Neverwinter Wood. Their allied orcs are driving farmers from their land and disrupting the beer supply. Xanth offers to guide the party to their ritual site after the expedition into Dragon Barrow.

### Elara Seasong Meliamne

A character in Neverwinter whom the party plans to visit, potentially to unlock or clarify some of the magical loot it has collected. Vukradin strongly favors the trip to see her and also has other personal business in the city.

### Ser Kaelen

A character who has information about an unidentified magical object the party found. Separately, Ser Kaelen wants the party to return the mermaid statue recovered near the lighthouse to an art collector in Neverwinter and ensure that the Order of the Gauntlet receives credit.

A forest increasingly controlled by orcs, ogres, and half-orc spellcasters known as the Anchorites of Talos. The Anchorites conduct dark rites at a circle of standing stones atop a cave-ridden hill.

### Dragon Barrow

A thirty-foot-high burial mound with a summit too flat to be natural. Ten-foot-tall, bone-white rocks rise from the grassy hilltop and arc toward the stormy sky like outstretched talons. Brewbarry correctly deduced that it is called Dragon Barrow because a dragon was buried there.

Inside, the barrow contains narrow passages, alcoves, sarcophagi, and shifting yellow lights. A western chamber holds four sarcophagi: one containing a lute, one sealed too tightly for Soma’s rat familiar to enter, and two holding moldy adventurer bones. One of the lights killed Soma’s familiar with a lightning attack.

The eastern chamber contains two empty sarcophagi that release corrosive dust when opened, along with a two-foot-wide passage. The party ended the session here without yet finding the Dragonblade.
