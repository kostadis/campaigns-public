# Chapter 3: Untitled

Date: 2025-05-28

## Summary
The party stood at a crossroads in the ancient dwarven ruins, debating which path to take — a suspected dead end on one side, a freshly discovered secret door on the other — before Vukradin settled it with a shrug (“Sure”) and the group pressed forward into a narrow hallway. At its end, they discovered a blood-stained altar, its stone surface dark with the remnants of old rituals. Before they could examine it further, a massive ochre jelly — a mindless, mustard-colored blob of acidic slime, “a big old pile of mustard” — lurched out of the shadows toward them, and to the party’s disgust promptly out-rolled every single one of them on initiative. Brewbarry swung his halberd at the creature, but the slashing blow only caused it to split apart into two smaller, equally hungry entities.

The battle that followed was chaotic and punishing. The two jellies lashed out with their pseudopods, one of them striking the barbarian Brewbarry with a twelve-point blow that dropped him to a single hit point in the opening moments. Valphine called down radiant light from above, searing one of the creatures for six radiant damage, while Vukradin unleashed a thunderous shockwave that battered both jellies for eight damage apiece and sent them skidding ten feet backward across the stone floor. From the safety of the corridor, the dwarf prospectors shouted their reactions — Dazlyn calling “Is everybody okay there?” while Norbus worried instead about the potential damage being done to the archaeological site around them: “Could you not use such damaging spells? It might damage some of the interior that we’re trying to preserve.”

As if the situation were not dire enough, a third ochre jelly dropped from the ceiling directly into the middle of the fray, prompting more than one party member to seriously consider fleeing — “Guys, this is fleeing time,” Soma announced, adding, “Look, I got a few years left. I’d like to spend them not dead.” Mercifully, the new arrival hit the ground stunned by the deafening noise of the battle, buying the party a round. Soma struck the ground with trembling force — in open defiance of Norbus’s plea about damaging spells — cracking the stone floor and turning the surrounding area into treacherous, uneven terrain that slowed the jellies’ advance. Brewbarry drew on a deep well of fury and delivered a crushing critical blow that obliterated one of the creatures entirely, his fists doing what blades could not — still nursing the single hit point the earlier pseudopod strike had left him.

The fight ground on, with Valphine weaving healing magic to keep her companions standing even as the jellies continued their relentless assault. Vukradin whispered discordant, maddening sounds at one of the creatures, causing it to recoil in psychic agony and flee as far from him as it could manage — after which the bard withdrew to a far corner, declaring, “I can play my music from across the room. You’ll be able to hear it. Trained musician, I play loudly.” Soma, battered but resolute, withdrew into her shell and planted herself as a living barrier at armor class 21 — until the last jelly split into two and a pair of pseudopod strikes, one for ten damage and one more behind it, overwhelmed her defenses and knocked her unconscious, forcing Valphine to rush a healing word to bring her back before her death saves ran out. Then, in a moment that stunned everyone present, Vukradin drew his daggers — having recalled from a childhood memory that piercing weapons do not split oozes the way slashing ones do — and struck twice in rapid succession, killing two separate jellies in a single fluid motion and bringing the chaotic battle to an abrupt end.

In the aftermath, Norbus and Dazlyn crept cautiously into the room, visibly relieved that the oozes had been dealt with — “Are they dead? Is it safe to go in there?” — to which the party invited them to stick their heads in and find out. The party demanded their sending stones, grumbling that the dwarves could have helped; the prospectors protested that with four hit points apiece they would have been useless. The dwarves asked the party to finish clearing the rest of the ruins first, sweetening the offer with Norbus’s promise of an excellent meal, wonderful stories, and a place to rest for the night. Vukradin agreed on the condition that there be no stories — at which point Dazlyn thumped his partner and declared that they had a bard, and the bard would tell the stories. The group agreed, and after a much-needed long rest, they pressed deeper into the complex. To the north, they found a room with three stone bed frames — bare frames with no mattresses, built of stone to last an eternity — and beyond a hidden door, a small chamber containing an empty stone font and a wardrobe holding the rotted remains of two suits of red leather armor, vestments once worn by priests of Abbathor, the dwarven god of greed, now ruined and worthless.

Deeper still, in a section of the ruins where collapsed tunnels had buried entire passageways — rubble piles the party recognized as the beginnings and ends of tunnels that could be dug through only with time-consuming labor — the party came upon a grim discovery. Half-buried in the rubble was the skeleton of a dwarf priest, killed when part of the room collapsed, still clad in the same rotted red leather armor, a holy symbol hanging around his neck — a tiny jeweled dagger on a silver chain, inscribed in dwarvish with the words “greed is good,” worth fifty gold pieces. Valphine palmed the symbol while Soma and Brewbarry looked on — Vukradin, out of sight-line, none the wiser — before she announced the body itself: “Hey, there’s a body in here. Did you see this?”

True to his nature, Vukradin sprinted back through the ruins to inform Norbus and Dazlyn about the body — “Oh, a body. Let me go tell the dwarves” — while three rounds elapsed. When he returned, the party moved on to investigate the area behind the blood-stained altar, where a hidden passage led to a cramped channel. No sooner had Valphine stepped inside — eagerly volunteering despite having nine hit points — than another ochre jelly, a massive one, slithered out of the darkness to meet her.

The second major battle was swift but brutal. Soma hurled a shard of ice that struck the jelly and exploded into freezing fragments, while Valphine called down radiant light that the creature narrowly avoided. Brewbarry waded into the tight space with his fists — rolling his second natural one of the night — and Vukradin followed with his daggers, the two of them hammering the creature from close range. The jelly retaliated with a devastating pseudopod strike against Brewbarry, seventeen points of acid landing with nearly maximum force, though Brewbarry gritted his teeth and absorbed nine points of the impact through sheer toughness — his rage useless against acid. Soma launched another volley of ice for eleven more damage, emptying her last spell slot, and Valphine struck with her mace, and when the creature finally split into two smaller oozes under the sustained assault, the party redoubled their efforts and cut both halves down before they could cause further harm — Brewbarry pulverizing one with a critical punch (“He’s dead, Jim”), Soma poison-spraying the other for a single triumphant point (“Hey, that’s 20% of its hit points, guys”), and Vukradin’s daggers finishing it.

Beyond the altar channel, the party discovered what the dwarves had been hoping to find all along — the Hall of Greed. Soma used magic to shift the loose earth and rubble aside, revealing a sacred site once devoted to Abbathor, while Valphine’s natural-20 perception took in the whole chamber. In a northern alcove stood an intact statue of a horned dwarf, its stone face twisted into an expression of naked avarice, its hands cradling a glowing green gem. Valphine sensed magic radiating from the gem — the GM had already named it an emerald out of character — but when the party examined it more closely, Valphine’s investigation came up a natural one, leaving them uncertain whether it was a priceless emerald or an elaborate piece of glass (“Or tanzanite,” Soma offered; “Or, you know, peridot,” added Vukradin). Valphine pronounced it “a worthless glass gem,” and the party left it in place — their belief, not the fact; the gem itself is an emerald. A second alcove to the south held only rubble — the shattered remains of what had once been a matching statue, its gem long since taken or destroyed. Vukradin insisted on digging through the rubble for artifacts himself, tiring himself out to find nothing at all.

The party returned to Norbus and Dazlyn and reported their findings. The two dwarves erupted with excitement at the mention of the Hall of Greed — “You found the Hall of Greed?” — launching into an enthusiastic explanation of its historical significance, though it quickly became apparent to Vukradin that they were making most of it up as they went, spewing words and hoping no one would notice. When the offended bard pressed them — “Well, why can’t they just be honest with me?” — they sheepishly admitted they were amateur archaeologists who had only recently taken up the label; by the party’s estimate, days ago at most. Months of prospecting the ruins for gold had turned up hints the site was something more, and only then did they start calling themselves archaeologists. Despite the revelation, the party counted the job done — “You weren’t killed by the dragon... we warned you, and we cleared out the ochre jellies, so I think job well done, gentlemen” — and the dwarves held up their end of the bargain and handed over the sending stones, the party’s hard-earned reward for clearing the ruins.

Earlier in the expedition, the party had also stumbled upon a secret cavity hidden within one of the stone pillars near the altar. When the door swung open, a cascade of dwarf skulls tumbled out onto the floor, and at the bottom of the cavity sat an unlocked stone coffer containing fifteen small gemstones worth ten gold pieces each. Vukradin immediately announced the find to the entire group and insisted on informing the archaeologists — “It’s a good thing I’m such a moral individual” — to the visible frustration of his companions (“Goddamn your moral streak,” muttered Soma, who went so far as to set the coffer down on the altar). Valphine quietly suggested they had found only ten. Vukradin instead framed the gems as cursed archaeological artifacts to be handled with the utmost care, taken to a museum, and brought to a wizard to be uncursed — leaving the dwarves so bewildered they whispered to each other, wondered aloud whether it was some drow trap, and nervously asked the rest of the party, “Is he serious?” while Valphine pointedly pulled out her dagger and sharpened it and Brewbarry sat pushing a rock with a stick. In the end, after a tense and morally charged negotiation in which Norbus ventured that “the museum only needs half of it,” the party agreed to split the gems with Norbus and Dazlyn — the GM’s “actually make it eight” setting the party’s share at eight, the dwarves at seven — a compromise that satisfied no one entirely but allowed everyone to walk away with their conscience more or less intact.

With the ruins cleared and their reward in hand, the party set off down the road toward town — Soma framing it as heading back to collect their reward for delivering the dragon warning, though per the Ch. 2 archive that reward may already have been paid; the ordering between the two chapters is an open question (see GitHub #183). The hills around them were quiet, the sky wide and open, and for a brief moment it seemed as though the worst was behind them — “Message delivered. Wasn’t a dragon.” Then four orcs crested the hill ahead, weapons drawn and eyes fixed on the party with unmistakable intent; nobody in the party was caught by surprise. Vukradin wanted to know why they were being attacked and asked to talk; Valphine stepped forward, her fluency in Orcish explained with chilling drow economy — “I mean, we had servants” (the line is labeled Soma on the transcript though it answers for Valphine, and the immediate reaction to it is also labeled Soma, which can’t both be right; this session’s ASR-based diarization is known-unreliable, so the attribution stands unconfirmed) — as the rest of the group readied themselves for yet another fight, their spell pouches nearly empty, their bodies still aching from the battles below.

That’s nice.”
- The DM wonders aloud whether Valphine’s dice luck reflects “moral guilt about the stealing of the treasure” — the answer: “Moral guilt? No. In a drow? Nah.”
- A final sweep of the chamber (18 perception) turns up nothing else of value; for once, Vukradin declines to run back and report (“no, it’s okay... He can find out later”) and the party presses on past the channel — framing the fight as duty: “We’re doing our job in clearing out these ogres [transcript garble for “oozes”] for the dwarves so we can earn our sending stones.”

### The Hall of Greed
#### The party uncovers a sacred site dedicated to the dwarven god of greed and finds a mysterious statue with a glowing gem.
- Vukradin canvasses the party for anyone with Mold Earth — “It’s a very valuable spell, mold earth” — and Soma has it as a cantrip: “Let’s go.”
- Soma uses magic to clear away loose earth and rubble, revealing a sacred religious site — with Valphine’s natural-20 perception taking in the room. Soma needles Gary: “You save those for the out of combat rolls, I see.”
- The GM reveals the area was once important to the clerics of Abbathor. Soma deadpans “Who’s Abbathor?” before admitting the joke.
- In a northern alcove, they find a statue of a dwarf with horns staring greedily at a glowing green gem in its hands — an image Vukradin swears he has seen before: “I think I’ve seen that picture in the Dungeon Master’s Guide... A demon statue holding the glowing gem.”
- Valphine detects magic emanating from the gem (the GM’s own phrasing: “some magic on the gem” — “faint” is summarizer wording, not confirmed aura strength).
- The party investigates the gem, debating whether it is a valuable emerald or a worthless piece of glass — Valphine’s investigation comes up a natural one, leaving “this is an emerald, but it could also just be glass,” with Soma suggesting tanzanite and Vukradin peridot. Valphine’s in-character verdict to the party: “a worthless glass gem, I think it is,” and it is left in place — though the GM had already named it an emerald out of character before the roll. The party’s belief that it’s worthless glass is the character-knowledge state going forward; the gem itself is an emerald. Vukradin: “I’m sure it has great historical significance despite its worthless value in the present day.”
- A southern pile of rubble is revealed to be the remains of a second, shattered statue, with no gem remaining — Vukradin insists on digging through it for artifacts himself (“Let him tire himself out,” Soma advises) and finds nothing.

### Amateur Archaeologists
#### The party returns to their employers to report their findings and claim their reward.
- Before heading back, the party notes that at least “a dragon didn’t find us.” They then inform Dazlyn and Norbus about the Hall of Greed and the green gem — “You found the Hall of Greed?”
- The dwarves launch into an enthusiastic account of the site’s historical significance, but Vukradin’s insight (even at a nine) catches them “spewing words hoping you won’t notice that they have no idea” — prompting an indignant “Are you guys cheating?... Well, why can’t they just be honest with me?”
- Dazlyn and Norbus admit they are merely amateur archaeologists who only recently took up the hobby: “To be quite honest, I don’t know whether it is or is not a matter of great historical significance... we’re amateur archeologists.” A 17 insight pegs the hobby’s age at “several days ago” — “Yeah, moments ago. Hours, even.” Soma: “Do we still see, like, an Archeology for Dummies book under their arm or something?”
- The dwarves express excitement over the discovery of the intact gem and the party’s success in clearing the ruins — “we were looking for the Hall of Greed... This is awesome.”
- Dazlyn and Norbus reward the party with a set of sending stones for their services, as Vukradin closes the account: “We warned you, and we cleared out the ochre jellies, so I think job well done, gentlemen.”

### Ambush on the Road
#### As the party begins their journey back to town, they are intercepted by a group of orcs.
- The party departs the ruins, heading back toward town — Soma frames it as collecting their reward for delivering the message, though per the Ch. 2 archive that reward may already be paid; see the open Ch. 2/Ch. 3 ordering question (GitHub #183). “Did you hear something? I don’t know. Whatever. Message delivered. Wasn’t a dragon.”
- Four orcs appear over a hill, blocking the party’s path and preparing for an assault. Nobody in the party is surprised; separately, they enter the encounter with spell slots nearly spent.
- Vukradin protests — “Why are they attacking us?... I’d like to talk to them” — and the DM assures him the orcs “would love to have that conversation.”
- Valphine prepares to communicate with the attackers in Orcish as the party readies for combat — her fluency explained with chilling drow economy: “I mean, we had servants.” (Soma: “Oh, you just made me feel bad.”) Vukradin speaks Goblin, but not Orc.
- Vukradin proposes he and Valphine hold “our side conversations in Undercommon so the other players can’t understand us” — to which Soma, the self-described “hired tour guide,” objects: “What are you even trying to hide from me?”
- Initiative is rolled and the session ends on the brink of combat, with Soma resigned to a long-term project: getting “what’s-his-face, Vukradin, wealthy enough to pay me for my services... It’s gonna take the entire campaign.”

## Locations
### Blood-Stained Altar Room
A chamber within the ruins featuring a central altar stained with old blood and several hidden secret doors concealed in the masonry — including the pillar cavity packed with dwarf skulls and, behind the altar itself, a concealed door into a narrow channel where a second large jelly laired. The site of a chaotic battle against multiple ochre jellies, during which the stone floor was cracked by Soma’s Earth Tremor and made difficult to traverse — much to the distress of the dwarves trying to preserve the “important archaeological find.” Vukradin hid behind the altar mid-fight and later played his music from the room’s far corner.

### Stone Bedrooms
A room to the north containing three stone bed frames with no mattresses — bare stone, in fine repair, built to “last forever” — and a secret door leading to a small chamber with an empty stone font jutting from the southwest wall and a stone wardrobe holding two suits of rotted red leather vestments of Abbathor. Reached through a hallway with rubble to the west and a normal door to the east.

### Collapsed Tunnels
A series of collapsed passages (nature unspecified — the GM corrected on tape: “No, that they were tunnels that collapsed,” not mining tunnels) — the rubble piles throughout the complex are the beginnings and ends of these passages — requiring physical labor or magic to navigate past the rubble. Digging through is possible but time-consuming, and at least one section was known to risk further collapse if disturbed. One rubble-choked room held the skeleton of a dwarf priest, killed when part of the ceiling came down.

### Hall of Greed
A sacred religious site dedicated to the dwarven god Abbathor — the find that turned Dazlyn and Norbus’s months of gold-prospecting into self-declared archaeology — featuring alcoves with statues of horned dwarves. The intact northern statue “stares greedily at a glowing green gem in its hands” — an emerald per the GM’s own out-of-character ruling, though the party’s failed investigation left them believing it’s worthless glass (tanzanite and peridot were also floated) — radiating detectable magic; a second statue lies shattered in rubble to the south, its gem gone. Soma’s Mold Earth cleared the debris to reveal the space, and the party left the glowing gem in place.

### The Road to Town
The hilly path leading away from the dwarven ruins where the party was ambushed by four orcs at the end of the session — appearing over a hill with the party’s spell slots spent, though nobody was caught by surprise.

## NPCs
### Ochre Jelly
Large, mindless mustard-colored blobs that lash out with pseudopods and split into smaller versions of themselves when struck with slashing weapons — but not, as Vukradin recalled from childhood, when pierced. Armor class 8 (“There’s an AC you don’t see every day”), sluggish initiative, a heavy dexterity penalty on saves, and acid-laden pseudopod strikes that hit for as much as 17 damage. Multiple jellies were encountered throughout the ruins, including one that dropped from the ceiling — falling stunned at the noise of battle — and a full-sized 52-hit-point specimen lurking in the channel behind the altar. “They can smell something tasty” even inside a turtle shell.

### Dazlyn
One of two dwarf prospectors who hired the party to clear the ruins. During the ochre jelly fight he called out from the corridor, “Is everybody okay there?” — a separate concern for the party’s safety, distinct from Norbus’s warning about damage to the site — though he too later pleaded, “Again, to the extent possible, please don’t damage any of the artwork. This is an important archeological find.” He is an amateur archaeologist who took part in the negotiation over splitting the found gemstones with the party — after Norbus ventured that “the museum only needs half of it” — privately confirming with Valphine, “So we’re going to split the gems, right?” He also thumped Norbus into rescinding his offer to tell stories: “Norbus, we have a bard, and we’re going to tell stories. The bard will tell us stories.”

### Norbus
Dazlyn’s partner and fellow amateur dwarf archaeologist. He urged the party to avoid damaging the ruins with powerful spells (“Could you not use such damaging spells? It might damage some of the interior that we’re trying to preserve”), offered a meal and stories in exchange for clearing the rest of the complex (“I can make an excellent meal. I can tell you all sorts of wonderful stories”), admitted under pressure that he and Dazlyn had only recently taken up archaeology as a hobby, and was excited by the discovery of the Hall of Greed. Notably timid — checking “Did somebody get hurt?” and “Is it safe to go in there?” from cover, eyeing the party’s weapons nervously during the gem negotiation (“Is everybody okay with this?... Is he serious?”) — and unable to remember Vukradin’s name despite a full night of the bard’s songs and stories.

### Orcs
A group of four warriors who appeared over a hill and intercepted the party on the road back to town, preparing for an assault as the session ended. Vukradin wanted to know why they were attacking and asked to parley; Valphine, who speaks their tongue, was positioned to translate.

## Items
### Halberd
A polearm used to strike the ochre jelly, but its slashing edge inadvertently caused the creature to divide into two smaller entities rather than destroying it — after which Brewbarry abandoned the weapon entirely in favor of his fists for the rest of the session.

### Vukradin’s Daggers
The bard’s paired blades, wielded main-hand and off-hand after his arcana check recalled that piercing weapons do not split oozes the way slashing ones do. They killed two jellies in a single round in the first battle (“Double death. Go bard go”) and finished the last ooze of the second, accounting for four jelly deaths in total.

### Stone Coffer
An unlocked stone container found hidden beneath a pile of dwarf skulls inside a secret pillar cavity. It held fifteen gemstones worth ten gold pieces each. Soma briefly set it — gems and all — down on the blood-stained altar in protest while Vukradin ratted the find out to the dwarves.

### Gemstones
Fifteen small jewels worth ten gold pieces each, found within the hidden stone coffer. Vukradin announced the full count out loud and branded them “cursed archeological artifacts” bound for a museum and a wizard’s uncursing; Soma tried to report only ten. After a moral debate that left the dwarves half-convinced they were walking into a drow trap, the party agreed to split them with the dwarf archaeologists, with the party receiving eight and the dwarves receiving seven.

### Rotted Red Leather Armor
Ancient, ruined vestments found as two suits in a stone wardrobe and also on the skeleton of a dwarf priest buried in rubble. Identified via a religion check as garments once worn by priests of the dwarven god Abbathor, now worthless due to decay. Vukradin ran the discovery back to the dwarves personally.

### Holy Symbol of Abbathor
A tiny jeweled dagger on a silver chain, found around the neck of a dwarf priest’s skeleton buried in rubble. Inscribed with dwarvish runes that translate to ‘greed is good,’ and worth 50 gold pieces. Valphine successfully pocketed it — a sleight of hand needing only to beat a five, and Gary’s first act of in-game larceny (“This is like a first for me in my role play”) — before blandly announcing the body itself to the party. It is in her possession.

### Glowing Green Gem
A gem held in the hands of a horned dwarf statue within the Hall of Greed. It radiates a detectable magical aura (“faint” is not GM-confirmed), and the GM named it an emerald out of character before Valphine’s investigation roll; her natural one left the party believing it’s worthless glass instead — Soma and Vukradin unhelpfully suggesting tanzanite and peridot along the way. Publicly declared “a worthless glass gem” and left in place, with a promise to tell the dwarves about it on the way out.

### Sending Stones
Magical stones given to the party by Dazlyn and Norbus as payment for clearing the ruins. They allow for long-distance communication. Soma tried to negotiate one stone up front (“Give us one sending stone now and we’ll talk”); the full set changed hands only after the Hall of Greed was found. A separate 50-gold-piece reward for delivering the party’s message was framed by the party as still owed at session’s end, though whether it was actually already paid (per the Ch. 2 archive) is an open ordering question — see GitHub #183.

## Spells
### Sacred Flame
Radiant light called down by Valphine on the ochre jellies from a distance — a DC 13 dexterity save the sluggish jellies usually failed. Cast multiple times during the session, dealing modest radiant damage (six on the first cast), though in one instance only a single point of damage was dealt (“Really, one?” — “Hey, he healed me very well, though. Minimum damage”), and the big jelly behind the altar made its save entirely.

### Thunderwave
A burst of thunderous force unleashed by Vukradin — a 15-foot cube forcing DC 13 constitution saves — that damaged both ochre jellies for eight points each and physically knocked them back ten feet across the stone floor, with one being pushed diagonally into a corner. The casting triggered both a table ruling discussion about how line and cube areas map to grid squares and Norbus’s plea to stop using “such damaging spells.”

### Earth Tremor
Soma struck the ground to cause a localized tremor — DC 13 dexterity, 1d6 damage — that damaged one ochre jelly for five (the other made its save for no damage) and turned the surrounding stone floor into difficult terrain, hindering the creatures’ movement. Cast in pointed defiance of the dwarves’ request to spare the site, with Vukradin’s blessing: “I love it. Do it.” Soma deliberately shifted the epicenter to catch both jellies while sparing the barbarian.

### Healing Word
A quick invocation of healing magic — 2d4+3 — used to restore hit points to fallen or wounded allies, cast as Valphine’s one leveled spell for the round alongside a damage cantrip. Both castings targeted Soma — once after an early pseudopod hit (eight points: “Hey, I’m back to 11. Thank you”), and again to revive her after a later strike knocked her fully unconscious (“Keep bouncing between one and 10 hit points”). Brewbarry, despite absorbing the fight’s other near-fatal pseudopod strike in the opening moments, was never healed and finished the encounter at a single hit point.

### Dissonant Whispers
Vukradin whispered discordant sounds that caused psychic pain to an ochre jelly — 3d6 psychic on a failed DC 13 wisdom save, landing for ten — forcing it to use its reaction to flee as far away as possible “using the safest route,” removing it from the immediate fight and granting Valphine an opportunity attack as it went. The jellies proved immune to frightened but fully susceptible to psychic damage. “Goodbye, you are not gonna attack me next round.”

### Starry Wisp
A mote of radiant light launched by Vukradin — his standby cantrip — that struck an ochre jelly for radiant damage on two occasions, once for four and once for eight, the latter contributing to the damage that eventually caused it to split into two smaller oozes.

### Poison Spray
A puff of toxic gas projected at a nearby ochre jelly by Soma — the jellies, it turned out, were not immune (“Guess we’ll find out”). One cast killed a weakened jelly outright with seven damage; a later cast against the final ooze dealt exactly one point, which Soma defended as “20% of its hit points, guys.”

### Ice Knife
A shard of ice hurled by Soma at the ochre jelly lurking behind the altar. On a hit, the target takes 1d10 piercing damage, and the shard then explodes into cold fragments regardless of whether it hits, forcing a dexterity save against 2d6 additional cold damage to nearby creatures — a two-part mechanic the whole table stopped to re-litigate mid-combat. The first cast landed for nine total; the second for eleven, dropping the jelly to 26 hit points and leaving Soma out of spell slots for the day.

### Mold Earth
A cantrip used by Soma to move loose earth and clear rubble from around the shattered statues in the Hall of Greed, revealing the sacred site dedicated to Abbathor. Vukradin canvassed the party for it by name — “It’s a very valuable spell, mold earth” — and the table noted it could in principle be applied to the complex’s collapsed tunnels as well.

### Detect Magic
Cast by Valphine to inspect the glowing green gem held by the horned dwarf statue, revealing that the object possessed magical properties — though her follow-up investigation, a natural one, left its actual worth an open question: “To your best guess, this is an emerald, but it could also just be glass.”
