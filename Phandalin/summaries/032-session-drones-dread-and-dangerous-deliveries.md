# Chapter 32: Drones, Dread, and Dangerous Deliveries

Date: 2026-01-28

## Summary

Picking up after Aletra escaped through her portal and the interdimensional device collapsed into the void, the party searched the room where the machine had been. Nothing remained of the device itself, but they discovered strange strands of interdimensional spider silk left behind by the Rift Weavers. Valphine, a former devotee of Lolth, considered collecting the unusual silk for her temple, insisting that it was “perfectly reasonable decoration.” The party debated the nature of these creatures—whether they were a natural phenomenon, a defense mechanism against planar damage, or summoned beings sent by some unknown deity.

Valphine’s Arcana knowledge suggested that the Rift Weavers might be a naturally occurring response to planar incursions, almost like antibodies protecting reality. The GM explained that naturalists would view their appearance as evidence that “nature is healing,” while interventionists might argue that another deity or power had summoned them. Whatever their origin, Aletra had not been surprised by their arrival and possessed equipment specifically designed to counter them, indicating that whoever built and operated the spatial machinery knew the Rift Weavers were a predictable consequence.

The party opened a door to the west and discovered Aletra’s makeshift quarters in what had once been a barracks. A high-quality silk bedroll and a folding desk made of black Zurkhwood cluttered with modern parchment revealed where she had been working. The lingering scent of lavender mixed with perfume identified the space as belonging to House Sotorra’s poison operations: the smell of its high-end poison laboratory disguised beneath Aletra’s expensive perfume.

Soma investigated a ledger on the desk and uncovered Aletra’s forgeries—she had crossed out “iron for 500 pounds” and written “Midnight Tears Crate 4, do not drop” in Undercommon, providing clear evidence of her tampering with the books. The party concluded that this was likely the forgery the Chief Accountant had detected and took the ledger as evidence.

They also found the *Operator’s Guide to the Model 1489 Spatial Rectification Calliope*. The manual treated the machine largely as a black box but included Aletra’s handwritten notes, among them “Why does this thing have a moral compass?” and “use only if KP isn’t watching.” Brewbarry had less interest in the technical material and contemplated using its soft paper to wrap sandwiches.

After Vukradin left the room, Valphine discovered a hidden bag containing a small platinum coin worth only seven gold pieces and five silver pieces, a square-cut malachite gem worth eight gold pieces, a bottle of fine wine, and four vials of Midnight Tears poison worth two thousand gold pieces total. These four hidden-bag vials were a separate cache from the several vials Aletra dropped in the device room during her escape; the party’s recovery of that earlier dropped shipment was not resolved this session. The group joked that Valphine might have a legitimate claim to her sister’s belongings, particularly if Aletra were to meet with an unfortunate “accident.”

The party returned to the Chief Accountant, who had become calm and relaxed now that the spatial distortions had disappeared. When they informed her that Aletra had fled and the machine had imploded, she immediately worried about whether one final bill remained unpaid. The party presented her with the interplanetary bill for forty-two thousand gold pieces. She erupted: “This is outrageous! This is absolutely incorrect! This is unbelievable! I cannot believe we are being charged for this!” After screaming for approximately ten minutes, she sighed, declared that they owed no more money and “The book is closed,” then disappeared. The party understood that her anxiety over unsettled accounts had bound her to Axeholm and that, with no remaining bill to pay, she had finally gone in peace.

The party then explored the accountant’s old room, finding a dusty bedroom with a stone-faced bed, moldy mattresses, and a soot-stained fireplace. Valphine discovered a secret door hidden in the back of the fireplace. When they opened it, four steampunk-like audit drones—resembling mechanical mosquitoes—emerged and attacked. One drone drove its needle into Valphine, filling a vial halfway with her blood. Brewbarry destroyed two drones with his weapon, Vukradin killed the drone that attacked him, Soma’s Starry Wisp missed its target, and Valphine captured the final drone by pulling it from her body and holding it for later investigation.

Behind the secret door, they found a ten-by-ten room containing an eighty-pound iron chest with clawed feet and a tiny circular indentation in place of a conventional keyhole. After multiple failed attempts to pick or force the lock, Brewbarry smashed the chest open with brute strength. The noise echoed through the fortress, but the party had already killed every hostile creature in the dungeon, so nothing answered it.

Inside, they discovered a dread helm and gauntlets of ogre power that had belonged to the deceased Chief Accountant. Vukradin argued that they should locate the accountant’s next of kin and return the items, while the others suggested that someone could use them in the meantime to ensure they worked. Brewbarry immediately put on the dread helm, causing his eyes to glow an ominous red. He delighted in the effect, while Vukradin warned that appearing in shops and government buildings looking like “a giant killer robot” might alarm patrons and politicians. Their final unexplored door proved to lead only to a privy.

After taking a long rest during the several-day journey, the party returned to Phandalin. Townmaster Harbin Wester was ecstatic that Axeholm had been secured and paid them two hundred fifty gold pieces for their efforts. However, he clarified that Axeholm was meant as a last-resort refuge in case of an imminent attack, not a replacement home for the town. Vukradin was disappointed, having imagined that the party was creating a noble new home for Phandalin rather than merely establishing a backup plan.

When Brewbarry, still wearing the dread helm with glowing red eyes, approached Harbin, the townmaster nervously asked whether he had been possessed by a demon, an undead spirit, or some other dark power. Vukradin asserted that he was the head of the Privy Council and would not bring a demon into the middle of town. Harbin was sufficiently intimidated and later flattered Vukradin by agreeing that he did not merely sit on the Privy Council but led it, while Harbin remained its formal leader. Brewbarry leaned into his new appearance with ominous lines about faith and aspirations, leaving Harbin to interpret him as Vukradin’s religious enforcer.

Harbin Wester then asked about the party’s arrangement with Don-Jon Raskin and the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine. Although Qelline Alderleaf’s cheese was ready, nobody else wished to transport it through territory threatened by dragons and orcs. Harbin stressed that the party’s matchmaking efforts involving Qelline and Big Al had healed a long-standing source of pain in the community, but the practical problem of delivering the cheese remained. He also emphasized that until the dragon was removed, displaced orcs would continue putting pressure on the lowland farming communities.

The party reviewed their many obligations: obtaining the Dragon Slayer sword, visiting the Emerald Enclave, waiting for Sister Kaella to contact Valphine, returning a statue, and ultimately confronting the dragon. They nevertheless decided that delivering the cheese was both time-sensitive and the fastest way to get their gold mine operating. As Soma observed, clearing the route permanently might mean they would never have to make the delivery again. The party also agreed that good cheese should not be left sitting too long, prompting a debate over whether cheese spoils or merely ages and Soma’s conclusion: “Time makes fools of us all.”

Before departing, they spoke briefly with Ser Kaelen. Vukradin mentioned the Chief Accountant and expressed an interest in learning her history so her possessions could eventually be returned to the proper heirs. Ser Kaelen was pleased by his concern but immediately reminded the party that they still needed to return one of the statues and questioned why they were prioritizing the gold mine.

Vukradin explained that he was a musician who did not earn much money and needed the mine’s income to start a music studio and pay its mortgage. When he suggested that Ser Kaelen could deliver the cheese while the party recovered the statue, Ser Kaelen refused on the grounds that cheese delivery was not official Order of the Gauntlet business. He delivered a speech about honor, private business, and personal sacrifice, but Vukradin’s repeated persuasion eventually made him concede the party’s point.

Jenna Roscoe then pointed out a faster route to funding Vukradin’s studio: relaxing his restrictions on funding sources and effectively selling out. Vukradin declined. Although he wanted friendly relations with all the factions, he insisted that he would build the studio his own way.

The party traveled fifteen miles to the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine, moving quickly rather than scouting cautiously. They knew the route might be dangerous but were tired of delays, considered the cheese delivery unexciting, and preferred to get it done. Vukradin jokingly encouraged them to “go right into the ambush.”

Upon arriving at the mine’s west entrance, they encountered two ogres and eight orcs confronting the wererats. The ogres were shouting in Common to the wererat leader, demanding that the mine be surrendered to them in exchange for the wererats’ lives: “We make deal. You, you, you give mine to us!” The party briefly entertained rearranging the region’s workforce—sending the wererats back to their old home and making the orcs work the mine—but ultimately prepared for violence.

The possibility of negotiation vanished when the ogres recognized the party as the adventurers who had killed two of their tribesmen. The party had acquired a reputation among the tribe, and the ogres became enraged and determined to take revenge with their orc minions. Because the party had rushed openly toward the mine, there was no opportunity for stealth or surprise.

Combat erupted as the orcs advanced and hurled javelins. One struck Brewbarry, though Vukradin attempted to alter the outcome with Silvery Barbs; after some confusion over the reroll, the attack still dealt nine points of damage. Another orc moved to attack Vukradin after seeing him cast the spell. An ogre charged toward Brewbarry, who struck it for thirteen points of damage as it approached, before the ogre hit Vukradin for eleven.

Vukradin cast Slow, catching both ogres and two orcs and substantially reducing their movement and combat effectiveness. Soma then conjured a Flaming Sphere among nearby enemies. Two orcs and a slowed ogre failed their saving throws and each took nine points of fire damage; the spell did not kill them. Soma followed by transforming into a bear.

Valphine moved forward and called forth Spirit Guardians. Every affected enemy failed its Wisdom save, and the spectral guardians dealt seventeen points of damage apiece, killing three orcs and badly wounding an ogre. Brewbarry entered a rage and struck down the wounded ogre with two attacks, declaring, “I am your father,” and then, “And you’re a goner, baby.” The remaining ogre fired a ranged weapon at Valphine for eleven points of damage before backing away, but she maintained concentration on her guardians. Another orc threw a javelin at her and missed. The session ended with the party firmly in control but the battle still underway.

A figure from a previous campaign recalled in table conversation. The GM said Bob fought Iymrith and wounded her significantly while that campaign’s party dealt with fire giants. The group also recalled bringing Bob back from the Astral Plane. The GM concluded that Bob had received the heroic scene he wanted and made sure everyone knew he “wasn’t a patsy,” while cautioning the players not to bring him back again.

### Iymrith

An ancient blue dragon from a previous campaign who was significantly wounded by Bob while the former party dealt with fire giants and later escaped to Avernus rather than being killed.

### KP

An individual mentioned in Aletra’s notes within the device manual, suggesting Aletra was trying to avoid their oversight when operating the machine. KP also appeared on the equipment invoice and was jokingly described as collecting data and trying to raise money for a thesis.

### Chief Accountant

A banshee who remained bound to Axeholm by anxiety over outstanding bills and debts. Once the interdimensional distortions ended, she became noticeably calmer. Learning that Aletra and the machine were gone did not fully release her because she still feared that one final bill might remain.

When shown the 42,000-gold-piece invoice, she spent about ten minutes denouncing it as outrageous and incorrect. After deciding that Axeholm owed nothing further, she closed her ledger, declared “The book is closed,” and disappeared in peace. A dread helm and gauntlets of ogre power were later found in her hidden chest.

### Audit Drones

Four steampunk-like mosquito creatures that emerged from a secret passage in the Chief Accountant’s old room. They attack by driving needles into victims and drawing blood into attached vials. One filled its vial halfway with Valphine’s blood before she pulled it free and captured it for later investigation. The party had apparently encountered similar drones previously and already possessed one in a container.

### Harbin Wester

The Townmaster of Phandalin who rewards the party 250 gold pieces for securing Axeholm. He clarifies that the fortress is an emergency refuge rather than a new permanent home for the town.

Harbin is alarmed by Brewbarry’s dread helm and asks whether he has been possessed by a demon, an undead spirit, or some other hostile power. Vukradin intimidates him by invoking his leadership of the Privy Council. Harbin praises Vukradin’s community work, particularly the matchmaking involving Qelline Alderleaf and Big Al, before asking the party to deliver cheese to the gold mine and ultimately deal with the dragon displacing orcs into the lowlands.

### Qelline Alderleaf

A resident of Phandalin who supplies Petunia’s cheese for delivery to the wererats at the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine. Harbin Wester describes her connection with Big Al as the resolution of a long-standing source of pain and misery in the community. She cannot find anyone willing to make the delivery while dragons and orcs threaten the roads.

### Ser Kaelen

A member of the Order of the Gauntlet who reminds the party about returning a statue. He approves of Vukradin’s concern for the Chief Accountant’s history and next of kin but questions the party’s decision to prioritize the gold mine.

He refuses to deliver the cheese himself because it is not official Order business and gives a speech about honor, private interests, and personal sacrifice. Vukradin’s persistent persuasion forces him to concede that the party has a point, even if he does not volunteer for the delivery.

### Jenna Roscoe

A Lord’s Alliance agent who suggests that Vukradin could achieve his music studio goals faster by relaxing his restrictions on funding sources—in Vukradin’s words, selling out. He appreciates the suggestion but declines, insisting that he will pursue his goals his own way while remaining friendly with all factions.

### Ogres

Two large humanoids encountered at the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine entrance, members of a tribe whose kin the party had previously killed. They speak Common and Giant. They were attempting to force the wererats to surrender the mine in exchange for their lives.

The ogres recognize the party and become enraged, seeking revenge with the help of eight orc minions. Both are affected by Vukradin’s Slow spell. One is burned by Soma’s Flaming Sphere, badly wounded by Valphine’s Spirit Guardians, and finished by Brewbarry. The other remains alive at the end of the session after shooting Valphine and retreating.

### Orcs

Eight minions accompanying the ogres at the gold mine entrance. They attack with javelins and melee weapons. Several are slowed by Vukradin, burned by Soma’s Flaming Sphere, or killed by Valphine’s Spirit Guardians. They are part of the broader orc population driven into the lowlands by the dragon.

### Zeleen Varnaster

Described as a “big goblin” by the ogres, this character is the head of the wererats working the gold mine and was being threatened by the ogres when the party arrived. The ogres demanded that the wererats surrender the mine, although the party’s arrival and the ensuing battle interrupted the confrontation.

## Locations

### Device Room

A room in Axeholm where an interdimensional device recently collapsed into the void after receiving its shutdown code. The machine itself vanished completely, while strange interdimensional spider silk remained. The room was also the site from which Aletra escaped and where she dropped a shipment of poison vials whose recovery remains unresolved.

### Aletra’s Quarters

A former barracks room in Axeholm that Aletra converted into a personal working area. It contains a high-quality silk bedroll, a folding desk made of black Zurkhwood, crisp modern parchments stamped with red wax, and the lingering scent of House Sotorra’s poison laboratory mixed with expensive lavender perfume.

The quarters contained Aletra’s forged ledger, the Calliope operator’s manual, hidden valuables, wine, and four concealed vials of Midnight Tears.

### Chief Accountant’s Office

The office in Axeholm where the Chief Accountant was found and where she received the final equipment bill. After furiously disputing the charge and declaring all accounts closed, she disappeared from this location and passed on.

### Accountant’s Old Room

A dusty bedroom in Axeholm containing a stone-faced bed with moldy mattresses and a soot-stained fireplace. Valphine discovers that the back of the fireplace conceals a secret door. Four audit drones emerge from the passage when it is opened.

### Secret Room

A hidden ten-by-ten room behind the fireplace in the accountant’s quarters. An 80-pound iron chest with clawed feet rests against its south wall. The chamber serves as the audit drones’ ambush point and contains the Chief Accountant’s dread helm and gauntlets of ogre power.

### Phandalin

A small town where the party returns to report its success at Axeholm, collect its reward from Townmaster Harbin Wester, and review its growing list of obligations. Harbin asks the party to deliver Qelline Alderleaf’s cheese to the gold mine and again urges them to address the dragon threat. The party also speaks with Ser Kaelen and Jenna Roscoe before leaving.

### Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine

A gold mine located 15 miles from Phandalin and currently worked by wererats under the party’s arrangement with Don-Jon Raskin. The party arrives at its west entrance while attempting to deliver cheese and finds two ogres and eight orcs demanding that the wererats surrender control. A battle begins when the ogres recognize the party as the killers of their tribesmen.

## Items

### Interplanetary Bill

A generated bill for 42,000 gold pieces addressed to the owners who rented the interdimensional equipment. Its reference information and bureaucratic presentation resemble an elaborate expense report. The Chief Accountant vehemently protests its accuracy for approximately ten minutes before determining that Axeholm owes nothing and declaring all accounts settled.

### Interdimensional Spider Silk

Very odd strands of silk left behind by the Rift Weavers after the interdimensional device collapsed. Unlike any silk the party has seen before, Valphine considers collecting it for her temple. As a drow and former devotee of Lolth, spider silk is something she still finds suitable and attractive as decoration.

### Aletra’s Forged Ledger

A modern parchment ledger found on Aletra’s desk and stamped with red wax. It contains her forgeries, specifically showing where she crossed out “iron for 500 pounds” and wrote in Undercommon, “Midnight Tears Crate 4, do not drop.” The entry provides evidence that she disguised poison shipments as ordinary material purchases and confirms the financial tampering detected by the Chief Accountant.

### Operator’s Guide to the Model 1489 Spatial Rectification Calliope

A user manual for the interdimensional device found on Aletra’s desk. It treats the machine largely as a black box rather than providing the detailed technical information Vukradin hoped to find.

Aletra’s annotations include “Why does this thing have a moral compass?” and “use only if KP isn’t watching.” Brewbarry considers its soft paper potentially useful for wrapping sandwiches.

### Small Platinum Coin

A coin found in a bag in Aletra’s quarters, worth only seven gold pieces and five silver pieces because it is too small to count as a standard platinum coin. The party compares it to an unusually sized collectible penny.

### Square Cut Malachite Gem

A square-cut gem found in a bag in Aletra’s quarters, worth eight gold pieces.

### Bottle of Fine Wine

A beverage found in a bag in Aletra’s quarters. Brewbarry immediately identifies it as the only genuinely exciting piece of treasure in the bag and begins drinking it, although he later complains that it “tasted like crap.”

### Midnight Tears

Four vials of rare drow poison found by Valphine in a hidden bag in Aletra’s quarters after Vukradin left the room. Worth 2,000 gold pieces total, they are controlled substances that cannot easily be sold in normal stores. Neverwinter is mentioned as a possible place to find a buyer.

They are distinct from the several poison vials Aletra dropped in the device room during her escape; whether the party recovered that earlier shipment remains unresolved.

### Dread Helm

A wondrous helm found in the iron chest in the secret room, identified as belonging to the deceased Chief Accountant. Its principal visible effect is to make the wearer’s eyes glow red.

Brewbarry immediately dons it and takes great pleasure in displaying his glowing eyes. The helm makes him resemble a giant killer robot, alarms Harbin Wester, and helps create the impression that Brewbarry is Vukradin’s ominous religious enforcer.

### Gauntlets of Ogre Power

Gauntlets found in the iron chest in the secret room, identified as belonging to the deceased Chief Accountant and noted as being valuable. Vukradin believes they should ultimately be returned to the accountant’s next of kin, while the others suggest using or testing them in the interim.

### Iron Chest

An 80-pound iron chest with clawed feet and a tiny circular indentation in place of a traditional keyhole. It is found in the secret room behind the accountant’s fireplace. After Vukradin cannot open it and Valphine fails to pick it with thieves’ tools, Brewbarry smashes it apart with brute force. The resulting noise has no consequences because the fortress has already been cleared.

### Cheese

Cheese supplied by Qelline Alderleaf for the party to transport to the Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine, crucial for maintaining the arrangement with the wererats working the mine. No other courier is willing to make the trip while dragons and orcs threaten the area, prompting the party to take on what they call a “reverse fetch quest.”

## Spells

### Silvery Barbs

A spell used by Vukradin to force an attacking orc to reroll its javelin attack against Brewbarry. Vukradin also grants Brewbarry advantage through the spell. After some confusion over the result, the rerolled attack is determined to still hit, dealing nine points of damage.

### Slow

A spell cast by Vukradin that affects multiple enemies by requiring DC 15 Wisdom saving throws. It successfully slows both ogres and two orcs at the gold mine, reducing their speed, limiting their actions and attacks, and imposing a minus-two penalty to Armor Class and Dexterity saving throws. Its penalty contributes to the enemies’ failure to avoid Soma’s Flaming Sphere.

### Flaming Sphere

A spell cast by Soma that creates a five-foot-diameter sphere of fire. The table applied an initial area ruling to two orcs and an ogre; all three failed their Dexterity saving throws and took nine points of fire damage. The sphere did not kill the orcs. Soma then transforms into a bear while maintaining the sphere.

### Spirit Guardians

A powerful spell cast by Valphine that creates a 15-foot emanation of spectral guardians. Valphine moves forward to catch an ogre and three orcs in its area. Every affected enemy fails its Wisdom save and takes 17 points of damage, killing three orcs and severely wounding an ogre. Valphine later takes 11 points of damage from the surviving ogre but succeeds on her Constitution save and maintains concentration.
