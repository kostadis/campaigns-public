# Chapter 11: Untitled

Date: 2025-08-12

## Summary
The party arrived at the rocky plateau of the Tower of Storms, where three harpies circled and sang their deadly, enchanting songs. Vukradin, the quick-thinking bard, conjured a twenty-foot-radius sphere of absolute silence around his companions, cutting off the harpies’ magic mid-note and freeing those who had already fallen under the creatures’ spell — as he put it, the harpy could sing all she wanted; nobody inside could hear her. The DM ruled that anyone who had shaken off a harpy’s song was immune to that particular harpy for a full day, though the songs carried a punishing 300-foot range, so stepping outside the bubble remained a gamble. Within the quiet, the party was safe from the charm, though it meant they could not easily strike back at the flying foes without stepping into danger — and could not cast spells with verbal components at all. The harpies, frustrated and confused, began swooping down to claw at the party before retreating back into the air, turning the battle into a grinding war of attrition; one even disengaged entirely once it realized it could not charm anyone, since — as the DM noted — silence spells are very effective at dissuading harpies.

Soma, the druid, shifted into the form of a massive wolf spider and skittered up the exterior stone walls of the tower, bringing the fight directly to the harpies on the upper parapets — the spider’s climb speed of 40 feet let her ignore the difficult terrain entirely. The spider’s powerful bite found its mark more than once, landing 11- and 12-point chomps that chipped away at the creatures’ resilience even as they screeched and slashed back; one harpy proved so reluctant to strike the spider that the DM diagnosed it with a case of arachnophobia stemming from “this whole incident with spiders.” Meanwhile, Brewbarry hurled hand axes at the airborne foes with more enthusiasm than accuracy, his throws landing so wide that the axes barely cleared his own boots. Valphine provided steadier support with her crossbow, landing a critical bolt on the harpy that had tried to charm her — though the creature held its concentration and kept singing. The harpies themselves — intelligence seven, and playing to type — compounded their problems: when the first singer’s song failed, a second harpy concluded the problem was the *other* guy’s song and started singing as well.

The battle took a dangerous turn when Brewbarry climbed the battlements to press the attack, only to be caught by a harpy’s enchanting wail — an effective 1 on the wisdom save — a rolled 2, minus 1, “a net of a one”. Helplessly charmed, unable to take actions or reactions, the barbarian stood frozen while his companions fought on around him, his rage lapsing from disuse. Vukradin, refusing to let the combat drag on — and standing at a mere three hit points while still holding concentration on the silence — gambled boldly: he granted himself a surge of bardic confidence, stepped outside the protective silence, and resisted the harpy’s song through sheer force of will (turning a failed roll into a success with his inspiration die) before blasting the creature and breaking its concentration. His companions watched in disbelief as the bard turned his own near-failure into a pivotal strike, drawing an amused aside from the table that Vukradin had a talent for failing upward — which the DM corrected: he was turning his failures into successes.

The harpies grew desperate, singing and swooping in turns, but the party slowly wore them down. Soma, still in spider form, darted across the walls and snapped at the creatures whenever they came within reach; her save against the song was never in doubt — the DM ruled that Soma had simply heard better songs. A final 12-damage bite broke the last singer’s concentration and set Brewbarry free. Valphine’s crossbow bolts found their marks with increasing regularity until one harpy fell dead from the parapet — softened up first by a swat from her mace as it fled — and Brewbarry brought down a second with his halberd. The final creature met its end not by steel or fire, but by Vukradin’s razor-sharp mockery — a torrent of cutting insults (“it smells of elderberry”) so psychically devastating that the harpy simply ceased to exist, leaving the plateau in blessed silence. The bard’s verdict on the harpies’ vocal stylings: they were not joining the academy, that’s for sure.

After a short rest — ruled at thirty minutes — to bind their wounds and retrieve Brewbarry’s embarrassingly close-range hand axes (the DM noted they landed “a few feet away from where you threw them,” thrown as though they were halberds; Brewbarry admitted they narrowly missed his own toes), the party pushed into the tower itself. Rough-hewn stairs climbed the eastern face of the outcropping to wooden doors with rusty iron hinges and decorative lightning bolts carved into them. The first rooms they explored were stark and salt-worn, with dirty windows overlooking the graveyard of ships broken on the rocks below — a sight that set off a running argument about whether the drowned sailors’ possessions were loot or “blood money,” with the DM observing that the party was engaged in revolutionary behavior against the world order of a colonial paradise. One chamber held frescoes of a dark and terrible god looming over storm-tossed seas — every religion check at the table whiffed, prompting the observation that the party’s cleric is not very religious — and a stone altar carved with lightning bolts stood beneath a metal rod that descended from the ceiling, splitting in two before embedding into the stone: a lightning rod of ominous design. On the outer ledge, enclosed by a three-foot retaining wall, Valphine discovered a potion of water breathing nestled in the foul-smelling harpy nest — “quite obvious” despite her blown search rolls — and Vukradin torched the whole wretched thing without even searching it.

Deeper in the tower, in a fifty-foot chamber with two north-facing windows, the party found Moesko — a half-orc anchorite clad in armor fashioned from the hide of a giant octopus, seated in a barnacle-crusted chair with an opalescent conch resting in his lap. The conch had been stolen from a sea elf named Miraal, and the party knew they had to take it back. Soma struck first with a lash of thorns, following it with a spray of poison, but Moesko shrugged off the worst of it and rose to his feet. Brewbarry raged and swung — whiffing again, which Vukradin graciously blamed on the earlier silence (“the fact that you couldn’t hear yourself scream”) — Valphine fired her crossbow, and the half-orc answered them all with a single, devastating bolt of lightning — eight dice rolled by hand for 25 damage — that tore through nearly the entire party and left them staggering on the edge of collapse, the table openly muttering about a TPK. With the party barely standing, Soma made a desperate gamble and transformed into a massive brown bear, filling the cramped chamber with fur and fury (the bear had been too large to fit until Brewbarry shuffled aside). The bear’s powerful claws and jaws tore into Moesko with tremendous force — a multi-attack for 20 damage — and Vukradin conjured a swirling vortex of spinning blades in a five-foot cube to hem the half-orc in and shred him further. The DM’s internet chose that exact moment to die, stranding the table mid-combat (“The tubes are clogged. It’s a series of tubes”) until he flickered back with tales of the industrial-strength router required to feed his relatives’ devices at his house on Santorini. Moesko, out of lightning, unleashed a concussive burst of thunder that rattled the walls and pushed the party back ten feet, but it was not enough. Valphine fired one last bolt before retreating to the doorway — everyone cheerfully abandoning the bear, who would be fine — and Brewbarry charged in to batter the anchorite down to his last hit point. Soma shed the bear’s bulk to move in the cramped chamber and delivered the final, decisive strike that dropped him to the floor. The opalescent conch clattered free from Moesko’s lifeless grip, and the party stood victorious, battered but alive, ready to uncover the secrets of what they had won next week.

## Locations
### Tower of Storms
A rocky outcropping and tower rising roughly 80 feet above the water, featuring a plateau, stone parapets, and rooms decorated with lightning bolt motifs and frescoes of stormy seas. The site where the party battled harpies and confronted the half-orc anchorite Moesko. Rough-hewn stairs climb the eastern face of the outcropping to wooden doors with rusty iron hinges and decorative lightning bolts carved into them. Inside: a stark 15-foot-high entry room whose dirty south window overlooks the graveyard of wrecked ships; a frescoed shrine room (T4) depicting a dark and terrible god looming over storm-tossed seas, holding a lightning-bolt-carved stone altar beneath a salt-encrusted west window and a metal lightning rod descending from the ceiling; a harpy nest on an outer ledge enclosed by a three-foot-high stone retaining wall (now torched); and, deeper in, a 50-foot chamber with two north-facing windows and a barnacle-encrusted chair — Moesko’s seat.

## NPCs
### Harpies
Three monstrous winged creatures with an intelligence of seven that use enchanting songs to charm sailors and adventurers. They employed hit-and-run tactics at the Tower of Storms, swooping down to claw at the party before retreating into the air — flyby strikes that repeatedly exposed them to opportunity attacks. Their songs carried 300 feet, but a successful save granted 24 hours of immunity to that harpy’s voice, and their low cunning betrayed them: when the first singer’s song was neutralized by Vukradin’s silence, a second harpy concluded the problem was *“the other guy’s song”* and simply started singing too, while one disengaged entirely once charming became impossible. One of the three showed a marked reluctance to strike Soma’s spider form — the DM attributed it to arachnophobia from *“this whole incident with spiders.”* All three were defeated by the party: one shot down by Valphine, one felled by Brewbarry’s halberd, and the last mocked to death by Vukradin.

### Moesko
A half-orc anchorite clad in armor made from giant octopus hide, found sitting in a barnacle-encrusted chair at the top of the Tower of Storms — one of the same order of strange orc clerics the party has encountered before, this one alone. He possessed an opalescent conch stolen from a sea elf and defended himself with powerful lightning and thunder magic — a single devastating lightning bolt (his only one) and a thunderwave — before being slain by the party. AC 13, 58 hit points, and nearly a party-killer regardless.

A unique set of armor worn by Moesko, crafted from the tough, leathery skin of a giant octopus.

## Spells
### Silence
Vukradin created a 20-foot radius sphere of absolute silence that proved essential in protecting the party from the harpies’ charming songs. Everyone within the sphere was deafened, preventing the charm condition from taking hold, though it also prevented the use of spells with verbal components from within the area. The spell shaped the entire battle: one harpy disengaged outright once charming became impossible, and Vukradin held concentration through two claw attacks — including one that dropped him to three hit points — before finally releasing it late in the fight to free up his spell options. The party’s peek-out-attack-retreat rhythm around its edge defined the war of attrition.

### Wild Shape
Soma used her druidic power to transform, first into a giant wolf spider (climb speed 40) to scale the vertical stone walls of the tower and engage the harpies, and later into a massive brown bear to maul Moesko with a powerful multi-attack for 20 damage. The table settled the 2024-rules question mid-fight: the form now grants temporary hit points based on druid level rather than a separate hit point pool, so losing them no longer kicks the druid out of the shape — a change the DM said he actually prefers. The bear, being Large, couldn’t fit into the cramped chamber until Brewbarry shifted aside to make room.

### Bardic Inspiration
Vukradin granted himself bardic inspiration, allowing him to turn a failed wisdom saving throw (against DC 11) into a success against a harpy’s charming song, enabling him to step outside the silence and strike the creature. His own summary: *“So I turned my failure into a success.”* — a maneuver the table immediately christened *“failing upward.”*

### Vicious Mockery
