# Chapter 20: Blood Money, Clean Gold, and Fine Wine

Date: 2025-10-15

## Summary

The party gathered at the tavern to discuss their recent encounter with Sister Kaella, an enemy who had offered to work with them against the Carver. Valphine reported that Kaella had told her how to find the Carver and offered the reward intended for Locutus as a down payment for cooperation. Kaella’s actionable intelligence on the remaining lieutenant, however, was promised in a few days; eliminating that lieutenant was intended to draw the Carver out. This revelation sparked immediate outrage from Vukradin, who adamantly refused to work with evil people or accept what he considered stolen goods. When Valphine argued, “She is our enemy, but she is also enemy of our enemy, which provides good negotiation options,” Vukradin answered, “No. She IS our enemy!” He was equally appalled that Valphine had let Kaella walk away after having her within reach.

The revelation that Kaella had offered the party the reward originally intended for Locutus only made matters worse. Valphine insisted, “Oh, we earned that, though,” but Vukradin retorted, “You can’t earn stolen… how many times do I have to tell you people you can’t earn stolen goods?” Soma offered a narrower distinction: “You can earn it, you just can’t keep it.” Vukradin finally declared, “I need to go take a walk outside. I need some air and a cigarette,” and stormed out of the tavern. Soma explained his position to the others by calling him “a man of principle” and observing that he believed possessions continued to belong to their original owners. Valphine resigned herself to “this overbright, adventure” of discovering whether those owners were still alive, while Soma warned that Vukradin might object to keeping the money even if they proved dead.

While Vukradin was outside, two pale-skinned, sunburned villagers named Brin Bundlewine and Giles Slipper-Shine approached Valphine with great excitement. They asked whether she was “Lathander’s cleric” and a believer in “the purifying pain of the sun.” They had waited years for someone who understood that Lathander’s glory could be found in feeling sunlight sear the body and bring purity. They had especially embraced rumors of a drow worshiper, believing only a drow could truly comprehend their doctrine through direct experience of the sun’s pain.

Valphine agreed that founding a chapel was a good idea and gave Brin and Giles their first assignment: clean up the dilapidated Tresendar Manor, which she declared would become their temple. Harbin Wester conveniently appeared and offered to arrange the paperwork necessary to transfer the property out of receivership. He proposed a modest fee of fifty gold pieces and carefully suggested that this could be “a private matter that the entire council doesn’t need to be exposed to.” When Valphine asked whether this was how business was conducted in the Overbright, Harbin quickly adopted her terminology: “Yes, this is how things are done in the overbright.” Soma helped translate the arrangement into plainer language: “It’s a deal we make. You help me, I help you.”

The party paid the fee from the two hundred gold pieces received through Kaella’s arrangement, leaving a recorded balance of one hundred fifty gold pieces categorized as blood money immediately after the paperwork fee, before the later materials discussion. Tresendar Manor was renamed the Temple of Lathander’s Searing Pain of Justice. Valphine contemplated decorating it with spikes, chains, an open roof, and skylights to maximize exposure to Lathander’s painful radiance. Brin and Giles agreed to provide free labor but requested twenty-five gold pieces for beams, furniture, and other construction materials. They tried several honorifics before settling upon Matron Mother of Lathander. *(Title editorially normalized from the ambiguous ASR “Patron Mother,” per GM ruling.)*

Jenna Roscoe used the transaction as an object lesson in the moral laundering of adventuring wealth. She told the party, “See, this is what I mean about how money, when you take it as adventures and then put it back into the community, it becomes clean money.” She hoped they might eventually help Vukradin understand that returning wealth to the people redeemed it. Soma counseled patience: “He’s young, he’s got a lot to learn, give him time.”

When Vukradin returned from his walk, Harbin quickly claimed that the temple funds had been “a donation made by the community.” Vukradin was pleased that something good had apparently happened in his absence and even suggested Harbin might not need replacing after all. The group carefully avoided explaining the actual source of the funds. Harbin directed Toblen to keep the drinks coming, and the party toasted the new temple.

The next morning, Vukradin spoke with Ser Kaelen about tracking down the rightful owners of the treasure they had found. Ser Kaelen explained that he was waiting for information from Neverwinter and should have answers in a few days. He suggested the party could check out Mountain’s Toe or deliver supplies to the Loggers’ Camp while they waited. Sister Kaella’s promised intelligence would likewise take several days, so the party still did not know the Carver’s location.

The party chose the supply delivery, partly because Harbin’s supposed generosity toward the temple left Vukradin feeling obliged to help him. At Barthen’s Provisions, Barthen handed them an inventory listing two heavy crates filled with dried meats, blocks of cheese, loaves of bread, casks of ale, and flasks of oil. The crates were loaded onto a two-wheeled cart pulled by an ox named Vincent, both of which Barthen expected to be returned. Brewbarry immediately objected to the culinary imbalance: “Those crates have bread and cheese and no wine? That doesn’t seem right.” Barthen recommended a detour to the Falcon Hunting Lodge, renowned for fine wine.

Before they departed, Barthen described Falcon’s lodge as a sanctuary in the heart of Neverwinter Wood. Falcon served as a guide to nobles who would not last long in the forest without his protection and survival skills. The compound included a main house, guest accommodations, a combined stable and smithy, an outhouse, and livestock pens. Vukradin persuaded Barthen to provide a signed letter of introduction confirming that the party came on his recommendation. Valphine also considered selling her old chain shirt from the Underdark; Vukradin accepted her assurance that it had genuinely belonged to her and therefore was not blood money.

After a peaceful two-day journey through Neverwinter Wood, the party arrived at the fortified Falcon Hunting Lodge. The trip was so uneventful that Vukradin jokingly complained about the absence of magical confusion, obscured ruins, or navigation trouble. The party instead enjoyed a nature hike, sang songs, drank, and—by Vukradin’s accounting—did not steal anything.

A thin fog surrounded the lodge, which stood in a clearing on the east side of a narrow river. A ten-foot-high log palisade enclosed a two-story main building with a high-pitched roof, gables, shutters, and a stone chimney. A blocky gray stone tower with battlements adjoined it, while a gatehouse, stable house, stone bridge, and bell completed the visible defenses. Soma rang the bell and was met by Corwin, who demanded to know who they were. Once shown Barthen’s note, he welcomed them inside. Corwin explained, “My name’s Corwin, although my friends called me Chef. You’re not my friends yet, so you can call me Corwin.”

Brewbarry ignored most of the social preliminaries and demanded to hear about the wine. Falcon boasted that he kept some of the best wine in the region, although it was not cheap. He offered several bottles—and potentially more—if Vukradin entertained the household. Brewbarry towered over him and countered, “Make that barrels, and you got it here.” Falcon laughed heartily and declared that they would be good friends, without explicitly promising any barrels.

When Brewbarry asked about the unusually large fence surrounding the compound, Falcon explained that orcs had left their mountain homes since a dragon appeared in the area and descended into the valley. The orcs had become more numerous and more organized, laying traps and cooperating with evil half-orcs. These half-orcs lived in an abandoned stone manse overgrown with vines roughly ten miles southwest of the lodge. Falcon maintained that neither group currently troubled him and boasted that any orcs foolish enough to attack would become dead orcs.

Falcon also described his retirement philosophy. He observed, “There’s young adventurers, and there’s retired old adventurers. There’s no old adventurers who are still adventuring.” Rather than continue risking his life, he preferred managing the lodge, hunting, guiding wealthy guests, caring for his animals, and enjoying fine meals and wine. Brewbarry countered, “Well, it’s not about how long you live your life, it’s how you live your life,” but Falcon believed he was doing exactly that.

The party performed in Falcon’s dining room. Vukradin’s playing was excellent, although Valphine’s accompanying death chant was less successful and sounded sufficiently unlike Lathander’s worship that it later contributed to Sridar suspecting an elaborate Lolthite ruse. The performance nevertheless earned the rest of the party ample wine.

During dinner, Falcon claimed that his former adventuring group had killed a dragon and divided its hoard four ways. According to his account, his share had financed the lodge. Vukradin immediately stopped playing and declared, “I will not perform in this venue.” He accused Falcon of taking wealth stolen from the dragon’s victims: “It wasn’t enough that you had to steal from all those people killed by the dragon. You continue to take more money!” Falcon was bewildered and offended, eventually barring Vukradin from the free wine.

Falcon had heard rumors of a “new movement against adventurers using plunder,” which he characterized as an attempt to disrupt the ruling elite, impose law and order, and abandon the traditional violence of adventuring. In his view, adventurers were supposed to take treasure and plow it back into communities. Soma suggested that “The adventure can be the reward on its own,” earning Falcon’s approval and an invitation to dinner. Vukradin withdrew to sulk outside and eat his own rations, although he took some satisfaction in having already played a set that secured free wine for his friends.

The party was also joined at dinner by Sridar, another drow staying as Falcon’s guest. Valphine asked whether she had come to enjoy “the pain of the overbright” and explained that she had moved to the surface because it was “much, much more delightfully painful.” Sridar was astonished to discover that Valphine genuinely served Lathander. She whispered, “You’re really a cleric of Lolth, right?” Valphine denied it and insisted that her faith was not an elaborate ruse.

Sridar explained that she had become a collector of curious artifacts and items that were “not quite right,” particularly those connected to planar events. Her fascination began after the blink in the Underdark, when the Faerzress changed and demons appeared. She was sincerely interested in planar transformations and offered to pay for either useful stories or physical artifacts.

Valphine recounted in considerable detail the party’s encounter with a strange creature collecting objects at a planar disturbance. Sridar had never heard of such a creature and was fascinated that the location remained accessible after the party departed. She initially offered twenty gold pieces for the information. Vukradin was summoned back to negotiate, but Falcon first demanded an apology for being called a common thief. Vukradin offered, “I’m sorry, you’re a good host,” with no genuine retraction of his beliefs. His silver tongue nevertheless convinced Falcon, who accepted the apology.

Vukradin negotiated Sridar’s payment for the information up to thirty gold pieces. The party then showed her the Talisman of Moesko. Sridar immediately recognized a planar connection and offered to buy it. Soma remained suspicious that someone would pay so much for an object she claimed merely to collect, while Vukradin argued that wealthy collectors spent extravagantly in search of novelty. Soma ultimately accepted that reasoning and agreed to the sale. The final arrangement was one hundred twenty gold pieces for the talisman and thirty for the information, for one hundred fifty gold pieces in recorded proceeds. Sridar also paid Falcon thirty gold pieces to provide Brewbarry with three bottles of wine; an explicit physical handover of either the talisman or the money was not recorded.

The conversation turned to theories about why the planar incursions were happening. Sridar speculated that the cause could be magic users, extraplanar entities, or “fools meddling with things they don’t understand.” She had previously assumed the incidents were natural phenomena analogous to the Faerzress. Brewbarry challenged that assumption: “I’m not the brightest barbarian, but all those events happening within the same area, how likely is it that it’s a coincidence?” Sridar conceded that intentional interference was possible and that she had never seriously considered it before.

The party also discussed the Emerald Enclave’s division between interventionists and naturalists. Falcon characterized Adabra and the naturalists as believing that “everything evil comes from anyone intervening and trying to protect the world, and so we should just let it all burn to the ground.” Soma, a late-in-life druid who had once thought herself a naturalist, rejected that view as “batshit insanity” and affirmed that she wanted a world to exist tomorrow. Sridar was not a member of the Emerald Enclave but considered herself an interventionist, especially in light of what had happened in Menzoberranzan. She offered to help the party piece together future clues, information, and artifacts connected to planar activity.

Before the evening ended, Falcon mentioned a potential quest involving the half-orcs at the Woodland Manse. He initially cautioned that the party needed more experience before attempting it, but promised a pair of boots of elvenkind if they eventually dealt with the occupants. When Brewbarry asked about their size, Falcon explained that the magical boots adjusted to fit the wearer. Mindful of Vukradin, Falcon emphasized, “Don’t worry, I purchased those boots,” specifying that they had been bought with proceeds from his establishment.

As the party settled in for the night, Vukradin privately resolved to search the lodge for information about the dragon hoard—not to steal it, but to identify its original owners. He intended to bring those owners to Falcon and force the retired adventurer to confront the consequences of keeping treasure originally stolen by the dragon. The group noted that properly tracing such provenance would probably require magic such as *Legend Lore*.

## Locations

### The Tavern

A gathering place in Phandalin where the party discusses its dealings with Sister Kaella and where Vukradin expresses his strong objections before leaving for a cigarette. It is also where Brin and Giles approach Valphine, Harbin negotiates the transfer of Tresendar Manor, and Jenna explains how adventuring wealth can become “clean money” when returned to the community. Toblen keeps the party supplied with beer, particularly at Harbin’s direction.

### Tresendar Manor / Temple of Lathander’s Searing Pain of Justice

A formerly dilapidated manor in Phandalin that is converted into a temple dedicated to Lathander’s purifying pain. Harbin claims the property is in receivership and arranges its transfer for 50 gold pieces through a private deal that does not involve the full Privy Council. Brin Bundlewine and Giles Slipper-Shine provide free labor, with 25 gold pieces requested for beams, furniture, and other materials. Valphine envisions abundant sunlight, skylights, spikes, and chains as appropriate elements of worship.

### Barthen’s Provisions

A shop in Phandalin where the party picks up supplies for the Loggers’ Camp. Barthen provides an inventory, two crates of provisions, a two-wheeled cart, and an ox named Vincent. He also supplies the party with an authenticated letter of introduction to Falcon the Hunter and recommends Falcon’s lodge when Brewbarry complains that the delivery contains bread and cheese but no wine.

### Loggers’ Camp

A remote camp in the forest that requires a supply delivery, serving as the party’s primary destination for this journey. The party does not reach it during this session because it stops overnight at the Falcon Hunting Lodge.

### Falcon’s Hunting Lodge

A fortified compound in a clearing on the east side of a narrow river in the heart of Neverwinter Wood. A thin fog surrounds its ten-foot-high log palisade. A stone bridge leads to the gatehouse, where a bell announces visitors.

The compound includes a two-story main building with a high-pitched roof, gables, shuttered windows, and a stone chimney; an attached gray stone tower crowned with battlements; a stable house; guest accommodations; a smithy; an outhouse; and livestock pens. Falcon maintains the property as a sanctuary, hunting retreat, and base for guiding nobles through the wilderness. It possesses a large dining room, deluxe guest quarters, and a cellar stocked with fine but expensive wine. Falcon claims his share of a dragon’s hoard financed its construction.

### Woodland Manse

An abandoned, overgrown stone manse located roughly 10 miles southwest of the Falcon Hunting Lodge. Evil half-orcs currently inhabit it and appear to be cooperating with increasingly organized orc groups in the region. Falcon says its occupants do not currently bother him, but he offers boots of elvenkind if the party eventually deals with them and cautions that they may need more experience first.

### Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine

A gold mine mentioned by Ser Kaelen as a potential quest destination. The associated task involves escorting someone to the mine. The party considers combining that journey with the Loggers’ Camp delivery but decides against risking Barthen’s cart and ox on the detour.

### Neverwinter Wood

A large forest through which the party travels on its two-day journey to the Falcon Hunting Lodge. Despite previous experiences with magical navigation problems and obscured ruins, this trip is peaceful and uneventful. The party enjoys nature, sings, drinks, and arrives without losing or damaging the cart.

## NPCs

### Sister Kaella

An enemy of the party who offered cooperation against the Carver. Valphine reports that Kaella told her how to find him, but actionable intelligence on his remaining lieutenant was promised in a few days, to help draw the Carver out. She offered the party the 200-gold reward she had intended to give Locutus as a down payment on their relationship. Vukradin considers the payment stolen goods and is outraged that Valphine allowed Kaella to walk away.

### Jenna Roscoe

An associate present at the tavern, still nursing her wounds. She advocates using adventuring money to benefit communities, believing that returning it to the people “cleans” the money and makes it ethical. She uses the new temple as her central example and hopes the party can eventually persuade Vukradin to accept this reasoning.

### Brin Bundlewine

A fair-skinned villager with a pageboy haircut, pale complexion, and numerous sunburns. He is a zealous believer in Lathander’s purifying pain and believes a drow is uniquely equipped to understand their doctrine. Along with Giles, he approaches Valphine to establish a chapel and volunteers his labor to renovate Tresendar Manor.

### Giles Slipper-Shine

A pale, sunburned villager with restless hands who shares Brin’s beliefs about Lathander’s purifying pain. He is visibly excited to meet Valphine and assists Brin in founding and renovating the new temple.

### Harbin Wester

The townmaster of Phandalin and leader of the Privy Council. Opportunistic and somewhat corrupt, he arranges the transfer of Tresendar Manor for 50 gold pieces and proposes keeping the arrangement away from the full council. He quickly learns Valphine’s word “Overbright” and assures her that private reciprocal deals are how business is conducted there. When Vukradin returns, Harbin falsely presents the temple’s financing as a community donation. He also requests the delivery of supplies to the Loggers’ Camp and keeps beer flowing during the temple negotiations.

### Ser Kaelen

A knight investigating the rightful owners of treasure the party has found. He has sent a message to Neverwinter and expects information in a few days. While the party waits, he suggests checking Mountain’s Toe or delivering supplies to the Loggers’ Camp.

### Barthen

A merchant and proprietor of Barthen’s Provisions. He provides the party with two crates of supplies, a cart, and an ox named Vincent for the Loggers’ Camp delivery. He describes Falcon’s lodge and wilderness-guide business, recommends its wine to Brewbarry, and writes a signed letter introducing the party to Falcon.

### Toblen

The tavern keeper who serves beers to the party, particularly keeping Soma well supplied at Harbin’s direction. After Vukradin returns and accepts the story of a community-funded temple, Toblen provides a celebratory round.

### Corwin

The chef and one of Falcon’s retainers at the Falcon Hunting Lodge. He greets the party at the gate and initially insists that only his friends may call him “Chef.” After seeing Barthen’s note, he welcomes them inside and fetches Falcon. He avoids answering questions that he believes should be directed to his employer and repeatedly supplies wine during dinner.

### Falcon the Hunter

The owner and proprietor of the Falcon Hunting Lodge, a retired adventurer with full mutton chops who abhors city life and prefers a rustic existence. He serves as a guide to nobles traveling through Neverwinter Wood and enjoys hunting, caring for livestock, entertaining guests, serving fine meals, and drinking fine wine.

Falcon is arrogant but hospitable. He believes the nearby orcs know not to attack him and boasts that any who try will die. He reports that orcs displaced by the dragon have become more organized and are working with evil half-orcs at the Woodland Manse.

Falcon believes successful adventurers should retire after accumulating a fortune because “There’s no old adventurers who are still adventuring.” He claims his lodge was financed by his quarter-share of a slain dragon’s hoard. Vukradin’s accusation that this makes him a thief genuinely confuses and insults him. Falcon dismisses the anti-plunder movement as an attack on the ruling elite and traditional adventuring. He is an interventionist regarding planar threats and offers boots of elvenkind as a future reward for dealing with the Woodland Manse.

### Sridar

A drow traveler and refugee staying at the Falcon Hunting Lodge under Falcon’s protection. She is neither an adventurer nor a member of the Emerald Enclave. She is initially suspicious that Valphine’s Lathander worship is an elaborate Lolthite ruse, especially after hearing Valphine’s death chant.

Sridar collects curious artifacts with planar connections and became fascinated with planar transformations after the blink in the Underdark, when the Faerzress changed and demons appeared. She initially assumes the local phenomena are natural but takes Brewbarry’s suggestion of intentional coordination seriously. She identifies as an interventionist, particularly after what happened in Menzoberranzan.

Sridar agrees to pay 30 gold pieces for Valphine’s information and 120 for the Talisman of Moesko, with three bottles of wine arranged for Brewbarry. The party records 150 gold pieces in proceeds, though an explicit physical handover is not recorded. She invites the party to bring her future artifacts, stories, and clues so that she can help piece together the cause of the planar events.

## Items

### Talisman of Moesko

A peculiar inscribed talisman with a definite planar connection. The party had nearly forgotten it was carrying the item before showing it to Sridar. She identifies it as one of several trinkets imbued with planar energy that have recently appeared in the region.

Soma is reluctant to sell without understanding Sridar’s intentions, while Vukradin favors converting the unused talisman into legitimate fair-trade gold. The party agrees to sell it to Sridar for 120 gold pieces, with 30 gold pieces separately agreed for information and three bottles of wine also arranged. They record 150 gold pieces in proceeds; an explicit physical handover is not recorded.

### Boots of Elvenkind

A pair of magical boots offered by Falcon the Hunter as a reward for dealing with the half-orcs at the Woodland Manse. They magically adjust to fit feet of any size. Mindful of Vukradin’s objections, Falcon specifically emphasizes that he purchased them with proceeds from his establishment rather than taking them as plunder.

### Supply Crates

Two heavy crates filled with dried meats, blocks of cheese, loaves of bread, casks of ale, and flasks of oil. Barthen provides an inventory of their contents and entrusts them to the party for delivery to the Loggers’ Camp. Their lack of wine inspires the detour to Falcon’s lodge.

### Two-wheeled Cart

A cart provided by Barthen to transport the supply crates through Neverwinter Wood. Barthen expects it to be returned after the delivery. Concern for the cart’s safety discourages the party from combining the supply run with the Mountain’s Toe escort quest.

### Vincent the Ox

An ox provided by Barthen to pull the cart of supplies. Barthen explicitly tells the party that he expects both Vincent and the cart to be returned safely.

### Barthen’s Letter

A handwritten, signed, and authenticated note from Barthen introducing the party to Falcon the Hunter. Vukradin asks that it explain who they are and “how awesome” they are. Corwin’s attitude changes immediately after reading it, and Falcon enthusiastically welcomes them as friends of Barthen.

### Wine Bottles

Fine wine from Falcon the Hunter’s cellar. Falcon initially offers several bottles in exchange for Vukradin’s musical performance and implies that more will be available if the entertainment is good. Brewbarry demands barrels, prompting Falcon to laugh and predict that they will become good friends, but no barrel quantity is explicitly granted.

Sridar later pays Falcon 30 gold pieces to arrange three bottles specifically for Brewbarry as part of her transaction with the party. Corwin repeatedly brings additional wine during dinner.

### Chain Shirt

Valphine’s old chain shirt from the Underdark, which she considers selling before the party leaves Phandalin. Vukradin accepts that it is not blood money because Valphine assures him it genuinely belonged to her.
