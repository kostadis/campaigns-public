# Chapter 4: Untitled

Date: 2025-06-03

## Summary

The party had been warned by the innkeeper at the Stonehill Inn that orc tribes were descending from the north, and they did not have long to wait. On a steep canyon incline the group spotted the raiders coming down toward them. Ever the idealist, Vukradin strode forward first and opened negotiations in earnest, asking the orcs whether they needed help, whether they were looking for a place to stay, or whether they were hungry — and then, when none of that landed, offering them a choice between his clarinet and his trumpet. The orcs interpreted this peaceful advance as something altogether different. As the DM put it, one warrior announced to his fellows: *“He recognizes our rights to this territory, and is offering himself in sacrifice.”* The first orc swung a greataxe and missed — the DM noting it proved that God loves fools and sinners — before the battle properly erupted.

What followed was a brutal and chaotic skirmish. Soma lashed out with a conjured vine of thorns, dragging one orc tumbling down the twenty-foot incline and leaving it prone. Orc four then charged and caught Vukradin for twelve points of damage, dropping him unconscious. Valphine responded with a powerful Healing Word that restored eleven hit points (a double-high roll on 2d4+3), bringing Vukradin back to his feet, while Brewbarry flew into a thunderous rage and carved through the orcish ranks with his halberd, catching orc two with an attack of opportunity as it advanced and cutting it down. Vukradin, briefly conscious, managed three points of damage against orc one before the orc swung back and knocked him out again; he failed a death save on the ground while combat continued around him. With Valphine reduced to two hit points by an orcish greataxe, the situation turned farcical when Soma hurled a shard of conjured ice — the primary target was missed entirely, the exploding shards struck every creature within five feet, both remaining orcs rolled successfully on their saving throws, and Valphine failed hers, dropping unconscious alongside the enemies she had been fighting. Brewbarry, undeterred, finished the last two orcs with consecutive halberd strikes of nine damage each, ending the fight. As the table summarized it: the net result of Soma’s ice knife was that it killed an ally and did no further damage.

The party stabilized their wounded and searched the fallen orcs, recovering a broken hand axe, javelins, hide armor, and four battle axes — the weapons immediately flagged by the group as probable blood money and tracked accordingly in their shared party ledger, which now sports a dedicated “blood money” column in the party’s ledger. The group made their way back to the dwarven excavation site to recover. Notably, when they arrived, Dazlyn and Norbus had been in hiding, watching to see whether the party was alive or dead — they had been weighing their options about the gem they knew the party was carrying. The dwarves emerged with a telling first line: *“You’re not here for the gem, right?”* Reassured on that point, they broke out a fire pit and cooked a meal. That evening Vukradin performed an impromptu original composition on the flute for his hosts, apparently titled something like “Slaughtered Us.” Dazlyn was unimpressed; Norbus was encouraging; both politely noted they would have preferred one of the popular local ditties.

Rested and recovered, the party set out toward Phandalin and were interrupted mid-journey by cheerful whistling drifting over the hills. The source turned out to be a wandering ogre. When the party called out to ask whether he was a musician or a slave, the ogre’s response was immediate and definitive: *“Me? Musician? Music? No. Me kill you. How dare you?”* Combat followed. The ogre was eventually brought down by a combination of Soma’s poison spray, Vukradin’s Starry Wisp, Valphine’s Sacred Flame, and Brewbarry’s halberd — including a critical hit that alone took twenty-three points off the creature’s health. Among the ogre’s belongings the party discovered a pouch of walloping ammunition, enchanted to knock its targets prone, which would prove relevant almost immediately.

Arriving in Phandalin, the party went directly to the Townmaster’s Hall to collect their reward. They were greeted by Harbin Wester’s voice from behind a firmly locked door: *“If you’re a dragon, know that I’m far too thin and bony to make a good meal.”* Vukradin rolled a twenty on persuasion, and Harbin — recognizing the bard from his Stonehill Inn performance — opened the door and counted out fifty gold pieces. The atmosphere soured when Vukradin described the dwarves at the excavation as something like grave robbers. Harbin cited Lord Neverember’s authority: adventurers were permitted to seize whatever treasure they wished. Vukradin’s response was simply, *“Well, that doesn’t seem right at all.”* Valphine quietly read the room and picked up that Harbin was not merely annoyed — he was anxious, unsettled in a way that went beyond distaste for Vukradin’s morality. Harbin then offered two new quests: rock gnomes at Gnomengarde and something involving a midwife. The gnomes paid better; the party voted for gnomes.

The group made the rounds of Phandalin’s merchants. At the smithy they offloaded the damaged orcish battle axes at half price. At the Lionshield Coster, Linene greeted them with *“Oh, you’re still alive,”* bought the salvaged hide armor at full value, and explained her supply disruptions in terms of tariffs, supply chains, and market force dynamics — with a faint air of someone who did not entirely understand the cause herself. She shared a rumor that strange magic had begun to pervade Neverwinter Wood, confounding navigators and obscuring the ancient ruins of bygone kingdoms. The party also noted, during their shopping, that Valphine — a drow without a hand crossbow — was not quite a full drow in anyone’s estimation; she accordingly acquired one before leaving town. The evening ended at the Stonehill Inn, where Toblen Stonehill greeted the returning heroes with *“Sit, sit, sit, sit, sit, sit, sit. It’s so wonderful to see you,”* and asked Vukradin to play. The bard chose the clarinet, performed one of his own compositions, and rolled unremarkably well. Attempts to gather rumors from the patrons produced nothing useful.

The road to Gnomengarde wound through a narrow mountain pass, and there the party’s luck ran out again. A second ogre blocked their path, announcing with great conviction: *“I hungry. I eat you.”* When questioned about food, it specified: *“Turtle. I eat turtle”* — fixing its attention on Soma, the Tortle. The party offered rations; the ogre was not interested in rations. Intimidation attempts from Soma, Vukradin, and Valphine all failed to register. When Vukradin announced that they had already slain one of its kind, the ogre did not pause — it announced *“You killed my brother and sister”* and entered combat in a fury. Soma opened with poison spray. Valphine loaded her new hand crossbow with the recovered walloping ammunition; the ogre failed its strength save and crashed prone. Vukradin’s Command spell froze the creature in place, leaving it unable to move or act. Brewbarry raged, moved in, and hammered the incapacitated giant for fourteen damage, then Soma landed a final Poison Spray for four more. The table ran the ogre’s skipped turn and cleared the halt before stopping, leaving it prone but no longer halted, and badly damaged.

## Scenes

### A Clash of Ideals and Orcs
#### The party encounters a group of orcs on an incline, leading to a disastrous attempt at diplomacy followed by a brutal skirmish.

- The party had been told by Toblen Stonehill at the Stonehill Inn that orc tribes were coming down from the north — a detail that comes back to Vukradin too late when he finds himself surrounded.
- Vukradin goes first in initiative and chooses to advance and parlay, asking the orcs if they need help, shelter, food, or want to hear music; he offers both clarinet and trumpet as options.
- The DM voices the orc response: *“He recognizes our rights to this territory, and is offering himself in sacrifice”* — the warriors interpret his approach as a sacrificial gesture.
- Orc one swings a greataxe at point-blank range and misses; the DM notes this proves “God loves fools and sinners.”
- Soma retaliates by using Thorn Whip on orc two, dragging the warrior down the twenty-foot incline; the orc takes 1d6 fall damage (six points total) and lands prone at the base of the slope.
- Orc four closes on Vukradin and connects for twelve points of damage, dropping him unconscious on his first serious blow of the session.
- Vukradin fails a death save on the ground.
- Valphine responds with Healing Word, restoring eleven hit points (2d4+3, rolling exceptionally high) and bringing Vukradin back to his feet, then directs Sacred Flame at orc four — the orc makes its save, taking only one point of damage.
- Orc two, now prone, stands and charges Brewbarry; Brewbarry catches it with an attack of opportunity (18 to hit), kills it outright on the free strike.
- Brewbarry attacks with the halberd, benefits from the prone condition on a separate target to reroll a miss, and cleaves through orcish armor; he is raging but initially forgets to add the +2 rage damage to his rolls.
- Orc three hurls a javelin at Brewbarry, taunts him with insults about his heritage and the circumstances of his birth; Brewbarry shrugs off the hit through sheer barbarian resilience (damage halved by rage).
- Vukradin, back on nine hit points, attacks orc one for three damage but misses with his second strike.
- Orc one hits back and knocks Vukradin unconscious a second time.
- Soma uses Poison Spray to kill orc one, removing the immediate threat to the downed bard.
- Orc four attacks and misses Brewbarry entirely.
- Orc three swings its greataxe at Valphine and connects for eight points, dropping her to two hit points.
- Valphine, at two hit points, attempts to press the attack.
- Soma attempts to finish the remaining orcs with Ice Knife — the projectile misses its primary target; the exploding shard cloud catches every creature within five feet; both remaining orcs succeed on their dexterity saves; Valphine at two hit points fails hers and is knocked unconscious. The attack deals zero net damage to enemies.
- Brewbarry delivers back-to-back halberd strikes of nine and nine damage, killing orcs three and four and ending the fight.
- Valphine succeeds on her first death save; Vukradin fails a second after his own knockdown, and both are stabilized once combat ends.
- The group loots the fallen orcs: a broken hand axe, a javelin, hide armor (in fine condition), and four battle axes. Vukradin flags the weapons as probable blood money — likely stolen from prior victims — and they are tracked in the party’s shared ledger under a dedicated “blood money” column in the party’s ledger. Vukradin declines a share in blood-money proceeds; Brewbarry is not similarly constrained.

### Return to the Excavation Site
#### The party returns to the dwarven excavation to rest and interact with the miners they previously assisted.

- When the party arrives back at the excavation, Dazlyn and Norbus are not immediately visible — they had been hiding, watching to see whether the group survived the orcs and quietly weighing whether to flee or attempt to take the gem the party was carrying.
- Seeing the party alive, both dwarves emerge cautiously. Dazlyn’s first words: *“You’re not here for the gem, right?”*
- Reassured on that point, the dwarves welcome the group and move to a fire pit where they cook a pleasant meal.
- Vukradin plays flute for the assembled group — an impromptu original composition apparently centered on the phrase “The orc slaughtered us, slaughtered us” — a mournful piece about the day’s events.
- Dazlyn is unimpressed by Vukradin’s original composition; Norbus is more encouraging, keeping him at it.
- Both dwarves express the same mild disappointment: they would have preferred one of the more popular local ditties over a personal original work.
- The party takes their long rest here before heading back to Phandalin in the morning.

### The Whistling Ogre
#### While traveling through hilly terrain toward Phandalin, the party encounters a lone ogre with an unexpected habit.

- The party hears an ogre whistling cheerfully as it approaches through the hills; the sound drifts over the terrain before the creature comes into view.
- Vukradin attempts to harmonize with the creature but rolls only a 10 on performance; the attempt does not land as intended.
- A debate breaks out within the party: Valphine, familiar with Underdark ogres used as slave labor, wonders whether this might be a surface escapee from a slave pit; Gary rolls 11 on insight but the DM rules that is not enough to distinguish a surface ogre from an Underdark ogre by appearance alone.
- Vukradin calls out asking whether the ogre is a musician or a slave; the ogre’s reaction is immediate and violent: *“Me? Musician? Music? No. Me kill you. How dare you?”*
- The party rolls initiative; Vukradin rolls a 21 and takes the top of the order, but waves Valphine ahead of him — *“All right, Valphine, you’re up.”*
- Valphine attacks first with Sacred Flame — the ogre makes its save, taking four points of damage; she retreats to let Brewbarry anchor the front.
- Vukradin strikes with Starry Wisp for eight radiant damage from 60 feet back, staying out of melee range.
- Brewbarry rages and charges in, missing his first swing. When the ogre then leaves a square he threatens, his opportunity attack crits — nineteen points of base damage plus four, taking the creature from forty-seven down to twenty-four.
- The ogre answers with an ordinary hit for ten, halved to five by rage resistance.
- Soma uses Poison Spray from range for seven damage — an attack roll rather than a save, and no secondary condition.
- Soma considers using Mold Earth to loosen the hillside terrain and trigger a rockslide onto the ogre; the DM rules this would deal 1d6 damage but the logistics of measuring it prompt Soma to abandon the idea: *“Screw being creative.”*
- The ogre is killed by a combination of Brewbarry’s halberd, Soma’s poison, Vukradin’s radiant blasts, and Valphine’s sacred fire before it can seriously threaten the party.
- Searching the ogre’s belongings, the party recovers walloping ammunition — a specialized projectile enchanted so that any creature struck by it must make a saving throw or be knocked prone. The ogre, the DM notes, preferred walloping people personally and never figured out how to use it.

### Return to Phandalin
#### The party returns to town to collect their reward, sell loot, gather information, and equip themselves for the next mission.

- The party arrives at the Townmaster’s Hall and hears Harbin Wester’s voice through the firmly locked door: *“If you’re a dragon, know that I’m far too thin and bony to make a good meal.”*
- When the party points out that dragons typically do not knock, Harbin remains unmoved, suggesting they could be a trick; he refuses to open the door until Vukradin rolls a 20 on persuasion.
- Harbin recognizes Vukradin as “that bard that played in the Stonehill Inn” — this is what finally opens the door.
- The fifty gold pieces are paid without incident; the mood then sours when Vukradin characterizes the dwarves at the excavation as essentially grave robbers: *“they were, you know, digging in a ruin for artifacts, and I don’t think they were intended to put all those artifacts in a museum.”*
- Harbin defends the practice by citing Lord Neverember’s authority: *“his Lordship, Lord Neverember, said that adventurers are allowed to seize and control whatever treasure they wish.”* Vukradin’s response: *“Well, that doesn’t seem right at all.”*
- Valphine rolls insight and reads that Harbin is not merely annoyed by Vukradin’s moralizing — he is anxious, unsettled in a way that goes beyond ordinary impatience with an idealistic bard.
- Harbin offers two new quests: rock gnomes at Gnomengarde and another involving a midwife. Vukradin confirms the gnomes pay better (fifty gold), and the party votes for Gnomengarde.
- At the smithy, the damaged orcish battle axes are sold at half price, as they are damaged goods.
- At the Lionshield Coster, Linene greets the party with *“Oh, you’re still alive”* — and buys the salvaged hide armor at full value (ten gold). She explains her disrupted trade routes in terms of supply chains, tariffs, and market force dynamics, with a slightly evasive air, as though the underlying cause is not something she can name precisely.
- Linene shares a rumor she has heard: *“Strange magic pervades Neverwinter Wood, confounding navigators and obscuring the ancient ruins of bygone kingdoms lost in its depths.”* The DM pastes the full text into the chat for the party’s records.
- Linene asks whether the party will be staying at the Stonehill Inn; the party confirms they will.
- During the shopping visit, Gary notes that Valphine — as a drow — really ought to have a hand crossbow; the DM agrees that a drow without one is *“less of a drow.”* Valphine acquires a hand crossbow in Phandalin before the party moves on.
- At the Stonehill Inn, Toblen Stonehill’s welcome is effusive: *“Sit, sit, sit, sit, sit, sit, sit. It’s so wonderful to see you.”* He recalls Vukradin’s previous performance warmly and requests a repeat — the clarinet specifically.
- Vukradin plays one of his own compositions on the clarinet; the roll is average. Neither the roll nor the original material impresses particularly.
- Valphine attempts to read the mood of the assembled patrons via an insight check; the roll is similarly unimpressive, yielding nothing actionable.
- The party tries to gather new rumors from the inn’s patrons but fails to uncover any further leads beyond what Linene has already shared.
- The party pays one gold for the night’s lodging and beds down, new quests in hand.

### Ambush in the Mountain Pass
#### On the road to Gnomengarde, the party is confronted by a hungry ogre blocking a mountain pass.

- The route to Gnomengarde is fifteen miles through hilly terrain; the DM establishes the encounter in a narrow valley where the terrain counts as difficult for movement between elevation levels.
- A hulking ogre blocks the path and announces without preamble: *“I hungry. I eat you.”*
- When the party offers rations and attempts to negotiate, the ogre specifies its preference: *“Turtle. I eat turtle”* — fixing its attention on Soma, the Tortle. Wade’s character is visibly displeased: *“Excuse me.”*
- Vukradin attempts to defuse the situation by offering food and asking the ogre to simply stand aside: *“Can we just give you some food and you, you can stand over there? ’Cause we’re just walking past you. Can we do that?”*
- The ogre is not interested in a negotiated solution. Attempts at intimidation from Soma, Vukradin, and Valphine all fail; the DM notes that the ogre is if anything encouraged by the party’s failed attempts, viewing them as evidence of an easy victory.
- Vukradin, growing frustrated, announces that the party killed another ogre just recently. Rather than giving the creature pause, this prompts the DM’s best line of the encounter: *“You killed my brother and sister.”* The ogre enters combat in a fury.
- Initiative is rolled; Soma goes first. The marching order is hurriedly corrected — Vukradin had been at the front again; he moves to the back. Brewbarry takes the vanguard.
- Soma opens with Poison Spray for eight damage; the cantrip lands on an attack roll and carries no secondary condition.
- The ogre swings its greataxe and rolls a natural one — misses entirely, described as cutting off its own toe.
- Valphine, using her new hand crossbow, loads the walloping ammunition recovered from the previous ogre and fires; the ogre must make a DC 10 strength save (it has +4 to strength saves) but fails, crashing prone.
- Vukradin uses Command (DC 13 wisdom save); the ogre fails and is halted — unable to move or take any actions until the end of its next turn.
- With the ogre prone and halted, Brewbarry rages and moves in, landing a halberd strike for fourteen damage (twelve points plus the +2 rage bonus).
- Soma delivers a second Poison Spray for four more damage before the DM calls time at 10:30.
- The ogre’s skipped turn is played out and the halt condition removed before the table stops. The session ends with the ogre alive, prone, no longer halted, and badly damaged; Valphine has the next action when play resumes.

## Locations

### Canyon Incline
A steep, hilly slope with a twenty-foot drop where the party was ambushed by orcs descending from the north. The elevation made Soma’s Thorn Whip especially effective, dragging orc two down the full drop and leaving it prone at the base.

### Dwarven Excavation Site
A ruin site where dwarves Dazlyn and Norbus are camped. When the party returned after the orc skirmish, the dwarves were initially in hiding, watching to see whether the group had survived — and considering their options with the gem. Once the party’s survival was confirmed and the gem question was settled, Dazlyn and Norbus offered hospitality: a fire pit, a cooked meal, and an audience for Vukradin’s flute performance.

### Phandalin
A frontier town where the party returns to collect rewards, sell loot, and pick up new quests. Home to the Townmaster’s Hall, the Lionshield Coster, and the Stonehill Inn. Valphine acquired her hand crossbow here.

### Townmaster’s Hall
The administrative building in Phandalin where Harbin Wester hides behind a sturdy locked door, shouting at visitors he suspects might be the dragon. He opened the door only after recognizing Vukradin from a previous performance at the Stonehill Inn. The meeting here produced fifty gold pieces and two new quest assignments, but ended on a note of visible anxiety on Harbin’s part — unsettled by Vukradin’s moral challenges to the adventuring economy.

### Lionshield Coster
A merchant post in Phandalin run by Linene, currently suffering from supply chain disruptions she describes in terms of tariffs and market force dynamics without fully explaining the underlying cause. The party sold recovered hide armor here for ten gold. Linene shared a rumor about strange magic pervading Neverwinter Wood.

### Stonehill Inn
A local tavern and inn in Phandalin run by Toblen Stonehill, who greeted the party’s return with remarkable enthusiasm. Vukradin performed on the clarinet for the assembled patrons; the party attempted to gather rumors from the crowd but rolled too poorly to learn anything new. One gold for the night’s lodging.

### Neverwinter Wood
A nearby forest rumored to be filled with confounding magic that obscures ancient ruins of bygone kingdoms, mentioned by Linene at the Lionshield Coster. The full text of the rumor was pasted into the session chat: *“Strange magic pervades Neverwinter Wood, confounding navigators and obscuring the ancient ruins of bygone kingdoms lost in its depths.”*

### Mountain Pass (Road to Gnomengarde)
A narrow, hilly valley fifteen miles from Phandalin on the route to Gnomengarde. The terrain is difficult — moving between elevation levels costs double movement. It was here the party encountered the second ogre. The DM established that the fight takes place in a natural valley with rocky slopes on either side, which inspired Soma’s brief consideration of a Mold Earth rockslide before defaulting to Poison Spray.

## NPCs

### Orcs
A group of aggressive tribal warriors descending from the north, as the Stonehill Inn innkeeper had warned. They interpreted Vukradin’s peaceful advance as a sacrificial offering and attacked without hesitation. They fight with greataxes and javelins. Four were present; all four were killed in the skirmish.

### Dazlyn
A dwarf miner camped at the excavation site. When the party returned, Dazlyn was the first to speak — leading with *“You’re not here for the gem, right?”* — revealing that the dwarves had been watching the fight’s aftermath and weighing their options. Unimpressed by Vukradin’s original musical compositions; they would have preferred a popular local tune.

### Norbus
A dwarf miner camped at the excavation site. More encouraging than Dazlyn about Vukradin’s musical efforts, telling him to keep at it. Like Dazlyn, he would ultimately have preferred a popular local ditty to an original composition about being nearly slaughtered by orcs.

### Whistling Ogre
A wandering ogre encountered in the hills between the excavation and Phandalin, cheerfully whistling as it walked. Harbored a violent contempt for music and art when directly confronted about it: *“Me? Musician? Music? No. Me kill you. How dare you?”* Was carrying a pouch of walloping ammunition it did not know how to use, preferring to wallop people directly. Killed in combat.

### Harbin Wester
The cowardly and paranoid Townmaster of Phandalin. Refuses to open his door for fear of the dragon, shouting: *“If you’re a dragon, know that I’m far too thin and bony to make a good meal.”* He recognized Vukradin from a prior Stonehill Inn performance and opened the door on that basis. Paid the fifty-gold reward, cited Lord Neverember’s authority for the adventuring economy when challenged, and was visibly anxious — more than merely annoyed — by Vukradin’s moral objections. Offered two new quests: Gnomengarde (gnomes, fifty gold) and a quest involving a midwife.

### Linene
The master of the Lionshield Coster’s Phandalin post — an outpost of the Yartar-based Lionshields, which she runs rather than owns. Greeted the party’s return with *“Oh, you’re still alive.”* Bought the salvaged hide armor at full value. Shared that her trade routes have been disrupted — she describes it in terms of supply chains, tariffs, and market force dynamics, without being able to fully articulate the root cause. Also shared a rumor about strange confounding magic in Neverwinter Wood.

### Toblen Stonehill
The friendly tavern keeper of the Stonehill Inn. Welcomed the party back with *“Sit, sit, sit, sit, sit, sit, sit. It’s so wonderful to see you.”* Remembered Vukradin’s previous performance warmly and specifically requested the clarinet. Could not provide any rumors himself but directed the party toward his patrons as sources.

### Hungry Ogre
A dim-witted and aggressive ogre blocking the mountain pass on the road to Gnomengarde. His opening move is *“I hungry. I eat you.”* He specifies his preferred meal as turtle (targeting Soma, the Tortle). When Vukradin boasted about killing an ogre the previous day, the creature announced *“You killed my brother and sister”* and entered a fury rather than retreating. Still alive at session end — prone, with the Command halt already expired, and facing Valphine’s action when play resumes.

## Items

### Hide Armor
Armor looted from the orcs after the skirmish, found to be in fine condition despite the combat. Sold at the Lionshield Coster for ten gold pieces (full value, since it was undamaged).

### Walloping Ammunition
Specialized ammunition recovered from the whistling ogre’s body — the ogre was carrying it but had not used it, preferring direct violence. A creature struck by it must succeed on a DC 10 Strength save or be knocked prone (the GM ran it as Strength; the module calls for Constitution). Used immediately and effectively in the mountain pass fight: Valphine’s shot with the new hand crossbow knocked the second ogre flat despite its +4 strength bonus, setting up the Command halt and Brewbarry’s full-damage press.

### Hand Crossbow
A compact ranged weapon acquired by Valphine in Phandalin — Gary notes that a drow without one is *“less of a drow”* and the DM agrees. Used to deliver the walloping ammunition against the mountain pass ogre in the session’s final combat, knocking the creature prone.

### Clarinet
A reeded woodwind instrument carried by Vukradin. He offered it — along with the trumpet — to the orcs as a peace gesture before combat. Played for the Stonehill Inn patrons at Toblen’s specific request. Performance rolls have been consistently middling this session.

### Flute
Vukradin’s other woodwind instrument, played for the campfire performance at the dwarven excavation site.

## Spells

### Thorn Whip
A magical vine covered in thorns conjured by Soma to snag orc two and drag it down the twenty-foot canyon incline. The orc took 1d6 (six points) of thorn damage plus 1d6 fall damage (adjudicated as a compromise between full fall and no fall), landing prone at the bottom of the slope. Soma also attempted to use it to reposition the whistling ogre mid-combat, pulling it away from Brewbarry to trigger a free opportunity attack — the whip missed on that attempt.

### Healing Word
A divine restorative spoken by Valphine to bring Vukradin back from zero hit points mid-battle during the orc skirmish. The roll was exceptional — 2d4+3 yielding eleven hit points. It was the only reason Vukradin survived long enough to be knocked unconscious a second time.

### Sacred Flame
Divine radiance called down by Valphine to burn orcish attackers during the canyon skirmish. Orc four made its save (taking only one point of damage); used again twice against the whistling ogre, for four points and then five, taking it to three hit points.

### Ice Knife
A shard of ice hurled by Soma during the orc fight. The primary target was missed entirely. The resulting cold-burst explosion (seven points of cold damage) hit every creature within five feet; both remaining orcs succeeded on their dexterity saves; Valphine — at two hit points — failed and was knocked unconscious. Net damage to enemies: zero.

### Poison Spray
A puff of noxious gas projected by Soma. Used to kill the wounded orc one after Vukradin went down a second time. Used repeatedly against both ogres — delivering consistent 1d12 damage with no saving throw involved, making it reliable when rolls cooperate. The cantrip’s lack of a secondary condition is a noted limitation, but its damage ceiling makes it Soma’s most dependable offensive tool this session.

### Starry Wisp
A luminous magical projectile launched by Vukradin against the whistling ogre. Struck for eight radiant damage on a hit (range 60 feet), allowing Vukradin to contribute from a safe distance. Roll variance was unkind later in the fight — a subsequent cast dealt only three radiant damage.

### Mold Earth
Considered by Soma during the mountain pass ogre fight. The intent was to loosen the rocky slopes and cause a directed rockslide. The DM was willing to adjudicate this as 1d6 damage, but the logistics of measuring the effect and the need for Brewbarry to access the ogre freely prompted Soma to abandon the idea. *“Screw being creative.”* The cantrip was not cast.

### Command
Cast by Vukradin during the mountain pass ogre fight. The ogre failed a DC 13 wisdom save and was halted — unable to move or take any actions until the end of its next turn. Combined with the prone condition from the walloping ammunition, this left the ogre completely defenseless for Brewbarry’s raging follow-up strike of fourteen damage. The halt cost the ogre its next turn, which the table played out as a skipped turn before stopping; the creature is still prone when the session ends.
