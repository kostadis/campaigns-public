# Chapter 41 Maerimydra: City of Fire and Lies

Date: Feb 8th, 2026

## Summary

The path forward, on the Path of Shadows, lay down the storeroom well. Before descending, the party searched the room and discovered a chest beneath a ragged blanket. It contained a potion of flying, a potion of fire giant strength, and coral gemstones worth 400 gold pieces, which the DM instructed them to divide as 100 gold pieces each.

Concerned that reinforcements might deduce their route, the party discussed hiding their tracks. Disposing of every corpse throughout the outpost was impractical, so Daein proposed arranging the bodies to appear alive, inspired by *Weekend at Bernie’s*. Rather than spend a preparation token to guarantee success, he attempted the deception with Felkur’s assistance and achieved a 27. The DM offered the alternative of staging a quarrel between the giant and ogres, but the party chose to make everyone appear alive and resting.

The party descended into a dark pool of warm, murky water, about five feet deep and fed by an underground stream connected to the Lake of Shadows. Bramgrim and Felkur, too short to stand comfortably, rode on the Owlbear. A five-foot-wide opening at the bottom of the pool led into a submerged passage. After taking deep breaths and swimming about forty feet, they emerged into a large cave used as Solom Ned’razak’s private office.

Finding no creatures nearby, they lit a lamp to read the papers. Solom’s notes described his efforts to undermine Lolth’s clerics and his theories about the Blink, an event in which the Underdark’s faerzress went dark, blazed brightly, went dark again, and returned to normal. Felkur’s Arcana check of 22 established that faerzress provides illumination, blocks teleportation, and has an uncertain origin. Demons began appearing after the disturbance.

Solom blamed powerful wizards and liches from Menzoberranzan, complained that he had not known such a feat was possible, and insisted that he would have executed it better himself. His attempts to reach an accommodation with the demons had failed because they preferred chaos and destruction. His notes also recorded that Lolth’s clerics regained contact with their goddess after the Blink. The DM separately explained the connection to an earlier adventure: Lolth had used the Blink to escape imprisonment and was now rebuilding her power, organizing her clerics, and punishing those who had abandoned her.

The final approach to Maerimydra grew warmer over the last half mile. After clearing rubble from the tunnel mouth, the party emerged among sheltered northern ruins near the Shattered Tower. Beyond them sprawled a city of fire and ash within a cavern six hundred feet high, with smoke obscuring everything above 250 feet. Braziers kept the city in perpetual dim light. One-hundred-foot-high plateaus and broad, exposed stairs complicated movement between districts.

Maerimydra was controlled by demonic forces aligned with Graz’zt. Fire giants, armored ogres, hellhounds, and demons occupied its streets, while drow wore chains. The western courtyard displayed Graz’zt’s six-fingered hand, the eastern castle housed Queen Hledh, and a massive Coliseum dominated the southern residential district. The DM emphasized the party’s two objectives—obtain a fiendish giant and steal the book—and warned that attempting to fight the entire city would be disastrous.

Shortly after entering, the party encountered four ogres tormenting an imp, swinging him by his barbed tail while he clung to a bloodstained satchel. They initially hesitated, fearing that another group of bodies might reveal their presence. The imp promised detailed knowledge of the city, and the threat of his imminent death persuaded them to intervene. The DM also established that the party had taken a long rest before this encounter.

Daein and Akritas brought down the first ogre, allowing the imp to escape behind rocks. Felkur then caught the three surviving ogres in a Fireball without hitting the party or their rescue target. While their companions fought, Bramgrim insisted on settling the imp’s obligations immediately, spending his turn negotiating. The remaining ogres fell to Daein, Akritas, the Owlbear, and Felkur’s final Firebolt.

The imp introduced himself as Pipyap. His first written contract would have made the party work for him, so they rejected it and moved into nearby rubble to negotiate. He explained that a summoner’s mistake had brought him to the Material Plane, where contractual loopholes allowed him to remain free of his infernal employer. His former mortal master’s contract had forbidden Pipyap from killing him but, under certain circumstances, had not required him to provide a warning.

Drawing on their prior experience with an ultroloth, Felkur and Bramgrim secured a favorable agreement. The party specified confidentiality, obedience, and prohibitions against harming or stealing from them. Pipyap repeatedly interrupted his explanations to beg for food, offered Daein a possible soul sale and warlock connection, and eventually received provisions—only to complain that they needed salt.

Pipyap identified two fiendish giants: Veperyd, who ran the Coliseum and its betting pool, and another who managed the Six-Fingered Courtyard. He also revealed that a visitor from Mulmaster was attending dinner with Vraxen at the Spire of Inevitable Reckoning. This mattered immediately because Mulmaster was associated with the search for Felkur, and Vraxen’s tower contained both the desired book and the escape teleportation circle.

Veperyd had been stealing betting money from Vraxen. Pipyap suggested exploiting that theft to turn them against each other. The party chose to capture the courtyard’s fiendish giant first, enter the spire, provoke the dispute, steal the book, and escape through the circle to Sporedome. They discussed a hole, breathing apparatus, and a ten-minute transport constraint while considering how to move their captive.

The dinner was that night, leaving no opportunity for another long rest. Bramgrim’s recollection that Hillsfar had been controlled by Graz’zt suggested a merchant cover for entering Vraxen’s residence; posing as staff remained another possibility. Neither approach was finalized. Daein and the Owlbear were to use Second Wind to recover from the rescue fight, though no healing totals were recorded. The session ended with the operation planned and the courtyard giant established as the first target.

The southeastern room where ogres hauled water under a fire giant’s supervision. Its well provided access to the Path of Shadows. A chest concealed beneath a ragged blanket held two potions and coral gemstones. Before leaving, the party arranged the dead giant and ogres to appear alive.

### Well (Black Ember Outpost)

The storeroom well leading down to the Path of Shadows. The party concealed their use of it by staging the surrounding bodies. Below lay an unlit pool of warm, murky water.

### Path of Shadows

A route connected to the Lake of Shadows by an underground stream. Its initial pool was about five feet deep, with a five-foot-wide submerged tunnel opening in the southern area. A forty-foot swim led to Solom Ned’razak’s office cave, followed by an onward tunnel to Maerimydra. The party intended to leave the city by a different route.

### Solom Ned’razak’s Office

A private work area within a large cave along the Path of Shadows. A lamp and papers remained, with no creatures present when the party arrived. The writings concerned the Blink, faerzress, demons, and Solom’s opposition to Lolth’s clerics.

### Maerimydra

A former drow metropolis controlled by demonic forces aligned with Graz’zt. It occupies a cavern 600 feet high, with smoke obscuring everything above 250 feet. Braziers maintain perpetual dim light and a heavy charcoal smell.

Its districts are divided by 100-foot-high plateaus with exposed, 100-foot-wide stairs. Huge central buildings accommodate fire giants. Fire giants, ogres, hellhounds, demons, and chained drow fill the city. The party entered among sheltered northern rocks and ruins near the Shattered Tower; a lower southeastern district remained beyond their view.

### Six-Fingered Courtyard

A courtyard on Maerimydra’s western plateau, surrounded by black spires. A six-fingered hand above an ebon archway identifies its connection to Graz’zt. Demons and giants gather here for worship, and an unnamed fiendish giant manages it. The party selected this giant as their capture target.

### Coliseum

A massive arena dominating the southern residential district. It hosts frequent gladiatorial fights and a betting pool managed by Veperyd. His theft from the betting arrangement with Vraxen offers a possible source of conflict.

### Eastern Castle

A castle on Maerimydra’s eastern plateau, with two towers stretching from the floor to the cavern ceiling. Queen Hledh resides here. Pipyap strongly advised avoiding it.

### The Spire of Inevitable Reckoning

Vraxen’s residence and the location of the desired book, the evening dinner, and a teleportation circle leading to Sporedome. A visitor from Mulmaster was expected at the dinner. The party discussed entering as Hillsfar merchants or staff, but had not selected a final cover.

## NPCs

### Owlbear

A loyal companion who fought at the outpost and during Pipyap’s rescue. Carried Bramgrim and Felkur through the five-foot-deep pool.

During the rescue, the Owlbear dealt 18 damage to Ogre #4 and 35 to Ogre #1, while taking 13 damage from Ogre #3. The party proposed Second Wind for recovery afterward; no healing total was recorded.

### Solom Ned’razak

A male drow wizard whose office and papers the party discovered along the Path of Shadows. He opposed Lolth’s female clerics, resenting their demands for male subservience and their distrust of magic users.

His notes connected the Blink to the arrival of demons and blamed powerful wizards and liches from Menzoberranzan. ### Pipyap

An imp devil with a barbed tail and a bloodstained, button-fastened satchel. Rescued from four ogres, he became the party’s contracted guide and informant.

Pipyap prefers the Material Plane to the Nine Hells. His infernal employment contract omitted service on the Material Plane, allowing him to avoid that obligation while remaining there. His former summoner’s contract prohibited murder but contained exceptions to his duty to warn; that summoner is dead.

He initially offered a contract that would have made the party serve him. The negotiated replacement required assistance, obedience, confidentiality, and protection against harm and theft. Talkative, hungry, and opportunistic, he interrupted strategic advice with requests for food, criticism of its seasoning, and offers involving soul sales and warlock patronage.

### Hledh

The huge fire giant queen ruling Maerimydra from its eastern castle. Pipyap recommended staying well away from her.

### Veperyd

A fiendish giant who runs the Coliseum and manages its betting pool. He shares a money-making arrangement with Vraxen but has been stealing from him. Pipyap described him as an idiot and suggested exposing the theft to provoke a fight.

### Vraxen

A Yugoloth living in the Spire of Inevitable Reckoning. He possesses the book sought by Maram il Vass, works for Graz’zt, and has a betting arrangement with Veperyd. His tower also houses the escape teleportation circle.

A dinner at his residence that night would include a visitor from Mulmaster. The party planned to exploit Veperyd’s theft to distract him while stealing the book.

### Visiting Mulmaster Wizard

A wizard from Mulmaster attending dinner at Vraxen’s residence. Mulmaster’s connection to the search for Felkur made the visit personally significant.

The party feared that the visitor might acquire the book at dinner, establishing their deadline. His name and the exact terms of any intended transaction were not revealed.

### Graz’zt

The demon lord controlling Maerimydra, who had previously controlled Hillsfar. His six-fingered hand appears prominently at the western courtyard.

He made a deal to imbue giants with fiendish blood and brought in Vraxen to ensure his plans were carried out. His shared connection to Hillsfar, Maerimydra, and Vraxen suggested a possible merchant cover for the party.

### Lolth

The drow goddess whose clerics had been unable to commune with her. Their communication returned after the Blink.

The DM explained that Lolth used the event to escape an earlier imprisonment. She is now rebuilding her power, organizing her clerics, and punishing those who abandoned their faith, prioritizing those tasks over revenge on the adventurers who imprisoned her.

The party intended to recover it before the visiting Mulmaster wizard could acquire it at the evening dinner.

### Pipyap’s Satchel

A bloodstained satchel secured by an oversized button. Pipyap struggled to hold onto it while the ogres swung him by his tail. Its contents were not revealed.

## Spells

### Mage Hand

Used by Felkur during the recalled outpost assault to remove the ogres’ ill-fitting uniforms, distracting them and the fire giant sergeant.

### Fireball

Used by Felkur during the previous session’s outpost assault and this session’s Pipyap rescue.

In Maerimydra, Felkur waited until the imp had fled behind the party before catching all three surviving ogres without hitting allies. Each failed its save and took 28 fire damage. The DM confirmed that these ogres had neither fire immunity nor resistance.

### Auditory Illusions

Used by Daein during the recalled outpost assault to supply the voice of an illusory female hellhound and later impersonate the victorious fire giant.

The false report preserved surprise and included a vain remark about the beauty of the human prisoner.

### Spirit Guardians

Used by Bramgrim during the recalled outpost assault. In the storeroom, he cast it at fifth level but lost concentration after being struck by an ogre.

### Silence

Cast by Bramgrim around the outpost well during the previous session to prevent alarms during the storeroom fight.

### Mass Healing Word

Cast by Bramgrim during the recalled storeroom battle. After Daein’s offensive display, Bramgrim also used Toll the Dead for 27 necrotic damage.

### Hunter’s Mark

Used by Akritas to enhance bow attacks against ogres, contributing to their swift defeat in the previous session’s outpost encounter and this session’s Maerimydra encounter.

During Pipyap’s rescue, she marked Ogre #2, transferred the mark to Ogre #3, and then marked Ogre #1. Favored Foe was also invoked during her attack calculations.

### Protective Field

Used by Daein during Pipyap’s rescue to reduce Ogre #4’s 13-damage hit by 7, leaving 6 damage. The reduction was rolled as 1d8 plus 1.

### Firebolt

Used by Felkur to kill Ogre #1, the final surviving ogre, after the Owlbear left it with 5 hit points.

### Second Wind

Proposed for Daein and the Owlbear to recover hit points after Pipyap’s rescue and before the next stage of the mission. The DM accepted the proposal, but no healing total was recorded.
