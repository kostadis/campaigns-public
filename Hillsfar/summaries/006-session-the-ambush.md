# Chapter 6: The ambush

Date: Greengrass – 4 Mirtul 1494 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-16); written 2022-12-17 (Drive createdTime; play date not stated)

## Summary

The chapter opens on Felkur. Fleeing from his guild, he had suffered a series of misfortunes until, by chance, he fell in with a group of heroes: the barbarian Falofa, the warrior Daein, and the cleric Bramgrim. Together they had gone to the aid of local farmers and met strange phenomena along the way, shadows that seemed to move on their own and trickery of light. His original plan had been to go to Hillsfar and establish his own little artificer shop, but the city had gone mad. The First Lord, Torin Nomerthal, had re-introduced the two great laws of Hillsfar: Trade ("Do not interfere with legitimate trade") and Humanity ("Only humans are allowed within Hillsfar"). Felkur agreed with the first; the second was appalling. The Red Plumes had gone mad with the city, seizing non-humans from the surrounding area and handing them over to the arena to die horrible deaths. He had barely avoided dying, and had a bad meal: "A bad meal, imagine that. My last meal was going to be bad," he grumbled. A few days with Olisara Lightsong and the Tinfellows filled his belly with good food.

As he sat in the sun, the patriarch asked a favor: "The Tinfellow Valley needs a militia." He asked them to go south to the town of Elventree, where rumor had it that those who fled the ravages of the Red Plumes had established a military presence, and to "Find me some folks who will come help us."

The answer was "Okay." The cramped quarters of the halflings were hard on Daein, a man of 1m90+, and he wanted something to do, not least so that the widow, Vigdas, would not start matchmaking him with any of the eligible brides she knew. Elbus Tinfellow, the Harper agent, patriarch of the Tinfellow clan and friend of Olisara Lightsong, told them not to use the main road, since the Red Plumes might still be on the prowl: they were to cut north above the town of The Stop and from there head south on the main road, and if anyone asked, "you are fleeing Phlan." When someone said the Red Plumes were finished and the city burned to the ground, Elbus answered that Olisara, "who knows everything," said that although there was a riot, the First Lord was not deposed and was still firmly in charge.

Two days after leaving the Tinfellow Valley, Daein sighted the High Road. A group of heavily adorned riders in crimson tabards and bright red plumes, on battle-bred black horses with gleaming barding, stormed past without a glance. "Red Plumes!" he muttered. From the top of a small hill he then saw, down by a creek, a group of Red Plumes fighting non-human assailants who had ambushed them. One soldier hung lassoed by the neck from a branch, legs twitching; horses in Red Plume livery were scattered about and a handful of bodies lay on the ground.

Bramgrim shouted, "murderous Red Plumes! I want to kill some Red Plumes!", then, realizing this was out of character for a cleric of life, muttered "Sharindlar, forgive me!" Daein tried to urge caution, but Falofa was already charging. The bandits called for help to "kill the pillaging monsters," while the Red Plumes called the bandits a "menace to the city of Hillsfar and humanity!" That last word enraged Falofa, and a few sword strokes later the Red Plumes were dead.

More bandits emerged from the forest, cleared the soldiers off the road, gathered their horses and stripped them of equipment and valuables. A dwarf tossed Falofa a purse cut from a soldier's belt, "for your trouble... and discretion," and invited her to join them against a Red Plume caravan on the move. "Kill more Red Plumes. Let's go!" was her answer. He introduced himself: "My name is Charles not Karl," a farmer reduced to crime by the First Lord.

Bramgrim, as a cleric of Sharindlar, asked what was going on. Charles explained that farming had once been good business: farm, take the produce to The Stop, sell to a merchant representative. Llewelyn Brown, a male Tiefling missing an arm, said that was fine "until the human farmers started getting better treatment." A bespectacled halfling, Friedrich Watson, a historian of the area, gave the history: Hillsfar was once a trading outpost of the Elves of Myth Drannor; its humans rebelled, declared independence and, overcorrecting, banned non-humans; merchants learned that trade required openness and the old hatreds faded, helped by the Elves abandoning Myth Drannor; the new First Lord seemed reasonable, "but something evil happened, and he returned to the old ways." Charles cut him off ("enough, Professor!") to name the real problem: when the new First Lord, whom he called "No-sanity," took over, the merchants saw an opportunity, and with Hillsfar closed to them and Phlan a mess, the farmers had to sell at lower prices. The Professor added that the money was not enough to buy crops for the next year, so they borrowed, and when they could not pay, the merchants took their farms. Lou added that the Red Plumes work for the merchants, not the people.

That brought Charles to his plan: a shipment of important cargo in heavily armored wagons was coming down the road. "With those guards, it's not grain." His contact said it would be a big score.
