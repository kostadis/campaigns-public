# Chapter 43 The Spire Heist: A Dinner of Disguise and Disarray

Date: Mar 22nd, 2026

## Summary
The party prepared to capture Brandyr alive before infiltrating the Spire of Inevitable Reckoning, stealing the Lexicon of Oaths, and escaping through the teleportation device above.

Their first obstacle was the fire giant Brandyr, standing watch over a ritual courtyard of polished obsidian, its ebony pillars branded with Graz’zt’s crimson six-fingered hand. Felkur spent a magical token to obtain Sleep, leaving four tokens remaining. Brandyr failed the saving throw, and Bramgrim and Daein each applied reduction magic. Akritas used Nature’s Veil to approach invisibly and shoved the shrunken giant into a portable hole fitted with a breathing apparatus.

Escaping required further coordination. The courtyard was open ground rather than the sheltered rooftop the party had imagined, and securing Brandyr consumed Akritas’s action. Felkur’s excellent stealth, together with Akritas and Daein, kept the group concealed despite Bramgrim’s noisy armor. The others then prepared to move with Akritas as she cast Pass Without Trace.

The cambion guards began supplying their own explanation for Brandyr’s disappearance: perhaps Graz’zt had disapproved of the ritual or abandoned his experiment with demonic fire giants. The party exploited that resentment. Felkur prepared a projected sound effect, and Daein performed Graz’zt’s voice, directing the supposed demon lord’s displeasure toward the giants. A natural 20 turned the deception into a triumph. All four cambions fell to their knees, then abandoned their posts to spread the revelation. The party avoided a citywide manhunt and gained advantage on upcoming social checks intended to turn Vraxen and Veperyd against one another.

Before entering the Spire, they questioned Pipyap. The imp tried to charge one piece of food per question, but Bramgrim’s intimidation and a reminder of their existing protection arrangement ended that negotiation. Pipyap described the entrance, dining hall, and specially commissioned table that allowed guests of different sizes to see one another at eye level. He also explained that Vraxen carried the key needed to reach the upper vaults, although Pipyap himself had never been upstairs.

Learning that High Inquisitor Veris of Mulmaster would attend the dinner prompted Felkur to recognize Maram il Vass’s double cross. Maram had probably directed Veris toward the Lexicon as a source of information about the Phantom Ghost while threatening Felkur with exposure unless he retrieved it. Both bargains could stand; whoever reached the book first would determine the outcome.

The party chose to enter as Hillsfar traders with information recovered amid the upheaval on the surface, including a lead concerning the Phantom Ghost. They saved their Seeming scroll for an emergency disguise. At the entrance, guards called them armed slaves, but Bramgrim and Daein persuaded them that they carried critical information requested by Vraxen. Inside, the party secured seats and food before beginning their supposed business.

The dinner was already close to eruption. Vraxen demanded an explanation for 3,000 missing gold pieces, while Veperyd tore into hydra heart and defended his management of the arenas. High Inquisitor Veris immediately focused on Felkur’s smith-stained hands and apparent familiarity with Mulmaster imprisonment. Daein and Bramgrim interrupted with questions about Colosseum betting and commissions, drawing Vraxen back into his financial dispute with the general.

As the argument intensified, Vraxen’s runic key slipped from beneath a napkin and dangled from the table. Felkur directed the owlbear to create a distraction. It leapt onto the table and began pounding and screaming, apparently deciding that everyone else’s behavior meant this was a pounding room. Under that cover, Daein used telekinetic movement to steal the key unnoticed.

The party then used an illusion of Veris’s voice to push the dispute into violence. Their initial proposal implicated him in Veperyd’s theft; they refined the provocation toward conflicting financial interests that would turn all three diners against one another. Felkur’s successful check supported the deception. Vraxen ordered his guards to flay the general, Veperyd threatened repayment in blood, and the dinner collapsed into a three-way fight.

The party slipped away and ran upstairs with the key. The Lexicon remained unrecovered, and they still needed to reach the uppermost room and activate the teleportation device. As the session ended, the DM revealed one more complication: Pipyap was no longer with them. His whereabouts and reasons for disappearing remained unknown.

## Scenes
### Planning the Ambush in Maerimydra
#### The party coordinates a complex plan to capture the fire giant Brandyr alive before infiltrating the Spire of Inevitable Reckoning.
- The group discusses a specialized Sleep spell followed by reduction magic. The DM rules that incapacitation will allow Reduce to work without a saving throw.
- Two reductions are needed because each casting reduces the giant by only one size category. The DM supplies the second reduction without charging another magical token, reasoning that the party would have accounted for this while planning.
- Early assignments change during discussion. The final arrangement places Sleep with Felkur, whose spell save DC is 19, while Bramgrim and Daein handle the reductions.
- Brandyr’s Wisdom modifier is established as +2, making Felkur’s spell an attractive opening move.
- Akritas prepares to approach using Nature’s Veil, carrying the portable hole and its breathing apparatus. Pass Without Trace is discussed as protection for the retreat.
- Daein initially considers keeping his psionic power available to bring Akritas back.
- The owlbear prepares to attack if the ambush goes wrong.
- The DM characterizes the scheme as an opening maneuver whose later complications will be resolved as they arise.

### Ambush at the Obsidian Courtyard
#### The party executes a high-stakes magical ambush to kidnap a fire giant named Brandyr from a ritual site in Maerimydra.
- The party positions itself around the polished obsidian courtyard and its ebony pillars, each bearing Graz’zt’s crimson six-fingered hand.
- Felkur obtains Sleep with a magical token, leaving four tokens, and casts it successfully. Brandyr fails the saving throw and becomes incapacitated.
- Akritas activates Nature’s Veil, becoming invisible along with her equipment.
- Bramgrim and Daein both cast reduction spells, shrinking Brandyr to a manageable size.
- Akritas shoves the unconscious giant into the portable hole equipped with a breathing apparatus.
- The DM rules that putting Brandyr into the hole takes an action, preventing Akritas from also dashing that turn.
- A map misunderstanding becomes significant: the party is on the ground in an open courtyard, rather than on a roof overlooking an enclosed space. The pillars offer cover, but the surrounding area remains exposed.
- Akritas takes cover while the others begin repositioning. Her invisibility lasts until the start of her next turn.
- The party cannot yet rely on Pass Without Trace; Akritas still needs to cast it.
- Group stealth preserves their surprise. Felkur rolls 22, Akritas 21, and Daein 21, forming a successful majority despite Bramgrim’s clanking armor.
- The cambions remain confused rather than immediately identifying an abduction. In their experience, supernatural disappearance could plausibly indicate Graz’zt’s intervention.

### The Great Deception at the Six-Fingered Courtyard
#### The party uses stealth and illusions to escape the courtyard after capturing a fire giant, successfully tricking the demonic guards into believing their god has intervened.
- Akritas casts Pass Without Trace. Because it takes an action, she moves without dashing.
- The others prepare to move with her, keeping the group within the spell’s protection.
- The cambions fail their Perception checks and do not locate the party.

- The DM explains that the cambions resent other infused humanoids receiving comparable powers and fear the giants might displace them in the Abyss. Daein would understand this rivalry.
- Felkur prepares the projected sound effect, with Daein performing Graz’zt’s voice. The party knows the voice from hearing Graz’zt scream at them in Hillsfar.
- Daein chooses Performance to reinforce the cambions’ smug interpretation rather than Deception to issue an instruction. The DM sets the respective difficulties at 12 and 16.
- Daein rolls a natural 20 for a total of 24. A display discrepancy briefly obscures the result, but the DM accepts it.
- All four cambions fall to their knees, convinced Graz’zt has personally removed or smitten the unworthy giant.
- They abandon their posts and fly off to spread the revelation. The route toward the elite district still has guards, but the party no longer has to evade these demons.
- The party avoids a citywide manhunt and receives advantage on upcoming Deception, Intimidation, or Persuasion checks to incite conflict between Vraxen and Veperyd.
- Felkur urges the group to take immediate advantage of the opening and proceed.

## Locations
### Six-Fingered Courtyard
A ritual area on a plateau in Maerimydra featuring a polished obsidian floor surrounded by ebony pillars, each emblazoned with a crimson six-fingered hand — the mark of Graz’zt. This is where the party ambushed and kidnapped the fire giant Brandyr.

- Located on the southern end of the plateau at the western end of the cavern.
- Nearby broad estates appear to house important giants. Their slaves are well dressed, and the city’s usual fiery chaos is somewhat muted here.
- The courtyard is open ground. The party initially misread its layout as a structure with a roof or elevated hiding positions.
- Fire burns atop the pillars; surrounding rocks and pillars provide opportunities for cover.

### Maerimydra
A city in the Underdark currently occupied by fire giants who have made a pact with the demon lord Graz’zt, who has infused them with demonic power.

- The opening recap connects this threat to the aftermath of Malkyn Grenefeld’s defeat in Hillsfar and warnings from the Emerald Enclave and Charles.
- The demonic giants threaten the Underdark and eventually the surface.
- The party’s earlier journey involved retrieving an alchemist for Solom Ned’razak, who hoped to develop a solution to the demonic infestation.
- Rivalry between cambions and demonic fire giants provides an exploitable division within the occupying forces.
- Veperyd claims drow rebels threaten the city’s gates.

### Spire of Inevitable Reckoning
A formidable tower in Maerimydra overseen by the Arcanaloth Vraxen. It features a dining hall with a magically distorted table that allows creatures of vastly different sizes to converse at eye level, and upper vaults the party plans to reach using Vraxen’s runic key.

- The main entrance leads into the dining hall.
- According to Pipyap, Vraxen maintains relatively few guards because he trusts his position and always has a way to escape.
- The dining hall contains an obsidian table, Soul Fire candles, expensive wine, and servants carrying food.
- The party’s intended escape requires reaching a teleportation device in the topmost room.
- The upper rooms remain unexplored at the session’s end.

### Mulmaster
A surface city known for its powerful wizards, mentioned as the origin of High Inquisitor Veris and the location from which Felkur accidentally stole a potent divination artifact years ago.

- Malkyn Grenefeld also originated there.
- The artifact’s theft and eventual recovery became a major humiliation for the city’s hierarchy.
- Felkur’s accidental protection from scrying helped create the legend of a powerful, invisible infiltrator who had penetrated secure locations and returned the artifact as an insult.
- The investigation lasted years, and several inquisitors were executed.
- Veris’s search for the Phantom Ghost now intersects with the party’s theft of the Lexicon.
