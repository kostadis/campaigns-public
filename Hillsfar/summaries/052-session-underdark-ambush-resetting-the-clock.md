# Chapter 52 Underdark Ambush: Resetting the Clock

Date: 2026-06-28

## Summary
Before play resumed, the players reviewed a letter — not addressed to the party, but about them. Written by the Arcanaloth Vraxen to the demon prince Graz'zt, it meticulously accounted for the adventurers' catastrophes: riots, destroyed soul-trapping apparatus, and damages denominated in soul coins. An initial confusion between silver coins and soul coins prompted an explanation that each infernal coin contained a mortal soul. The table debated enormous gold equivalents without settling on a consistent total. Vraxen dismissed one adventurer as a “liar with a mandolin,” provoking particular indignation, and characterized the party's disruption as a subscription: a recurring expense in humiliation for a prince whose tormentors did not even know his full title.

The new planning began the following morning. Murssvayas Dhuurniv, hoarse and exhausted, pulled the party aside to a scratched-out map. The fire giants had preserved Szith Morcane's forges to manufacture weapons for the surface. Loaded carts departed for Maerimydra and returned empty. Murssvayas urged the party to destroy production before its output appeared on an enemy wall, admitting that Solom had been the general and he was a wizard handed a war. He pointed them toward four possible sources of support: the Hidden followers of Vhaeraun, the stone giant carvers of the Lodge of Faces, the Nerevenans of Kiaransalee, and roughly two hundred enslaved workers breaking ore for the forge.

Recruitment competed directly with the shipping schedule: a cart was expected every three days, and securing an ally could consume about that much time. Daein explored intercepting a shipment, taking its weapons, returning the cart empty, and even disguising the party as its giant guards. The DM warned that missing escorts would eventually bring larger forces to investigate. Emerald Enclave troops were too distant, and Szith Morcane's restrictions on teleportation prevented an immediate shortcut. Splitting the party remained possible, but separated characters would have to act without one another's help or knowledge.

Religious rivalries and slavery complicated any coalition. Vhaeraun's followers would require an approach without the Lolth-worshipping Drow, while Bramgrim and Akritas refused to serve as envoys to Kiaransalee's followers. Murssvayas acknowledged that the forge workers had previously been Drow property, and Quil'bryn still regarded them as protection for elite troops. Nevertheless, defeating the fire giants — including their power in Maerimydra — was everyone's immediate priority. The party could limit what each faction knew about the others and address the consequences of their alliance later.

The final agreement gave the party three extra days for stopping the cart and another three for freeing the slaves, although the latter would alert the giants and bring more troops. Capturing Szith Morcane and destroying its forge would eliminate the production threat. The party therefore chose to ambush the cart first, then free the workers, creating time to pursue the remaining allies.

Quil'bryn supplied a secret passage to a suitable ambush point. The party selected five Drow elite warriors from its twenty available soldiers, accepting that casualties would reduce their later strength. After a few hours' travel, they had half a day to prepare. Akritas, the Owlbear, and two Drow positioned themselves to block retreat toward Szith Morcane; Daein, Bramgrim, Felkur, and three Drow held the other side. Akritas cast Pass Without Trace and scouted a fire giant pulling the cart, two Azer guards, and a Hellhound riding atop it.

Three Drow in the primary group rolled natural ones on stealth. Akritas's separate group retained surprise, but the operation lost its clean opening. Felkur missed his freezing attack despite his magical wand, and Daein spent Action Surge to land only one hit in four attacks. The Owlbear badly wounded an Azer. The concealed Drow then changed the fight: one outlined the giant with Faerie Fire, and another landed poisoned crossbow shots. The giant took twelve damage and failed its poison save badly enough to fall unconscious.

Akritas finished the wounded Azer with her bow and Favored Foe, then attacked the remaining Azer with Hunter's Mark. The party confirmed that damaging the sleeping giant would wake it and discussed clearing its escorts first, while recognizing that those escorts could also rouse it. The session ended during the opening exchange. The giant was unconscious and one Azer dead, but the cart had yet to be secured and the promised reprieve had not yet been earned.

## Scenes
### The Demon's Balance Sheet
#### The players review a letter from the Arcanaloth Vraxen to the demon prince Graz'zt, detailing the astronomical damages they have caused.
- Vraxen's letter is addressed to Graz'zt. The DM clarifies that the players are reading an accounting of the party’s actions, rather than receiving a direct demand for payment.
- An initial misreading of soul coins as silver coins leads to an explanation: soul coins are rare, cursed infernal currency containing trapped mortal souls.
- The table debates the bill's gold equivalent, cycling through several enormous estimates without establishing a consistent final total.
- The listed damage includes riots and the destruction of a soul-trapping apparatus.
- Vraxen describes the adventurers by dismissive labels, including the ranger, cleric, gnome, and “liar with a mandolin.”
- During the read-through, Dimos objects to that last description and laments that the party could have been millionaires instead of merely costing someone else millions.
- The DM explains that an Arcanaloth values souls over ordinary gold and silver, which powerful magic can produce.
- Discussion of Bramgrim's interest in money recalls earlier adventures in which Daein had to restrain his negotiating demands. The DM invokes Bramgrim's identity as a lawful good life cleric.
- Vraxen's subscription analogy becomes the letter's defining insult: Graz'zt is paying repeatedly for humiliations inflicted by adventurers who do not know his full title.

### Strategic Planning and the Forge Clock

- The Lodge of Faces consists of stone giant carvers whose strength could transform the fighting if the party can rouse them.
- Kiaransalee's priestesses and their dead followers form another possible faction.

- A shipment is expected every three days; travel and negotiation to secure an ally take roughly three days. The DM explicitly frames recruitment as time during which more weapons reach the enemy.

- The DM warns that an absent cart or missing escorts will provoke investigation and eventually bring larger forces, including troops from Maerimydra.
- Reconnaissance of Szith Morcane and its forge remains an available alternative.
- Akritas's proposed Emerald Enclave reinforcements are days away — roughly ten days in the discussion — allowing several shipments to pass before they arrive.
- The DM explains that Szith Morcane cannot simply be reached by teleportation. Difficult tunnel routes constrain travel and communication throughout the Underdark.
- Daein proposes splitting the party to recruit in parallel. The DM permits it, with separated characters unable to help one another or automatically know what the other group discovers.
- Factions are also separated by days of travel, allowing the party to conceal the full membership of its coalition.
- The DM reviews the hostility among Lolth, Vhaeraun, and Kiaransalee. Vhaeraun's objections include Lolth's encouragement of Drow infighting and persecution of men.
- Stone giants appear less entangled in those religious disputes, but slaves have strong reasons to hate Drow.
- Bramgrim and Akritas refuse to approach Kiaransalee's followers because of her association with vengeance and undeath. They would prefer no alliance, but defer to Felkur and Daein.
- Bramgrim's Insight check of 16 produces the key observation that freeing the workers would delay manufacturing.

- A further Insight check of 15 establishes that the shared threat can sustain cooperation now, even if the factions turn against one another afterward.
- The final timing agreement grants **three extra days for stopping the cart** and **three more for freeing the slaves**. Freeing the slaves alerts the giants, who will bring more troops; doing so first would also make the cart more heavily guarded.
- Capturing Szith Morcane and destroying the forge eliminates the production threat.
- The party settles on the cart first, then the slaves, using the delay to pursue the other allies.
- The cart ambush is an unexpected direction for the DM, who improvises the encounter and draws a map.

### The Ambush at the Tunnel

## NPCs
### Vraxen
An Arcanaloth who serves as a meticulous accountant for Graz'zt. His letter tallies the party's damage in soul coins, including riots and destroyed soul-trapping apparatus, and describes their interference as a subscription of humiliation. His contemptuous labels for the adventurers include “liar with a mandolin.” His separate magical ledger remains a target of interest to the Emerald Enclave High Council.

### Graz'zt
A Demon Prince receiving Vraxen's financial damage reports. According to Alder's earlier warning, he plans to flee to Maerimydra if the adventurers sent toward Menzoberranzan succeed.

### Charles
A flamboyant former revolutionary the party met in Hillsfar in the previous session. He warned of the fire giants' approaching surface threat and asked the party to rally the Drow resistance. He called their tavern a cafe, then abandoned his revolutionary persona to discuss Felkur's Stargate venture, guild paperwork, taxes, and land through Isthana Ro. His promise to handle arrangements during the party's absence remains outstanding.

### Alder
The blunt, world-weary head Harper overseeing Underdark operations. She has sent Celia, Mor, Spots, Draco, and Connor toward Menzoberranzan. In the previous session, she assigned this party to counter Graz'zt's escape plan, saved them days of travel by teleporting them deeper into the Underdark, and signed Bramgrim's sword before vanishing.

### Gromph Baenre
The Archmage of Menzoberranzan whose attempt to free Lolth from an interdimensional prison accidentally opened a passage to the Abyss and released demon lords into the world.

### Galeb Duhr
A sentient rock being whose right half bears a beautifully carved stone giant face with a shattered nose. Exiled by Elder Decha for imperfection, it believes itself too ugly to enjoy the Stone Father's glory. In the previous session, the party's encouragement lifted its spirits, and it supplied directions to the Lodge of Faces before withdrawing into the stone.

### Murssvayas Dhuurniv
A Drow mage who inherited the fractured resistance after Solom's death. Hoarse and overwhelmed, he briefs the party using a scratched-out map and urges action against Dengor's forge before more weapons reach Maerimydra. He identifies possible allies but admits that twenty Drow cannot retake the outpost alone. His acknowledgment that the forge workers were once Drow property exposes a major obstacle to recruiting them.

### Quil'bryn
A Drow elite warrior who killed Solom while under a mental fog. In the previous session, the braincap restored his senses, leaving him horrified; he pledged his sword to Felkur. He supplies tactical advice, a map, and a secret route to the cart's path. His support for the coalition still treats other recruits as expendable protection for elite Drow.

### Solom Ned'razak
The former resistance leader, murdered by Quil'bryn eight days before the party's arrival. His loss deprived the camp of its general and left Murssvayas attempting to hold together an increasingly divided force.

### Drow Elite Warriors
Five of the resistance's twenty soldiers accompany the ambush. Two deploy with Akritas, while three take the primary position and all roll natural ones on stealth. The concealed soldiers prove decisive: one casts Faerie Fire and another lands poisoned crossbow shots that incapacitate the giant. Their survival matters to the later campaign.

### Fire Giant
The giant pulling the weapons cart toward Maerimydra. It takes Daein's single successful strike and twelve damage from Drow crossbow fire before failing its poison save and falling unconscious. Damage or intervention from its escorts could wake it; it remains alive when play stops.

### Azer
Two fiery, dwarf-like guards escorting the cart. The Owlbear badly wounds Azer number two, which Akritas finishes with her bow and Favored Foe. Akritas then attacks Azer number one with Hunter's Mark; that guard remains in the fight at the stopping point.

### Hellhound
A fiery canine beast perched on the supply cart. It remains an unresolved part of the escort when the session ends.

### Owlbear
The party's loyal sidekick. It deploys with Akritas to block retreat, benefits from her stealth aura, and savages Azer number two with its claws, setting up Akritas's finishing shot.

## Locations
### Hillsfar
The surface city where, in the previous session, Charles briefed the party over drinks about the fire giants and the resistance, and Felkur's Stargate proposal became a discussion of guild administration, taxation, and land.

### The Waydown
The gateway village through which the party descended into the Underdark in the previous session. The opening recap describes an Emerald Enclave contract allowing passage through its guards without incident.

### Szith Morcane
A Drow outpost occupied by fire giants. Its preserved forges manufacture weapons under Dengor, supplied by roughly two hundred enslaved workers. Carts carry the output toward Maerimydra every three days. Destroying the forge would end this production threat. The resistance operates nearby, and teleportation restrictions complicate outside reinforcement.

### Maerimydra
The destination of the weapons shipments and Graz'zt's intended refuge. Failure to disrupt production means facing those weapons there later. Forces from Maerimydra could also investigate a missing shipment, and the prospective allies share an interest in defeating the giants there as well as at Szith Morcane.

### Lodge of Faces
The home of stone giant carvers led by Elder Decha, who exiled the damaged Galeb Duhr. Murssvayas considers its giants potentially powerful allies if the party can persuade them to act.

### Underdark Tunnels
The narrow routes connecting the occupied outpost, resistance, and potential allies. Travel and poor communication make even nearby objectives costly in time. Quil'bryn's secret passage provides access to the cart's route, where the party prepares positions on both sides to prevent retreat.

## Items
### Soul Coins
Rare, cursed infernal coins containing trapped mortal souls. Vraxen uses them to value the party's damage. Initial confusion with silver coins leads to a discussion of their nature and enormous gold equivalents, without a consistent final total.

### Vraxen's Bill
A letter to Graz'zt accounting for the adventurers' destruction. Its examples include riots and ruined soul-trapping apparatus. Its subscription analogy and dismissive descriptions of the party make it both a financial report and a sustained insult.

### Bramgrim's Sword
The weapon Alder signed with a quill before disappearing in the previous session.

### Braincap Mushroom
The fungus Daein required Quil'bryn to consume in the previous session as a public test of his claimed sanity. Its immediate effect restored his personality and brought home the horror of his killing Solom.

### Vraxen's Ledger
A powerful magical ledger sought by the Emerald Enclave High Council. Akritas's disclosure of that objective sparked an earlier debate. The party's previous-session agreement to attempt a heist if circumstances permit remains in place, with the understanding that the ledger can wait if the mission becomes too dangerous.

### Supply Cart
The guarded vehicle carrying weapons from Szith Morcane to Maerimydra on a three-day schedule and returning empty. This shipment has a fire giant pulling it, two Azer escorts, and a Hellhound riding atop it. Securing it would buy three extra days, but missing escorts or shipments will eventually provoke investigation.

### Wand of the War Mage
Felkur's magical implement. During his opening attack, the table checks its benefits, including a +2 attack bonus and its interaction with partial cover. Even with the corrected bonus, his shot misses.

### Drow Poison
The sedative coating the Drow's crossbow bolts. The giant's exceptionally poor save renders it unconscious. The table confirms that damage would wake it and recognizes that its allies could also rouse it, making the condition a temporary tactical opportunity.

## Spells
### Teleport
Alder's means of transporting the party deep into the Underdark during the previous session's journey. It saved days of travel. Later planning establishes that Szith Morcane's restrictions prevent outside reinforcements from simply teleporting to the objective.

### Pass Without Trace
Akritas casts this before scouting and preparing the rear ambush. The stated rules give chosen creatures within a thirty-foot aura +10 to Stealth and prevent tracks. Akritas rolls 30 and the Owlbear 27. Her nearby companions retain surprise; the three Drow in the other position fail their checks. The DM also cautions that the spell does not bypass every magical ward.

### Faerie Fire
A Drow elite warrior uses this to outline the fire giant and grant advantage to subsequent attacks. He chooses it over Darkness to avoid obstructing his allies. The DM clarifies that its name does not mean it deals fire damage, and the next Drow exploits its advantage with poisoned crossbow shots.

### Hunter's Mark
Akritas invokes Hunter's Mark against Azer number one after killing Azer number two with Favored Foe. She rolls the additional d6 during the final attack sequence before play stops.
