# Chapter 44 Scent of a Golem, Soul of a Contract

Date: Unverified — editorial partition

## Summary

Felkur bypassed the lift access with exceptional precision, leaving no trace of the party’s passage and buying them additional time. He mocked the quality of its Abyssal 23 operating system as they gained access to the upper corridor.

The corridor’s tiled floor concealed magical traps. Felkur stopped just before touching the second tile, identifying a Glyph of Warding that would have exploded on contact. Bramgrim used True Seeing, and the party identified the pattern: tiles corresponding to prime numbers were trapped. By stepping on the safe tiles, they crossed the corridor unharmed.

A stone golem guarded the vault door. It could not be persuaded, and the party judged that its magical immunities made fighting it difficult. Felkur determined that it recognized Vraxen by scent. The Owlbear had acquired that scent while bouncing on Vraxen’s table during the earlier dinner. With a magical token enabling the use of his personal flavor-extraction gadget, Felkur extracted and applied the scent. The golem stepped aside.

Beyond it stood a reinforced Mithril door with three locking mechanisms. Felkur easily picked the first physical lock. The account establishes that the party gained entry, but does not describe how the remaining mechanisms were opened.

The Vault of Oaths was a chilled chamber lined with soul coins, infernal ledgers, and contracts written in the language of devils. At its center, beneath a crystal dome, rested the Lexicon of Oaths: a devil-hide tome whose glowing pages were made from processed souls. It automatically recorded and updated infernal contracts struck in Maerimydra. Bramgrim checked the room with his active True Seeing and reported finding no further traps. The account suggests that the keeper wished to protect the records.

Felkur searched the Lexicon and found Transaction 44781. The account describes the entry as recording a sale by Felkur Oldenwood to Limping Oric for forty-five gold pieces, dated to the Twin Moons of Winter Deep, with verified identification and a blood alcohol content of 0.18%. It also records complaints about a bad trade and keeping the shiny thing.

Those recorded details must remain distinct from the GM-confirmed history. Felkur picked the Watcher’s Eye Brooch up from a table, accidentally activated its antiscrying effect, and sold it for 40 silver to drink beer. The brooch allowed free passage into the Tower of Blades of Mulmaster. Its theft and rapid return caused conspiracy theories. The confirmed history establishes neither the actual buyer nor the returner or date. Whether the conflicting sale account represents an inaccurate fictional ledger or an error in the authored notes remains unresolved.

Other historical embellishments also remain attributed to the source account. These include a workbench-and-craftsman explanation, competing associations with wizards of the Brotherhood of the Cloaks and with priests, and differing locations. The notes credit Limping Oric with an onward transfer and an inability to remember the seller, and connect the brooch to failed searches and executions of investigators. The confirmed antiscrying effect does not establish those memory-loss or absolute-divination claims. The notes also preserve both Phantom Ghost and Phantom Thief as titles for Felkur’s legend.

Reading the entry supplied recorded details of Maram il Vass’s sale of Felkur’s information and the contract requiring the party to retrieve the Lexicon. Their suspicion that Maram il Vass had double-crossed them already existed before they entered the vault.

Felkur identified a loophole: the delivery contract depended on the Lexicon containing the information needed to identify him. Without that information, the contract would be void. He chose to alter the entry rather than tear out the page, avoiding an obvious sign of interference. Using his thieves’ tools and magical expertise, he successfully replaced his own name with Veris of Mulmaster. The substitution was intended to misdirect future investigators. This name replacement is the chapter’s final event.
