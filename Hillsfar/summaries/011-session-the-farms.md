# Chapter 11: The Farms

Date: 15–16 Mirtul 1494 DR (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-16); written 2023-06-10 (Drive createdTime; play date not stated)

## Summary

"Let's head out to the farms to see what information we can gather," mumbles Bramgrim. Glenna Moonsmith, the love interest of Karl (pronounced Sharles), had told the group of a demonic event a few days earlier: a pair of farmhands from the Arcrus Ranch told tales of goblin knights riding monsters and laying waste to the farms of the area, and as they fled they had seen the Callidell Homestead in flames and the Lumbus Farm burning. Bramgrim sees yet another sign of an evil demonic power taking hold in the area, after the curious events at the farm with the six fingered children and the red-eyed goats, the events in the city of Hillsfar, and most recently the events at the small village of Hillpicket. "By the gods," he says, scratching his beard and braiding his hair, "there is some great evil going on here."

After securing horses from the livery at the waystation from its racist owner Kendrick, Daein has his friends saddle up. Bramgrim tells him, "My dear Daein, this madness of the law of humanity must come to an end." Daein keeps quiet, since anything he says would probably be received poorly. The road toward the coast of the Moonsea runs through gently rolling hills and meadows of scrub brush and wildflowers, with the occasional copse of ancient trees more suited to Cormanthor. The weather is sunny and mild, with light drizzle as they near the first of the three farms. Daein is pleased he insisted on the horses: on foot the journey would have taken eight hours, and twice that at his companions' pace.

The first stop is the Lumbus Farm, and the farmhands' tales prove true: smoke rises from scorched fields and orchards around a low sod house, and a barn with an attached paddock is on fire, its door torn partly off its hinges and holes chopped clumsily into it at the waist level of a human. A goat thrusts its head through one hole, bleats and withdraws. After some searching Daein finds a halfling child. "We are here to save you!" The boy, a self-declared goblin fan who kept a notebook of every detail about goblins, insists these were different: the one in charge was tall and thin with coal-black hair and horns all around his head, six fingers on each hand, metal armor like a knight. "He looked as much like a demon as a goblin!" The knight goblin pointed at the fields and fire shot out; two others wore metal armor and had spikes in their heads like the boss's horns, and the rest were sneaky and stabbing and were bossed around by the knights. Asked by Bramgrim how he survived, he explains that goblins burst into the house and stabbed everyone while they slept; he slipped out the window and hid in the barn with the goats, three of which he hid from the goblins, and has not left since. "If a goblin poked his head around here, I'd stick him good." He tells them of family friends a few miles to the north, the Bitterwoods: "I know they'll take care of me now that nobody's here." Everyone looks at Daein, who sighs and says, "Come on, let's go."

Two hours later Daein returns to the smell of roasted goat meat: Falofa is cooking one of the goats. "That's not our property." "I was hungry." Bramgrim nods; Felkur chews. Daein cuts a hunk off, sits down, eats, and tells the barbarian, "You are going to pay for this." She grumbles that there is no one to pay, and Daein points out that they could give the money to Karl, who could arrange for it to go to the Bitterwoods. He chooses to ignore how unhappy Falofa is.

With a full belly, Felkur examines the area and finds tracks leading to and from a secluded cave 2 miles to the South: large dogs, a giant toad, an oversized centipede-like creature, and goblins on foot. "Should we follow this trail?" Daein prefers to "go find more information," and the group heads to the Callidell homestead. Behind high hedges stand a stone cottage and three wooden outbuildings. Felkur muses that it is "Not a bad place to have a group of witches praying to evil gods" or for people of a different religion wanting privacy from ignorant villagers. The cottage's roof is badly burned and partially collapsed; seven bodies, mostly hacked to pieces, lie in the yard; to the west a large wheat field shows tatterdemalion scraps of cloth, apparently the remains of scarecrows. The dead are clearly related, with thin dark hair and wide-set eyes, and were killed fighting the goblins, yet none carries a weapon; instead each holds strange knots of gnarled wood wrapped in human skin. "Score one for the ignorant villagers?" he thinks. He finds an identical iron key on two of the corpses.

At Daein's urging they go into the golden wheat field. Rustling, and four scarecrows with demonic eyes spring to life and attack. "This is evil magic!" shouts Bramgrim. "The spirits of the murdered Callidell have animated these creatures." Felkur, Daein and Falofa fight with all their might, and at first the scarecrows seem invulnerable. Eventually the scarecrows are dead and the Callidell's evil is gone, but it was a close call. "What the heck is going on with this part of the world!" grumbles Bramgrim, and Felkur, breathing heavily, nods.
