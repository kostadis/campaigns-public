# Chapter 55 An Alliance in the Slave Pens

Date: 2026-07-27

## Summary
With Rilira's support secured, the party turned their attention to the broader campaign to liberate Szith Morcane and gather support for Maerimydra. They needed allies, and they needed weapons. Rilira demanded that Rylzyn and Sorn explain how they would arm her followers, then sneered when they invoked Quil'bryn's plan; the exchange again exposed the gender tensions within drow society. The forge of Surtr in the ruins of House Morcane supplied the fire giants' armaments, and the palace of the fire giant king Dengor, marked as Area F on the map, loomed as the final obstacle. House Morcane offered another route toward the palace, allowing the forge and Dengor to be addressed during the same operation.

The party had two more days to recruit after securing the slaves. They were warned that removing the fire giants first would deprive the remaining factions of an incentive to cooperate. They debated approaching the Hidden, a secretive group of Vhaeraunite rebels; the stone giants of the Lodge of Faces; and the Nerevenans, worshippers of a goddess of death whose altar the party had previously destroyed. They had previously learned that three enslaved stone giants were forced to build Dengor's palace, making help from the Lodge potentially valuable for the assault. Deciding that Bramgrim and Akritas were poorly suited to negotiate with death cultists, the group resolved to split up — Bramgrim and Akritas would seek out the Hidden, while Daein and Felkur approached the Lodge of Faces. The two visits would happen simultaneously. The Hidden's hatred of Lolth's followers also meant the party could not bring their existing drow allies to that meeting.

Bramgrim and Akritas made themselves conspicuous on the webs of Szith Morcane, following Quil'bryn's advice to wait until the Hidden found them. A drow scout approached and directed them to a meeting at a set of gnome ruins four hours later. After checking that he was telling the truth, they followed his directions and met Hamadh, the leader of the Hidden. He questioned their motives carefully and was satisfied to learn they had made no binding commitments to any particular drow faction: they had promised only that drow would govern the liberated city. Hamadh declared that words and feats of combat were not enough — to earn the Hidden's support, the party would have to prove their worth by stealing without a fight. He led them to a giant-held weapon cache protected by a glowing rune lattice and guarded by roving patrols, and told them simply to bring back what was inside.

Using Pass Without Trace, Bramgrim and Akritas slipped past the patrol and reached the cache. The Faerzress-warded lock drew on a natural phenomenon that Dispel Magic could not remove. Felkur's purchased shard of crystallized Faerzress could have helped, but it was with him on the other mission. Equipped with borrowed thieves' tools and assisted by Bramgrim's Guidance, Akritas recognized the alarm risk and successfully opened the gate — though her result fell just short of opening it silently. A magical alert summoned the guards; ordinary Silence would not have prevented it.

Rather than abandon the treasure or risk pursuit through unfamiliar tunnels, Bramgrim and Akritas stood their ground after determining that the approaching patrol contained no fire giants. A hellhound and two azer sentinels came charging down the tunnel. Akritas struck first with Hail of Thorns, badly wounding the hellhound and catching an azer in the burst. The hellhound breathed searing fire across both adventurers, and the azers pressed in with their hammers and burning proximity, but Bramgrim's divine spirits ground the attackers down. Akritas finished the hellhound and one azer with follow-up shots. The final azer attempted to flee, survived an opportunity attack, resisted Toll the Dead, and briefly benefited from two missed arrows before Bramgrim brought his Spirit Guardians close enough to finish it. The two secured the treasure without drawing a larger fight. Despite the alarm and skirmish, Hamadh was impressed and pledged the Hidden's support on the condition that they defeated Dengor and allowed the drow to reclaim Szith Morcane.

Meanwhile, Daein and Felkur made their way to the Cathedral of Faces with Grek, the Galeb Duhr who had previously told them about the Lodge. Half of Grek's body had been carved into a beautifully rendered stone giant face with a shattered nose, while the other half remained rough and unfinished. Decha had exiled him for imperfection. Grek had also warned that the once honorable stone giants had become obsessed with their work and ignored the fire giants' trespasses.

The cathedral was a vast cavern where every surface — walls, pillars, and ceiling — was carved with hundreds of giant visages, each deliberately and beautifully flawed: a missing ear here, a split lip there, an unfinished cheek left rough and raw. At the center sat the stone giant elder Decha, gray and still as the rock he had spent his life shaping, staring unblinking into a crystal the size of a war shield — a perfectly symmetrical, flawless carving of the stone giant god Skoraeus Stonebones. The surrounding stone giants grieved in motionless silence. Grek explained that to carve a perfect face was to carve a tomb, for the carver would see nothing left to improve and stop forever.

Neither envoy spoke Giant or Undercommon, so Grek translated. Felkur discerned that the deliberate flaws were religious doctrine and that the other giants understood the problem but could not bring themselves to deface their god's image. A small amount of damage would suffice. While Daein considered how to proceed, Felkur produced a pocket bottle of beer and hurled it at the flawless crystal statue. His natural 20 sent it into precisely the wrong place for restraint: both bottle and sacred artifact shattered. Decha collapsed in shock as the object into which he had poured his soul broke into a thousand pieces. Grek and the surrounding giants stared in horror, and Felkur remarked, “I think I broke him.” With the stone giant alliance lost, the envoys fled before their hosts recovered.

The party ended the session intending to approach the Nerevenans on the remaining third day. Felkur explained afterward that he had hoped merely to splash beer onto the statue, while Daein imposed a simple restriction: “No more beer, Felkur, no more beer.”

## NPCs

A drow ally who, in the previous chapter, scouted ahead and dealt with duergar guards blocking the party’s path. His exchange with Rylzyn distinguished dead overseers from two others whose fate remained unclear. In this chapter, he and Rylzyn field Rilira’s demands for weapons and invoke Quil’bryn’s plan.

### Rylzyn
A drow associate of the party who was once a prisoner in the slave pens and has a deep blood feud with the local duergar, having bought his freedom by killing several of Grundar’s cousins. In the previous chapter he reached toward his sword when Grundar confronted him. In this chapter he joins Sorn in discussing how to arm Rilira’s followers.

### Grundar
A duergar prisoner with a blood feud against Rylzyn who, in the previous chapter, accepted a guarantee of escape in exchange for cooperation. His agreement revealed a large group of armed duergar hidden nearby, and he guided the party toward Rilira. His hunger for freedom outweighed his desire for revenge.

### Rilira
A haughty and imperious drow noblewoman imprisoned in the slave pens. In the previous chapter she demanded the deaths of Korkan and the fire giant overseer before pledging her followers’ support, and warned the party there was nowhere safe if they betrayed her.

After their victory, she demands weapons for her followers and dismisses Quil'bryn with contempt that reflects drow gender politics. She considers Korkan useful only in the manner of a dog and rejects the possibility that drow would submit to hobgoblin rule. Her followers will assist against Maerimydra once armed through the liberation of Szith Morcane.

### Korkan
An arrogant hobgoblin who, in the previous chapter, attempted to seize leadership of the slave uprising. He threatened the party if it supported Rilira, then Daein manipulated him into leading a suicidal charge against the fire giant. On realizing the betrayal he attacked Bramgrim before dying in the cleric’s divine spirits. In this chapter, Rilira’s remarks reveal how little regard she had for him.

### Quil'bryn
A drow leader who provided the party with information on how to contact the Hidden. Rylzyn and Sorn mention his plan during the discussion of arming the freed slaves, but the weapons question remains unresolved. Rilira sneers at the mention of him.

His practical instruction is to wait on the webs until the Hidden make contact, rather than attempt to locate them directly.

### Dengor
The fire giant king currently occupying the palace in Szith Morcane, identified as the primary obstacle the party must overcome to liberate the city. His palace is marked Area F. Defeating him and returning the city to drow control are conditions of Hamadh's promised support.

### Hamadh
The leader of the Hidden, a group of Vhaeraunite rebels. He is cautious and values stealth and cunning over brute force, requiring the party to prove its worth by stealing from a giant-held weapon cache before pledging his support.

He first tests their political commitments, accepting that they have promised drow governance without choosing a ruling faction. He warns that choosing the wrong side could make them targets of the others. Although the cache mission results in an alarm and a fight, its successful completion impresses him enough to secure a conditional alliance.

### Grek
A Galeb Duhr with a partially carved face who acts as a guide and translator for the party among the stone giants. He explains the doctrine of imperfection central to the Lodge of Faces and was exiled by the stone giant elder Decha for being imperfect.

His right half bears a beautifully rendered stone giant face with a shattered nose, while the other half remains rough and unfinished. His concern for his appearance is unusual for an elemental. He remembers the Lodge as honorable before its obsession with stonework caused it to overlook the fire giants' actions. He reacts with horror when Felkur shatters the sacred crystal.

### Decha
The stone giant elder and king of the Lodge of Faces who became catatonic after carving a perfectly symmetrical, flawless crystal statue of his god, unable to look away from it.

He sits before the crystal on its plinth while his people grieve, unable to deface the sacred image themselves. When Felkur destroys the work into which Decha has poured his soul, the elder collapses in shock. His eventual recovery or response is not shown before the envoys flee.

Rilira points out that the ruins provide another approach toward Dengor's palace, and the party can address the forge during its eventual assault.

The seat of power for the fire giant king Dengor, located in a fortified area of Szith Morcane that the party must eventually assault. It is marked Area F on the reviewed map. The vast, spidery network of Szith Morcane where Bramgrim and Akritas waited to be contacted by the Vhaeraunite rebels known as the Hidden. Following Quil'bryn's advice, they deliberately make themselves conspicuous until a scout approaches.

### Gnome Ruins
A small set of ruins within Szith Morcane where Hamadh, leader of the Hidden, conducts secret meetings with potential allies. The scout directs Bramgrim and Akritas here for a meeting four hours after their initial contact.

### Giant-held Weapon Cache
A secure storage area within the tunnels of Szith Morcane, protected by a magical Faerzress-warded lock and guarded by patrols of azers and a hellhound. A glowing rune lattice marks the entrance, and opening the lock carelessly triggers a magical alert.

The approaching patrol reaches the cache through a tunnel where the envoys stage their defense. Hamadh requires the visitors to recover its contents as proof of their abilities; the exact recovered goods are not specified.

### The Cathedral of Faces
A vast, dark gallery within the Lodge of Faces where every wall, pillar, and surface is carved with hundreds of intentionally flawed giant visages. The stone giant elder Decha was found here, entranced by a perfect crystal statue.

Its carved faces include giants, gods, and strangers. Missing ears, split lips, uneven eyes, and unfinished cheeks express doctrine rather than careless craftsmanship. The flawless crystal sits on a central plinth while grieving stone giants remain at the gallery's edge.
