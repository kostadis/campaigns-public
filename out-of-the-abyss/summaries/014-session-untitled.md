# Chapter 014

Date: 2025-04-07

## Summary
The party began by taking stock of their situation, rifling through what little they had recovered during their escape: a bedroll, rope, tinderbox, torches, and a waterskin. They used the store credit provided to them to stock up on essentials. The credit came to 175 gold pieces, given as an advance and good only at this particular store, for common potions and scrolls, and priced from the GM's "Sane Magical Prices" price book. They bought a week's worth of rations and a generous supply of healing potions to see them through whatever dangers lay ahead in the Underdark. Gyrgum first hoped to buy scrolls of Thunderwave and Heroism, but learned that he could only cast scrolls of spells on his own class list. He instead acquired a scroll containing a powerful divine bolt of radiant energy, reserving the remainder of his funds for future needs. The shopkeeper was visibly disappointed that "of all the adventures in all the world, she gets the two who don't need any magic, who don't need any weapons."

Their patron, Captain Errde Blackskull of the Stone Guard, pulled them aside before they departed and asked them to look into a disturbing rumor circulating through the city of Gracklstugh. She introduced her subordinate, Grimholl Forgebrand, who described finding bodies with no visible wounds: no sword cuts, no burns, no magical residue, nothing at all to explain why the victims were dead. The clerics could not revive them. Errde was deeply skeptical of the legend behind the killings, a supposed order of psionic assassins known as the Empty-Scabbard Killers or the Forak-Erach-Naek. Still, she admitted that two members of her own clan had died under these same mysterious circumstances, and that she herself had found one of the bodies. She believed the myth was being weaponized by corrupt forces within the city to cover their tracks. She pointed the party toward the Whorlstone Tunnels beneath Gracklstugh as a possible hiding place for whoever, or whatever, was responsible.

After a night spent crammed into a shared room with bunk beds, the party woke to find that the Topsy-Turvy twins had quietly slipped away during the night. Jimjar urged the group to gather their things and make for Blingdenstone, pointing out that they were finally equipped and ready to leave. But Daz, curious about the dragon and the Keepers of the Flame, insisted they first pay the Keepers a visit. Gathering intelligence, he argued, was just as important as stocking up. The rest of the party agreed, and they set off through the stone corridors of Gracklstugh.

They never made it far. At the end of a pier on the Darklake Docks, they spotted a Duergar merchant standing alone. Two figures suddenly shimmered into visibility beside him: masked, hooded assassins who drove glowing blades into the merchant with vicious precision. Thorin immediately coveted the glowing weapons. Without hesitation, the party charged, and dubbed themselves "Firefly Tactics" on the way in.

Gyrgum rushed forward and tried to shove one of the killers into the water, but the assassin held his ground. Daz unleashed a torrent of magical energy that tore into one of the attackers for nine damage. Zalthir conjured a sphere of impenetrable magical darkness over the pier, blinding the assassins and leveling the playing field. His first strike missed on a natural 1, but he spent a focus point to strike again and killed one assassin outright. Within the darkness:
- Thorin salvaged a miss with a Precision Attack and landed a precise blow for six damage.
- Gyrgum channeled radiant holy energy through his mace.
- Daz hurled three searing rays of fire into the black void.

When the darkness lifted, both assassins lay dead. The glowing blades they had wielded had vanished entirely, dissolving back into the psychic energy from which they had been conjured, much to the party's collective disappointment.

Gyrgum knelt beside the wounded merchant and spoke a quick prayer, closing the man's wounds. Thorin cautioned that the party had gone in "guns blazing" without knowing who the villain really was. The merchant, Werz, claimed to have no idea why he had been targeted. He thanked them profusely and invited them to meet him at the Shattered Spire to receive a proper reward.

Before leaving the scene, the party searched the bodies and found eighteen silver pieces, a trinket, a pair of bloodstained gloves, and, somewhat absurdly, a box of scented candles. More importantly, they discovered a fish-skin parchment bearing a charcoal likeness of Werz himself, confirming that the attack had been a deliberate, pre-planned assassination. Gyrgum shoved the corpses off the pier into the dark water below, and the party slipped away before the city guard could arrive.

After a brief rest to recover their strength, during which Daz used Arcane Recovery to regain a second-level spell slot, the party was escorted by Duergar priests through the stone corridors of the Flowstone District toward the territory of the Keepers of the Flame. They traveled on the strength of their permit through a district inhabited by no one but Duergar.

The ground trembled before they arrived, and a thunderous voice rolled through the cavern as a massive red dragon came into view. His scales were the color of cooling lava, and his bright yellow eyes gleamed in the shadows. He announced himself with the joke, "You didn't say I was having surfacers for dinner tonight." The priests introduced him with great reverence as Themberchaud, the Wyrmsmith of Gracklstugh, Father of Flame. He was enormous, and clearly well-fed, carrying the unmistakable look of a predator who had not hunted in a very long time.

Themberchaud dismissed the priests, who were visibly uncomfortable at leaving him alone with strangers, and led the party into his private lair. There he threatened to turn anyone invisible to ash, thrashed his wings about, and breathed fire in every direction before settling onto a mountain of gold. He then interrogated each member of the party in turn:
- He dismissed Gyrgum's devotion to Bahamut with a curt "Next!"
- He expressed mild disdain for Zalthir's brass dragonborn heritage.
- He showed a flicker of approval for Daz's Drow origins, from Menzoberranzan, and his capacity for secrecy.
- When Thorin explained that he had simply been minding his own business before waking up in a jail cell surrounded by Drow and strangers, the dragon seemed almost amused. He was delighted that the party had "nobody to be blackmailed."

Without waiting for the party to formally agree, Themberchaud declared them his personal agents, summoned his liaison Gartokkar, and presented each of them with a golden badge bearing his own profile.

Gartokkar then drew the party aside and revealed the true political landscape of Gracklstugh. The Keepers' power derived "in equal measure from our psionic abilities, the leadership of the Deepking, and our influence over Themberchaud." The dragon had no idea that they were already planning to replace him. A red dragon egg, intended to hatch his successor, had been stolen by a shadowy organization known as the Gray Ghosts, and the Keepers had been waging a quiet war against the Thieves Guild ever since.

When Thorin revealed that Themberchaud wanted the party to report directly to him, Gartokkar was grateful for the tip. He insisted that the old dragon would not be disposed of but "set free." Gartokkar also described a great psionic disturbance rippling through the Underdark, a sensation like a hole being torn through the world, which he believed the Gray Ghosts were responsible for. The party's lore checks failed to turn up any further clue about it.

He tasked the party with tracking down a derro messenger named Droki, who carried messages and supplies for the Gray Ghosts, and with recovering whatever powerful magic had caused the disturbance. To sweeten the arrangement, he gave the party a holy symbol of Laduguer and gold pins bearing Themberchaud's profile, granting them passage beyond the Darklake District. He made clear, with a thin smile, that refusal would result in the Stone Guards inventing an appropriate crime to charge them with.

The party accepted the mission, though not without pushing back. Thorin provocatively suggested that perhaps the Stone Guards ought to know about the stolen egg, sending Gartokkar into barely concealed alarm. When pressed on whether they would return the egg to the Keepers once found, Thorin simply replied that they would decide what to do with it when the time came. That answer left Gartokkar calling them "funny" through gritted teeth, and led to a brief exchange about everyone's height.

The party now found themselves operating on three fronts at once:
- as agents of the dragon who didn't know he was being replaced,
- as servants of the faction that was replacing him,
- and as investigators for a Stone Guard captain who was hunting the same derro they had just been sent to find.

Thorin was already floating the idea of escaping "in a blaze of glory on the back of a dragon." Whatever came next, it was clear that Gracklstugh's web of secrets was only going to grow more tangled.

## NPCs
### Errde Blackskull
Captain of the Stone Guard and the party's patron in Gracklstugh, who provides the party with store credit and tasks them with investigating the Empty-Scabbard Killers. She is deeply skeptical of the legend, believing the myth is being exploited to cover up government corruption, though she is personally motivated by the mysterious deaths of two members of her own clan. She is also hunting the derro messenger Droki, the same agent the Keepers of the Flame have now sent the party after.

She personally found one of the bodies, a clan member in the prime of health, and bristled when Gyrgum asked who had discovered it. She is consistent in her conspiracy thinking: "the powers that be know that I am onto their corruption." She refers to her subordinates, awkwardly, as "resources."

### Grimholl Forgebrand
A subordinate of Errde, also known as Hulg to his friends. The party, not being his friends, is to call him Grimholl. He was brought in to describe the mysterious deaths he discovered: bodies with no visible wounds, no magical residue, and no signs of trauma. He firmly believes they are the work of the psionic Empty-Scabbard Killers, who, he says, cast invisible blades with their minds.

### Jimjar
A member of the party's traveling crew who encourages the group to head toward Blingdenstone now that they have supplies and equipment. He points out that they "could just get up and leave today," but is fine with further investigation.

### Werz
A Duergar merchant who was specifically targeted and viciously attacked by hooded psionic assassins at the pier. He was saved by the party and, after being healed, invited them to the Shattered Spire to receive a proper reward. A fish-skin parchment bearing his charcoal likeness was found on one of the assassins, confirming he was a deliberate target.

He claims to have "no idea why I was targeted by assassins." His clan affiliation remains unknown to the party.

### Psionic Assassins
Two masked and hooded killers who used invisibility and glowing psionic blades to attack the merchant Werz at the pier. Their weapons vanished upon their deaths, confirming the blades were psychic in nature. Their bodies were found to carry silver coins, a trinket, bloodstained gloves, scented candles, and the fish-skin parchment targeting Werz.

### Themberchaud
The Wyrmsmith of Gracklstugh, a massive red dragon with lava-colored scales and glowing yellow eyes. He is arrogant, overweight from lack of hunting, and speaks of himself in the third person. He recruited the party as his personal agents without waiting for their acceptance, and is unaware that the Keepers of the Flame are planning to replace him with a new dragon hatched from a stolen egg.

His other traits, as shown at the table:
- He is paranoid about invisible spies.
- He is contemptuous of Bahamut and dismissive of brass dragons.
- He approves of Drow for their "conspiracies and secrecy."
- He is pleased by agents with no one to blackmail.
- He doesn't play chess, but "another kind of game."

### Gartokkar
A high-ranking member of the Keepers of the Flame who serves as a liaison between Themberchaud and the Duergar leadership. He revealed to the party that the Keepers secretly control the dragon and are desperate to recover a stolen red dragon egg before Themberchaud discovers he is being groomed for replacement. He threatened the party with fabricated charges if they refused the mission.

He believes the Gray Ghosts caused the psionic disturbance in the Underdark. He claims the old dragon will be "set free" once replaced. He tolerates Thorin's needling with a mix of alarm and grudging amusement.

### Droki
A derro agent for the Gray Ghosts Thieves Guild who carries messages and supplies for the organization. He is the party's primary lead in investigating the stolen dragon egg and the psionic disturbance in the Underdark, and is also being sought by the Stone Guard captain Errde.

A Duergar city in the Underdark where the party is navigating a web of local politics, corruption, and competing factions including the Stone Guard and the Keepers of the Flame. Here the Empty-Scabbard Killers are a folktale told to keep children quiet at night.

### Whorlstone Tunnels
An extensive network of tunnels located beneath the city of Gracklstugh, rumored to be a hiding place for the Empty-Scabbard Killers and potentially the Gray Ghosts. Errde: "if you were going to be such an organization, that's a place you might go."

### Gracklstugh Lodgings
A cramped, low-cost establishment where the party shared a single room with bunk beds for five copper pieces per person per night. It is the same place the rest of their crew had been put up.

### Darklake Docks
A stone pier on the Darklake in Gracklstugh where the party intercepted two psionic assassins in the act of attacking the merchant Werz. Lingering too long here would draw Stone Guards and other guards.

### The Shattered Spire
A tavern on the Darklake where the merchant Werz asked the party to meet him for a reward for saving his life, either later that day or the next.

### Themberchaud's Lair
A massive cavern in the Flowstone District of Gracklstugh where the red dragon Themberchaud rests upon a mountain of gold. The party was brought here in private to receive the dragon's offer of employment. It is reached through Duergar-only territory, past a stone building outside a huge entrance.

### Darklake District
A district of Gracklstugh that the party now has permission to travel beyond, thanks to the golden badges, the holy symbol of Laduguer and the gold pins provided by the Keepers of the Flame, though they remain limited in moving freely through the city.

## Items
### Adventuring Pack
A collection of essential survival gear including a bedroll, rope, tinderbox, torches, and a waterskin, recovered by the party during their escape prior to this session.

### Rations
A week's worth of preserved food purchased by the party to sustain them during their travels in the Underdark. Thorin added them at the GM's suggestion.

### Potions of Healing
Common magical tonics used to restore vitality. The party ended up with nine of these after shopping with their store credit, distributing them among the group for use during their upcoming journey. The table's count was nine: two already on hand, two each from Daz and Thorin ("So that's plus four"), and three added by the GM. The GM called it "enough potions of healing for the rest of this adventure."

### Scroll of Guiding Bolt
A magical parchment containing the clerical spell Guiding Bolt, purchased by Gyrgum to bolster the party's offensive capabilities. He chose it after learning that Thunderwave and Heroism were off-limits because they aren't on his class list.

### Soul Blade
Glowing, psionic weapons wielded by the hooded assassins at the pier. The blades vanished into thin air the moment their wielders were slain, confirming they were conjured from psychic energy rather than being physical objects.

### Fish-Skin Parchment
A piece of parchment made from fish skin found on one of the assassins, bearing a charcoal likeness of the merchant Werz. Its discovery confirmed that Werz was a deliberate, pre-planned target.

### Bloodstained Gloves
A pair of gloves recovered from the bodies of the psionic assassins, grimly coated in blood. They are not magical.

### Scented Candles
A box of candles found among the assassins' gear, apparently used for relaxation, a surprisingly mundane possession for a pair of killers. They are not magical, but they are scented.

### Golden Badge
A badge made of real gold featuring the profile of Themberchaud, given to each party member to signify their status as agents of the Wyrmsmith. It is not magical.

### Holy Symbol of Laduguer
A religious icon provided to the party by the Keepers of the Flame. Along with the golden badges, it grants the party the legal right to travel beyond the Darklake District of Gracklstugh. It was given alongside gold pins bearing Themberchaud's profile, brought by an acolyte.

### Red Dragon Egg
A highly valuable and politically sensitive egg stolen by the Gray Ghosts from the Keepers of the Flame. It was intended to hatch a replacement for Themberchaud, and its recovery is the central mission the Keepers have assigned to the party. Themberchaud himself is unaware of its existence.

## Spells
### Magic Missile
Daz fired multiple bolts of magical energy that unerringly struck one of the psionic assassins during the ambush at the pier, for nine damage. It could not be used later, because it requires seeing the target inside magical darkness.

### Darkness
Zalthir conjured a 15-foot radius sphere of magical shadow during the pier ambush, blinding all creatures within it regardless of darkvision. The sphere proved tactically decisive, allowing the party to fight within it while the assassins were disoriented. Allies inside it attacked with disadvantage, except Thorin, who used blindsight.

### True Strike
Gyrgum channeled this magic to guide his mace strike against the remaining assassin within the sphere of darkness, dealing two points of radiant damage. The GM confirmed that under the current rules the attack is part of casting the spell.

### Scorching Ray
Daz hurled three separate rays of fire into the magical darkness, rolling to hit for each ray individually at disadvantage and targeting squares. The combined strikes, of four and then six damage, proved enough to finish off the last assassin.

### Healing Word
Gyrgum uttered a quick prayer to close the wounds of the merchant Werz immediately following the battle at the pier, restoring enough of his health that Werz expressed sincere gratitude.

### Minor Illusion
Discussed as a cantrip Zalthir could use to conjure the soothing sounds of whales to accompany a bath with the scented candles recovered from the assassins. It was a lighthearted moment of levity after the combat. Zalthir: "I can make a minor illusion of your whale sounds right now."
