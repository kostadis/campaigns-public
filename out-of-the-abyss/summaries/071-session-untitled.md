# Chapter 71

Date: 2026-10-05

## Summary

The chase was brief. Zalthir, faster than the fleeing halfling-shaped figure by a wide margin, caught up to Edvaldo on the bridge spanning the lava pit below Candlekeep's vault. The doppelganger's eyes went wide with fear as he was seized, his gaze darting down to the molten depths beneath them—the same fate that had claimed another intruder not long before. He made his desperation plain immediately: “I watched where you put the last one. I would very much like not to go there.” Zalthir, unmoved but calculating, began dragging him back across the bridge and up into the tower.

As Zalthir hauled the doppelganger upstairs, Edvaldo wasted no time making his case for survival. He reminded the party that he had been the one to warn them about the glyphs in the library—that without his intervention, they might have triggered a catastrophic magical trap. He wanted that entered into whatever “book of good deeds” the party kept. His fuller argument was blunt: “Kill me, and I go gray and quiet, and you've got a robe and a story.” He claimed his long service to Manshoon made him valuable. He warned that Manshoon was not truly dead and was already learning what had happened.

Edvaldo asked for the same arrangement given to A'lai: confinement in a room where magic could not reach him. “A'lai got a room with no magic in it. I would like the room next door. I'll be a very quiet neighbor, but lock me up, ask your questions there, and if one answer doesn't hold, the lava will still be here.” When the party questioned whether a doppelganger could escape by assuming an absurdly small form, Edvaldo indignantly clarified that doppelgangers could range from halfling to Goliath but could not become objects. “As a doppelganger, I feel it's my job to explain to you that no, doppelgangers cannot turn into pizza slices.”

To prove his value further, he demonstrated the full unsettling range of his abilities. He assumed Thorin's appearance and then shifted his face into one pulled directly from Thorin's childhood, a visage no stranger should have known. Thorin's recollection of obscure lore confirmed that doppelgangers were both face changers and mind readers. It was a chilling display, though it did little to make Edvaldo appear trustworthy. Glabbagool watched the discussion with unusual interest, drawing its eye sockets closer together as the party speculated about whether a sentient ooze might someday learn similar transformations.

While Zalthir managed the prisoner, Daz had been doing something far more dangerous on his own. Using a spectral, floating hand conjured at a careful distance, he turned the pages of the Book of Vile Darkness—an artifact of absolute evil discovered behind a hidden door in the vault. Daz had asked the book how to defeat the enemy, and its successive pages answered with cryptic revelations concerning the demonic threat, the “rotting lady,” and secrets it plainly wanted him to pursue. Daz narrated and shared the messages with the others through the Roll20 chat while continuing to turn pages without touching the artifact himself.

The book eventually demanded more. To reach a later page, Daz would have to turn it with his own hand rather than his mage hand. Kalan warned him that a non-evil creature touching the Book of Vile Darkness would suffer catastrophic necrotic damage, and Daz's considerable arcane knowledge told him that a simple shield spell would offer no meaningful protection. The safest way not to be killed by a book was not to touch it. Daz ultimately chose caution and later used his mage hand to close and clasp the volume.

Meanwhile, Gyrgum sat with the four remaining prophecy crystals, turning them over in his hands and examining every surface—including the top and bottom, as though looking for a price tag—for some clue as to how they worked. He had already identified them as vessels for the prophecies of Alaundo, the legendary seer of Candlekeep, but the mechanism for unlocking their voices eluded him. He also gathered the surviving pieces of the two crystals Edvaldo had destroyed during the preceding confrontation, hoping the fragments might still offer a clue.

The quiet of the vault was broken when lamplight appeared on the stairs. Gyrgum readied his crossbow while Zalthir maintained an active grapple on Edvaldo, having learned from an earlier attempt that ordinary bindings were unreliable against a creature able to change size. A procession of Candlekeep's senior scholars arrived: Fheminor Scrivenbark, the halfling Great Reader widely expected to succeed the late Janussi; the prophecy scholar Inda; Teles Ahvoste; and Kalan Strongbranch, the Gatewarden.

Kalan's paranoia had reached a fever pitch following the revelation that a doppelganger had walked among them. Before allowing Inda to explain the crystals, he demanded, “Before anyone tells me anything, who here is who they say they are, and how would you prove it to me?” Gyrgum replied, “I don't think I've made any claims as to who I am,” while Thorin observed that the scholars had barely known the party a week ago. Kalan proposed consulting the dragon on the bridge who could not lie, prompting the party to point out that an honest dragon was not necessarily an omniscient one.

After an academic digression over which spell would settle the matter—and whether anyone had prepared one—Fheminor used a powerful spell of true sight, showing the party were not disguised shapechangers and revealing Edvaldo's true form. Thorin still dryly asked the scholars to consider what they would actually have done if the party had proved to be doppelgangers. The answer was simply, “We had plans.”

Fheminor's next concern was the library. She asked how many books had burned and carefully recorded the answer: two prophecies lost, zero books burned. Gyrgum explained that he had deliberately ensured the fighting stayed out of the library. Fheminor was delighted, remarking that this judgment demonstrated precisely why Gyrgum deserved to be a Reader of Candlekeep. Gyrgum's reasoning was more practical: “Well, you know, if I let other people's books get burned, maybe they'll burn mine. Self-defense mechanism.”

With identities confirmed and the immediate crisis contained, Inda turned her attention to the crystals. She showed Gyrgum how to make them speak, and he stepped forward to do the honors. As each stone activated, Alaundo's voice repeated its prophecy while the scholars scribbled furiously. The privilege carried enormous academic weight for Inda, though Gyrgum compared his delivery skills to a dramatic reading of “it's decorative gourd season, motherfuckers.”

The first prophecy referenced a prince who was not a prince—a figure the party recognized as Prince Derendil, the Quaggoth who had believed himself royalty and died during their escape from captivity. It contained enough familiar events that Gyrgum remarked, “I've never been prophesied about before. It's special.” A second prophecy further increased his confidence: “I trust the second one a little bit more now that I know the 1st one's true.” Inda immediately corrected him: Alaundo's prophecies were always true; that was what made them special.

The party learned that the crystals had not necessarily been stored in chronological order. Several prophecies had already been spoken before they reached the chamber. Edvaldo had destroyed two of the six stones, leaving four intact. The intact stones therefore could not provide a reliable, uninterrupted sequence. Their known prophecies nevertheless included references to the party, a sword afraid of the dark, a question of what to keep, and a crow whose choices would matter to a city.

The fourth prophecy spoke of a crow who would be offered a name and must choose to wear it, refuse it, or burn it, with the city itself remembering the choice. It continued: “The city has a clock, lungs, heart, and all three can be made to burn. The city will burn. This is not a curse. This is the whole of my prophecy.” The scholars quickly identified the crow as Daz, interpreting his dark Drow complexion through Alaundo's fondness for animal similes. Their agreement immediately dissolved into a familiar academic dispute over whether that interpretation was truly established.

As the prophecies were absorbed and debated, Edvaldo continued his campaign for survival from across the room. He repeatedly pointed out that the people with the authority to arrange his anti-magic cell were now standing nearby. When the party noted that the prophecy suggested they would receive only one truth from him, Edvaldo protested that he had not yet said anything valuable—and then tied himself in knots insisting that this did not mean his earlier statements had been lies. “I'm a good… I'm, you know, I'm a professional. You're professionals, we're all professional here. This was not personal.”

When pressed to offer something of genuine value, Edvaldo finally revealed the full scope of Manshoon's plan. The legendary wizard had not wanted the Book of Vile Darkness merely to become “slightly more powerful”; Manshoon did not play for such small stakes. He wanted specific rites contained within it. Since the Sundering, summoning a Demon Lord into the Material Plane required enormous, conspicuous rituals that tended to attract bands of meddling adventurers. Edvaldo said someone had already brought all the Demon Lords into the Material Plane. The book's rites would have allowed Manshoon's mastery of necromancy to seize control of several of them, creating the army with which he intended to pursue his ambitions.

To get inside Candlekeep's vault, Manshoon had consumed extraordinary resources. He burned every charge of a staff of power and combined it with enchanted iron gloves and additional spells to penetrate a small section of the vault's defenses. Those resources were expended before he entered and could not simply be recovered. The Avowed promised that the vault would now be sealed properly, without convenient trapdoors or escape routes. Ten years of preparation had gone up in smoke in a single night. Edvaldo said they had A'lai ready as part of that plan.

Edvaldo conceded that the party had thwarted Manshoon and acquired a permanent enemy, although he claimed Manshoon had initially been willing to make a deal. Had the party handed over the book and stayed out of his way, Manshoon supposedly would not have targeted them. Thorin and Gyrgum pointed out that the party was unlikely to remain out of the way while cities were being destroyed. When Edvaldo complained again about a decade of wasted preparations, Gyrgum answered, “Well, it's just clear we're better at our, our, you know, hobby than you are at yours.”

Questioned about Manshoon's lair, Edvaldo admitted that he had never visited it. One did not “hang out” with Manshoon; the great wizard manifested mystically before his servants, sometimes killing people when he appeared. This was another reason Edvaldo coveted an anti-magic cell. He knew little about the party or Manshoon's wider plans because, until the vault disaster, Manshoon had considered them beneath his notice. Nor could Edvaldo explain precisely which Demon Lords were where; as he put it, they were all running around the Underdark “like maniacs.”

An Avowed scholar repeated the familiar account: the faerzress had blinked, and the Demon Lords appeared in the Underdark. The scholar said the blink's cause was still unknown. Fheminor eagerly suggested that if Gyrgum uncovered the answer, they could write a paper together. Another scholar offered Daz a citation—or perhaps a monograph with Candlekeep's demonologist Sylvira Savikas—if he shared what the Book of Vile Darkness had told him. Daz was less concerned with academic credit than with the apparent prospect of his hometown being destroyed.

The scholars also explained that the City of Spiders was another name for Menzoberranzan. It was, the party noted with grim irony, the very city toward which they had originally been transported as prisoners from Velkynvelve before everything had gone sideways. The route toward stopping the Demon Lords now appeared to lead them back there. Zalthir confirmed that Menzoberranzan was an exceptionally hostile place for non-Drow, while Daz jokingly considered promising everyone his protection despite not knowing what awaited him there.

With the larger threat mapped out, attention turned to the vault's remaining contents. The special collection held several powerful magical books, including manuals capable of improving physical or mental attributes and spellbooks containing magic as potent as wish and power word kill. The Avowed made clear that these books belonged to Candlekeep, could not simply be copied, and would not all be handed over as spoils. The party could, however, make a case for one.

Daz felt a strange pull toward the Tome of the Stilled Tongue, a spellbook with a desiccated tongue painted on its cover and tied to Vecna. The tome could facilitate extraordinarily swift spellcasting, but Vecna watched anyone who used it. With an Arcana result of 27, Daz recognized that the desire did not originate wholly within him: it was residual influence from his contact with the Book of Vile Darkness. One of the book's messages returned to him: “When you find what is waiting there, I will know and so will the ones who keep my pages.”

Daz was not blind to the manipulation, but neither was he uninterested in understanding the game. He reasoned that knowing what an evil artifact wanted could itself provide information about what he should or should not do. Thorin compared the recursive reasoning to the battle of wits in *The Princess Bride*. Daz acknowledged the danger but noted that, as a strongly minded and chaotically neutral person, the fact that something was evil did not automatically make its interests less worthy of investigation than those of something good.

Daz made his case to the assembled scholars: “There are actions and powers greater than this institution that have just tried to destroy this institution. We have unwittingly found ourselves in a position to stop them. And so we did. We also now feel that this is just the opening gambit of a much larger move that could actually destroy all of the worlds that we know and love. We need that book.”

Fheminor Scrivenbark agreed. She had long wanted one of Vecna's books removed from Candlekeep and had hoped the intruders might take the Book of Vile Darkness itself, though Kalan had correctly refused to let that artifact leave. The Tome of the Stilled Tongue, however, could go. “At least Vecna's eyes will be on you, not on the next avowed leader who comes downstairs here to read one of these books.”

The fate of Edvaldo was settled with less drama than it might have been. The party weighed his usefulness against the consequences of indefinite imprisonment. Kalan warned that Candlekeep could confine a former Avowed member for a time but its cells were not intended for permanent occupancy; eventually, the scholars might tire of holding him and release him. Thorin did not believe in casually executing a captive, Gyrgum had already promised Edvaldo a cell, and Daz found that Edvaldo was adding more entropy than clarity but might still become useful. Zalthir finally accepted the majority's judgment.

Edvaldo was escorted to a magic-proof cell where he hoped Manshoon could not reach him. In gratitude, he shifted into the form of a Dragonborn Zalthir had once found attractive. The display was met with the weary tolerance of adventurers who had already endured a great deal of strangeness.

The party emerged from the vault to find Candlekeep's scholars waiting in celebration. The ovation began as a building clap and swelled into an enthusiastic crescendo. Candlekeep was somewhat worse for wear, but its protective shell had been restored, once again suppressing dangerous magic such as fireballs and lightning bolts.

Philemon, who had been serving as Thorin's therapist, eagerly requested one more day of therapy. Thorin agreed that he owed it to Dawnbringer, who had herself heard the prophecy in which she appeared. Sister Yvenne was eager to continue Daz's education in Drow history and lore. Brother Khell-Vire was also among those with unfinished business with the party. Brother Vareth was ecstatic: Gyrgum had not merely broken a three-legged academic model into a four-legged quadrangle with his theory concerning the Stroudite controversy, but the prophecies' fixed points now seemed to favor the emerging Gyrgumite school of thought. He wanted to discuss future collaborations and research.

The House of Mechanus requested the return of its borrowed tools and remained eager to study the mechanical symmetry and movement of Zalthir and Glabbagool. Its representatives were delighted that their tools had helped the party reach Alaundo's prophecies, though that did not mean Zalthir and Glabbagool would escape the promised dance maneuvers needed for the study.

Before those arrangements could be completed, a note arrived from Eldeth. The party was summoned to Gauntlgrym: the deep dwarves wished to speak with them. The party immediately recognized the apparent fulfillment of another prophecy. Inda insisted that Alaundo's prophecies always came true, though Thorin observed that no one knew when. Eldeth had brought magical transportation, sparing the party another long overland journey—or, to Thorin's mild disappointment, a dramatic flight on giant eagles.

The weight of what the party had survived was still settling, but the next stage of the journey had already begun. Gauntlgrym called first, and beyond it waited Menzoberranzan: the City of Spiders, their captors' original destination for them and now the city at the center of Alaundo's warnings.

## NPCs

### Edvaldo

A doppelganger who posed as the scholar Edvaldo Sedanur before fleeing in halfling form. He is a mind-reader and shapeshifter who served Manshoon but is now desperately bargaining for his life, seeking a magic-proof cell to escape his master's retribution. He can assume humanoid forms ranging in size from a halfling to a Goliath and claims to acquire the attributes of those forms, but he cannot transform into objects such as pizza slices. He demonstrates his telepathy by becoming Thorin and then assuming a face from Thorin's childhood. He warned the party about the library glyphs and uses that act as evidence that he deserves mercy. He reveals Manshoon's plan to control the Demon Lords and the enormous resources spent infiltrating Candlekeep. He is ultimately spared and placed in Candlekeep's prison, thanking Zalthir by assuming the form of a Dragonborn Zalthir once found attractive.

### Glabbagool

A sentient grey ooze accompanying the party, observing the events with curiosity and reacting with interest when doppelgangers and shapeshifting are discussed. Its eye sockets float closer together as it studies Edvaldo. The party speculates about whether Glabbagool might train itself into other shapes, wear armor and clothing to imitate a humanoid, or someday take a wizard level. The House of Mechanus remains eager to study its movements alongside Zalthir's.

### Manshoon

Manshoon founded and long led the Zhentarim, whose early power was intertwined with Bane’s clergy. After his apparent death or disappearance, his dormant clones awoke and fought the Manshoon Wars. The Manshoon active in this campaign remains alive despite the party’s destruction of two simulacra. He spent a decade preparing to seize control of the Demon Lords using rites found within the Book of Vile Darkness. He burned a staff of power, enchanted iron gloves, and other magical resources to infiltrate Candlekeep, and is now a permanent enemy of the party. Edvaldo describes him as a remote, terrifying master who manifests before his servants rather than meeting them normally and sometimes kills people during those appearances.

### Fheminor Scrivenbark

A Great Reader of Candlekeep and a halfling, intended as Janussi's successor. She arrives with the other senior scholars, shows the party are not disguised shapechangers through true seeing, and carefully records that two prophecies were lost but zero books burned. She is delighted by Gyrgum's decision to protect the library during the fight and considers it proof that Gyrgum deserves to be a Reader. She facilitates the party's request for the Tome of the Stilled Tongue and is relieved to have a dangerous Vecna relic removed from the library. She also hopes to collaborate with Gyrgum on a paper if the party discovers what caused the faerzress to blink.

### Kalan Strongbranch

The Gatewarden of Candlekeep, who arrives at the vault highly suspicious of everyone's identities following the doppelganger revelation. He initially suggests consulting a dragon that cannot lie and becomes embroiled in an academic discussion about which spell can expose shapechangers. He warns the party about the lethal necrotic energy of the Book of Vile Darkness, approves when Daz closes it without touching it, and oversees the renewed security of the vault. He promises that the chamber will be sealed properly and warns that Candlekeep's prison is not intended for indefinite confinement.

### Inda

A scholar at Candlekeep deeply committed to the study and cataloging of the prophecies of Alaundo. She guides the party in activating the prophecy crystals, stresses the privilege of hearing Alaundo's voice, and insists that his prophecies are always true. She is visibly distraught about the broken stones and the resulting gaps in the prophetic sequence. Inda identifies Daz as the “crow,” citing Alaundo's use of animal similes, and participates in the academic debate over the interpretation.

### Sylvira Savikas

A local demonologist at Candlekeep mentioned as someone who would be highly interested in Daz's insights from the Book of Vile Darkness. One of the Avowed suggests that Daz could receive a citation or coauthor a small monograph if he shares what the artifact told him.

### Philemon

An Avowed monk and sentient-artifact specialist who acts as a therapist for Thorin. He approaches Thorin after the party returns from the vault and eagerly asks to complete one more day of therapy, now that Thorin has become one of Candlekeep's celebrated defenders.

### Sister Yvenne

A scholar at Candlekeep who is excited to finish Daz's education regarding his Drow heritage and history. She is waiting for him after the party's emergence from the vault.

### Brother Khell-Vire

A half-elf monk at Candlekeep involved in the party's ongoing education, mentioned during the party's pending scholar meetings; his specific role is not established in this exchange.

### Brother Vareth

An elderly scholar who is ecstatic about Gyrgum's academic contributions and the fulfillment of Alaundo's prophecies. He believes Gyrgum's theory concerning the Stroudite controversy has transformed an established three-legged model into a four-legged quadrangle. He enthusiastically champions the “Gyrgumite school” of thought, sees the prophecies' fixed points as supporting evidence, and wants to discuss future collaborations and research.

### Eldeth

An ally who sends a written note from Gauntlgrym, summoning the party to speak with the deep dwarves. Her message appears to fulfill one of Alaundo's prophecies. She has arranged magical transportation, allowing the party to reach Gauntlgrym by teleportation rather than undertaking another long journey.

## Locations

### The Bridge

A span over a dangerous lava pit within Candlekeep's lower levels, where Zalthir intercepted the fleeing doppelganger Edvaldo. Edvaldo had already seen Zalthir dispose of Manshoon's duplicate over a fiery pit and immediately surrendered rather than risk receiving the same treatment.

### The Vault

A high-security chamber within Candlekeep containing powerful artifacts, prophecy crystals, the Book of Vile Darkness, and a special collection of magical tomes. It is the site of the party's confrontation with Manshoon's agents, Edvaldo's interrogation, and the arrival of Candlekeep's leadership. Manshoon breached its defenses only by expending a staff of power, enchanted iron gloves, and other magic. The Avowed intend to eliminate its vulnerable trapdoors and permanently strengthen its seals.

### Candlekeep

The great library fortress where the party thwarted Manshoon's infiltration. After the battle, its protective shell was re-enabled, again suppressing dangerous magic such as fireballs and lightning bolts. Its scholars celebrated the party's victory with an ovation that built to an enthusiastic crescendo. Candlekeep houses a special vault of powerful magical books that cannot simply be copied or claimed under the library's ordinary access rules.

### Menzoberranzan

Also known as the City of Spiders, this Drow city is identified as a key location in the unfolding prophecies and a later destination after Gauntlgrym. It was the party's original intended destination when they were prisoners at Velkynvelve. It is notoriously hostile to non-Drow, though Daz's role as the prophetic “crow” may give him a unique relationship with the city.

### Gauntlgrym

An ancient dwarven city mentioned in a summons from Eldeth. The deep dwarves there reportedly need to speak with the party, apparently fulfilling one of Alaundo's prophecies. Eldeth has arranged teleportation to bring the party there.

## Items

### Book of Vile Darkness

An artifact of absolute evil found behind a hidden door in the vault. It speaks telepathically to Daz's mind, offering dark secrets and information about defeating the Demon Lords while tempting him to keep reading. Daz asks it how to defeat the enemy and receives a sequence of cryptic pages, including references to the “rotting lady” and the warning, “When you find what is waiting there, I will know and so will the ones who keep my pages.”

It contains rites that Manshoon intended to use to control multiple Demon Lords and turn them into an army. A non-evil creature who physically touches it suffers catastrophic necrotic damage, and ordinary defensive magic such as shield would not prevent that harm. The book eventually demands that Daz turn a page with his physical hand, but he refuses. Its residual influence later causes him to feel a supernatural pull toward other dark relics of Vecna. Daz ultimately closes and clasps it using his mage hand. The Book remains in Candlekeep's custody behind the resealed wards.

### Prophecy Crystals

Crystalline stones containing the recorded prophecies of Alaundo. Edvaldo broke two of the six stones during the preceding confrontation, leaving four intact for the party. Gyrgum gathers the recoverable fragments. When activated by speaking specific words, the crystals reproduce Alaundo's voice while the Avowed transcribe his words.

The surviving stones are not numbered, may have been moved, and cannot be assumed to form an uninterrupted sequence. Their prophecies reference a “prince who is not a prince,” a sword afraid of the dark, a choice about what to keep, and a “crow” who will be offered a name. The crow may wear, refuse, or burn that name, and the city will remember. Another warning describes a city with a clock, lungs, and a heart, all of which can be made to burn.

### Staff of Power

A potent magical artifact belonging to Manshoon that was entirely burned up—all its charges expended—to help break through the vault's protective seals. Its power was combined with enchanted iron gloves and other spells to penetrate Candlekeep's defenses. The staff was consumed before Manshoon's arrival and no longer exists as a functional item.

### Tome of the Stilled Tongue

A macabre spellbook with a desiccated tongue painted on its front cover, identified as a relic of Vecna. It allows the user to cast a spell written within it with extraordinary speed, but Vecna watches anyone who uses it. Daz feels a supernatural pull toward the tome, influenced by his contact with the Book of Vile Darkness. He recognizes that manipulation but remains interested in learning what the artifact wants and why.

After Daz argues that the party stopped a conspiracy greater than Candlekeep and may be the only group positioned to stop the wider threat, Fheminor Scrivenbark allows him to take it. Fheminor is pleased that Vecna's attention will now rest on the party rather than Candlekeep's future leaders.

### Tome of Leadership and Influence

A magical book kept in Candlekeep's special vault that can permanently enhance the reader's Charisma. Gyrgum expresses interest in it, and Fheminor remarks that one of Candlekeep's leaders read it roughly 150 years ago to become a better Reader. She suggests that Janussi might have benefited from doing the same. The party ultimately does not take it.

### Eldeth's Note

A written summons delivered to the party in Candlekeep, calling them to Gauntlgrym. Its message summons the party to speak with the deep dwarves, apparently signaling the fulfillment of one of Alaundo's prophecies. Eldeth has also arranged magical transportation by teleportation.

## Spells

### Mage Hand

A spectral, floating hand used repeatedly by Daz to safely flip the pages of the Book of Vile Darkness, share several of its revelations, and ultimately close and clasp the artifact. It allows him to avoid the catastrophic necrotic damage that would result from direct physical contact. The book eventually refuses to reveal a later page unless Daz uses his actual hand, but he declines.

### True Seeing

Cast by Fheminor Scrivenbark to pierce through illusions and confirm the true forms of those present in the vault, showing the party are not disguised shapechangers and revealing Edvaldo's true form. Its use resolves Kalan's demand that everyone prove they are who they claim to be.
