# Chapter 45 Gnome More Elemental Slavery!

Date: 2026-01-26

## Summary
The party was in a desperate spot, completing an ancient ritual while fending off two basilisks whose petrifying gazes threatened to turn them to stone. Thorin, who speaks Giant, kept the plan and directed the ritual; his spare dart already lay in the circle as the weapon sacrifice. Meanwhile the rest of the party dealt with the reptilian threats. One basilisk stood frozen, its mind held by Daz's Hypnotic Pattern; the GM checked and it got no saving throw to break free. The second basilisk was more dangerous. It sat inside Zalthir's magical darkness, forcing the party to fight without looking at it.

Daz could not see Zalthir in the darkness, so he first asked whether he could shape a Fireball around him. The GM offered to let Zalthir roll with advantage for quarter damage. Daz switched to Scorching Ray instead, which requires targets, so he rolled each ray with disadvantage. All three rays went wildly astray, including a natural 1, and the table joked that Daz was nearly hitting Zalthir. The basilisk snapped at Daz and missed. Zalthir hoped to stack advantages, but the GM ruled that under the rules as written a single source of disadvantage cancels any number of advantages. Zalthir fought on at a straight roll. He missed twice, then landed a 26 to hit for 12 damage, leaving the creature at 4 hit points. His next punch brought it down "in a burning gusto of flame."

The GM noted they only needed three rounds, so the party left the hypnotized basilisk alone. The GM also ruled that a zero-damage punch would not wake it. The party then placed the two gems. The ground rumbled with a deep, soothing vibration, and "that which was wrong about the rocks has now become right." Entémoch's boon was released. It granted the ability to summon Earth Elementals and a homebrew Stone Skin buff, which lets each character turn their skin to stone once per long rest when struck, for the next week of game time.

The victory brought an unexpected complication. Dasco Pickshine was delighted that the gnomes could now hire Earth Elementals for mining and heavy lifting, since "they don't cost a lot other than the summoning spell." Thorin objected strongly. He argued that subjugating these beings as unpaid workers was morally wrong and risked provoking an uprising. The GM obliged by describing a meta-narrative in which the elemental princes rise up against summoners. The party decided to take a stand before Blingdenstone's leaders: Senni Diggermattock, Chief Dorbo, and Chipgrin. The GM reminded them that the Diggermattock clan was already split between the workers and the mining corporations.

Thorin argued that asking the elementals to help defend the realm and then forcing them into hard labor was unconscionable. Chipgrin dismissed Thorin's comparison to steampunk surface gnomes with "those are not deep gnomes," and Thorin's player floated a jab about deep gnomes being "backwards and enslavers," then rolled a 9 and simply stood his ground. Insight checks revealed that tales of the Ember Vanguard's rampage in Gracklstugh had reached Blingdenstone's bars, though the party chose to stay incognito. Gyrgum supported the position on religious grounds, arguing that Bahamut and the good dragons oppose such enslavement. Zalthir spoke of peace and harmony, light and shadow.

Daz delivered perhaps the most cutting argument. He compared the proposed labor system to the slave society of Menzoberranzan and asked whether the gnomes wanted to become like the Drow they had just freed themselves from. With three natural 20s on the party's persuasion checks, the arguments were devastating:

- Senni seized control of the meeting, credited with forming "a coalition of the willing" against the Pudding King.
- The religious leaders went on a 20-minute rant agreeing that enslaving summoned creatures violated the Gnomish gods.
- Senni, speaking for the labor groups rather than the business leaders, shouted "Gnome Labor, not Summoned Labor!"

The party convinced Blingdenstone's leadership that Earth Elementals could be summoned for defense and warfare, but not for building or mining. This upended the city's economic plans and made the party "the toasts of the town" with the working gnomes. Thorin conceded that remuneration for the elementals would have to wait: "one thing at a time."

As Blingdenstone prepared for battle against the Pudding King and his oozes, the GM ran a montage while singing a South Park parody of *Les Misérables*. Kazook Pickshine went off to brew the lichen into a weapon coating, Nomi prepared the summoning, and Chief Dorbo told the Captain of the Wall: "For tomorrow we shall go."

In the midst of this, a traveling merchant named Starlace set up Starlace Curios and Arcana in the Traders' Grotto. The party used their 10% merchant coupon to upgrade before the fight:

- **Daz** bought Dustsight Spectacles of glowing crystal that reveal invisible creatures. The GM later ruled they can also see through Zalthir's magical darkness.
- **Gyrgum** had his mace enchanted to +1 for 360 gold, which left him with exactly 1,024 gold pieces.
- **Thorin** had his shield enchanted to +1 for 270 gold, bringing his AC to 20.
- **The party** sold a set of studded leather armor +2 to Starlace for 600 gold after a persuasion contest in which both sides rolled 20s. Both sides walked away feeling good about the deal.

Finally, the party sought out Valimor Brightgem at The Gilded Sheath to commission a proper scabbard for Dawnbringer, the sentient glowing sword that had been drawing crowds of local children. Valimor offered two options:

- a 600-gold gem-encrusted custom showpiece, or
- a practical Vaultmaster locking scabbard that would hook the sword in securely, since she "doesn't quite have a blade," and prevent theft.

Thorin asked the price of the "Vaultmaster 9000," and Gyrgum chimed in with a whole tiered product line. Valimor's eyes lit up at the marketing possibilities: platform scabbards with replaceable, upgradable gems. Feeling generous, he sold the locking scabbard for 200 gold, or 180 after the discount. Dawnbringer was pleased to finally have a proper home, though the sword muttered about adding some bling. The session closed with the GM ruling on the Dustsight Spectacles. The GM said the heroic speech would come next week as the party heads out to fight, and Thorin's player announced he would be away until March 2nd.

- With the basilisks attacking, Thorin keeps the plan and directs the ritual because he speaks Giant. Gyrgum, who also speaks Giant, offers to back him up.
- One basilisk remains charmed by Hypnotic Pattern, unable to act. The GM checks the spell and confirms it gets no saving throw.
- The second basilisk is inside Zalthir's darkness, so no one can see it.
- Daz asks about sculpting a Fireball around Zalthir. The GM offers advantage on Zalthir's save for quarter damage. The GM explains that the darkness blocks spells that say you must see the creature; a spell aimed at a target or a location can still be fired into it.
- Daz attempts to attack the second basilisk with Scorching Ray but misses all three times with extremely poor rolls. The rays are rolled with disadvantage because he cannot see the target, and the first is a natural 1.
- The basilisk takes a bite at Daz and misses.
- Zalthir engages the second basilisk in melee combat, punching it repeatedly.
- The party discusses game mechanics regarding advantage and disadvantage stacking. Zalthir claims advantage from darkness and from grappling ("being a tavern bro"), but has disadvantage from looking away. The GM rules that under the rules as written one disadvantage cancels any number of advantages, adding that the designers wanted to avoid a Magic: The Gathering-style stacking game.
- Zalthir's attacks: a 15 (miss) and a 12 (miss), then a 26 to hit for 12 damage, leaving the basilisk at 4 HP.
- Zalthir defeats the second basilisk with a final punch, and it dies "in a burning gusto of flame."
- Gyrgum proposes hitting the charmed basilisk with a 5-foot-wide bolt spell from over 30 feet away. The GM points out that damage would wake it and that they only need three rounds.
- The GM rules as a house rule that a zero-damage punch does nothing and would not wake the creature.
- The party decides to leave the charmed basilisk alone rather than risk waking it.
- The party places two gems, causing the ground to rumble with a soothing vibration. "That which was wrong about the rocks has now become right."
- The ritual is completed, releasing Entémoch's boon and granting the party a Stone Skin buff. The GM added this homebrew reward so the payoff was "a little bit more than you get an earth elemental."
- Stone Skin details are clarified: resistance against a specific attack or damage type for one round, once per long rest, for the next week of in-game time. Zalthir volunteers to remind everyone.
- The party gains the ability to summon Earth Elementals for the gnomes of Blingdenstone. The GM notes the miners are happy they can now hire Earth Elementals for mining and work.

- The party discusses the potential for an elemental uprising if elementals are used as forced labor. The GM describes a meta-narrative, from an adventure of his own, in which the princes of the elements rise up against summoners.

## NPCs
### Basilisk
Two reptilian creatures with petrifying gazes encountered by the party. One was charmed by Hypnotic Pattern and remained immobile throughout the encounter; it got no saving throw, and the party left it untouched once they learned damage would wake it. The other lurked inside Zalthir's magical darkness. It bit at Daz and missed, and was defeated by Zalthir through repeated punches, dying "in a burning gusto of flame."

### Kazook Pickshine
A gnome in Blingdenstone who prepares potions from lichen to cover weapons for the upcoming battle against the Pudding King. He takes the lichen the party found to brew the weapon coating.

### Dasco Pickshine
Overseer of the Pickshine Mines. He is excited about the prospect of using Earth Elementals, as the party solved his Galeb Duhr problem. He points out that elementals "don't cost a lot other than the summoning spell."

### Chief Dorbo Diggermattock
The leader of the Diggermattock clan in Blingdenstone. He appears anxious during the party's meeting and is very relieved to see them. He is initially unhappy with the party's stance on elemental labor due to its impact on "gross margins," but ultimately agrees to the new policy. After the meeting the gnomes see him as the one who got them into the mess. He instructs the Captain of the Wall to prepare troops for combat against the Pudding King: "For tomorrow we shall go."

### Senni Diggermattock
A representative of the workers and people of Blingdenstone who do the mining. She is enthusiastic about the party's proposal to prevent the use of Earth Elementals for hard labor. She sees it as addressing fundamental problems with an economy built on summoned creatures: "this is exactly the problem with that, an economy built on summoned creatures." She seizes control of the meeting from Chief Dorbo after the party's persuasive arguments, credited with bringing Chipgrin aboard and forming "a coalition of the willing" to save Blingdenstone from the Pudding King.

### Chipgrin
Chipgrin Goldwhisker is the gnome in charge of the Goldwhisker wererat gnomes. When Thorin invoked inventive, "steampunky" gnomes, Chipgrin retorted, "those are not deep gnomes." His initial thoughts on elemental labor are swayed by the party's arguments during the meeting.

### Nomi Pathshutter
A gnome in Blingdenstone who prepares spells to summon Earth Elementals for the impending combat against the Pudding King: "I will prepare the spells to summon an earth elemental to help us fight."

### Captain of the Wall
A military leader in Blingdenstone who is instructed by Chief Dorbo to prepare his troops for combat against the Pudding King.

### The Pudding King
The primary antagonist, preparing his oozes for an upcoming battle against Blingdenstone. The GM shows this in the pre-battle montage.

### Starlace
A traveling magic merchant who sets up a store called Starlace Curios and Arcana in Blingdenstone's market. She offers various magical items, enchantments, potions, and services including magic item recharging and minor gear enchantment. She had no scabbards but pointed the party elsewhere in the market. She can enchant at most three items because of limited supplies. She is willing to buy magic items from adventurers and engages in negotiations over pricing. She praised the studded leather +2 as "a fantastic piece of armor" and muttered after the sale in a way that left it unclear who she thought the sucker was.

### Valimor Brightgem
A renowned gnome blacksmith in Blingdenstone who runs The Gilded Sheath, famous throughout the Underdark for his distinctive and polished scabbards. He leverages the presence of gem mines in his craft and travels for business. He steered Thorin toward the practical locking scabbard. He becomes excited by Thorin's marketing ideas about product numbering and platform-based business models ("opened my eyes to the possibility of marketing and financial management"), though he was offended by the titanium model. He offers the party a generous discount on a Vaultmaster locking scabbard for Dawnbringer.

### Dawnbringer
A sentient, glowing sword wielded by Thorin that appears like a flaming lightsaber. The sword is famous and impresses locals, particularly children, and is "totally digging" the attention. Dawnbringer expresses her own desires and preferences, initially wanting a fancy, over-the-top scabbard but ultimately being pleased to receive the Vaultmaster locking scabbard, while muttering about adding bling. The sword would also disapprove of Thorin buying a two-handed maul. She is described as kind and feminine in nature. At this session the table treated her as genderless; the party later settled on she.

## Locations
### Blingdenstone
A gnomish city in the Underdark where the party has been staying. The city is preparing for battle against the Pudding King and his oozes. The GM characterizes the city as intensely mercantile: "everybody in Blingdenstone is about the margin." The city has a market area called the Traders' Grotto where merchants set up shops.

A newly arrived traveling magic merchant's store in the Blingdenstone market (the Traders' Grotto), offering various magical items, enchantments, and potions. The merchant Starlace offers services including potion sales, spell scrolls, magic item recharging, and minor gear enchantment. Her special stock included the Dustsight Spectacles, the Stonegnarl Warband Token, a two-handed maul, a lantern, and the Whispering Cowl. The party's 10% coupon applied here.

### The Gilded Sheath
The workshop of Valimor Brightgem, a blacksmith famous throughout the Underdark for his distinctive and polished scabbards. The shop is filled with the scent of heated metal, polished gem dust, and oil. Valimor leverages the presence of gem mines in his craft and travels for business, so he is not always in Blingdenstone.

Lenses of faintly glowing crystal set in a silver frame, invented by a blind wizard. Purchased by Daz from Starlace Curios and Arcana; they require attunement. They allow the wearer to see invisible creatures and through magical darkness for up to ten one-minute periods per day, with the ability to switch between detecting invisibility and seeing through darkness. The darkness use was a GM ruling at Zalthir's request; rules as written they only detect invisible things.

### Stonegnarl Warband Token
A token that provides a +1 bonus to AC when adjacent to stone surfaces, making it very convenient for combat in the Underdark. Purchased by Thorin from Starlace Curios and Arcana.

### Mace +1
Gyrgum's mace, enchanted to +1 by Starlace for 360 gold pieces after the 10% discount. The enchantment involved labor and materials, 200 gold each before discount, against a typical price of about 500 for an enchanted weapon.

### Studded Leather Armor +2
A fantastic piece of enchanted studded leather armor that was in the party's inventory. It provides only a 14 armor class and wasn't well suited for any party member, none of whom are rogues. The party sold it to Starlace for 600 gold pieces from a 500 base price after both sides rolled natural 20s on persuasion checks.

### Shield +1
Thorin's shield, enchanted to +1 by Starlace for 270 gold pieces after the 10% discount (300 before), bringing Thorin's armor class to 20. Thorin chose the shield because it has fewer upgrade opportunities than armor.

### Cursed Elemental Gem
An elemental gem that was previously cursed but is no longer cursed after the party released Entémoch's boon and got rid of Entémoch's Doom. Gyrgum offered it for sale and removed the cursed note from the party inventory.

### Vaultmaster Locking Scabbard
A custom-made scabbard designed by Valimor Brightgem for Dawnbringer. It features a locking mechanism to prevent theft, and since Dawnbringer "doesn't quite have a blade," it hooks her in so she won't fall out. Valimor explicitly denied it was the "Vaultmaster 9000." Purchased by Thorin for 180 gold pieces after the 10% discount (200 before). Dawnbringer is pleased to finally have a proper scabbard to fit in.

### Whispering Cowl
An item on Starlace's list that Zalthir asked about. It allows the wearer to use Step of the Wind without spending ki points. The GM ruled on how it maps to the current edition: it replaces the focus-point cost of the combined Disengage-and-Dash bonus action. No one is recorded buying it.

## Spells
### Hypnotic Pattern
A spell Daz cast on one of the basilisks. It charmed the creature and left it immobile and paralyzed for a full minute, with no saving throw to break free. The creature remained charmed as long as no one damaged it or shook it awake, effectively taking it out of combat for the duration needed to complete the ritual. The GM ruled that a zero-damage punch would not break it.

### Scorching Ray
A spell Daz attempted against a basilisk in the darkness. Because the spell requires targets and he could not see his, he rolled each ray with disadvantage. He had first considered a sculpted Fireball, for which the GM would have given Zalthir advantage on the save for quarter damage. Daz rolled extremely poorly, missing all three rays with rolls including a natural 1.

### Stone Skin
A buff received by the entire party after activating Entémoch's boon; the GM added it as a homebrew reward for this adventure. Once per long rest, each player can use a reaction to turn their skin to stone when hit by an attack, gaining resistance to that attack's damage type for the rest of the round, so it could cover several sword blows in one round. The buff lasts for one week of in-game time. Zalthir volunteered to remind the party of it.

### Summon Earth Elemental
A spell prepared by Nomi to conjure an Earth Elemental for use in the upcoming battle against the Pudding King. The party advocated that this spell should only be used for defense and combat, not for hard labor.
