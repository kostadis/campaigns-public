# Chapter 74

Date: 2026-10-26

## Summary
Before the faction negotiations at Gauntlgrym’s royal feast, the DM explained the party’s growing political importance. Armies could conquer and hold territory, but adventurers could pursue threats such as powerful wizards who could simply teleport away from an army. Their abilities belonged to them, beyond a king’s power to revoke, making them unusually independent assets. Governments courted such people for protection, specialized missions, and plausible deniability. Thorin compared their role to special forces; Daz observed that employing adventurers also let a ruler demonstrate that something was being done.

Those principles shaped the feast’s negotiations. The great hall of Gauntlgrym was packed with dwarves, including Eldeth and warriors who had introduced themselves as the new Ember Vanguards. The party’s exploits were becoming part of the city’s legends. Scattered among the dwarves stood representatives of five powerful factions of Faerûn, conspicuous with their retinues and guards.

Before any representative could make an offer, King Bruenor drew the party to the high table and addressed the hall. “They have not said yes,” he reminded everyone, before adding, “They don’t owe me a yes, so before anyone in this hall makes them an offer, here’s mine.” He offered a hall within Gauntlgrym, crown provisioning, the Iron Mandate, titles, and a great forge commission for each adventurer upon success.

His personal offers were equally deliberate. Daz received the prospect of sanctuary and citizenship as a friend of Drizzt. Gyrgum was offered standing in the dwarven city, prompting discussion of founding a university. Zalthir was offered a secure, silent retreat, and Glabbagool a place of honor. The ooze’s immediate telepathic response was, “With cheese?” His enthusiasm specifically concerned Rothé cheese.

Bruenor then restored the lost Underdark land claim in Thorin of Clan Feldrun’s name. The clan leader climbed onto a table and led the cheering. Thorin discreetly asked another dwarf what had happened and learned that the claim had been lost when Eldeth’s expedition was wiped out. Alongside a headquarters for the party, he now had the opportunity to reclaim a portion of the Underdark with his new clan.

The faction representatives visibly recalculated. Zelraun appeared to be repricing his offer; Eravien stopped moving as he considered the standard against which his own offer would be judged. Lanniver spoke to an aide, who wrote on a slate. Davra merely turned a page in her ledger, with a composure that could have concealed either irritation or indifference.

Daz asked that the party hear everyone’s needs before answering anyone. Most accepted this gracefully, but Clan Feldrun muttered about Drow and trust. Daz made clear that he could hear them, then recorded the incident in his notes. The party’s strategy remained to identify overlapping objectives and, if possible, secure support from several factions for the same course of action. They understood that apparently compatible promises might later pull them in different directions.

Lord Eravien of the Lords’ Alliance opened with exquisite amuse-bouche pastries, including cheese for Glabbagool. Zalthir encouraged the ooze to savor the special food; Glabbagool narrowed his pseudopod and took a delicate bite. A chess piece then arrived in front of Gyrgum with the invitation, “His Lordship’s compliments. White moves first.” Gyrgum accepted for the group.

The invitation led to Lord Zelraun Roaringhorn, a self-assured, fickle human wizard wearing a Harper pin. Beside his wand sat three small spice boxes; he smelled a spice before selecting a dish. He poured rare, expensive wine for the party and invited Gyrgum to play his chess-obsessed Shield Guardian while they discussed Harper business.

Gyrgum lost the first game in two moves. With Daz’s help, he won the second. The construct then wanted a rematch against Daz himself, having found Gyrgum’s unaided play disappointing. Daz delivered a decisive final victory, prompting Zelraun to clap in admiration and add the Shield Guardian’s companionship to his proposed offer.

The Harpers wanted to know what caused the demonic “blink,” why the demons were in the Underdark, and who was responsible. An initial description involving seven agents to locate was corrected: the actual offer was access to an intelligence network already operating throughout the Underdark. Through the Whisper Chain, the party could ask two questions before operations over the campaign, one receiving a completely truthful answer and the other an answer that might contain uncertainty or misinformation. They would know which kind they were requesting. Daz thanked Zelraun but explicitly withheld acceptance until everyone had been heard.

Nearby, a Vanguard veteran reported that the Duergar in Gracklstugh had hatched something with two heads and were keeping it chained. The party remembered the demonic egg left behind there. The DM confirmed that it had remained corrupted.

The party next approached Sir Lanniver Strayl of the Order of the Gauntlet and his squire, Rhiele Vannis. Daz privately remembered Milo Goodbarrel’s account of the Order but said nothing about it. Lanniver offered Aldric and a team of four veterans for a single agreed tactical task per operation: a breach, chokepoint, extraction, or comparable maneuver. Their continuing support depended on the party protecting innocents and avoiding gratuitous slaughter, genocide, and unnecessary destruction or tomb desecration.

Daz pressed him on what doing the right thing meant when everyone had competing schemes. When Lanniver warned against treating the Underdark as a pillaging ground, Daz asked whether he looked like someone who wanted to do that. With a Drow and an Orc standing before him, Lanniver stumbled into a deeply awkward clarification. His underlying position remained that the threat should be defeated without sacrificing innocent communities or the party’s principles. Daz said those values aligned with theirs, while stressing the importance of proper equipment.

Then the doors at the low end of the hall opened too hard. A road-dusted dwarf, mask pulled down and still coughing, entered without waiting to be announced. The outpost wells were no longer producing water: buckets came up gray and furred. The shaft air was foul, and the runner coughed up something pale. The report made the spreading fungal corruption impossible to dismiss.

Morista Malkin of the Emerald Enclave immediately moved toward the runner. Zelraun closed his spice boxes. Lanniver’s veterans fell silent. Eravien set down his spoon and asked which outpost was affected, while Davra turned to Barundin, who opened a map and placed a finger on it. Eldeth’s response was furious vindication: “Finally, those dipshits believed me!” She had been warning them for months. The party understood that further delay would make their journey more difficult and dangerous.

When they approached Eravien, he recovered enough to ask about his food. Glabbagool supplied an enthusiastic, chemically precise critique: he could taste food and break it down into its constituent components, though he could not reassemble it. Eravien admitted that he had neither received such a polite critique from an ooze nor expected an ooze to speak.

The Lords’ Alliance wanted the threat eliminated or actionable evidence sufficient to send an army against it. Eravien promised a great reward upon the party’s return and offered access to information: one answer with certainty and another in part, clearly distinguished, with a full day required to obtain an answer. Continued private access could be arranged in exchange for escorting an archivist on one future non-combat observation errand. The party again deferred commitment and planned to deliberate with Gyrgum present.

Davra subsequently sent Daz a note requesting a private meeting later that evening. Neither that meeting nor the Emerald Enclave negotiation took place before the session ended. The factions shared a desire for the party to descend into the Underdark, but their priorities differed: the Harpers wanted explanations and information, the Lords’ Alliance wanted a neutralized threat or a military target, the Order wanted innocent lives protected, and the Emerald Enclave appeared concerned with the unfolding ecological crisis. The Zhentarim’s request remained undisclosed.

The DM emphasized that the offers were not a trap, but accepting several would create obligations that might later conflict. Losing support was one possible consequence; spectacular betrayal could bring active retaliation. Bruenor’s immediate need was simpler: after months of Eldeth’s warnings, and now visible evidence of corruption reaching his outposts, he needed the party to go.

## Locations
### Gauntlgrym
An ancient dwarven stronghold recently liberated from the Drow by King Bruenor Battlehammer. It serves as the setting for a grand diplomatic feast where the party is courted by representatives of five major factions of Faerûn. The great hall is filled with dwarves and the towering non-dwarven faction representatives stand out conspicuously among the four-foot-tall crowd.

Bruenor seats the party at the high table, making them the focus of the competing delegations. Representatives attend with guards and retinues. Eldeth and the new Ember Vanguards celebrate the adventurers, while Clan Feldrun’s cheering demonstrates the importance of Thorin’s restored claim.

The city is also a prospective home: Bruenor offers the party a hall, Daz sanctuary and citizenship, Gyrgum standing, Zalthir a quiet retreat, and Glabbagool a place of honor. Gyrgum raises the possibility of establishing a university there.

### The Underdark
The vast subterranean world beneath the surface, currently plagued by demonic influence and ecological collapse. A runner reports that the wells at one deep outpost have turned gray and the air has become foul, reinforcing warnings of spreading fungal corruption.

The reported contamination includes gray, furred bucketfuls and foul shaft air; the runner also coughs up something pale. The DM associates the ecosystem’s deterioration with Zuggtmoy’s fungal activity. The party understands that delaying departure will make travel more dangerous.

Clan Feldrun’s lost expedition had claimed a stretch of the Underdark for Gauntlgrym. Bruenor restores that claim in Thorin’s name, giving him and his clan a future reclamation objective.

### Gracklstugh
A Duergar city in the Underdark, mentioned in a rumor overheard during the feast. A two-headed creature reportedly hatched there and is being kept on a chain, possibly connected to a demonic egg the party previously encountered.

The DM recalls the city’s association with Themberchaud and confirms that the egg the party left behind remained corrupted. The Vanguard veteran’s report suggests the Duergar keeping the hatchling chained are losing sleep over it.

His mask is pulled down, and he enters without waiting to be announced. He describes gray, furred bucketfuls instead of water and foul shaft air, and coughs up something pale. His arrival turns the celebratory feast into an immediate discussion of an advancing crisis.
