# Chapter 63 The Key is secured.

Date: 2026-07-20

## Summary

In the wreckage of Deneir's Sanctum, three helmed horrors lay in heaps of broken armor, and the party was left to wonder how mindless constructs had known exactly who held a key that no detection spell in Candlekeep should have been able to find. The only person who had witnessed the key change hands from Gyrgum to Daz was Fembris, the acolyte who had been quietly present throughout their stay. Suspicion settled over the group like a cold fog. Daral initially rushed toward the door the horrors had smashed open, then stopped when he realized that the only four people who apparently did not want him dead were still inside the room. Looking to the party, he asked, “What do we do next, gentlemen?”

The party tried to reconstruct what they knew. Alkrist had killed Janussi, though they could not yet prove that Bookwyrm had covered it up or that A'lai Aivenmore had participated. Two keys were required to open the high tower: Kalan had supposedly possessed one, while Janussi had worn the other in a locket that disappeared after his death. Bookwyrm had entered Janussi's room before anyone else, knew about the key, and cut out Janussi's heart. Alkrist, by contrast, had wanted Janussi dead but had shown no interest in the key. Their newest problem was the squad of automatons that had somehow penetrated one of the most heavily fortified places in the realms to seize a supposedly undetectable object.

The inert key itself now demanded closer scrutiny. Gyrgum remembered Kalan's peculiarly careful words when Kalan handed it to him: “I have given it now to someone who is not me.” Gyrgum wondered whether the phrasing reflected a deadly game of hot potato, with danger following whoever visibly carried the artifact. He showed the key to Daral and asked whether it looked genuine. Daral admitted, “Well, I'm not a locksmith, so I couldn't tell you for sure,” but insisted that the true keys were immensely magical artifacts, not “some lowly, cheap-ass keys that you acquire at a locksmith.”

Daral produced his trusty scroll of Detect Magic and read it aloud. Every piece of magical equipment in the room blazed with light — armor flashed, and Dawnbringer shone more brightly than usual — but the key itself remained stubbornly, completely dark. Daral's conclusion was immediate and emphatic: “This is not the key. This is not the key.” The party had been carrying a mundane decoy.

Before they could fully process the deception, a Watcher's bell began tolling through Candlekeep in a pattern that was not the hour. The sound was impersonal and institutional, as though the keep itself were responding to a violation. An Avowed messenger rushed in with devastating news: “Bookwyrm is dead.” Her throat had been torn apart, although her heart remained in place. The hysterical messenger could barely reconcile the murder with his faith in the fortress: “This is Candlekeep. These crimes don't happen in Candlekeep.” Gyrgum answered grimly, “Well, reality disagrees.”

The party raced to Bookwyrm's office with Daral and Fembris. They found a gruesome scene. Her throat had been ripped open with savage, animalistic force — the work of something with claws and teeth, not the surgical precision that had claimed Janussi's life. Whoever had killed her had somehow overcome an eighteenth-level magic user in the presumed safety of her own tower. The party joked darkly that an academic of her stature might prepare spells such as *Create Citation*, *Validate Citation*, or *Identify False Citations* instead of *Time Stop* and *Wish*, but the blood-soaked office made the danger unmistakable.

On Bookwyrm's desk lay a half-finished handwritten note: *He is using the beast to—* Daral had no idea what “the beast” meant. Daz initially struggled to interpret the evidence, but with Gyrgum's assistance he reached out with his arcane senses and felt that the pressure of Candlekeep's wards was subtly wrong. Something seemed broken, like a firewall had been breached and another port opened. The connection clicked into place: Sylvira had an abyss-born son whom she normally kept within a magical sanctuary, but the creature had been out of its home for some time. The abyssal madness afflicting demon spawn offered a possible explanation for its behavior, while A'lai Aivenmore's known interest in collecting the high tower keys supplied a motive.

Daral confirmed what the party already suspected about the wards. Candlekeep's primary magical defense prevented teleportation into or out of the fortress, and the same effect suppressed ordinary combustion. The source of that protection lay in the high tower room locked by the two magical keys. To test whether it still held, Gyrgum attempted to conjure a small flame with Thaumaturgy. The spell erupted, but as soon as it tried to ignite a piece of paper the fire evaporated instantaneously, “a burning bush kind of situation.” The ward remained active, but if an enemy obtained both keys, Candlekeep's defenses could be disabled from within.

The party pieced together the likely timeline. Janussi had been killed by Alkrist, and Bookwyrm had subsequently entered Janussi's chambers to protect her nephew and secure the missing high tower key. Whoever stole the sapphire that could summon a key from a dead body was now hunting both keys. Bookwyrm had almost certainly possessed one of them, and the creature that tore out her throat had apparently taken it.

They urgently needed to find Kalan. Daz remembered that Kalan had warned, “If I don't see you tomorrow, do not look for me at my post.” Daral inferred that Kalan would not be at the Gatehouse but hidden among the Watchers in the Sea Warden's Tower, rallying the troops and perhaps celebrating his reinstatement with them. The party raced there and found Kalan frantically buckling on his armor. He was already moving toward them when he called out, “Gyrgum! Daz! I heard the bell. Is everything okay?”

When told that Bookwyrm was dead and no key had been found on her body, Kalan immediately understood the stakes. At first he tried to comfort himself with the thought that the death might have been an accidental act of madness by Sylvira's son and that the enemy therefore possessed only one key. Once the party revealed that helmed horrors had deliberately attacked Daz to seize the other, his reaction was more direct: “Gadzooks. Someone is definitely after the keys.”

Kalan then admitted the full truth. The key he had given Gyrgum had always been a fake, a decoy intended to make the party the visible target while the genuine key remained hidden elsewhere. The real artifact had been entrusted to Tadric, Kalan's loyal lieutenant and the bowl-cut young guard the party had met at Janussi's murder scene. Kalan had posted Tadric alone in the North Galleries, away from the barracks, without even telling him what he carried or what might be coming for it.

Faced with an attack on Candlekeep itself, Kalan grandly declared, “I hereby deputize you as Watchers of Candlekeep.” The party immediately asked what powers, privileges, and stipend accompanied the title. Kalan offered a small badge and a small honorarium, emphasizing that it was “truly an honorarium,” before exasperation overtook ceremony: why were they discussing honorariums while Bookwyrm lay dead?

The party then pressed Fembris about his role in exposing the key transfer. Frightened and confronted with the consequences of his loose tongue, he admitted that A'lai Aivenmore had been in the room when he reported the information to Bookwyrm. Thorin made the lesson explicit: “Loose lips sink ships, dumbass.” That revelation explained how the conspirators knew to target the party while leaving Kalan untouched, and it suggested that the attacks on Daz and Bookwyrm were coordinated attempts to seize both keys. Kalan was delighted by the deduction, congratulating them for capturing two pillars of his Threefold Proof. Thorin preferred the simpler formulation “three strikes and you're out,” though Kalan suggested “three slashes, perhaps?”

Kalan pressed a Watcher's stair key into their hands, providing a shortcut through a barred door to the North Gallery. Thorin eyed the new key skeptically and asked, “Is this key for real this time? Are you just fucking with us again?” Kalan answered, “On my honor,” prompting Thorin to ask, “And the other one wasn't?” They decided the argument could wait and ran.

Kalan tried to follow but, being older and somewhat out of shape, soon fell behind, struggling for breath. As they raced onward, he defended his deception: “Just for the record, I figured that if they saw you with the key, it would be fake. The fake key would be safer. I couldn't trust you with a key of that importance.” The party left him huffing behind them and continued toward Tadric.

They arrived at the top of a domed rotunda to find the attack already underway. A massive and visibly dangerous creature had leapt onto Tadric, grappled him, and was tearing him apart. The assailant was Moziqodo, Sylvira's abyssal spawn son. If the creature killed Tadric and escaped with the key, A'lai would possess both artifacts needed to open the high tower.

Thorin acted first. He charged directly into the creature, planted himself between Moziqodo and everyone else, and invoked his Guardian training so that attacking anyone else would expose the fiend to retaliation. Dawnbringer reminded him that her power was radiant rather than fiery and took offense that he had forgotten. Thorin apologized: “I just, I was fooled for a second because you are a flaming sword, but you're right. You're a radiant sword.” Dawnbringer replied, “Thank you.” Thorin struck repeatedly, used a sapping blow to hinder the creature's next attack, and tried unsuccessfully to knock it prone. He then landed a Menacing Attack that pierced the fiend's composure and left Moziqodo frightened of him.

Zalthir followed with a ferocious assault, driving his magically enhanced fists into the creature. His stunning strike succeeded, leaving Moziqodo completely helpless. Zalthir immediately spent more of his inner reserves to press the advantage, delivering another relentless flurry of blows. Gyrgum maneuvered to keep his companions clear of the blast from his treasured javelin of lightning, but the weapon narrowly missed the fiend. Daz avoided fire, knowing it would likely be ineffective against an abyssal creature, and instead struck Moziqodo with unerring bolts of magical force.

Before Moziqodo could recover, Thorin attacked again. His second strike landed critically, and the radiant blow from Dawnbringer killed the demon spawn outright. Moziqodo collapsed without ever managing an attack of its own. The party had won initiative across the board, concentrated every available resource on the creature, and overwhelmed an encounter that could have killed Tadric with a single successful action. Even the Dungeon Master admitted that the all-out opening assault had been exactly the right strategy.

Gyrgum immediately knelt beside Tadric and cast Cure Wounds. One application was enough: the torn flesh closed and the bleeding stopped. Tadric clutched at the key and stared at his dead attacker in disbelief. “The creature, Moziqodo,” he said. “Sylvira's son, what has happened here?” After the party explained the conspiracy, he sat down in shock. When he asked whether Kalan was still alive, the party could only note that Kalan had been breathing very hard when they left him and joke that he might have suffered a coronary trying to keep pace.

With one real key now secured and Moziqodo slain, the party took stock. A'lai Aivenmore held the key stolen from Bookwyrm and was probably already waiting in the high tower for Moziqodo to arrive with Tadric's key. The party still possessed Kalan's mundane decoy alongside the real artifact, giving them a potential means of confusing anyone who did not know which was genuine. Daz suggested that claiming to possess two keys might even expose the person who already knew where the second real one was.

The immediate victory came with a warning: evil geniuses rarely rely on only one plan. The party ended the session at level nine with one genuine key, one fake key, Tadric alive, Moziqodo dead, and A'lai likely waiting at the high tower with Candlekeep's fate hanging in the balance.

## Locations

### Candlekeep

A legendary fortified library-fortress where a series of murders and the theft of magical high tower keys have thrown the academic community into chaos. Its primary defense is a magical ward that prevents teleportation and suppresses fire within its walls. Despite its reputation as one of the most secure places in the known world, three helmed horrors penetrated Deneir's Sanctum and Bookwyrm was murdered in her own office. The Watcher's bell uses a distinct institutional alarm pattern to announce major crises.

### Bookwyrm's Office

The site of two significant events: first, a tense confrontation where Kalan was reinstated after evidence was presented; later, the scene of Bookwyrm's gruesome murder. Her throat was torn out with claws and teeth, considerable blood covered the room, and a half-finished note reading *He is using the beast to—* was found on her desk. The killing demonstrated that Bookwyrm's confidence in the safety of her tower was disastrously misplaced.

### Deneir's Sanctum

A supposedly secure room within Candlekeep where the party was escorted for protection and perhaps to prevent further investigation. The door was locked from outside. Three helmed horrors smashed through the windows and door, targeting Daz for the key he carried and leaving the chamber filled with broken armor and wreckage. The destroyed door later offered Daral a route of escape, though he chose to remain with the party.

### Alkrist's Room

A room containing a safe with gold, two potions, and a gold coin engraved with a red herring — a fitting symbol for an investigation full of misdirection. The Avowed considered the contents unremarkable, but Zalthir covertly removed the potion of flying through Glabbagool.

### Great Library

A central hub of knowledge within Candlekeep where the party began its frantic run to find Kalan Strongbranch after Bookwyrm's murder was announced. The party was specifically in the Exaltation before setting out toward the Sea Warden's Tower.

### Sea Warden's Tower

A defensive structure within Candlekeep where Kalan Strongbranch was found frantically buckling on his armor after hearing the alarm bell. Daral expected Kalan to be hidden among the other Watchers there rather than at his normal Gatehouse post. It was here that the party learned the truth about the fake key, was deputized as Watchers, and discovered that Tadric carried the real artifact.

### North Galleries

A section of Candlekeep away from the main barracks where Kalan had hidden Tadric to protect the real key. Tadric had not been told what he carried or what might be coming for it. The area was accessed through a barred shortcut opened by a Watcher's stair key and featured a domed rotunda at the top, which became the site of the confrontation with Moziqodo.

### High Tower

The ultimate destination of the two magical keys and the suspected location where A'lai Aivenmore waited for Moziqodo. A secured room within it contains the source of the ward that prevents teleportation and suppresses combustion throughout Candlekeep. Whoever acquires both keys may be able to reach that ward and compromise the fortress's primary defense.

## NPCs

### Kalan Strongbranch

The reinstated Head of the Avowed and Gatewarden of Candlekeep, whose Threefold Proof methodology was recently vindicated. He values the validation of his academic framework so highly that, offered the choice between putting a criminal in prison and seeing his theory of crime validated, he chose validation.

Kalan admitted to giving the party a fake key as a decoy to protect the genuine artifact. His careful statement at the time — “I have given it now to someone who is not me” — allowed him to create the appearance of a transfer without surrendering the real key. He entrusted the genuine artifact to Tadric and hid him in the North Galleries.

After learning of the coordinated attacks, Kalan exclaimed, “Gadzooks. Someone is definitely after the keys.” He deputized the party as Watchers of Candlekeep, offering a badge and a purely symbolic honorarium. He directed them toward Tadric with a Watcher's stair key, though his recent deception caused Thorin to question whether even that key was real.

Kalan was a little out of shape, on the older side, and unable to keep pace with the adventurers. As he fell behind, huffing and puffing, he continued defending his decoy scheme as the safest way to draw danger away from the true artifact.

### Bookwyrm

A high-ranking eighteenth-level magic user of the Avowed who was suspected of covering up Janussi's murder. During the party's confrontation with her, she performed grief and outrage convincingly, though Daz perceived relief beneath the surface.

Bookwyrm had entered Janussi's room after his murder, knew about his key, and cut out his heart. She was later found dead in her own office with her throat torn apart, likely murdered for the high tower key she possessed. Her heart remained untouched, distinguishing the killing from Janussi's death. A half-finished note on her desk read *He is using the beast to—*, implying that she recognized both the immediate threat and the intelligence directing it.

### Daral

An Avowed scholar who was cured of a lingering affliction by Dawnbringer and launched into an enthusiastic monologue about making sentient magical swords an area of serious academic study. His discussion included the semiotics of sentient weapons and delighted Dawnbringer.

After the helmed-horror attack, Daral initially tried to flee, then realized that the party members were the only people who did not seem to want him dead. He remained close to Thorin and Dawnbringer for protection.

Daral used his trusty scroll of Detect Magic to prove that Kalan's supposed high tower key was mundane, declaring, “This is not the key. This is not the key.” He also explained the relationship between the high tower, Candlekeep's anti-teleportation ward, and the magical suppression of ordinary fire.

### Fembris Lancer

An acolyte adjutant who served as a food tester for the suspicious party in the sanctum. He was present when Gyrgum transferred the apparent key to Daz and therefore became the party's primary suspect for leaking its location.

Fembris was paralyzed with fear after Bookwyrm's murder. Under pressure, he admitted that A'lai Aivenmore was present when he reported the key transfer to Bookwyrm. This explained how A'lai could have learned whom to target and shifted suspicion away from Bookwyrm as the sole architect of the helmed-horror attack. Thorin warned Fembris not to disclose Tadric's location, telling him, “Loose lips sink ships, dumbass.”

### Alkrist

The individual whose confession and physical evidence linked him to the murder of Janussi. He wanted Janussi dead but apparently had no interest in the missing high tower key. His room contained a safe with gold, two potions, and a gold coin engraved with a red herring.

### Janussi

The deceased member of the Avowed whose murder remains central to the investigation. Alkrist killed him, while Bookwyrm later entered his room, removed his heart, and apparently secured the high tower key hidden in his locket. Two people visited the room between his murder and the later investigation, and the locket was found missing.

### Helmed Horrors

Three towering animated suits of plate armor with cold, glowing eyes that smashed through the windows and door of Deneir's Sanctum. They ignored most of the party and converged almost entirely on Daz, seeking the key he carried. Though mindless, they somehow knew the key's precise holder despite the object having no detectable magical aura. The party destroyed all three, reducing them to rust and broken metal.

### Tadric

Kalan's loyal lieutenant, recognizable as a short young human with a bowl-cut hairstyle. He had previously guarded Janussi's murder scene. Kalan entrusted him with the genuine high tower key and hid him alone in the North Galleries without telling him what he carried.

Moziqodo grappled Tadric and began tearing him apart before the party arrived. The demon spawn could have killed him with a single attack and fled with the key, but the party stunned and killed it before it could act. Gyrgum healed Tadric's wounds, after which the shocked lieutenant clutched the key, identified his attacker as Sylvira's son, and asked what had happened.

### Moziqodo

A massive and powerful demon spawn revealed to be the abyssal spawn son of Sylvira. His mother normally kept him within a magical sanctuary, but he had been outside it for some time.

Moziqodo was apparently the beast that tore out Bookwyrm's throat and took her key; he then attacked Tadric in the domed rotunda to seize the second. He was apparently expected to deliver Tadric's key to A'lai at the high tower. Thorin frightened him, Zalthir stunned him, Daz struck him with Magic Missile, and Thorin killed him with a critical blow from Dawnbringer before he could take a single action.

### A'lai Aivenmore

A powerful male drow antagonist revealed to be associated with the abyssal spawn Moziqodo. He was present when Fembris reported the key's location to Bookwyrm, giving him access to information that few others possessed.

The party believes A'lai coordinated the attacks on both apparent key holders: helmed horrors were sent after Daz while Moziqodo attacked Bookwyrm and then Tadric. A'lai likely possesses the key stolen from Bookwyrm and is believed to be waiting in the high tower for Moziqodo to arrive with the second.

### Dawnbringer

A sentient magical sword wielded by Thorin that deals radiant — not fire — damage. It cured Daral of his lingering affliction, inspired his enthusiastic plans for an academic field devoted to sentient swords, and glowed exceptionally brightly under Daral's Detect Magic spell.

Dawnbringer expressed offense when Thorin momentarily forgot the nature of her power. He corrected himself by telling her, “You're a radiant sword,” and she answered, “Thank you.” They agreed the slight would require discussion in a future therapy session.

During the battle in the rotunda, Dawnbringer delivered repeated radiant blows to Moziqodo and struck the final critical hit that killed him.


## Items

### Potion of Flying

A magical elixir that Zalthir surreptitiously stole from Alkrist's safe using Glabbagool while the Avowed were distracted and no one was watching. The possibility of using it to pursue a fleeing Moziqodo was later noted, though the creature never escaped.

### Gold Coin with a Red Herring

A gold coin engraved with a red herring, found in Alkrist's safe. Thorin referred to the contents as “Jimmy Hoffa's chest.” The coin proved a fitting symbol for an investigation dominated by misdirection, false appearances, and decoy keys.

### Scroll of Detect Magic

A standard tool carried by Candlekeep sages, used by Daral to examine the key. It caused the party's magical equipment to glow brightly while revealing the key held by the party to have no magical aura whatsoever. Daral's test conclusively exposed Kalan's decoy.

### Bookwyrm's Note

A half-finished handwritten note found on Bookwyrm's desk at the scene of her murder. It read: *He is using the beast to—* The abrupt ending suggested Bookwyrm was killed while attempting to record that Moziqodo was being directed by another party.

### Fake High Tower Key

A non-magical decoy key given to Gyrgum by Kalan Strongbranch and later transferred to Daz. Kalan intended the visible transfer to make the party a target while keeping the genuine artifact safely hidden.

The fake was confirmed to have no magical properties when examined with Detect Magic, even as every genuine magical object around it glowed. The party retained it after securing Tadric's real key and considered using the pair to mislead their enemies.

### Real High Tower Key

One of two essential magical artifacts required to access the high tower and the room containing Candlekeep's protective wards. Kalan entrusted it to Tadric, who was hidden in the North Galleries without knowing what he carried.

Moziqodo nearly killed Tadric to obtain it, but the party intervened and secured the key. A'lai is believed to hold its counterpart, stolen from Bookwyrm.

### Watcher's Stair Key

A key provided by Kalan Strongbranch that grants access to a barred shortcut leading directly to the North Gallery. Thorin questioned whether it was genuine after learning about Kalan's earlier deception. Kalan swore “on my honor” that it was real, allowing the party to reach Tadric before Moziqodo could kill him.

### Javelin of Lightning

A magical javelin wielded by Gyrgum. During the battle against Moziqodo, he carefully repositioned to avoid catching his companions in its line of effect and hurled it at the demon spawn, though the attack narrowly missed.

## Spells

### Cure Wounds

A restorative power channeled through Dawnbringer to heal Daral of his lingering affliction, filling the scholar with such gratitude that he launched into an enthusiastic monologue about sentient magical swords and their semiotics.

After Moziqodo was slain, Gyrgum cast Cure Wounds on Tadric. A single casting closed the young man's lacerations and stopped the bleeding, saving him from dying after the battle.

### Detect Magic

Cast by Daral using a scroll to examine the key held by the party. The spell caused all magical equipment in the room — including armor and Dawnbringer — to glow brightly, while the key remained stubbornly inert. Daral immediately concluded that it was not one of the magical keys to the high tower.

### Thaumaturgy

Cast by Gyrgum to test the status of Candlekeep's protective wards. The resulting fire erupted briefly, but as soon as it attempted to ignite a piece of paper it evaporated instantaneously. Daral described the result as a “burning bush kind of situation.” The test confirmed that the fire-suppression and anti-teleportation ward remained active.

### Magic Missile

Cast by Daz at first level against the stunned demon spawn Moziqodo. Daz selected force damage after concluding that the creature would likely resist fire. Three unerring missiles struck for a total of twelve points of damage and helped bring the fiend within reach of Thorin's killing blow.
