# Chapter 64

Date: 2026-07-27

## Summary

The party paused beside the newly healed Tadric to take a short rest before pursuing A'lai Aivenmore. When Tadric surrendered the genuine High Tower key, he admitted, “I held it all night and I didn't know what it was. Now that I know what it is, I'm not giving it to you and going to bed.” Though Gyrgum warned that accompanying them would be dangerous, Tadric insisted, “I faced Moziqodo,” driven by what appeared to be a desperate desire to prove himself heroic. Gyrgum relented, noting that he now possessed resurrection magic if the worst happened.

Then something else changed: the endless chant of fulfilled and unfulfilled prophecies that had filled Candlekeep without interruption fell suddenly and completely silent. Gyrgum joked that he had long since stopped consciously hearing it—“Once it stops, I notice that it stopped”—but Tadric froze, stunned by something that simply never happened. Tadric turned to the party and urgently reminded them, “Sylvira, someone needs to tell Sylvira that her son is dead.” An awkward silence followed. Gyrgum suggested that the messenger should be someone who knew Sylvira, and Daz argued that such a person would have “the best understanding of how to be empathetic to her.” The party ultimately classified it as a later problem for the Ember Grapple and pressed toward the High Tower with Tadric accompanying them.

Gyrgum cast a protective blessing over his companions as they moved. Along the way they discovered a dead Watcher on an indoor walkway, killed by a crossbow bolt. Daz's exceptionally sharp investigative eye immediately recognized that the bolt's fletching was foreign to the area and inconsistent with the ammunition carried by Candlekeep's Watchers—proof that outside attackers had already passed along the same route. Examining the body, Gyrgum estimated that the Watcher had been killed within the last half hour. The direction of the shot showed that the killers had climbed the stairs behind the party's present position, murdered the guard, and continued toward the tower.

They arrived at the High Tower lobby to find two Zhentarim raiders looting books from shelves according to a written manifest. Their swords were bloodied, and one carried the bow that had killed the Watcher. From the entrance, the party could not see the entire room. On a landing roughly fifteen feet above them, near the sealed inner door that required both High Tower keys, stood A'lai Aivenmore himself. He was waiting for Moziqodo to arrive and did not yet realize that the demonspawn was dead or that the party possessed the other key.

The party first planned to take out the raiders and then concentrate on the powerful wizard, but the thugs were not lined up for a single spell from the doorway, so Gyrgum switched targets and launched a guiding bolt in an attempt to catch A'lai Aivenmore by surprise, but the shot failed to penetrate his magical defenses. Zalthir and Glabbagool surged into the room and delivered punishing blows to the wizard. Daz attempted to neutralize A'lai with Maximilian's earthen grasp, conjuring a hand of compacted soil. A'lai countered the first casting, but Daz immediately tried the same spell again, declaring, “I'm persistent.” This time A'lai chose to risk the saving throw rather than expend another counterspell, failed spectacularly, took fifteen points of bludgeoning damage, and was restrained. He escaped by teleporting across the chamber with Misty Step, but doing so consumed more of his magic and prevented him from unleashing one of his more devastating spells that turn.

A'lai retaliated with repeated bursts of raw arcane force. One missed Daz only on a roll of one or two, but another struck him for twenty-six points of damage; Zalthir was hit for twenty-nine. Then, unexpectedly, the wizard stopped to speak. “You've been so thorough,” he told the party. “I want that on the record before anything else happens, because I don't expect either of us will have time for compliments in the moment.” When Gyrgum asked whether the murders were merely megalomania—or an attempt to improve his academic standing—A'lai replied, “You understand me.”

A'lai explained that Janussi had given him ten days and believed the ultimatum merciful. “I have been at Candlekeep for 31 years. I do not have a house or a family or a country. I have a chair. He proposed to take the chair. And I found, I was surprised at this, that I would do a great deal to keep it.” He insisted, “I didn't set out to be a murderer. I didn't set out to be exiled.” Daz compared him to “the dude with the red stapler in Office Space,” while Gyrgum dryly concluded that the entire conspiracy had begun because A'lai was about to lose his academic honor and privileges.

The wizard then corrected the party's assumption that the scheme was entirely his own. “I have been removing books from this library for 11 years, and I have never once chosen which ones. A list arrives. I fill it. The courier who brings the list wears a glove on his right hand, and under the glove, the hand is metal, and I have never in 11 years worked up the nerve to ask why.” The master he served was Manshoon. “You have not heard of it. That is not an accident. It is a career.” The party's historical knowledge identified Manshoon as the infamous wizard tied to the Zhentarim, the Moonsea, and Zhentil Keep—a man who had been dead more than once.

A'lai produced the stolen sapphire and declared, “He wants one book out of this keep, and he was always going to have it. It was simply the version where nobody had to die.” Looking over the accumulating dead, he amended that to “fewer people had to die” and moved to smash the gem. Daz reacted first, using telekinesis to wrench the sapphire from A'lai's hand. He then feigned a retreat toward the hallway and, with a deft sleight-of-hand maneuver, secretly passed the gem to Gyrgum. A'lai believed Daz was fleeing with it, while Gyrgum actually held both the real High Tower key—key #2, recovered from Tadric—and the sapphire.

Robbed of the gem and furious at being outmaneuvered, A'lai turned his arcane barrage on Zalthir. Multiple attacks struck the monk and knocked him unconscious. Glabbagool settled protectively over his fallen companion, positioned to stabilize him if necessary. Thorin engaged the Zhentarim raiders directly, striking one hard with Dawnbringer and following with a menacing maneuver, though the hardened thug resisted the intimidation. Gyrgum then surged forward and unleashed Tasha's caustic brew across both raiders. Both failed to evade the stream. One suffered eight points of acid damage and died immediately; the other took only two, ending the session still standing, coated in corrosive fluid, while Zalthir lay unconscious and the confrontation with A'lai Aivenmore remained unresolved.

## Scenes

### The Silence of Candlekeep

#### Standing over a healed Tadric, the party notices an ominous change in the atmosphere of the keep as the eternal prophecies suddenly fall silent.

- The party takes a short rest beside the healed Tadric. Daz, who had been badly hurt, struggles briefly with the interface for spending his hit dice before restoring himself.

- Tadric hands over the genuine High Tower key but refuses to withdraw from the crisis: “I held it all night and I didn't know what it was. Now that I know what it is, I'm not giving it to you and going to bed.”

- Gyrgum warns Tadric that he could be helpful but that accompanying the party will be dangerous. Tadric answers, “I faced Moziqodo,” plainly seized by a desperate desire to be heroic.

- Gyrgum permits Tadric to accompany them and observes that he now has resurrection magic “if worst comes to worst.”

- The endless chant of prophecies—a constant feature of Candlekeep, in which prophecies are continuously read and removed when fulfilled—abruptly stops.

- Gyrgum admits that, like any constant background sound, he had ceased consciously noticing the chant: “Once it stops, I notice that it stopped.”

- Tadric is stunned because the chanting never stops. His reaction makes the silence's significance unmistakable to the rest of the party.

- Tadric looks at the party and says, “Sylvira, someone needs to tell Sylvira that her son is dead.”

- An awkward silence follows as Tadric looks expectantly at the party. Gyrgum suggests, “It might best be someone who knows Sylvira.”

- Daz frames the same deflection more diplomatically: “We want to be empathetic, and like they would have the best understanding of how to be empathetic to her as they explain this unfortunate news.”

- The party decides that informing Sylvira is a problem for later. Daz calls it “a later Emerald Vanguard or whatever we're calling ourselves these days,” and the correct name is supplied: the Ember Grapple.

- Tadric knows the way to the High Tower and takes the lead in guiding the party there.

### The Path to the High Tower

#### The party heads toward the High Tower to confront A'lai Aivenmore, discovering signs of violence along the way.

- Before departing, Gyrgum casts Bless on the companions present, protecting them with an additional measure of divine fortune on attacks and saving throws.

- While traversing an indoor walkway reached by stairs, the party discovers the body of a Watcher, one of Candlekeep's guards.

- The Watcher has been killed by a crossbow bolt. Daz initially asks whether the body looks conspicuously out of place before the blunt answer is clarified: the man is simply dead.

- Daz's passive investigation is twenty-four, prompting the observation that he is “basically Sherlock Holmes at this point.”

- Daz describes his new level of deduction through the example of noticing that a cab driver failed to shave the back of his neck and inferring that his wife must be dead because she would otherwise have pointed it out.

- Applying that acuity to the corpse, Daz immediately recognizes that the bolt's fletching is not from the surrounding region and does not match the crossbow bolts used by the Watchers.

- The foreign ammunition establishes that the killer came from outside Candlekeep. The direction of the shot further indicates that the intruders climbed the same stairs, killed the Watcher, and continued toward the High Tower.

- Gyrgum examines the body using his medical knowledge and estimates that the Watcher was shot within the last half hour.

- With the attackers close ahead, the party abandons an ordinary approach and advances cautiously, prepared for further violence.

### Infiltration of the High Tower Lobby

#### The party reaches the High Tower lobby and observes the conspirators looting the archives while A'lai Aivenmore waits above.

- The party arrives at the High Tower lobby and finds two raiders pulling selected books from the shelves and bagging them according to a written manifest.

- The raiders wear combat equipment and carry swords stained with blood. One also carries a bow, resolving the mystery of who killed the Watcher on the walkway.

- The entrance does not offer a complete view of the chamber, leaving open the possibility that additional enemies are concealed elsewhere inside.

- A staircase rises to a landing roughly fifteen feet above the lobby floor. A'lai Aivenmore waits there in front of the sealed inner door, expecting Moziqodo to arrive at any moment.

- A'lai possesses one High Tower key but needs the party's second key to pass through the inner door.

- The party recognizes A'lai as a particularly powerful wizard, but also notes that, like many powerful wizards, he is more interested in remaining alive than in fighting to the death.

- Glabbagool asks whether this is the moment to use “the flying spell.”

- Daz briefly considers literally boxing A'lai into a confined space while the party handles the other enemies, but abandons the idea after confirming that the wizard is taller than five feet.

- Gyrgum proposes eliminating the two weaker raiders first so the party can concentrate on A'lai. Daz agrees that reducing the enemies' damage output is sensible. From the door, though, the thugs are not lined up for one spell, so Gyrgum decides to target A'lai while the others deal with the thugs.

- A'lai has not noticed the party at first. He expects Moziqodo rather than the Ember Grapple and is about to receive an unpleasant surprise.

- Daz summarizes the situation as “the dude with the red stapler in Office Space, and he snapped.”

- A'lai insists, “I didn't set out to be a murderer. I didn't set out to be exiled.”

- Asked who he works for, A'lai answers, “His name is Manshoon. You have not heard of it. That is not an accident. It is a career.”

## Locations

### Candlekeep

The great library fortress where the party is investigating a conspiracy involving stolen keys, murdered scholars, and ancient protective wards. Its prophecies are read aloud in a continuous chant, with each fulfilled prophecy removed while the remaining words continue without interruption. The chant falls completely silent during this session; Tadric is stunned, because this never happens.

### Walkway to the High Tower

An indoor passage reached by stairs and leading toward the High Tower. The party discovered a recently slain Watcher here, killed by a crossbow bolt with foreign fletching. The bolt's direction showed that the attackers had climbed the stairs, murdered the guard, and continued along the party's route. Gyrgum estimated that the killing occurred no more than half an hour earlier.

### High Tower Lobby

The entrance to the High Tower of Candlekeep, containing bookshelves, stairs, and a landing approximately fifteen feet above the main floor. The entrance provides only a partial view of the room. It served as the site of the session's climactic confrontation, with Zhentarim raiders looting selected books from a written manifest while A'lai Aivenmore waited above near a sealed inner door requiring both High Tower keys. The battle ended with one raider dead, another covered in acid, Zalthir unconscious, and A'lai still active.

## NPCs

### Tadric

Kalan's lieutenant, freshly healed as the session opens. He had carried the genuine High Tower key all night without knowing what it was. Once he learns its importance, he refuses simply to surrender it and retire: “Now that I know what it is, I'm not giving it to you and going to bed.”

Shaken but emboldened by surviving Moziqodo's attack, Tadric insists on accompanying the party and protests, “I faced Moziqodo.” He appears seized by a desperate desire to be heroic. The sudden silence of Candlekeep's prophetic chant leaves him stunned, and it is Tadric who reminds the party, “Sylvira, someone needs to tell Sylvira that her son is dead.” He also guides the group toward the High Tower.

### Moziqodo

Sylvira's demonspawn son, killed before this session opens. His corpse remains with the party and Tadric at the beginning of the session. His death creates an uncomfortable obligation to inform Sylvira, which the party postpones. A'lai Aivenmore remains unaware of Moziqodo's death and is waiting for him to arrive at the High Tower with the second key.

### A'lai Aivenmore

A powerful drow wizard and long-time scholar of Candlekeep who orchestrated the immediate conspiracy to protect his academic position. Janussi had given him ten days before taking away his chair, the only home or institutional identity A'lai believed he possessed after thirty-one years at Candlekeep.

A'lai admits that he did not initially intend to become either a murderer or an exile, but discovered he would do “a great deal” to retain his chair. His crimes reach much further back than the current crisis: for eleven years he has removed books selected by lists delivered on Manshoon's behalf.

He is self-preserving, desperate, and tactically formidable. He uses Counterspell, Misty Step, Mage Armor, and repeated Arcane Bursts, but consistently prioritizes escape and survival over fighting to the death. During the confrontation, Daz repeatedly forces him to spend magic defensively and ultimately steals the sapphire from his hand.

A'lai possesses a strange mixture of vanity and fatalistic candor. He pauses during combat to compliment the party's thoroughness and seems pleased when Gyrgum recognizes the academic grievance beneath the bloodshed. His attempt to justify Manshoon's theft as the version where nobody needed to die collapses under the weight of the bodies around him, and he revises it to the version where “fewer people had to die.”

### Manshoon

A legendary and feared wizard associated with the Zhentarim, the Moonsea, and Zhentil Keep. A'lai identifies him as the true authority behind eleven years of directed book thefts from Candlekeep. Manshoon sends lists naming the required volumes, and a courier with a metal right hand concealed beneath a glove delivers them.

A'lai warns that ignorance of Manshoon is deliberate and professionally useful: “You have not heard of it. That is not an accident. It is a career.” The party's historical knowledge establishes that Manshoon has died more than once and nevertheless returned. His present objective is a single book out of Candlekeep.

### Glabbagool

An intelligent ooze and loyal companion to the party. Glabbagool accompanies Zalthir into the High Tower battle and asks whether the elevated landing makes this the right occasion to use “the flying spell.” After A'lai knocks Zalthir unconscious, Glabbagool remains on top of the fallen monk, protecting him and positioned to stabilize him if necessary.

### Zhentarim Raiders

Mercenaries working for the Zhentarim, found looting the High Tower lobby and bagging specific books from a written manifest. Their swords are bloodied, and one carries the bow used to kill the Watcher on the walkway. They are hardened enough that one resists Thorin's Menacing Attack. Both fail to evade Gyrgum's caustic brew; one dies when the acid first burns him, while the other remains standing and coated in the corrosive substance when the session ends.

### Dawnbringer

Thorin's sentient radiant sword, explicitly referred to as female during the party's rest. Thorin's attack bonus with her is now +10. When the party considers how to destroy the sapphire, the GM initially rules that Dawnbringer's blade cannot simply be used as a smashing implement. Thorin proposes using the pommel instead: “You could use the Hilt. She wouldn't mind.”

During the battle, Thorin uses Dawnbringer against a Zhentarim thug, dealing thirteen points of damage with his first strike.


## Items

### Real High Tower Key

The genuine artifact required to manage Candlekeep's wards and open the sealed inner door of the High Tower. This is High Tower key **#2**; key #1 is still in A'lai Aivenmore's possession, and the door on the second-floor landing requires both.

Tadric had carried key #2 all night without understanding its importance. Once informed, he hands it over but insists on accompanying the party rather than abandoning responsibility for it. Gyrgum holds the genuine key by the time the party reaches the High Tower. He also retains Kalan's fake decoy key.

### Foreign Crossbow Bolt

A crossbow bolt found in the body of a dead Watcher on an indoor walkway. Daz identifies its fletching as foreign to the region and inconsistent with the bolts used by Candlekeep's Watchers, confirming that the attackers came from outside the keep. Its angle establishes the route the intruders took, and the party later finds a bow-carrying raider among the looters in the High Tower lobby.

### Written Manifest

A list used by the Zhentarim raiders to identify specific books to be removed from the High Tower's collection. Its existence confirms that the looting is targeted rather than opportunistic. It also matches A'lai's confession that for eleven years he has received lists of books chosen by someone else and filled them without ever choosing which ones.

### Sapphire Artifact

A magical gem held by A'lai Aivenmore, who produces it while explaining that Manshoon wants a single book from Candlekeep. A'lai intends to smash the sapphire to trigger an effect associated with the High Tower keys, although the party cannot recall with certainty whether it summons something, teleports a key, or produces some other result.

Before A'lai can break it, Daz uses Telekinesis to wrench it from his grasp. Daz then pretends to retreat with the artifact while secretly transferring it to Gyrgum with a successful sleight-of-hand maneuver. Gyrgum ends the session holding the sapphire, the genuine key #2, and Kalan's fake decoy key, while A'lai believes Daz fled with the gem.

## Spells

### Bless

Cast by Gyrgum on “the three of us who are here” as they approach the High Tower, granting each target an additional 1d4 on attack rolls and saving throws. The group briefly forgets to apply it to Gyrgum's opening Guiding Bolt, but the added die is insufficient to turn the miss into a hit. Bless remains active through most of the confrontation but ends when Gyrgum begins concentrating on Tasha's Caustic Brew.

### Guiding Bolt

Launched by Gyrgum at A'lai Aivenmore from the High Tower entrance in an attempt to surprise the wizard. A'lai does not initially notice the attack, but Gyrgum's attack roll is poor even after accounting for Bless, and the radiant bolt fails to penetrate the wizard's Mage Armor.

### Mage Armor

A protective magical force surrounding A'lai Aivenmore. It raises his defenses enough to turn Gyrgum's attempted surprise Guiding Bolt aside without effect.

### Maximilian's Earthen Grasp

Cast twice by Daz during the confrontation. The spell causes a medium-sized hand of compacted soil to rise from a five-foot section of ground, seize a nearby target, inflict 2d6 bludgeoning damage, and restrain it.

A'lai negates the first casting with Counterspell. Daz immediately casts it again, forcing A'lai to choose between spending another spell slot or risking a strength save. A'lai chooses the save, fails spectacularly, takes fifteen points of damage, and is restrained. He escapes by using Misty Step rather than physically breaking free.

### Counterspell

Used by A'lai Aivenmore as a reaction to negate Daz's first casting of Maximilian's Earthen Grasp. It consumes one of A'lai's spell slots and temporarily limits his other magical options. Daz deliberately repeats the spell afterward, forcing A'lai to decide whether to expend still more magic.

### Misty Step

Used by A'lai Aivenmore to teleport across the room and escape the restraining earthen hand after failing his save against Daz's second Maximilian's Earthen Grasp. Because teleportation does not use ordinary movement, the restrained condition does not prevent it. The escape costs A'lai another spell and keeps him from responding with a more devastating leveled spell during the same turn.

### Arcane Burst

A magical ranged attack used repeatedly by A'lai Aivenmore to strike multiple party members with raw arcane energy. One burst misses Daz despite his low armor class, but another deals twenty-six points of damage. Zalthir initially takes twenty-nine points without falling. Later, after Daz steals the sapphire, A'lai unleashes four more attacks against Zalthir; enough connect to knock the monk unconscious.

### Telekinesis

Cast by Daz when A'lai Aivenmore tries to smash the sapphire. Because A'lai is carrying the gem, he must succeed on a strength saving throw to retain it. He fails against Daz's spell save DC of seventeen, allowing Daz to wrench the artifact from his hand and pull it into his own possession.

Daz considers using the spell on A'lai himself but applies it only to the object, so the wizard is not restrained. The successful theft prevents A'lai from triggering the sapphire and enables Daz's concealed transfer of it to Gyrgum.

### Tasha's Caustic Brew

Unleashed by Gyrgum in a line that catches both Zhentarim thugs. Each must make a DC 17 Dexterity save, and both fail, becoming coated in acid. The spell inflicts 2d4 acid damage at the start of each affected creature's turn until that creature uses an action to scrape or wash it away.

One thug takes eight points and dies immediately. The other takes only two and ends the session still standing, covered in acid and due to suffer another 2d4 at the start of each turn unless he removes it. Concentrating on Caustic Brew causes Gyrgum to end Bless.
