# Chapter 65 Look Who's Coming to Candlekeep

Date: 2026-08-03 (played) — exported 2026-08-09

## Summary

A'lai told the surviving Thug to ignore the cleric and go after Daz, shouting, “Don't go after Gyrgum. Don't go after the cleric. Go after the wizard. The wizard's the one that we want. He's got the key.” In truth, Gyrgum quietly held the real key, the fake key, and the sapphire. The acid-soaked thug broke away from Gyrgum and Thorin, giving both a chance to strike, but survived long enough for Daz to reposition around the corner and launch a fireball. The thug endured the blast by a single hit point, but the successful detonation sent shockwaves of a different kind through all of Candlekeep: bells rang, screams echoed, and scholars scrambled for their notebooks, because the fireball’s very existence proved that the library’s legendary magical wards had been broken. As Daz put it, “Everybody is going to realize that the Candlekeep is now in its underwear.”

A'lai killed the wounded thug himself and then abruptly proposed a pause, carefully distinguishing it from capitulation: “I'd like to stop, but not surrender.” Distracted by sounds from the stairwell, he admitted that his spells were nearly exhausted and offered information instead. “But I know which two lines of the cryptogram matter. I know where the room under the keep is, and I know the name of the person in Menzoberranzan who has been paying to keep your drow breathing.” Canon records this as an intuition about a *house*, not a name. He wanted to disclose everything behind a locked door before the people ascending the stairs reached him. He begged not for freedom, but for a cell inside Candlekeep’s magic-nullifying prison—the one place he believed he might survive what was coming. Gyrgum used restorative magic to bring Zalthir back to consciousness, and the party agreed to arrest A'lai and turn him over to the Avowed.

Zalthir bound the cooperative wizard, but before the party could act further, a Zhentarim assassin appeared. A'lai held out his tied hands and greeted the newcomer coolly: “Took your time. They have had me since the landing.” He then presented his capture as an advantage rather than a defeat: “It's not a loss. It's a position. I've been in the room. I know exactly who is carrying what.” He nodded toward Daz, directing the assassin toward the wizard whom both men believed still held the sapphire.

The assassin launched a brutal assault, striking Daz twice for a combined forty-three points of damage and dropping him unconscious. Daz managed one successful death save before suffering a failure. The assassin searched his body for the sapphire and found nothing, his horror plain on his face, for Daz had secretly passed the gem to Gyrgum during the preceding confrontation.

Thorin’s fury was immediate and absolute. After an initial retaliatory strike, he battered the assassin with repeated attacks, imposed disadvantage, terrified him with a Menacing Attack, and then used Action Surge to unleash another full sequence of blows. A critical radiant strike left the operative barely standing, and a final Hill Strike killed him. Thorin immediately searched the assassin’s body. Gyrgum rushed to Daz and poured healing energy into him, restoring forty hit points and pulling him back from the edge of death. A'lai looked on in tearful panic, his last hope of rescue lying dead at his feet—and his attempted betrayal now exposed.

The party debated whether A'lai deserved justice, academic disgrace, or something more inventive. Thorin suggested making the Zhentarim believe A'lai had become an informant, while Glabbagool wondered aloud whether an Avowed wizard dissolved like anyone else. A'lai pleaded, “Keep that thing away from me,” and then began to cry. Despite their complete lack of sympathy, the party kept him alive.

The party stripped A'lai’s High Tower key from him, taking care not to bring the two real keys together. Gyrgum quietly slipped the key to Zalthir, and it was ultimately tucked inside Glabbagool for safekeeping. The grey ooze seemed to grow half a centimeter taller and become “a little less pancakey” with pride at the responsibility.

Sensing that remaining in the High Tower lobby, outside the security control room’s door, would be catastrophic, the party moved A'lai out before questioning him further. That decision proved wise when Manshoon’s simulacrum arrived. It conjured a wall of force and shattered the magical door that the two keys were meant to protect, breaching Candlekeep’s inner sanctum through sheer, overwhelming power. He arrived alone and visibly weakened, having expended enormous magical resources and artifacts to penetrate Candlekeep, while the keep’s senior defenders were dead, disgraced, captured, or missing.

The breach rendered the struggle over the keys less decisive, but not pointless: securing the keys had forced Manshoon to spend much of his power, prevented his retinue from accompanying him, and left him facing the party alone. A'lai, trembling in the party’s custody, revealed the one card he still held: he had never told Manshoon the truth about what lay inside the depository. His knowledge could hinder Manshoon, but A'lai intended to trade it for protection long enough for Candlekeep’s wards to be restored. That bargain—and the confrontation with the diminished simulacrum—would have to wait for another day.

## NPCs

### A'lai Aivenmore

A wizard who has lived at Candlekeep for thirty-one years, with no house, family, or country—only his academic chair. In the previous session (ch64) he was revealed as a traitor who secretly removed specific books from the library for eleven years on behalf of Manshoon, whose courier always arrived wearing a glove over a metal hand, and he attempted to smash the stolen sapphire before being outmaneuvered by the party.

During this session, A'lai killed the last acid-wounded Zhentarim thug and proposed a pause that he initially distinguished from surrender: “I'd like to stop, but not surrender.” He claimed to know the two important lines of a cryptogram, the location of a room beneath Candlekeep, and the identity of whoever had been paying to keep the party’s drow alive. He wanted to deliver that information behind a locked door before the people ascending the stairs arrived.

He surrendered before the assassin appeared and willingly allowed Zalthir to bind him. He then betrayed the party by directing the assassin toward Daz, presenting his capture as “not a loss” but “a position.” When the assassin failed to find the sapphire and was killed, A'lai broke down weeping.

A'lai desperately wants confinement in Candlekeep’s magic-nullifying prison, both because it offers protection and because even imprisonment there would allow him to remain within the institution that defines his identity. Manshoon’s successful breach has made him doubt that even those cells are safe. He withheld key information from Manshoon about the depository’s contents and intends to trade that secret for protection.

### Tadric

A watcher and Kalan’s lieutenant who held the real High Tower key. **Alive.** When the party reached him, he handed the real key over to Gyrgum voluntarily. During the previous session he realized that Moziqodo was dead and urgently wanted Sylvira told about her son’s death.

### Kalan Strongbranch

A figure who admitted to giving the party a fake, non-magical key in order to use them as a distraction, while the real key remained with Tadric. He revealed the location of the real key when confronted.

Kalan had previously suggested imprisoning the young drow in Candlekeep’s magic-nullifying cells, which is why the party understood the prison’s function. By the end of this session he had fled—“Kalan is to the wind”—and his whereabouts were unknown.

### Moziqodo

Killed by the party in a single round of combat during their approach to the High Tower. Moziqodo had murdered Bookwyrm, taken her High Tower key, and **delivered it to A'lai Aivenmore** before the party killed him. The party later stripped that key from A'lai and hid it inside Glabbagool.

Moziqodo’s death also left the party facing the prospect of informing his mother, Sylvira, though they deferred that conversation while rushing toward the High Tower.

### Bookwyrm (First Reader)

**Dead.** Murdered by Moziqodo, who took her High Tower key and delivered it to A'lai. Her death was one outcome of A'lai Aivenmore’s wider plot, alongside the death of Janussi. With Bookwyrm gone and several other senior Avowed neutralized, Candlekeep’s intended final line of defense was critically weakened.

### Manshoon (Simulacrum)

A legendary and feared wizard tied to the Zhentarim, described as the man who died more than once and kept returning—“the ultimate bad penny.” He was associated with the Zhentarim’s transformation into an evil cult and repeatedly returned from apparent death without ever becoming a better person.

He is the mysterious figure A'lai Aivenmore served for eleven years. His courier concealed a metal hand beneath a glove, a clue to Manshoon’s identity.

What breached Candlekeep this session was **his simulacrum** (GM ruling 2026-08-19). The simulacrum expended massive magical resources and artifacts to enter, arriving alone and weakened rather than at full power with a retinue. It used a wall of force to facilitate the destruction of the High Tower’s magical door. It does not yet know the truth A'lai withheld about the depository.

### Glabbagool

An intelligent grey ooze and companion to the party. In the previous session (ch64) he fought alongside the party in the High Tower and stood protectively over the fallen Zalthir.

Glabbagool briefly contemplated whether an Avowed wizard would dissolve like anyone else and frightened A'lai merely by entertaining the possibility. He was ultimately entrusted with hiding one of the real High Tower keys inside his body. The responsibility made him appear half a centimeter taller and “a little less pancakey.”

Because slashing attacks can divide an ooze, the party briefly wondered whether splitting Glabbagool would produce two sentient Glabbagools or one nonsentient half. They declined to experiment on their friend.

### Zhentarim Thugs

Mercenaries and agents of the Black Network who infiltrated the High Tower to loot specific books from a written manifest. Their weapons were bloodied upon the party’s arrival.

In the previous session (ch64), Gyrgum coated both with *Tasha’s caustic brew*, and one was melted immediately. This session, the survivor continued taking acid damage, missed Gyrgum twice with his mace, and broke away after A'lai ordered him to pursue Daz. He survived Daz’s fireball with one hit point, only to be killed by A'lai himself.

### Zhentarim Assassin

A lethal operative dressed in Zhentarim attire who ambushed the party in the High Tower. He appeared after A'lai had surrendered and been bound. A'lai greeted him as a rescuer, claimed to know who carried each item, and directed him toward Daz.

The assassin remained focused on recovering the sapphire even after Thorin struck him. He hit Daz twice for twenty and twenty-three points, knocking him unconscious, then searched his body instead of making another attack. He was horrified to find no sapphire.

Thorin frightened him with Menacing Attack, battered him through an Action Surge, nearly finished him with a critical radiant strike, and killed him with Hill Strike. Thorin then searched his body. **The assassin is dead.**

### Dawnbringer

A sentient weapon wielded by Thorin; her female identity was reaffirmed on the record during this session. Thorin connected the name with a high-school girlfriend named Dawn, while Gyrgum associated Dawn’s female identity with the “rosy-fingered Dawn” of the *Iliad*.

Dawnbringer deals radiant damage. Thorin used her against the fleeing thug and throughout his punishing sequence of attacks against the Zhentarim assassin, including the critical radiant strike that left the assassin with only five hit points.


## Locations

### Candlekeep

A legendary library fortress. It is famously impossible to steal books within its walls, yet A'lai secretly removed selected books for eleven years. The keep also has a force field, yet there were raiders inside; whatever means smuggled the books out may also have brought them in.

Its magical security wards were compromised during this session, allowing Daz’s fireball to detonate on the High Tower walkway. The explosion prompted screaming, ringing bells, and panic, while scholars characteristically began taking notes and preparing monographs on a phenomenon they had waited years to study.

Manshoon’s simulacrum breached the defenses separately and at enormous magical cost. The attack became possible because five key Avowed defenders were dead, neutralized, captured, or absent, leaving Candlekeep without its intended final line of resistance.

### High Tower

A prominent structure within Candlekeep where the party confronted A'lai Aivenmore in a fierce magical battle on the second-floor landing. The tower’s magical dampening effects failed during the conflict, allowing destructive magic to function freely.

Daz’s fireball detonated on its indoor walkway, the last Zhentarim thug died there, and the assassin later knocked Daz unconscious before being killed by Thorin. A sealed magical door within the tower protected the security control room until Manshoon’s simulacrum shattered it.

### Candlekeep Prison

A specialized detention area within the library designed to nullify all spellcasting, making it a fortress against magical intrusion or escape. The party knew of it because Kalan Strongbranch had previously suggested placing the young drow there.

A'lai Aivenmore desperately sought imprisonment in these cells, believing the party could persuade the Avowed to incarcerate rather than kill him. He preferred a Candlekeep cell to losing the institution altogether. Manshoon’s direct breach subsequently made A'lai fear that even this prison might not protect him indefinitely.

### Security Control Room

A room within the High Tower protected by magical doors requiring two specific keys to open. It contains magical artifacts associated with control of Candlekeep’s security wards.

The party deliberately kept the two real keys apart and moved A'lai out of the High Tower lobby before questioning him. Manshoon’s simulacrum ultimately bypassed its protections by conjuring a wall of force and shattering the door, though doing so depleted much of the power used to reach Candlekeep.

## Items

### Stolen Sapphire

A magical artifact that A'lai Aivenmore intended to smash. Doing so would have summoned or recalled one of the High Tower keys to his hand.

Daz cast *telekinesis* to wrench the sapphire from A'lai’s grasp in the previous session, then secretly passed it to Gyrgum. A'lai and the assassin nevertheless believed Daz still had it.

The sapphire is bound to the real High Tower key already held by Gyrgum. Consequently, A'lai’s plan to smash it would not have located the other key; it would simply have recalled Gyrgum’s key. At session end, Gyrgum possesses both that key and its corresponding sapphire.

### Real High Tower Key

The genuine magical key handed over voluntarily by Tadric. It is one of two keys normally required to open the sealed security-control-room doors.

Gyrgum held this key along with the sapphire and the fake key throughout the confrontation. A'lai incorrectly assumed the party had hidden it somewhere else in Candlekeep because only an idiot would bring both necessary keys into the room where an enemy wanted to use them.

The second real key—the one taken from Bookwyrm by Moziqodo and delivered to A'lai—was stripped from A'lai this session and hidden inside Glabbagool.

### Fake High Tower Key

A plain, non-magical key given to the party by Kalan Strongbranch to serve as a decoy while the real key remained with Tadric. Its lack of magical aura was detected previously, explaining why the party’s attempts to use or analyze it had produced no useful magical response.

Gyrgum still held the fake key at the end of the session, giving the party another possible means of misdirection.

### Written Manifest

A clipboard carried by the Zhentarim raiders to track the specific books they were ordered to loot from Candlekeep’s collection. Its existence showed that the theft was planned and targeted rather than opportunistic. The party joked that the clipboard helped explain why their tactics worked: “People actually carry him around.”
