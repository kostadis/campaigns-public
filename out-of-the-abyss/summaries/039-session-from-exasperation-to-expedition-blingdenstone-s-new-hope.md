# Chapter 39 From Exasperation to Expedition: Blingdenstone's New Hope

Date: 2025-12-02

## Summary

With the party poised to recruit Clan Goldwhisker, Daz—whose takeover had come from exhaustion with political maneuvering rather than ambition—recognized exactly why Dorbo had become so accommodating. The chief had found a convenient fall guy: if the operation failed, he could blame the drow who had seized control; if it succeeded, he could claim that supporting Daz had been his own design. Daz remained determined to involve the Goldwhiskers so everyone would have warning if the Pudding King operation failed and because, as he argued, their political status could be settled only if they survived. Senni supported the appeal to common purpose, though Daz sharpened her rhetoric: “It's important for us to have a common outcome and a common drive to that outcome. The rest of the commonality would be a nice-to-have.” The bureaucrats began taking notes on Daz's pronouncements while papers shuffled around the chamber, each official seemingly preparing to explain how faithfully implementing Daz's leadership had produced any eventual success.

Before heading to meet the Clan Goldwhisker, Thorin's completionist curiosity got the better of him. Unable to tolerate unexplored portions of the map, he investigated the remaining doors in the bureaucratic hub. The eastern door opened into a kitchen or break room, where a gnome chef mistook the group for the newly hired heroes and Thorin asserted that their agreement included board and “unlimited access to the snack room.” The chef eventually agreed to provide a snack. Chief Dorbo insisted they eat elsewhere, at the Foaming Mug, where there were tables and chairs suitable for larger visitors.

The other unexplored door led toward Chief Dorbo's private quarters. Dorbo intercepted the party and dryly explained that he had no acid-resistant armor inside—only a desk, couch, bed, and private space. Glabbagool volunteered to squeeze through and investigate, but the party decided it was not yet time to antagonize their host that thoroughly.

The chef then noticed the intelligent gelatinous cube and asked whether Glabbagool could clean the kitchen floor. Glabbagool was delighted by the chance to be useful and productive, earning the affectionate nickname “Roomba.” This prompted a discussion of the legendary origin of gelatinous cubes as dungeon-cleaning creatures created to remove abandoned corpses and treasure between expeditions.

The party approached the massive, monumental double doors leading to the Goldwhisker Warrens. They briefly considered sending Glabbagool underneath to scout, but rejected the idea because the inhabitants might mistake him for a hostile ooze. Someone finally proposed the radical alternative of simply knocking. Daz announced that they intended to rid the warrens, grotto, and surrounding area of the slime problem and needed the Goldwhiskers' help. A startled guard told them not to leave and scampered away to consult Chief Chipgrin.

Four gnome guards escorted the party through the warrens. Their stringy mustaches, pronounced noses, and other subtle features appeared increasingly rat-like. Although Gyrgum had the wisdom not to bluntly point out their appearance, the party openly expressed curiosity about meeting wererats and assured the guards that they were not judgmental. The guards were thrilled to encounter visitors who had not arrived demanding the wererats' deaths. When the party observed that rats, lycanthropic or otherwise, occupied an important ecological niche, one guard triumphantly declared, “See, I told you.” The remark ignited a loud philosophical debate among the four guards about rats, wererats, and their respective ecological roles, which continued all the way to Chipgrin's court.

The party found Chief Chipgrin seated atop a rise carpeted with soft, well-manicured green moss. His stone chair was draped with a cave badger pelt, and he was flanked by two giant rats and numerous guards. Chipgrin immediately assumed that the Diggermattocks had sent the party to drive his clan out. Daz instead explained that the party wanted to escape the Underdark and had learned that helping Blingdenstone defeat its ooze infestation would secure that aid.

Daz offered to arrange the conversation Chipgrin had long wanted with Chief Dorbo, but imposed strict conditions: the meeting would be for cooperation, not political maneuvering, positioning, recrimination, or opportunities to say “I told you so.” Otherwise, the party would leave and allow the gnomes to deal with the oozes themselves. Chipgrin remarked, “I've been told that Drow are a difficult group to negotiate with, but you take it to the next level.” Daz replied, “It's not a negotiation. I'm here to ask you for your help.”

Chief Chipgrin agreed to join the fight but refused to place the Clan Goldwhisker under Dorbo's command or participate in a “grand crusade” designed to send his people to slaughter. Daz clarified that the party would formulate the plan and make the operational decisions. He personally accepted responsibility if Goldwhisker fighters died because of those choices, even acknowledging that Chipgrin could seek vengeance against him afterward. Daz had no ambition beyond eliminating the threat and leaving Blingdenstone. The blunt arrangement satisfied Chipgrin, producing a moment of “shock and awe” among his listeners before he agreed to attend a broader meeting of the clan leaders.

Chipgrin then shared valuable information about the Pudding King. He had known the gnome when he worked in the tunnels, his kin had witnessed the gnome's return, and he knew both what the Pudding King was doing and where he could be found. The Pudding King's fall appeared rooted in vengeance, pride, madness, resentment at being ignored, and demonic power that amplified his worst qualities rather than entirely replacing his personality.

Chipgrin asked whether the party had noticed anything strange about Blingdenstone's other gnomes. Reflecting on their encounters, they recognized that greed and aggression had become unusually pronounced throughout the settlement. Dorbo's fixation on profit seemed particularly unhinged in the face of an existential threat. The party told Chipgrin about Juiblex and connected the phenomenon to the demonic corruption they had already witnessed in Gracklstugh, Neverlight Grove, and among the kuo-toa. Chipgrin agreed that the influence centered on the Pudding King but was bleeding outward into the entire community.

After Daz used a forceful appeal to prevent the returning leaders from immediately derailing the negotiations, everyone agreed to convene a moot within a few hours. The gathering included Chief Dorbo, Senni, Chief Chipgrin and his Goldwhiskers, Nomi Pathshutter, Gurnik Tapfinger, Kazook Pickshine, and the ghostly Burrow Warden Uth-Jadger. Senni asked Daz to call the meeting to order because he was now leading the effort.

Daz summarized the crisis: an amphitheater full of oozes lay beneath Blingdenstone, commanded by a Pudding King heavily influenced by a demon lord of oozes whose corruption was already affecting the settlement's leaders. The party possessed magic that might let them remove the Pudding King directly, but they needed a convincing distraction. Every able-bodied gnome would launch an apparent full-scale assault on the ooze gathering while the party moved covertly to “snatch” the Pudding King and end his control. Uth-Jadger enthusiastically praised the plan as displaying the technical brilliance once associated with the Burrow Wardens.

The leaders then began proposing strategic modifications and side quests. Dorbo suggested that the army attack from the Temple of the Steadfast Stone, redirecting the oozes away from Blingdenstone's populated areas. The temple stood within the Rockblight and would first need to be cleansed. Gurnik Tapfinger produced a red spell gem containing the Hallow spell and explained that it must be placed in the temple's menhir. Ogrémoch's Bane would then send servants to dislodge the gem, forcing the party to defend it until its power awakened three temple guardians and cleansed the site.

Gurnik and Nomi Pathshutter delivered an obviously rehearsed exchange explaining that Earth Elementals would be ideal troops against the oozes but could be driven insane by Ogrémoch's Bane. Cleansing the Steadfast Stone would allow the Stoneheart Enclave to protect summoned elementals during the battle. Daz and the others questioned why the army could not simply attack from the north, avoiding the Rockblight entirely. The room's silence revealed the truth: Blingdenstone's leaders had discovered a capable adventuring party and were attempting to use it to solve additional community problems. The leaders hastily praised the northern approach, while Gurnik looked increasingly concerned that his opportunity to have the temple cleansed was slipping away.

The strategic case for the side quests was nevertheless real. Earth Elementals would help the gnome army survive, and completing preparatory tasks would reduce the number of hostile encounters before the final confrontation. Kazook Pickshine warned that ooze acid could destroy the soldiers' weapons and armor. He knew how to make a protective treatment but needed ingredients from Neverlight Grove. Alternatively, the gnomes could send salt to Gracklstugh in exchange for better weapons—assuming Gracklstugh still existed and remained willing to trade after the party's departure. Uth-Jadger offered assistance from other Burrow Warden spirits if the party completed a separate task for them.

Chief Dorbo's committee sweetened the Rockblight proposal by offering the party a Stone of Controlling Earth Elementals. The group objected to the idea of enslaving an elemental and began speculating about befriending one, freeing it by giving it its own control stone “like giving Dobby a sock,” or repeatedly summoning the same elemental until it became a recurring companion. They imagined Glabbagool and an Earth Elemental getting along and joked that a sufficiently good “summoner score” might pair them with the best elementals, like an extraplanar rideshare service.

The party seriously considered ignoring the preparations and trusting their magical “snatch and grab” plan. As a monk who did not wear armor, Zalthir was less worried about corrosion than the armored combatants, but everyone recognized that the gnome army depended heavily on its equipment. They also worried that allowing the entire army to be slaughtered would reflect poorly on them. Thorin settled the cautious-versus-reckless question with a d20: above ten meant safety, below ten meant “YOLO.” He rolled a 17, selecting caution.

The group therefore chose to cleanse the Rockblight first, earning both elemental support for the battle and the Stone of Controlling Earth Elementals. They would also revisit Neverlight Grove for the acid-protection ingredients. They explicitly rejected the Gracklstugh option because no one knew what condition the city was in or how the party would be received there.

To maximize their efforts, the party assigned Glabbagool, Eldeth, and Jimjar to assist Uth-Jadger and the other Burrow Warden ghosts while the main adventuring party handled the Rockblight and Neverlight Grove. Their initial joking description of this assignment as “fight the ghosts” was corrected to helping them, though the party compared the arrangement to defeating creatures in order to recruit them in a game.

The session ended with the Rockblight cleansing selected as the next immediate expedition. The players reflected that Daz had seized command when political doublespeak made action impossible, yet deferred to the group once meaningful decisions had to be made. Daz insisted, “I'm not the decision maker. I just couldn't put up with the BS anymore.” The party finally had a plan—long in coming, but supported by Blingdenstone's competing factions.

### Chief Chipgrin

Leader of Clan Goldwhisker, a community of wererat gnomes. He holds court on a moss-covered rise, seated on a stone chair draped with a cave badger pelt and flanked by two giant rats.

Chipgrin initially assumes the Diggermattocks have sent the adventurers to evict his clan. He has long wanted Dorbo to speak with him but refuses to let the Goldwhiskers be placed under Dorbo's command or sacrificed in a Diggermattock-led crusade.

Daz's blunt refusal to engage in political bargaining impresses Chipgrin. After Daz accepts personal responsibility for the party's strategy and any Goldwhisker losses, Chipgrin agrees to join the campaign and attend the moot.

Chipgrin knew the Pudding King when he worked in the tunnels. His kin saw the gnome after his return, and Chipgrin knows who he is, what he is doing, and where he can be found. He helps the party recognize that the demonic corruption centered on the Pudding King is spreading greed and aggression throughout Blingdenstone.

### Goldwhisker Guards

Four wererat gnomes guarding the entrance to the Goldwhisker Warrens. Their stringy mustaches, pronounced noses, and other features appear increasingly rat-like on close inspection.

They are accustomed to outsiders treating them with fear and hostility. The party's genuine curiosity and assurance that it does not judge lycanthropes therefore delights them.

When the party mentions the ecological importance of rats, one guard exclaims, “See, I told you,” triggering an animated philosophical argument about the ecological niches of rats and wererats. The debate continues while they escort the party to Chief Chipgrin.

### Pudding King

A former gnome tunnel worker who now commands Blingdenstone's oozes and slimes. Chief Chipgrin knew him before his transformation and knows his current location.

His actions are driven by vengeance, wounded pride, resentment, and madness. The party believes Juiblex or another demonic influence has empowered him and amplified his worst existing traits rather than simply replacing his personality.

His influence is not confined to his own body or ooze followers. Greed and aggression are bleeding into Blingdenstone's population and leadership, with Chief Dorbo's increasingly irrational fixation on money serving as one conspicuous example.

The party intends to use magic to seize the Pudding King while Blingdenstone's combined forces distract his ooze army.

### Nomi Pathshutter

An aide-de-camp working with Gurnik Tapfinger. She participates in the moot and helps present the argument for securing Earth Elemental support.

Her exchange with Gurnik is transparently rehearsed. Nomi points out that summoning elementals would require finding Entémoch's Boon and using its summoning circle, allowing Gurnik to explain why Ogrémoch's Bane must first be defeated.

### Gurnik Tapfinger

The head priest of Callarduran Smoothhands in the Stoneheart Enclave. He strongly desires to see the Temple of the Steadfast Stone and the surrounding Rockblight cleansed.

Gurnik provides the red spell gem containing Hallow and explains that it must be placed in the temple's menhir. Only after prompting does he make clear that Ogrémoch's Bane will send servants to remove it and that the party must defend the gem until three guardians awaken.

He and Nomi Pathshutter deliver an obviously prepared presentation advocating the use of Earth Elementals. Gurnik argues that elementals are ideal against oozes but would risk being driven mad unless the Steadfast Stone is cleansed and the Stoneheart Enclave can protect them.

He becomes visibly disappointed when the party exposes the gnome leaders' ulterior motives and proposes attacking from the north without undertaking his temple-cleansing mission.

### Kazook Pickshine

A representative of the Miners' Guild who is concerned about acidic damage to the gnome army's weapons and armor.

Kazook knows of a potion or protective treatment that can prevent acid corrosion, but he needs a large quantity of ingredients from Neverlight Grove to produce it. As an alternative, he proposes shipping salt to Gracklstugh in exchange for high-quality weapons.

The party chooses the Neverlight Grove option because Gracklstugh's present condition is uncertain and their last departure from the city was not amicable.

### Uth-Jadger

A ghostly Burrow Warden who attends the moot. He enthusiastically praises Daz's plan as an example of the technical brilliance once displayed by the Burrow Wardens.

Uth-Jadger identifies the Rockblight as an obstacle to using the Temple of the Steadfast Stone and frames Blingdenstone's unity as essential to victory.

He has spoken with other Burrow Warden spirits and says they will help in the battle if the party handles a quest on their behalf. When the party initially prioritizes the other preparations, he looks disappointed until Glabbagool, Eldeth, and Jimjar are assigned to assist the ghosts.

### Eldeth

A supporter of the party who is assigned to assist Glabbagool and Jimjar with the Burrow Warden ghosts' quest while the main party undertakes the Rockblight and Neverlight Grove missions.

### Jimjar

A supporter of the party who is assigned to assist Glabbagool and Eldeth with the Burrow Warden ghosts' quest. This division allows the party to pursue multiple preparations for the Pudding King battle simultaneously.

## Locations

### Blingdenstone

A deep gnome settlement in the Underdark and the current center of the party's efforts. Its bureaucratic leadership is divided among financial, mining, religious, civic, and clan interests.

The city is threatened by the Pudding King and an army of oozes gathered in an amphitheater beneath the settlement. Demonic influence appears to be spreading outward from the Pudding King, heightening greed and aggression among Blingdenstone's inhabitants.

The party is attempting to unite the Diggermattocks, Clan Goldwhisker, the Stoneheart Enclave, the Miners' Guild, and the ghostly Burrow Wardens for a coordinated attack.

### Chief Dorbo's Kitchen

A spacious kitchen and break room inside Blingdenstone's bureaucratic hub. A gnome chef works here and is protective of his workspace.

Thorin discovers the room while trying to explore every unopened door. The chef agrees to provide a snack, but Chief Dorbo refuses to let the adventurers eat there. Glabbagool is permitted to remain long enough to clean the floor.

The kitchen lies near Dorbo's private quarters, which contain a desk, couch, and bed—but, according to Dorbo, no acid-resistant armor.

### The Foaming Mug

An inn or cafe in Blingdenstone where Chief Dorbo directs the party to eat. It has tables and seating more appropriate for visitors larger than deep gnomes.

Chief Dorbo initially refers to it as the place with tables for “big people,” then acknowledges that Thorin himself is short and clarifies that the taller members of the party are the concern.

### Goldwhisker Warrens

Home of Clan Goldwhisker, a community of unaffiliated wererat gnomes. The warrens are accessed through locked, monumental double doors roughly fifty feet high.

Within is a raised audience area covered by soft, well-manicured green moss. Chief Chipgrin's stone chair stands there, draped with a cave badger pelt and flanked by two giant rats.

The party's accepting treatment of the wererat guards creates an unusually positive first impression before its negotiations with Chipgrin.

### Temple of the Steadfast Stone

A temple within the Rockblight proposed by Dorbo as a strategic staging point for the attack on the Pudding King.

Its location would allow Blingdenstone's army to draw the ooze forces away from populated areas. The temple has fallen under malign influence and cannot safely serve this role until cleansed.

A red spell gem must be placed within its menhir and defended until three temple guardians awaken. Once restored, the temple will allow the Stoneheart Enclave to protect Earth Elementals from the madness caused by Ogrémoch's Bane.

### Rockblight

The corrupted district surrounding the Temple of the Steadfast Stone. It is under the influence of Ogrémoch's Bane and must be cleansed before summoned Earth Elementals can safely participate in the battle.

The party selects the Rockblight as its next destination. It expects to place the Hallow gem, withstand waves of Ogrémoch's servants, awaken the temple guardians, and secure both elemental support and the promised magic stone.

### Neverlight Grove

A location the party has previously visited and one of the places where it encountered evidence of demonic corruption.

Kazook Pickshine needs a large quantity of ingredients from the grove to prepare a treatment that protects weapons and armor from ooze acid. The party chooses to revisit the grove rather than rely on trade with Gracklstugh.

Travel to the grove is treated as familiar enough that the route itself need not occupy substantial time.

### Gracklstugh

A city that could potentially provide high-quality weapons in exchange for a shipment of salt.

Its current condition is uncertain because of the party's previous activities there, and the party did not leave on favorable terms. Although the gnomes could theoretically send their own caravan, the party ultimately rejects this option in favor of obtaining protective ingredients from Neverlight Grove.

## Items

### Red Spell Gem

A red gem containing the Hallow spell, produced by Gurnik Tapfinger for use at the Temple of the Steadfast Stone.

The gem must be inserted into the temple's menhir. Doing so will provoke Ogrémoch's Bane to send servants to remove it, requiring the party to defend the site against waves of attackers.

If the gem remains in place long enough, it will awaken three temple guardians and cleanse the sanctuary. This will permit the Stoneheart Enclave to protect summoned Earth Elementals from Ogrémoch's corrupting influence.

### Stone of Controlling Earth Elementals

A magic stone offered by Chief Dorbo's committee as a reward for defeating Ogrémoch's Bane and cleansing the Temple of the Steadfast Stone.

The stone can summon an Earth Elemental that remains friendly to the user and their companions for a limited duration, approximately an hour, in a manner compared to the Conjure Elemental spell.

The party dislikes the implication of enslaving the elemental and prefers to treat it as a potential companion. They discuss freeing it, giving it control of its own stone, befriending it during repeated summons, or arranging for the same individual elemental to answer each call. They hope the relationship might eventually resemble Glabbagool's voluntary companionship.

## Spells

### Hallow

A powerful sanctification spell contained within Gurnik Tapfinger's red spell gem.

The spell is intended to cleanse the Temple of the Steadfast Stone. Once the gem is placed in the temple's menhir, servants of Ogrémoch's Bane will attack in an attempt to remove it.

The party must hold the position until three temple guardians awaken. The completed cleansing will restore the sanctuary and allow the Stoneheart Enclave to shield Earth Elementals from the Bane's madness during the battle against the Pudding King.

### Conjure Elemental

A spell mentioned as the closest comparison for the effect of the Stone of Controlling Earth Elementals.

The stone summons an Earth Elemental that is friendly to its user and the user's companions for a limited duration. The summoned creature is not understood to remain permanently, though the party hopes it may be possible to befriend a recurring elemental and persuade it to become a willing companion.
