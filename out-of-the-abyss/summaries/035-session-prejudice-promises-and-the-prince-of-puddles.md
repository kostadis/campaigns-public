# Chapter 35 Prejudice, Promises, and the Prince of Puddles

Date: 2025-10-27

## Summary

The party arrived on the shores of the Darklake drenched, cold, and miserable from their journey through the water. Jimjar was thrilled to be so close to home, announcing, “We are but a day's journey from the outskirts of Blingdenstone. I know it for sure this time.” He immediately wagered that someone would demand a rest. Once the party agreed to take a long rest, he celebrated: “Excellent. I made—I won, I won my bet.” Asked who had accepted the wager, Jimjar admitted to a “slight miscalculation,” then unsuccessfully tried to start another bet about whether Gyrgum would take the bet.

With no one pursuing them, the party settled in for a full night’s rest. Gyrgum puzzled over the unfamiliar idea of a “long rest” and concluded that it might explain why closing his eyes sometimes left him feeling refreshed. By morning, everyone had recovered their strength and was ready for the day-long march to Blingdenstone.

The approach to the city ended at a long, tedious staircase built for three-foot-tall people. Jimjar, Spiderbait, and the smaller members of the company climbed comfortably, while the stairs were difficult terrain for the larger travelers. At the top, Jimjar happily declared, “We've arrived at Blingdenstone!” He explained that the city had belonged to the gnomes, then the drow, and then the gnomes again. Although Blingdenstone was now “a little bit of a mess,” its inhabitants knew routes out of the Underdark and might be persuaded to help.

The party reached a massive gate made from steel and mithral, laid with magical, magic-resistant alloys, and plated in adamantine. Eight gnome guards waited behind it. Their leader demanded to know who the travelers were and why they had come. One guard immediately distrusted Daz, saying, “You are a drow, and we know that drow lie.” Gyrgum countered, “But I am an orc, and everyone knows that orcs only tell the truth,” bewildering the guards further when they realized he was an orc cleric of Bahamut.

Jimjar tried to reassure the guards by insisting Daz was interested in exploration and growth rather than domination or world conquest, even claiming he was reading a popular book called *The Up in the Overbright*. Daz then explained that the group had been detained and exploited against their will, had escaped their captors, and had gathered other good people seeking safe companions and a better destination. They had arrived at Blingdenstone hoping for help.

The conversation was disrupted when Glabbagool finally finished climbing the stairs and appeared behind the party. The gnomes panicked and prepared to attack the gelatinous cube, but the party intervened, explaining that he had saved their lives, fought other slimy creatures on their behalf, and could even clean the guards’ weapons. Glabbagool greeted them cheerfully and explained that he could squeeze through the gate without it being opened, but accepted the party’s warning that doing so would be rude.

The guards were astonished that a gelatinous cube could speak. After briefly and mistakenly referring to a fungi problem, they clarified that Blingdenstone was suffering from a serious ooze problem. Glabbagool explained that he had once been an unintelligent creature until an unknown event caused him to begin thinking and talking. Since then, he had noticed far more oozes in the region, all unusually agitated and eager to expand. Ordinary oozes, he said, were quiet creatures that waited for food to come to them. The new activity was more like an “oozapalooza.”

That discussion reminded the party of Daz’s earlier information about Juiblex. At the demon lord’s name, Jimjar’s expression shifted as though he were remembering something painful. He described Juiblex as the demon lord of oozes and shapeless things, embodying the corrupted nature of the Abyss and an unrivaled obscenity that dissolved everything into an unstructured mess. He contrasted Juiblex with Zuggtmoy’s corruption through decay, Demogorgon’s carnage, and Orcus’s endless death. When asked how he knew so much, Jimjar maintained that he was merely someone who collected information and made bets. Daz observed, “If you make enough bets with enough people, they start telling you things.”

Jimjar then offered Daz a remarkably specific fifty-gold-piece wager that, before the end of the month, the party would have to equip an army with acid-resistant gear. He followed it with a side bet that the ingredients for those weapons would have to be recovered from a fungal wedding celebration. The party began to suspect that Jimjar’s bets were his way of telling them what needed to be done without directly explaining himself. An attempt to read him revealed that he knew far too many precise details, while the surrounding gnomes treated him with peculiar deference. When one party member quietly questioned a guard, the guard admitted Jimjar looked very familiar but could not place where he had seen him, reinforcing the impression that their gambling companion was some kind of concealed dignitary.

The party also briefly considered whether Glabbagool could reproduce enough offspring to form a gelatinous-cube army. He explained that gelatinous cubes reproduce by leaving a smaller cube behind, but cannot do so rapidly and take a long time to grow. He had once left such a cube behind because he was lonely, but it could not speak. Glabbagool believed he might be the only speaking gelatinous cube in the universe.

The guards ultimately permitted the entire group to enter, but urged them to consult Chief Dorbo Diggermattock and Quartermaster Senni for full permission to keep Glabbagool in the city and for an explanation of Blingdenstone’s ooze crisis. The party then passed into the Maze, a defensive network of twisting, trapped tunnels intended to slow and kill invading armies. When a gnome pointedly mentioned those defenses while indicating Daz, Daz answered that the powerful fortifications made him feel safer and thanked them for hosting the group. His tactful compliment delighted the gnomes.

As they traveled, the group discussed relations between gnomes and dwarves. The two peoples competed for the same finite underground resources, particularly when expansionist factions were involved, but could become allies against deeper Underdark threats. Eldeth admitted that she considered gnomes clever with magic and gems but not particularly formidable fighters.

The conversation prompted Eldeth to reflect on her inherited prejudice toward drow. She told Daz that she had been raised to believe “The only good drow is a dead drow,” but traveling with him had forced her to reconsider. She now believed the true enemy was the fanatical cult of Lolth rather than every individual drow. Daz cautioned that even those raised knowing nothing but that cult might be redeemable people shaped by a terrible environment. Eldeth acknowledged that his example had convinced her that her culture’s reflexive treatment of entire peoples as inherently evil and worthy of massacre was something she needed to rethink.

## NPCs

### Jimjar

A gnome companion known for turning almost every situation into a wager. Delighted to be nearing Blingdenstone, he correctly predicts that the exhausted party will demand a rest, although he neglects to secure anyone to take the other side of the bet.

Jimjar knows Blingdenstone’s history, leaders, and present dangers in extraordinary detail. Mention of Juiblex causes him to look as though he is recalling something painful. He then predicts that the party will need to equip an army with acid-resistant gear and recover the necessary ingredients from a fungal wedding celebration. His companions increasingly suspect that his wagers are a disguised means of revealing information or directing their actions.

Jimjar claims to be someone who collects information and makes bets. Daz observes, “If you make enough bets with enough people, they start telling you things.” Nevertheless, Blingdenstone’s gnomes treat him with unusual deference, and at least one guard recognizes him without remembering why. The party concludes that he may be some kind of hidden dignitary or VIP.

### Glabbagool

A sentient gelatinous cube traveling with the party. He took longer than the others to climb Blingdenstone’s gnome-sized stairs, and his sudden appearance at the gate nearly provoked an attack by the guards.

Glabbagool was once an ordinary, unintelligent ooze. Something unknown caused him to begin thinking and speaking. He now considers himself possibly the only talking gelatinous cube in the universe. He is friendly, eats only dead garbage rather than living creatures, and has repeatedly aided the party in dangerous situations.

He reports that ordinary oozes are quiet creatures that remain in place and wait for food, but the local population has become unusually numerous, agitated, aggressive, and eager to grow. He refers to the phenomenon as an “oozapalooza.”

Glabbagool can reproduce by leaving behind a smaller cube, but cannot do so rapidly, and the offspring require a long time to grow. He once created such a cube because he was lonely, but it lacked his intelligence and ability to speak.

### Gnome Guards

Eight gnomes responsible for the main gate of Blingdenstone. They guard the city from behind an exceptionally strong steel, mithral, and adamantine barrier.

The guards are deeply suspicious of outsiders. They immediately distrust Daz because he is a drow and nearly attack Glabbagool on sight because of the city’s ongoing ooze crisis. They are astonished by Gyrgum’s status as an orc cleric of Bahamut and by Glabbagool’s ability to speak.

After hearing the party’s story and accepting Jimjar’s guarantee, they allow the entire company into Blingdenstone. They display peculiar deference toward Jimjar, although one guard can say only that he looks familiar. They direct the party to Chief Dorbo Diggermattock and Quartermaster Senni for proper permission and further information.

### Chief Dorbo Diggermattock

The chief of Blingdenstone. The gate guards recommend that the party report to him for full permission to remain in the city, particularly because they are accompanied by Glabbagool during an active ooze crisis. Jimjar’s pointed references suggest Chief Dorbo can also explain the city’s present problems and its possible need for acid-resistant equipment.

### Quartermaster Senni

Blingdenstone’s quartermaster. The guards instruct the party to speak with Senni alongside Chief Dorbo Diggermattock to clarify Glabbagool’s status and learn what is happening in the city. Jimjar’s references to equipment, weapons, and acid resistance suggest Senni may be important to Blingdenstone’s response to the ooze threat.

### Eldeth

A dwarven party member with a dim view of gnomes, whom she considers clever with magic and gems but less capable as warriors. She recognizes that dwarves and gnomes compete for finite underground resources, while also acknowledging that they can become allies against deeper Underdark threats.

Eldeth was raised to believe that all drow were evil and that “The only good drow is a dead drow.” Her captivity reinforced this prejudice, but traveling with Daz has forced her to reconsider it. She now distinguishes drow individuals from the fanatical cult of Lolth and accepts that people raised within that cult may still be redeemable. Daz’s example prompts her to question her culture’s assumption that entire peoples can be inherently evil and deserving of destruction.

### Dawnbringer

A sword carried by a party member that becomes upset in darkness. During the party’s entrance into the poorly illuminated Maze, its bearer remarks, “In darkness, my sword's getting very upset.” Dawnbringer’s reaction reinforces its association with illumination and its dislike of prolonged darkness.

## Locations

### Darklake

A cold, dark body of water in the Underdark. The party reaches its shores drenched, chilled, and miserable after leaving their boat. With no pursuers immediately behind them, they safely complete a long rest there before beginning the final day’s journey to Blingdenstone.

### Blingdenstone

A gnome city in the Underdark that has repeatedly changed hands, passing from the gnomes to the drow and then back to the gnomes. Jimjar describes it as “a little bit of a mess,” but believes its inhabitants can help the party find a route out of the Underdark.

The city is currently threatened by an unexplained surge of unusually active oozes. Its inhabitants appear aware of the danger and are sufficiently alarmed that the sight of a gelatinous cube at their gate nearly triggers an attack.

Blingdenstone is approached by a long staircase built for gnome-sized creatures. The stairs are comfortable for gnomes, Spiderbait, and similarly sized travelers but difficult for larger members of the party.

### Blingdenstone Gate

The heavily fortified main entrance to Blingdenstone. Its gate is constructed from steel and mithral, laid with magical, magic-resistant alloys, and plated with adamantine. It is meant to prevent hostile forces from entering, although visibility through the gate allows the guards and visitors to speak before it is opened.

Eight gnome guards defend the entrance. Their questioning reveals both the city’s longstanding fear of drow and its immediate concern about oozes.

### The Maze

A defensive network of twisting tunnels within Blingdenstone. It is deliberately designed to force invading armies through confined routes filled with deadly traps, allowing the gnomes to weaken or destroy attackers before they reach the city proper.

The guards openly explain its purpose to the party while assuring them they are not currently planning to kill their guests. Daz’s observation that such defenses make him feel safer earns the gnomes’ approval.
