# Chapter 61 The Colloquium was well received.

Date: Jun 22nd, 2026

## Summary

Gyrgum returned from his wildly successful public lecture to rejoin the party, who had been busy piecing together the threads of Janussi's murder. Daral, clearly desperate and believing himself to be dying of poison after licking his finger following contact with the very book he had reclaimed as a gift, laid on flattery so thick it was almost painful — calling Thorin a fantastic investigator and his charming partner Dawnbringer “stunning, absolutely stunning.” When Gyrgum confirmed that he could cast Lesser Restoration, Daral immediately praised his position on the “quadrangle issue” as a brilliant insight that had transformed international affairs. The party suspected Daral had mere hours before midnight claimed him, and they were not inclined to rush his salvation until his cooperation was secured. Gyrgum assured him, “In due time, my dear. In due time.”

Meanwhile, the enthusiastic scholar Fembris Lancer arrived breathless with excitement, pulling out a small set of notes containing his own theories and asking Gyrgum to collaborate. Gyrgum, with practiced academic dismissiveness, told him, “Please deposit them with my secretary,” and indicated the grey ooze Glabbagool. Glabbagool examined the notes and declared, “This is not nutritious,” followed by, “This is not food.” After reflection, the ooze added, “I remember when there was cheese. Perhaps if we had some cheese.” Fembris was visibly crushed but persisted, proposing a joint monograph. Gyrgum reminded him that a monograph had one author, prompting Fembris to suggest a “duograph,” or perhaps a monograph in which he was merely acknowledged. Gyrgum conceded that “There’s always room in the acknowledgement section,” and Fembris promised to produce a draft by the following day.

The party debated whether the sapphire and locket had been taken by different people. Possession of the locket protected it from being summoned away while its bearer remained alive, which made the sapphire unnecessary to the person already carrying it. That supported — though did not conclusively prove — the idea that separate intruders had taken the two objects. They also acknowledged that poisoning Janussi, removing his heart, and robbing his chambers could have been distinct actions undertaken by different conspirators.

The investigators still had several open leads: Alkrist’s unopened safe, the unidentified person who had assumed Sylvira’s appearance, Sylvira herself, Bookwyrm, and unexplored evidence in Janussi’s chambers. Glabbagool belatedly realized that he could have eaten through Alkrist’s safe, though doing so would have taken time. He reminded Zalthir, “Walls don’t stop us. I can eat through walls.” Zalthir observed that he could likewise have punched the safe open, but their earlier objective had been stealth rather than destruction.

A clerk named Elian arrived to praise Thorin’s “very thorough deductive work” with the kitchen staff and delivered a scholarly paper championed by the recently fired Kalan Strongbranch. Elian insisted that there had been significant institutional pushback from people who did not want murders properly investigated and suspected this research was the real reason Kalan had been fired. The paper argued that magical evidence was inherently adversarial: no single spell or magical result proved anything, because each could be evaded, manipulated, or interpreted misleadingly. Truth emerged only by collecting multiple independent facts and combining them.

Elian summarized the principle bluntly: “Magic does not make evidence meaningful. It makes evidence adversarial.” Thorin raised the problem of treating a dead witness as a reliable narrator merely because magic allowed investigators to question them, while Gyrgum offered the maxim, “Once you have eliminated the impossible, whatever remains, however unlikely, must be the truth,” and jokingly claimed to have invented it. Elian also angled for scholarly credit, first proposing a monograph with Thorin and then revising the offer when reminded that a monograph had only one author. He even suggested that if the party failed, they could write that the techniques would have solved the case had they used them properly.

The investigators divided their efforts. Gyrgum climbed to the third floor of Janussi’s private chambers and discovered that it served as a discreet love nest. There he found a note whose opening contained pointed academic insults and which made clear that Janussi considered Kalan Strongbranch’s proposed investigative controls ridiculous and solipsistic. Janussi had actively blocked Kalan’s professional agenda, establishing another powerful motive for murder.

Daz visited Sylvira, a tiefling researcher with copper-red skin, magnetic golden eyes, and tangled blue-black hair. A mage hand opened the door to her quarters. Sylvira sat up in bed beneath elegant lilac robes, feverish and shivering despite a magical fire that made the chamber uncomfortably hot. Initially warm and cooperative, she became alarmed when Daz revealed that someone bearing her likeness had been seen around Candlekeep. She feared that his claim she had been framed might itself be “a trick of the tongue,” but Daz’s careful persuasion convinced her that he was sincere.

Sylvira initially blamed Daral Yashenti, whom she regarded as a drunken and bitter academic forced to sell his worthless poetry to fund research in her own field. On further consideration, however, she dismissed him as too disorganized to execute a sophisticated frame-up. She instead named Kazryn Nyantani, Janussi’s former lover, bitter over being discarded in Sylvira’s favor. Sylvira also explained that she was a convenient target: an infernal-plane scholar and tiefling whom prejudiced people in the Overbright already regarded as an abyssal spawn.

She recounted that Kalan had asked her to intercede with Janussi to save his position, but she never did so because she agreed that Kalan was “a clown, a bore, and his research was third rate at best.” She and Janussi instead spent their meeting discussing a new library devoted to the Great Wheel of the planes before ending in an argument over a private matter. She denied calling Janussi a traitor.

Sylvira supplied a partial alibi for the crucial hours. Feeling ill, she left the feast early and secretly visited her son, hoping to coax him back into the Mordenkainen’s Magnificent Mansion she had built for his protection. She returned to her chambers sometime after midnight, roughly between 12:30 and 1:00 a.m., and saw no one afterward. She categorically denied being the Sylvira seen near the kitchens or drinking honey, exclaiming, “Honey? I would never drink honey. What kind of nonsense is this?” Her son, born in the Abyss, had infected her with an abyssal plague, explaining her worsening condition.

Sylvira also directed attention toward Teles Ahvoste, who had resented Janussi for almost twenty years after being passed over for Keeper of Tomes, and revealed that Bookwyrm had been the most forceful opponent of Kalan’s proposed investigative reforms. The timeline surrounding the Sylvira lookalike remained a puzzle: as the party had already noted, the standard one-hour duration of Polymorph could not readily explain sightings spanning several hours, suggesting repeated castings, a more powerful spell, or some other magical method.

Gyrgum retrieved the discarded wrapping paper from the dining-hall trash and reassembled its torn pieces with Mending. Once restored, the paper showed evidence of a second, deliberate cut, confirming that someone had opened Daral’s gift, tampered with the book, and then carefully re-wrapped it. The physical evidence supported premeditation rather than an impulsive crime.

Thorin and Zalthir conducted the interrogation in Alkrist’s chambers while Kalan Strongbranch, concealed outside the room, held a Zone of Truth that Alkrist did not realize was there. Alkrist, a thirty-three-year-old bronze dragonborn with the fashionable confidence of a Candlekeep “tech bro,” opened with an elaborate declaration of admiration for Janussi and gratitude for the investigators. Once he failed to resist the spell, however, his answers became increasingly uncomfortable.

Alkrist revealed that he had first spoken with his beloved aunt Bookwyrm, who was devastated because Janussi had told her she would not be his successor. Alkrist initially assumed A'lai would receive the position. He then attended his own annual review with Janussi, who praised some of his work but declared that Alkrist had no leadership potential and ordered him to abandon his research into germ warfare as dangerously irresponsible. Alkrist protested that the research was intended to understand how disease caused death and destruction so that people could be healed, but Janussi remained unmoved.

Alkrist next met A'lai at the baths at approximately 11:45 a.m., where they privately discussed their “mutual problem of Janussi.” A'lai had told him that he was more likely to be exiled than promoted to Keeper of Tomes. Under the Zone of Truth, Alkrist admitted proposing a method he had found in a book. As he tried to avoid identifying it, his hand betrayed him by drifting toward *1001 Tashalian Nights*, sitting openly on his shelf. Zalthir dramatically opened the volume to the bookmarked passage describing murder with Midnight Tears.

Alkrist confessed that he and A'lai went to the Oak Tree Apothecary. While A'lai distracted Leuwin in the garden, Alkrist stole a vial from a high shelf and spilled ground peppermint across the floor. He then went to the Deadwinter Tree, opened Daral’s gift to Janussi, coated the book with Midnight Tears, and carefully re-wrapped it. He tore the label from the poison vial, tied it to the back of the tree as decoration, and attempted to burn it with a candle. Irony and Orrin Glass arrived to prepare the supper table while he was doing so. Irony carried a basin of water, allowing Alkrist to wash his hands in case any poison remained on them.

Even after confessing, Alkrist insisted that he had not “actually” killed Janussi; he had merely put poison on a book. Alkrist then cycled through claims of entrapment, duress, and mind control, declaring, “All of these things I’ve said, they’re so true and yet so true.”

Alkrist further admitted that after midnight he brought Bookwyrm a bottle of fine wine and two glasses, believing that the death of Janussi had solved both their problems. Bookwyrm was appalled. They argued loudly, and she stormed away sometime between midnight and roughly 12:30 a.m., leaving Alkrist alone to drink before returning to his own quarters. He attempted to distinguish between telling Bookwyrm that Janussi had been poisoned and explicitly admitting that he had applied the poison, but the party recognized that Bookwyrm had concealed critically important information from them.

When asked when he had torn out Janussi’s heart, Alkrist reacted with genuine confusion and shock. He had not known the heart was missing. Attention shifted toward Bookwyrm, particularly because whoever hid the heart in the chalice had to understand its properties well enough to know the organ would remain undiscovered unless someone specifically searched for it. Alkrist could not meaningfully narrow the magical suspects; at Candlekeep, he explained, obtaining a powerful transformation scroll was as simple as contacting the right goblin and having the right Avowed retrieve it.

The full picture came into focus with grim clarity. On the day of Candlekeep’s annual reviews — when budgets, research directions, and careers were decided — Janussi had shut down Alkrist’s research, denied Bookwyrm the succession she believed was hers, left A'lai facing exile, and humiliated Kalan Strongbranch’s professional work. He had done all of this while surrounded by powerful wizards with access to innumerable ways of killing him. As the party saw it, Janussi might have possessed high Intelligence, but he plainly lacked Wisdom.

With Alkrist’s confession secured, the party turned to the disposition of the evidence. Thorin proposed handing it directly to Kalan Strongbranch and letting the former Gatewarden “clean house.” Gyrgum raised the possibility of blackmailing one or more of the conspirators. Daz required rare information, and A'lai’s drow background might give him access to precisely the Underdark knowledge Daz needed. The party also considered demanding greater payment from Candlekeep — perhaps a rare book for every suspect uncovered. Janussi’s poisoning had been solved, but the thefts, the removed heart, Bookwyrm’s actions, and the magical impersonation of Sylvira remained entangled in the larger conspiracy.

- After Bookwyrm left, Alkrist remained alone, drank some wine, and eventually returned to his own room.
- When asked whether Bookwyrm knows a powerful Polymorph spell, Alkrist laughs. At Candlekeep, obtaining such magic merely requires finding the right goblin and the right Avowed to retrieve a scroll.
- Asked when he tore out Janussi’s heart, Alkrist responds in genuine shock: “Ripped out his heart? What? His heart was ripped out?”
- His reaction separates the poisoning from the later mutilation and theft, turning suspicion toward Bookwyrm and other conspirators.
- The party notes that whoever placed the heart in the chalice had to understand its properties and know that the organ would remain hidden unless someone deliberately searched there.
- The investigators conclude that at least three people may have participated in the broader events, even though Alkrist physically poisoned the book and A'lai conspired in the murder.
- The party compares the killing to the assassination of Julius Caesar: determining a single “murderer” is difficult when multiple conspirators jointly arranged the fatal act.
- Looking across the motives, they realize Janussi spent annual-review day destroying or threatening the ambitions of numerous powerful spellcasters.
- The party concludes that Janussi may have had high intelligence, but not high wisdom.

### Concluding the Investigation

#### The party weighs their options after uncovering a web of conspiracy involving multiple high-ranking members of Candlekeep.

- The Zone of Truth provides the decisive shortcut that turns the accumulated evidence into Alkrist’s direct confession.
- The poisoning itself is solved: Alkrist applied the Midnight Tears, while A'lai conspired with him and helped obtain the poison.
- Important associated crimes remain less certain, including the magical impersonation of Sylvira, the removal and concealment of Janussi’s heart, and the separate thefts of the locket and sapphire.
- Bookwyrm knew shortly after midnight that Janussi had been poisoned but withheld that information when she later placed the investigation in the party’s hands.
- Kalan Strongbranch hears the confession from outside the room, and Thorin proposes giving him the evidence and allowing him to “clean house.”
- Gyrgum suggests that the evidence could instead, or additionally, be used to blackmail one or more suspects.
- Daz needs information that is exceptionally difficult to obtain. Because A'lai is a drow, he may possess useful knowledge about Daz’s personal concerns and the Underdark.
- The party considers the institutional murder secondary to securing information that could materially help Daz.
- Thorin argues that the number of suspects uncovered justifies a reward of at least one rare book per party member — or perhaps one book per suspect.
- The group ends the session with the murder method exposed, Alkrist’s confession secured, and the political disposition of the evidence still undecided.

## Locations

### Janussi's Chambers

The private quarters of the late Keeper of Tomes. Their third floor served as a discreet “love nest,” consistent with Janussi’s history of affairs. Gyrgum found a note there demonstrating Janussi’s contempt for Kalan Strongbranch’s investigative methodology and his role in blocking Kalan’s career. The chambers were also the site of Janussi’s poisoning and death, followed by the removal of his heart and the theft of his locket and sapphire.

### Sylvira's Quarters

An oppressively hot room kept blazing by a magical fire while Sylvira shivered beneath elegant lilac robes. A mage hand opened the door for Daz, and Sylvira’s familiar Jezebel arranged the seating. Sylvira returned here after visiting her son sometime after midnight and reported seeing no one afterward.

### Oak Tree Apothecary

Leuwin’s shop and garden. A'lai drew Leuwin outside by asking for fresh fennel for indigestion, allowing Alkrist to enter unseen, steal a vial of Midnight Tears from a high shelf, and spill ground peppermint over the counter and floor. Nibbles the squirrel saw the “lizard skin” intruder enter and make the mess.

### Alkrist's Chambers

The private quarters of the bronze dragonborn researcher and the site of his Zone of Truth interrogation. Alkrist’s copy of *1001 Tashalian Nights* sat openly on a shelf, bookmarked at the passage describing murder by Midnight Tears. His locked safe remained an unresolved subject from the party’s earlier covert search.

### The Baths

A private meeting place where Alkrist and A'lai met at approximately 11:45 a.m. on Deadwinter to discuss their “mutual problem of Janussi.” There they moved from shared grievances to planning the murder method Alkrist had found in *1001 Tashalian Nights*.

### The Deadwinter Tree

A festive tree within Candlekeep where gifts were placed for the Deadwinter feast. Alkrist opened Daral’s present to Janussi here, coated the enclosed book with Midnight Tears, and re-wrapped it. He tied the poison vial’s torn label to the back of the tree as a decoration and attempted to burn it with a candle before Irony and Orrin Glass arrived to prepare the supper table.

## NPCs

### Daral

A poet and scholar who reclaimed the book he had given Janussi after deciding the gift was insufficiently appreciated. While handling it, he licked his finger and may have exposed himself to Midnight Tears. Convinced he will die at midnight, he desperately flatters Thorin, Dawnbringer, and Gyrgum in hopes of receiving Lesser Restoration. Sylvira considers him a bitter drunk, forced to sell some of his poetry to help fund a new Great Wheel library, but ultimately doubts he possesses the organization required for the larger frame-up.

### Fembris Lancer

An enthusiastic adjutant and admirer of Gyrgum’s lecture. He brings personal notes and murder theories to Gyrgum, proposes co-authoring a monograph, and successively negotiates down to a “duograph” or mere acknowledgment. He promises a draft by the next day and later leaves to locate Kalan Strongbranch for Alkrist’s interrogation.

### Glabbagool

An intelligent grey ooze, Zalthir’s shadow apprentice, and Gyrgum’s ad hoc secretary. He rejects Fembris’s notes as neither nutritious nor food, reminisces about cheese, and accepts them for storage. He later remembers that he could have eaten through Alkrist’s safe and reminds Zalthir that walls do not stop them.

### Elian

A Candlekeep clerk who admires Thorin’s kitchen investigation. He delivers Kalan Strongbranch’s paper on magical criminal investigations and argues that “Magic does not make evidence meaningful. It makes evidence adversarial.” He suspects opponents of rigorous investigation forced Kalan out and eagerly seeks Thorin’s endorsement for a future monograph.

### Kalan Strongbranch

The former Gatewarden at Candlekeep. He championed a rigorous criminal-investigation methodology based on corroborating multiple independent forms of evidence rather than treating any single spell as dispositive. Janussi dismissed the project as solipsistic and wanted Kalan fired; Bookwyrm had pushed hardest against his methods when Janussi raised them, and later removed him from the investigation. Kalan assists the party by casting Zone of Truth during Alkrist’s interrogation.

### Sylvira

A tiefling researcher with copper-red skin, magnetic golden eyes, and blue-black hair, currently bedridden with an abyssal plague contracted from her son. She had an intermittent romantic relationship with Janussi and becomes distraught upon learning that someone has used her likeness. She identifies prejudice against tieflings and her infernal-plane research as reasons she would make a convenient scapegoat. She points suspicion toward Daral Yashenti, Kazryn Nyantani, Teles Ahvoste, Kalan Strongbranch, and ultimately Bookwyrm’s opposition to Kalan’s methodology.

### Kazryn Nyantani

Janussi’s former lover before his intermittent relationship with Sylvira. According to Sylvira, Kazryn never forgave Janussi for discarding her in Sylvira’s favor and remained deeply resentful of her replacement.

### Jezebel

Sylvira’s familiar. At Sylvira’s request, Jezebel organizes seating for Daz’s interview, while a mage hand opens the chamber door.

### Teles Ahvoste

A member of the Avowed whom Sylvira believes has remained hostile toward Janussi for nearly twenty years. Teles was passed over for Keeper of Tomes despite being older and generally regarded as a more skilled practitioner of magic, giving him a long-standing motive rooted in professional jealousy.

### Alkrist

A thirty-three-year-old bronze dragonborn researcher presented as a fashionable, confident young academic or “Dragonborn Tech Bro.” Janussi ordered him to abandon his germ-warfare research and denied that he possessed leadership potential. Under Kalan’s Zone of Truth, Alkrist confesses to conspiring with A'lai, stealing Midnight Tears, poisoning Janussi’s gift, destroying evidence, and informing Bookwyrm after midnight. He denies responsibility for removing Janussi’s heart and appears genuinely shocked to learn it happened.

### A'lai

A drow scholar initially assumed by Alkrist to be Janussi’s likely successor. Instead, A'lai told Alkrist that he expected exile. He distracted Leuwin at the Oak Tree Apothecary while Alkrist stole the poison and met Alkrist privately at the baths to discuss their mutual problem. Alkrist calls him “the real brains of the operation.” His Underdark connections may also give him information valuable to Daz.

### Bookwyrm

Alkrist’s beloved aunt and a high-ranking Candlekeep scholar who believed she deserved to succeed Janussi. Janussi told her directly that she would not become Keeper of Tomes, leaving her distraught and betrayed. Shortly after midnight, Alkrist visited with wine and announced that Janussi had been poisoned. Bookwyrm reacted with horror, argued with him, and stormed away. She later withheld this information from the investigators and had previously pushed hardest against Kalan’s investigative reforms, making her a central suspect in the post-mortem mutilation and cover-up.

### Janussi

The late Keeper of Tomes and murder victim. On Deadwinter annual-review day, he blocked Kalan’s methodology, ordered Alkrist’s germ-warfare research terminated, denied Bookwyrm the succession, and left A'lai facing exile. He was poisoned by reading Daral’s tampered gift and died at midnight. Someone subsequently removed his heart and stole his locket and sapphire. The party judges that his Intelligence far exceeded his Wisdom.

### Leuwin

The proprietor of the Oak Tree Apothecary. A'lai distracted him by requesting fresh fennel for indigestion and drawing him into the garden, allowing Alkrist to enter the shop and steal the Midnight Tears.

### Irony

A staff member who arrived with Orrin Glass while Alkrist was burning evidence near the Deadwinter Tree. Irony carried a basin of water as they prepared the supper table, giving Alkrist an opportunity to wash possible traces of Midnight Tears from his hands.

### Nibbles

A squirrel who witnessed a “huge lizard skin” person — believed to be Alkrist — enter the Oak Tree Apothecary while Leuwin was distracted and make a mess inside. This testimony helped connect Alkrist to the spilled peppermint and stolen poison.

## Items

### The Poisoned Book

Daral’s gift to Janussi. Alkrist opened its wrapping at the Deadwinter Tree, coated the book with Midnight Tears, and carefully re-wrapped it. Janussi later read the book, absorbed the poison, and died at midnight. Daral reclaimed the gift and licked his finger while handling it, potentially poisoning himself as well.

### The Sapphire

A magical gem used in connection with summoning the key associated with the locket. It was stolen during the events surrounding Janussi’s murder. Because a living bearer’s possession protects the locket from being summoned away, the party suspects the sapphire and locket may have been taken by different people.

### The Locket

An item stolen from Janussi’s person after his death. It is linked to the sapphire and its summoned key. The party learns that the sapphire cannot simply take the locket out of a living bearer’s possession, complicating the theory that one thief deliberately stole both items for the same purpose.

### Bag of Holding

A magical container available to the party. Glabbagool could place Fembris’s notes inside after determining that they were neither nutritious nor cheese.

### Investigation Methodology Paper

A scholarly work championed by Kalan Strongbranch and delivered to Thorin by Elian. It argues that magical evidence is adversarial and that no single spell establishes truth. Investigators must combine independent observations, physical evidence, testimony, and magical results. Institutional hostility toward the paper may have contributed to Kalan’s firing.

### Wrapping Paper

The discarded wrapping from Daral’s gift, recovered from a dining-hall trash bucket. After Gyrgum restored it with Mending, an additional deliberate cut became visible, proving that someone had opened the package before Janussi received it and then re-wrapped the poisoned book.

### Janussi's Note

A document discovered in the love nest on the third floor of Janussi’s chambers. Written in an authoritative and academically insulting style, it dismisses Kalan Strongbranch’s proposed investigative controls as solipsistic and unnecessary, documenting Janussi’s opposition to Kalan’s professional agenda.

### 1001 Tashalian Nights

A famous book kept openly in Alkrist’s chambers and bookmarked at a passage explaining murder by Midnight Tears. During the interrogation, Alkrist’s involuntary gestures draw attention to it, and Zalthir dramatically opens it to the exact passage that inspired the conspiracy.

### Midnight Tears

A rare poison stolen from the Oak Tree Apothecary. Alkrist applied it to Janussi’s gift book. Its delayed effect killed Janussi at midnight, allowing the poisoned object to be delivered and handled hours before the death. Alkrist removed the vial’s label, tied it to the Deadwinter Tree as decoration, and attempted to burn it.

### Janussi's Heart

The organ removed from Janussi’s body after his death and apparently hidden in a chalice whose properties would conceal it from anyone not specifically searching there. Alkrist’s genuine shock shows that he did not participate in this part of the crime. The party suspects Bookwyrm or another conspirator with detailed knowledge of the chalice.

## Spells

### Lesser Restoration

A healing spell capable of neutralizing Daral’s suspected poisoning. Gyrgum confirms that he can cast it but delays doing so while Daral remains cooperative, telling him, “In due time, my dear. In due time.”

### Polymorph

A transformation spell considered as the likely means by which someone assumed Sylvira’s appearance. It can affect a creature within sixty feet, but its standard one-hour duration does not comfortably explain sightings of Sylvira’s double spanning multiple hours. The party considers repeated castings, a special variant, or some more powerful transformation effect.

### Mage Hand

A spectral floating hand used to open the door to Sylvira’s quarters when Daz arrives. It allows the severely ill Sylvira to receive visitors without leaving her bed.

### Mending

Cast by Gyrgum to restore the torn wrapping paper from Janussi’s gift. Once the pieces reassemble, the spell exposes evidence of an earlier deliberate cut, proving that the package had been opened and re-wrapped before it reached Janussi.

### Zone of Truth

Cast by Kalan Strongbranch during Alkrist’s interrogation. Alkrist fails to resist it and cannot knowingly speak deliberate lies. He struggles against the compulsion, attempts evasions, and eventually confesses the full mechanics of the poisoning while claiming that the truthful testimony amounts to entrapment or mind control.

### Mordenkainen's Magnificent Mansion

A powerful magical sanctuary Sylvira constructed for her son’s protection. She left the feast to visit him and attempted to coax him back inside it. Her visit provides an alibi for part of the period during which someone else was seen wearing her likeness.
