# Chapter 52  The Ember Grapple

Date: Apr 14th, 2026

## Summary

Deep beneath the surface world, in the lightless corridors of the Underdark, near the Fungal Altar where Ilvara Mizzrym had held her ritual, the Ember Vanguard paused to take stock of how far they had come. The trials they had endured had forged them into something harder and more capable than before, and each member of the group felt the weight of that growth settle into their bones. Upon learning that they had reached eighth level, the party devoted the session to choosing feats, adjusting ability scores, reviewing spells, and exploring how their new capabilities might work together.

Thorin wrestled with a nagging vulnerability — his inability to strike at enemies from a distance had cost the party in the past, and he knew the creatures lurking in the dark ahead would not always close the gap for him. He initially considered Shield Master and then Speedy, reasoning that an extra ten feet of movement and the ability to dash across difficult terrain would help him close with enemies. He also contemplated raising his Strength to twenty or repairing his unusually low Dexterity.

After feeding his character sheet to Gemini, however, Thorin was encouraged to consider Sharpshooter and javelins. The AI then reversed its recommendation, prompting further debate, but the idea endured. With the GM permitting Sharpshooter's ability increase to apply to Strength rather than Dexterity, Thorin committed to mastering the art of the thrown javelin. His new training would let him attack at long range without disadvantage, cut through partial cover, and potentially trade accuracy for greater damage. He also selected the javelin's Slow weapon mastery, allowing a successful strike to reduce a fleeing enemy's speed by ten feet. By his own calculation, his future javelin attacks would have a range of 120 feet, a +7 attack bonus, and deal 1d6+4 damage.

Daz, ever the scholar of arcane possibility, turned his attention to the spellbook the party had recovered in an earlier foray, its pages brimming with knowledge he had not yet had time to absorb. He weighed straightforward ability improvements against War Caster, Telekinetic, and Fey Touched. Telekinetic intrigued him because allies could intentionally fail its saving throw, potentially allowing him to push them out of grapples or other dangerous positions. Ultimately, however, he chose Fey Touched.

Daz had long carried a gift for bending the fabric of space, slipping through shadows in an instant, but now he found a way to do so without drawing on his deepest reserves of power, preserving his strength for the battles that truly demanded it. Because he had already learned the spells granted by Fey Touched, he was able to remove those selections from his learned-spell choices and replace them with additional magic, expanding his spellbook and the number of options available to him.

It was Zalthir, however, who stumbled upon the idea that sent a ripple of excitement through the group. He had initially planned to take a conventional two-point Dexterity increase, but then discovered Street Justice and went “down a whole rabbit hole.” A length of rope — humble, mundane, utterly unglamorous — could become one of the most fearsome tools in his arsenal. He had always excelled at seizing enemies and holding them fast, but now he could bind them completely, leaving them restrained while granting his allies an advantage against them.

The party carefully worked through the action economy. Zalthir could use his martial abilities to grapple with a bonus-action attack and then spend his action binding the victim with rope, chains, or manacles. This consumed most of his turn, but he could still make bonus-action attacks before securing the target. The moment the Ember Vanguard understood what this meant alongside Daz's ability to conjure a swirling storm of blades over a fixed point, the strategy practically wrote itself: Zalthir would pin the enemy in place, Daz would cast Cloud of Daggers around the captive, and the target would be unable to leave the spell's area.

The group clarified that grappling does not pull the target into the grappler's five-foot space, so Daz could place Cloud of Daggers over the restrained enemy without automatically catching Zalthir in the effect. They also firmly rejected attempts to turn forced movement into unlimited damage; the spell could harm a creature only once per turn. Someone suggested that the Ember Vanguard might soon be known as the Ember Grapple, and no one entirely disagreed.

## Scenes

### The Level Eight Milestone

#### The party reaches a significant milestone in their journey through the Underdark, gaining new powers and specialized abilities.

- The party is informed they have reached level eight, triggering a period of reflection, character-sheet work, and mechanical growth.

- The announcement surprises everyone. Daz exclaims, “You buried the lead, GM,” while Thorin immediately predicts, “that's gonna take the rest of the hour.”

- Thorin considers whether to address his complete lack of ranged capabilities or double down on his current specializations. He asks Zalthir for advice because Zalthir has a reputation for building deliberately lopsided characters.

- Zalthir explains his philosophy: “I find it more fun to double down with some sort of obscure specialization and then try to break the universe with it.”

- The GM explains that D&D generally rewards deep specialization over general-purpose competency because the damage required to remain effective increases sharply with level. He estimates that approximately 10 damage is substantial at first level, 20 at eighth, 40 at fifteenth, and 70 damage per round may be expected at twentieth.

- The group also acknowledges the danger of over-specialization. A party with only one ranged attacker could be devastated by an enemy—such as a dragon—that remains sixty feet away and attacks without ever entering melee.

- The GM notes that the party is in the Underdark, where flying enemies may be less common, but warns that because this is Dungeons & Dragons and almost no one possesses a ranged weapon, such threats will inevitably appear.

- Heroic Inspiration is briefly reviewed. The GM explains that it effectively provides an additional d20 on a roll and can be granted when the group agrees that a character deserves it.

- The recovered spellbook is raised as an important consideration for Daz, since his feat choice could alter how many of its spells he can incorporate into his active repertoire.

### Choosing Feats and Abilities

#### The party members deliberate over their feat selections, weighing specialized combat options against general ability score improvements.

- Daz considers War Caster to improve concentration checks. The GM describes it as particularly useful for a wizard who expects to rely on concentration spells.

- Lucky is also discussed. Although it provides only three uses per long rest, the GM observes that many encounters last approximately three rounds, making those rerolls disproportionately useful.

- Daz explores Fey Touched, which provides additional magic and free castings, including a free use of Misty Step.

- Daz also investigates Telekinetic. He notes that a creature can intentionally fail its saving throw, allowing him to use the ability on allies to shift them five feet, potentially moving them out of a grapple or another dangerous position.

- Daz notes that Dawnbringer deals radiant damage rather than slashing damage, which influences his tactical thinking.

- Daz selects Fey Touched. Because he had already chosen the spells it granted, he removes them from his learned-spell selections and fills the newly opened spaces with other magic.

- Daz reports that this “opened up the number of spells” he had available, allowing him to take better advantage of the recovered spellbook.

- Thorin initially selects Shield Master so that he can shield bash, which would also raise his Strength to nineteen.

- Thorin then becomes interested in Speedy. It would increase his movement by ten feet and allow him to dash across difficult terrain without paying the usual additional movement cost.

- Thorin explains that this mobility would compensate for his lack of ranged attacks: if he cannot strike from afar, he at least needs to close the distance faster. The GM agrees that this would be highly useful for a fighter and might allow Thorin to almost keep pace with Zalthir.

- Thorin debates whether Speedy's ability increase should go into Dexterity or Constitution. His Dexterity is only eight, his Constitution is sixteen, and his Armor Class is already twenty.

- The group notes that odd-numbered ability increases provide no immediate modifier benefit, making Thorin's choice a longer-term investment.

- Thorin considers increasing Strength directly to twenty, the clearest mechanical improvement, but the discussion turns to the “Zalthir theory of micro-optimization”: rather than invest permanently in Strength, a character might use consumables such as potions of giant strength.

- Thorin admits that he habitually hoards consumables: “I'm always like, oh, I don't know, I'm gonna save this potion for later, I might need it.” The GM advises him to “just burn the potions.”

- Thorin exports his character to PDF and gives it to Gemini, asking which feat he should choose. The GM criticizes the broad prompt and suggests telling the AI which campaign Thorin is playing, what outcome he wants to optimize, and what he dislikes about the current build.

- Gemini suggests Sharpshooter because Thorin can use it with javelins, whose attacks use Strength rather than Dexterity. The feat would remove long-range disadvantage and allow him to ignore half and three-quarters cover.

- Sharpshooter would also permit Thorin to accept a −5 penalty to an attack roll in exchange for +10 damage on a hit, functioning as a ranged counterpart to Great Weapon Master. *(House rule: this table keeps the 2014 Sharpshooter's −5/+10 trade alongside 2024 weapon mastery.)*

- Gemini subsequently reverses itself and recommends increasing Strength instead. It then proposes that, if the GM allows a “common quality of life's house rule,” Thorin could take Sharpshooter while applying its ability increase to Strength rather than Dexterity.

- The GM approves the change, reasoning that a Dexterity increase would not help solve the particular problem Thorin was trying to address. He later acknowledges that the supposed common house rule might have been an AI hallucination but remains comfortable with the ruling.

- Thorin confirms that fighter weapon masteries can be changed after a long rest and that fighting-style feats can be swapped when gaining a fighter level.

- Thorin retains Blind Fighting, selects the javelin's Slow mastery, and settles on Sharpshooter with the house-ruled Strength increase.

- Zalthir initially plans to take a conventional +2 Dexterity improvement but discovers Street Justice while reviewing available feats.

- Street Justice grants allies an advantage against creatures Zalthir has grappled and supports binding such creatures with rope, chains, or manacles.

- The discovery throws Zalthir's original plan “into chaos,” but the GM calls Street Justice “the obvious Zalthir move.”

- Zalthir ultimately selects Street Justice.

### Tactical Synergies and Final Decisions

#### The party finalizes their level-up choices and discusses new combat synergies, particularly around grappling and area-of-effect spells.

- Daz asks whether his ability to shape spells would protect an ally grappling a target caught in Cloud of Daggers. The GM initially considers the question in terms of Sculpt Spells but explains that the grappler and target do not actually occupy the same space.

- The group clarifies that a grapple means the attacker is holding the enemy in place; it does not pull the enemy into the grappler's physical five-foot square.

- Consequently, Daz can center Cloud of Daggers on a creature Zalthir has grappled without automatically striking Zalthir.

- The tactic is particularly effective because the grappled creature cannot voluntarily leave the damaging area.

- The GM notes that Zalthir could theoretically grapple two enemies simultaneously if both of his hands were free, though Zalthir has never done so.

- Daz and Zalthir discuss moving a victim out of Cloud of Daggers and back into it. Daz reads the spell as potentially dealing damage upon entry and again at the end of the creature's turn, but confirms that the target can take this damage only once per turn.

- Zalthir enthusiastically volunteers to drag targets in and out “depending on how many times GM will let them take the damage.” The GM immediately limits the effect to once per turn.

- The discussion draws a comparison to a supposed Spirit Guardians “infinite damage hack.” The GM rejects any interpretation that would permit infinite damage regardless of the literal wording being argued.

- The group works through the action economy of Street Justice. A target must first be grappled, incapacitated, or restrained, after which Zalthir can spend an action binding it.

- Zalthir can use a bonus-action martial attack to grapple and then use his action to apply rope or restraints, completing the combination in a single turn but consuming nearly all of that turn.

- Zalthir notes that he can still make two attacks through his bonus-action expenditure before using his main action to tie up the target.

- The GM predicts that Zalthir will use the tactic to bind major enemies and then give Daz an opportunity to cast Fireball or Cloud of Daggers on them.

- The synergy inspires the suggestion that the Ember Vanguard might instead become known as the “Ember Grapple.” Zalthir agrees that the name seems appropriate.

- Thorin plans to acquire javelins at the next opportunity. With Sharpshooter, he expects his javelin attacks to have a +7 attack bonus, deal 1d6+4 damage, and reach out to 120 feet.

- Thorin assigns the Slow weapon mastery to his javelins. A successful hit reduces the target's speed by ten feet, helping the party pursue enemies attempting to kite them.

- Thorin notices that the rules classify the javelin as a melee weapon despite its thrown property, an ambiguity the group does not fully resolve before the conversation moves on.

- The GM confirms the party will not receive a long rest at this time, as no combats are imminent, while jokingly preserving the possibility of trouble “unless you guys provoke it.”

- By the end of the session, Daz has selected Fey Touched, Zalthir has selected Street Justice, and Thorin has selected the house-ruled version of Sharpshooter. The GM declares them ready to resume adventuring the following week.

## NPCs

### Dawnbringer

Thorin's sentient sunblade, who deals radiant damage rather than standard slashing damage, a distinction that influenced tactical discussions during the level-up session.

## Locations

### The Fungal Altar

Ilvara Mizzrym's ritual site, deep in the Underdark, where the party is currently traveling. The GM notes that while flying enemies are less common here, the party's limited ranged martial weapon coverage will still be tested. The confined terrain makes Thorin's contemplated mobility improvements useful, but the GM warns that the conventions of Dungeons & Dragons virtually guarantee that enemies capable of attacking from beyond the party's reach will eventually appear.

## Items

### Spellbook

A spellbook found by the party in a previous session. Its contents opened up additional spell options for Daz, and the Fey Touched feat freed prepared-spell capacity so he could prepare more spells from it. Because Daz had already selected the spells Fey Touched granted, he was able to remove those redundant choices and learn additional spells in their place.

### Potions of Giant Strength

Magical elixirs discussed as an alternative to permanently increasing a character's Strength score, cited as an example of the “Zalthir theory of micro-optimization” — using consumable items rather than investing feat slots in stat improvements. Thorin admitted that his instinct is to hoard potions indefinitely in case they are needed later, while the GM encouraged him to use such resources freely.

### Javelins

Simple thrown weapons that Thorin plans to acquire and use for ranged combat. Javelin attacks use Strength, making them compatible with his existing physical specialization despite his low Dexterity.

With his new Sharpshooter training, Thorin can hurl javelins at long distances without disadvantage, ignore half and three-quarters cover, and potentially accept a −5 attack penalty in exchange for +10 damage. Separately, the javelin's Slow weapon mastery reduces a struck enemy's speed by ten feet, allowing Thorin to hinder creatures trying to keep their distance.

Thorin calculates that his javelins will have a +7 attack bonus, deal 1d6+4 damage, and possess a maximum range of 120 feet. He observes that the rules still classify the javelin as a melee weapon despite its thrown property.

### Rope

A standard piece of adventuring gear that Zalthir intends to use in combat to bind and restrain opponents once they have been grappled, enabled by his newly selected Street Justice feat. Applying the rope requires an action and a target who is already grappled, incapacitated, or restrained. Zalthir can therefore grapple through a bonus-action martial attack and bind the enemy with his action during the same turn.

The group also notes that chains and manacles can serve the same general purpose. Once secured, the enemy becomes an easier target for Zalthir's allies and can be held inside persistent magical hazards such as Cloud of Daggers.

## Spells

### Misty Step

A teleportation spell that Daz already had access to; Fey Touched grants him one casting per long rest without expending a spell slot, after which he can still cast it normally using slots. This provides a reliable way to escape grapples or reposition during battle without provoking opportunity attacks. Because Fey Touched supplied the spell separately, Daz could replace his previously learned version with another spell from his available options.

### Cloud of Daggers

A spell that conjures a localized cube of spinning daggers. Daz plans to use this in tandem with Zalthir's grappling to trap enemies within the hazardous area, dealing continuous damage while the target remains unable to move away.

The group confirms that Zalthir and his grappled target occupy different five-foot spaces, so the spell can be positioned on the target without automatically harming Zalthir. Daz and Zalthir consider dragging a victim out of the area and back into it, but the GM rules that a creature can suffer the spell's damage only once per turn.

### Spirit Guardians

A protective spell referenced during a discussion about damage mechanics and the importance of following established rules regarding how often a creature can be harmed by an effect in a single turn. The GM recalls his son describing Spirit Guardians as “an infinite damage hack” and flatly rejects any reading that would actually permit unlimited damage.
