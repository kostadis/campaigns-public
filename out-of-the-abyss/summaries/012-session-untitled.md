# Chapter 012

Date: 2025-03-17

## Summary
After many grueling days of travel across the Darklake, the party finally tied up their boat at the grim duergar city of Gracklstugh, which loomed before them. A wave of acrid heat slammed against their faces as the light of countless forges reflected off the dark water, and the rhythmic hammering of the city's smiths blended with the shouting of merchants and slaves into a constant, disorienting din. Drow, svirfneblin, derro, orcs and other races moved through the crowds, so the party was not the only group of outsiders. Hemeth, the duergar arms dealer they had rescued, warned them that non-duergar were permitted only in the Darklake district, and that the city's laws were swift and merciless. Guards who could turn invisible at will enforced a code where theft was punishable by death on the spot, no trial required, and any duergar in sight counted as instant reinforcements. The party also gathered that if word got out they were escaped slaves, slavers might try to repossess them or sell them back to the drow. Thorin objected that they had merely been "temporarily detained." Their goals were clear: resupply with better weapons and armor, and find information that might lead them out of the Underdark for good. The longer route ran from Gracklstugh to the lost tomb marked on the dwarf's map, and from there to Blingdenstone, a possible exit point.

The party made their way to the Blade Bazaar, a sprawling marketplace named for the most abundant goods the duergar offered, though its stalls sold nearly everything a traveler could need. Gyrgum approached a merchant who hurled insults at every customer until he realized they intended to buy. At that point the duergar adopted an eerily posh accent, much like the Queen's English, and offered a ten percent discount on a new mace in exchange for Gyrgum's "rubbish" old short sword. Meanwhile, Zalthir recalled a mysterious list of names he had found while cleaning. The individuals on it had some connection to the people held as slaves by the drow, though whether they were slavers or worried family members was unclear, and one name corresponded to someone in Gracklstugh. The party debated how best to approach this contact, knowing that revealing themselves as escaped captives of the drow could put a price on their heads. Sensing an opportunity, they turned to their captive, Brannum Redmarch, a duergar who had previously plotted against them. They offered him his freedom in exchange for using his underworld connections to procure uncommon magical goods from the parts of the city closed to outsiders. Brannum eagerly agreed. Hemeth, deeply offended at being passed over, insisted on offering his own superior wares at a discount, boasting that his handaxes were so well-made they would be easily reused by whoever killed their owner.

The question of what to do with Buppido, their erratic and murderous derro companion, hung over the group like a storm cloud. Buppido had been growing increasingly anxious to leave, claiming his divine mission demanded he visit a personal shrine in the caverns near the city. The party weighed their options carefully. Letting him go risked him leading their drow pursuers straight to the tomb, since he had been present when they found its location. Killing him in cold blood sat uneasily with Gyrgum, the party's neutral good cleric. Buppido, sensing that his life was not immediately in danger, suddenly became a picture of philosophical accommodation, insisting that life was too short for grudges and that the divine plan simply required them to part ways. Zalthir then remembered the party's mushrooms, and Gyrgum suggested they have him "try one of these." Buppido ate a Tongue of Madness and unleashed a five-day backlog of petty insults against every single member of the party in one long, unbroken stream of consciousness, reciting every personal failing he had silently tallied during their journey together. When Jimjar, the deep gnome with an incurable gambling habit, was told to stop making bets, he immediately lamented that he should have wagered on whether Buppido would say exactly that. Gyrgum and Zalthir floated marooning him somewhere in the Underdark when they departed. By the end of his tirade, Zalthir was convinced they could simply cut him loose once they were done with the city.

While Gyrgum haggled for his scrolls, the merchant broke off mid-sale to threaten to kill him, then carried on as if nothing had happened. Gyrgum began to notice something deeply unsettling about the city's inhabitants. One duergar merchant kept consulting a twin brother who did not exist. Others quoted wildly different prices for the same goods from one moment to the next, insisting it was the buyers who kept changing the terms. Most disturbingly, one merchant turned completely invisible in the middle of a transaction and simply continued speaking as though nothing had happened. The creeping madness that had haunted the party throughout their time in the Underdark seemed to have seeped into the very stones of Gracklstugh itself. It had already shown itself in the frenzy of the kuo-toa and their summoned horror and in the erratic behavior of countless creatures they had encountered.

Before Gyrgum could make sense of what he was seeing, the rhythmic hammering of the city's forges was drowned out by a thunderous roar and the crack of crumbling stone. A two-headed stone giant, gray-skinned and howling with wild-eyed madness, burst through one of the district's gates and began lashing out in every direction, sending rock and debris scattering across the plaza. A duergar soldier's broken body flew through the air and crashed near the party's feet, and the giant's frenzied gaze locked onto them. Thorin recognized immediately that this was deeply wrong. Stone giants were among the most taciturn and reserved creatures in existence, and seeing one consumed by such violent chaos spoke to something terrible at work beneath the surface of the world. Zalthir's instinct was to stop the creature before it rampaged through the marketplace and smashed the merchants and their goods, and the GM pointed out that helping the law-respecting duergar might earn the strangers some goodwill. After due consideration, Gyrgum raised his holy symbol and brandished it at the giant.

The battle that followed was chaotic and desperate. Daz unleashed a volley of magical force that struck the giant unerringly. Zalthir sprinted into melee range and conjured a sphere of magical darkness that swallowed the giant whole, blinding the massive creature. Thorin exploited the giant's blindness to drive his blade home, and Gyrgum channeled searing radiant energy through his holy symbol that made the giant roar in frustrated agony. The creature lashed out blindly at Thorin and connected, but Zalthir deflected the blow with practiced precision. Just as the party began to find their footing, a squad of duergar city guards came rushing into the plaza, weapons drawn, ready to join the fray. The battle raged on with the giant's fate still very much undecided.

## Scenes
### Arrival at Gracklstugh
#### The party arrives at the duergar city of Gracklstugh after traveling across the Darklake, learning about its harsh laws and oppressive atmosphere.
- The party arrives at the Darklake district of Gracklstugh, greeted by acrid smog and the constant sound of hammering forges.
  - The Darklake reflects the light of countless fires burning within hollowed-out columns and stalagmites.
  - The streets are crowded with buyers, merchants and slaves. Drow, svirfneblin, derro, orcs and other races are in the throng.
- Hemeth explains the city's strict social structure, noting that non-duergar are restricted to the Darklake district.
  - The district is fully enclosed by walls. The GM: "So as long as you stay in the district, all will be safe."
- The party learns that duergar guards can turn invisible and that theft is punishable by immediate death without trial.
  - Duergar are described as grim, austere and methodical. Corruption among them is very rare, and any duergar in sight counts as instant reinforcements.
  - The GM warns that "slavery is a thing here, too." If word got out they were escaped slaves, slavers might repossess them or sell them back to the drow. Thorin: "We weren't slaves, you were just temporarily detained."
  - The GM notes they don't want to attract attention: "Don't try to steal anything."
- The group discusses their primary goals: acquiring better equipment and finding a way out of the Underdark.
  - The GM recaps the route: Gracklstugh, then the lost tomb from the dwarf's map, then Blingdenstone, a possible exit point. Gracklstugh was a detour to re-equip, because the party is low on weapons and gear.
  - The party is level 3 and "close, but not quite" to leveling up.
- The party decides to head to the Blade Bazaar to trade and purchase supplies.

### The Mad Giant's Rampage

  - Most duergar get out of the way, and the giant barrels straight at Gyrgum. The rest of the party is within five to ten feet.

  - The GM: stone giants "make Zalthir look like an overly talkative human being." Thorin: "I'm very confused by the whole thing."
- The GM lays out the trade-off. The party could flee, but helping the duergar might earn "brownie points," while running might get them blamed for the rampage. Zalthir asks whether this is a fight they can win or another kraken. The GM confirms they can fight it and that duergar troops will arrive.

  - Initiative: Daz 19, Zalthir 16. The giant has "about 100 and something" hit points.

- Zalthir rushes into melee range and conjures a sphere of magical darkness to blind the creature.
  - He follows with Flurry of Blows. The first extra attack misses, and a focus-point attack hits for 7.

  - The giant attacks with disadvantage and rolls a 25 (+9) for 10 bludgeoning damage. The deflection, a reaction that reduces damage by 1d10 plus a modifier, means no damage is taken.
- Duergar city guards arrive on the scene, rushing to join the fray against the giant. Two guards act at initiative 10.
- The party continues their coordinated assault with magical missiles, martial strikes, and crossbow fire as the guards close in, with the battle left unresolved as the session ends.
  - Daz moves to see around the darkness and hits with Magic Missile for 9.
  - Zalthir hits for 11 and attacks again with a focus point.
  - Thorin uses a feinting attack (+1d8), hitting on a 17 for 9 and then another 7.
  - Gyrgum moves around the darkness and hits with a crossbow bolt (17) for 9.

## Locations
### Gracklstugh
A grim and industrial duergar city in the Underdark, known as the City of Blades, characterized by constant heat, acrid smog, and the rhythmic hammering of forges that never stop. The city is divided into districts, with non-duergar restricted to the Darklake district.
- Crime is scarce because guards can turn invisible. Laws are "harsh and fast."
- Slavery is practiced. Escaped slaves risk being repossessed or sold back to the drow.
- One name on Zalthir's list belongs to someone in the city.

### Darklake District
The only area of Gracklstugh where non-duergar are permitted to stay and trade, located near the docks and containing the Blade Bazaar. The site of a rampaging two-headed stone giant attack during the party's visit.
- Fully enclosed by walls separating it from the rest of the city.
- Healing potions and heavy armor are available here.

### Blade Bazaar
A bustling marketplace in the Darklake district of Gracklstugh where merchants sell weapons, armor, and various goods amidst a distracting din of shouting and hammering. Named after the most abundant goods the duergar offer, though it sells almost everything available in the city.
- Sells non-magical weapons, armor and shields. Powerful magic items are not sold here.
- Its merchants display strange behavior: phantom twins, shifting prices, abrupt threats and unexplained invisibility.
