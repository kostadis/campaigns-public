# Chapter 76

Date: 2026-11-09

## Summary
Before departing Gauntlgrym, the party reviewed its rewards, new equipment, and faction agreements. Daz received the Stone Staff from the royal vaults, and the group worked through how to record unfamiliar magical items in their equipment lists. The Lords’ Alliance supplied the Alliance Brief under the Counter-Doctrine offer: an initial historical overview of Menzoberranzan and a larger tome that could answer one question before each questline. Answers were marked certain or in part, reflecting what the Alliance knew when it was written. Lord Eravien Haund, remembered for the special meal and cheese that had delighted Glabbagool, offered further rewards for further service.

Daz also considered how to handle his recent experience involving Shar. He would share his matter-of-fact journal account with the party, but would not ask outsiders about the goddess. With so many people pursuing political schemes, he preferred to listen for anyone mentioning Shar or related matters, relying on his keen investigative instincts.

King Bruenor personally presented Glabbagool with a glowing Ioun Stone, remarking on the novelty of a dwarven king willingly giving a magical item to an ooze. Glabbagool promptly ate it. The gift acknowledged his place among the four associated with Alaundo’s prophecy of the Anvil, prompting questions about how the party’s five members fit the count. The explanation was that Glabbagool counted together with Zalthir. The stone granted Glabbagool additional protection; provisionally, he could also protect Zalthir instead of attacking, spending one of his own focus points to change roles.

Sigrun, a veteran ranger turned archivist, joined the expedition at Lord Eravien Haund’s arrangement. She brought a survey gadget, gladly accepted responsibility for mapping, and bonded with Thorin over their shared interest in cartography. Though she claimed no faction allegiance, she believed a strong state protected the innocent. Two guides employed by Davra also joined them: Rainaldus, an experienced Zhentarim agent who read at every halt, and Octavia, his charming gnome trainee. Rainaldus seemed both protective of and exasperated by her; Octavia’s friendliness did not entirely conceal the impression that approaching her carelessly could earn a knife in the back.

Clan Feldrun’s farewell surrounded Thorin with embraces, advice, and plentiful snacks. Dawnbringer received straps, polish, leather conditioner for the Fancy Scabbard 2000, and even an impractical sharpening tool. She accepted the gifts graciously, though her formal membership in the clan remained a question for a future visit. The clan head’s speech urged Thorin to do good and return after neutralizing the threat, then wandered into casualties before his wife intervened. Daz recognized that the speaker was somewhat drunk.

With horses supplied by Bruenor, the expedition traveled east toward the Surbrin Hills. Over eight days and roughly 180 miles, Octavia lost a pouch and recovered it two mornings later, Rainaldus read whenever they stopped, and the party adjusted to Sigrun’s pace. At a hidden cave entrance, six black-clad Zhentarim sentries checked Davra’s authorization and recorded the crossing. No money changed hands; the guides’ papers and the logged headcount established the expedition’s right to pass.

Saddled giant lizards waited just inside. Gyrgum eagerly inspected them, Octavia admired their saddle stitching, and Rainaldus reluctantly abandoned his preference for walking. Glabbagool remarked that one female lizard smelled like water. Zalthir’s initial magical scrutiny revealed nothing, and a later attempt to analyze a skin sample established only that she was a lizard. The party also resolved the fate of the captured drow noble spy previously carried in their bag of holding: she had been left with the dwarves of Gauntlgrym as a valuable bargaining piece for future negotiations or a prisoner exchange.

On their first night underground, the party discovered a slaughtered camp. Its fire had been dead for days, its packs and bedrolls were torn open, and gutted bodies lay arranged with disturbing neatness. The air tasted of copper and old fruit. Gyrgum examined gray growth covering the wounds and learned that it matched the plague wells, probably linking the scene to Zuggtmoy. Zalthir found only mundane supplies, avoided a corpse bearing a pale fungal cap, and discovered an unfamiliar mark high on the wall. Daz identified its demonic nature without determining whose sign it was.

On an evening before the stream crossing, the Shield Guardian traveling with the party presented Daz with its control amulet. The construct, supplied through the Harpers, showed more awareness than its kind normally possessed. Daz’s experimental command to reveal itself produced a confused attempt to remove its armor. After correcting that misunderstanding, Daz proposed a quick game of speed chess, which the Guardian accepted. His attempt to let it win failed; it cleared the board and demanded another game. Daz then played seriously and won. Thereafter, every long rest brought another match, and the Guardian refused to accept less than his best effort.

Around the seventh day underground, a clear stream crossed their route, its water carrying pale glowing fish and its air smelling clean for the first time in days. Sigrun immediately went swimming, delighted by the chill water and asking that her granddaughter not be told. Gyrgum tested the temperature, declined to swim, and let his lizard drink. Octavia discovered that she had left her testing tool at the cave, so Glabbagool sampled the stream with a pseudopod. After offering a detailed chemical analysis that bewildered his listeners, he reduced his conclusion to a single word: “clean.”

On the ninth night, Octavia introduced the name Khalessa Draga while stirring the camp pot. Khalessa was a merchant associated with Mantol-Derith’s Zhentarim trading circles, a name heard wherever deals were made. Rainaldus appeared uncomfortable with the disclosure and qualified how much her reputation established about her actual whereabouts. Questions about the massacre brought Octavia back to her insistence that the Black Road was supposed to be safe. The party also confirmed that the Iron Mandate letter should be presented at Mantol-Derith; they had not yet arrived.

On the nineteenth day underground, the expedition reached a cavern filled with waist-high pale fungus and thick stalks. Octavia again protested that the obstacle should not be there. Rainaldus reminded her that reality did not always follow the book, while Sigrun and Thorin returned to their shared observations about maps and changing places.

The party considered flying over the roughly sixty-foot fungal field, dashing through on lizards, or using the Shield Guardian to help carry the mounts. Sigrun nearly volunteered to scout, but protecting her remained part of the party’s Lords’ Alliance obligation. Daz’s investigation revealed ten motionless figures among the stalks: duergar covered in gray growth, all facing the entrance. He identified them as spore servants, akin to fungal zombies made by myconids and creatures previously used by Ilvara. No myconids were present, and the surrounding spores were not ordinary myconid spores. The session ended before anyone entered the field, with the party weighing a rush across against drawing the servants into a narrow approach for ranged attacks.

## NPCs
### Lord Eravien Haund
A gourmet and member of the Lords’ Alliance who uses fine food and special deals to influence the party. He arranged for Sigrun to accompany the expedition and offers further rewards in exchange for more services.

His earlier meal included cheese that Glabbagool adored, making the lord’s culinary approach an effective means of securing the group’s attention. An additional note accompanying the Alliance material extends his offer of further work.

### Sigrun
A veteran ranger turned archivist who joins the party to map their journey. In her younger days she traversed the north on foot; now she goes on expeditions into the Underdark. She is pragmatic, fond of philosophical sayings, and enthusiastically swam in a freezing underground stream. She claims no faction allegiance but was arranged by Lord Eravien Haund.

### Glabbagool
An intelligent grey ooze who travels with the party. He absorbed a magical Ioun Stone gifted by King Bruenor, uses telepathy to communicate observations, and can analyze substances with his unique physiology, acting as a living spectrum analyzer.

His status prompts dwarven speculation about citizenship, guest rights, and taxes. For the prophecy’s count of four, he is treated as part of Zalthir rather than a separate figure.

The stone grants him +1 AC. A provisional ruling lets him defend Zalthir for +1 AC instead of attacking, using his own focus points to change roles. He notices that one female lizard smells like water, but his subsequent analysis fails to explain why.

### Octavia
A young gnome and Zhentarim trainee serving as a guide to Mantol-Derith. She is pleasant and charming but potentially dangerous. She is easily excited by fine craftsmanship, tends to be unsettled when reality contradicts her maps and notes, and has a habit of leaving things behind.

She loses a pouch on the first morning of the overland journey and finds it on the third, then later discovers that she left a water-testing tool at the cave. She admires the giant lizards’ saddle stitching, recognizes the Shield Guardian’s control amulet, and enthusiastically proposes using the construct to test traps.

Her disclosure of Khalessa Draga’s name makes Rainaldus uncomfortable. Both the slaughtered camp and the fungal cavern challenge her repeated expectation that the Black Road should be safe and predictable.

### Rainaldus
A senior Zhentarim guide who is experienced, somewhat cynical, and protective of his trainee Octavia. He manages the group’s passage through checkpoints, reads at every halt, and is gruffly reluctant to ride a lizard rather than walk.

He presents Davra’s papers, records the expedition’s headcount, and identifies Morista’s involvement in the mounts’ provision. He estimates a twenty-five-day underground journey.

At the slaughtered camp, he observes that the ashes have been cold for days. He cautions Octavia about forgotten tools, indiscreet conversation, and expecting reality to match written descriptions.

### Shield Guardian
A sentient automaton traveling with the party that possesses more awareness than such constructs typically display. It presented Daz with its control amulet and insists on playing chess against him every night, refusing to accept anything less than a genuine effort.

The Guardian accompanies the party through the Harpers. It carries a chessboard in a bag and remembers Daz as a previous victor. It agrees to speed chess but recognizes his attempt to lose deliberately, clears the board, and insists on another game. Daz wins their serious rematch.

Its response to Daz’s first command reveals an overly literal interpretation of “reveal yourself,” leading to an aborted attempt to remove its armor.

### Khalessa Draga
A merchant and trader mentioned by Octavia, known by name in rooms where deals are done. She is associated with Zhentarim trading circles in Mantol-Derith, though Rainaldus implies her actual presence is often uncertain.

Octavia introduces her while preparing the ninth night’s meal. This is the party’s first confirmed introduction to the name, despite an initial suggestion that they might already know it.

### Spore Servants
Ten duergar who have been transformed into fungal zombies, standing immobile and broad-shouldered among the fungus stalks of the cavern, covered in gray growth and all facing the party. Daz recognizes them as similar to creatures created by myconids or previously encountered under Ilvara.

No myconids are visible in the cavern, and the spores present differ from ordinary myconid spores. The servants remain motionless throughout the encounter’s opening; their response to intrusion is not tested before the session ends.

## Locations
### Gauntlgrym
The ancient dwarven stronghold where the party concludes their business, receives rewards and lore documents, and prepares for their next expedition into the Underdark.

Bruenor supplies horses for the surface journey and personally gives Glabbagool his Ioun Stone. Clan Feldrun provides a crowded farewell, food, and equipment gifts. The captured drow noble spy remains with the dwarves as a potential asset for negotiations or prisoner exchange.

### Surbrin Hills
A remote range of hills containing a hidden cave entrance to the Underdark, accessible only to those who know the way. Guarded by Zhentarim sentries who log every crossing.

The cave lies roughly 180 miles east of Gauntlgrym, reached after eight days. Six sentries wait near a small fire, with saddled lizards sheltered just inside. Light fades quickly beyond the mouth, and the first stair is steeper than it appears.

### The Black Road
A secret Zhentarim-controlled network of paths and tunnels through the Underdark, intended to be a safe passage for traders and agents, though the party finds evidence of a massacre along the route.

Passage depends on recognized guides and authorization rather than a toll paid at the entrance. Sentries check Davra’s papers, record headcounts, and monitor crossings without questioning each guest individually. The hidden overland approach is also described as a Black Network road known only to those who know its route.

Octavia’s confidence in the road’s safety increasingly conflicts with the expedition’s discoveries.

### The Underdark
The vast subterranean world the party travels through via giant lizards, encountering abandoned camps with fungal-infected bodies and strange demonic markings.

Rainaldus estimates that their journey to Mantol-Derith will take approximately twenty-five days. Significant encounters include the slaughtered camp on the first night, the stream around day seven, camp gossip on day nine, and the fungal cavern on day nineteen.

### Underground Stream
A clear, loud stream cutting across the tunnel path around the seventh day underground, featuring pale glowing fish beneath the surface and unusually clean air. Sigrun swam in it enthusiastically.

The far bank is a substantial distance away. Gyrgum finds the water too cold for swimming, while Glabbagool’s analysis establishes that it is clean and the lizards are allowed to drink.

### Fungal Cavern
A subterranean chamber encountered on the nineteenth day underground, where the floor is entirely covered in waist-high pale fungus with stalks as thick as a man’s arm. Ten duergar spore servants stand immobile within the stalks, all facing the party’s entrance.

The crossing spans about sixty feet, the ceiling is fifteen feet high, and the fungal floor is difficult terrain. No clear route around the growth exists within the chamber. The entrance offers a possible narrow position from which to engage the servants at range.
