# Chapter 72 The Fixed-Point Theory of Fate

Date: 2026-10-12

## Summary

Morning came to Candlekeep after a long night of celebration, and the party woke fully rested. With the morning came a note from Eldeth Feldrun, the dwarf they had come to know:

> “I've spoken to the lords of Mithral Hall and the lords of Gauntlgrym. They believe me. They have believed me for two months. They were waiting for someone whose word weighed more than a soldier's. Yours does. Come to Gauntlgrym. The dwarves of Gauntlgrym have questions only you can answer, and the forge has been cold enough that someone is going to need to relight it. The way is arranged, it goes at dusk. Do not make me a liar.”

Whoever held the paper would be teleported to Gauntlgrym when the hour came. That left the party a single day to finish its business in the great library.

Thorin carried the sentient blade Dawnbringer to Master Archivist Philemon's reading salon, where the scholar had built a small stand for her scabbard beside Thorin's seat. Philemon, thrilled, explained that the ritual had to move to its next layer. Dawnbringer had to speak the name of her true wielder. When he asked, she erupted in anger and cursed Brysis, on whose body the party had found her. Thorin had believed Brysis was her former wielder, so he sensed there was more to the story. He told her gently:

> “Look, you know, I don't know what happened before, but you're not trapped with me.”

He made clear that he was not her captor and would never enslave her. With a remarkable moment of insight, Thorin understood that Brysis had never been Dawnbringer's wielder at all, but her captor. Philemon immediately began scribbling notes and told Thorin, “You may have a gift here.” He thought the relationship between Thorin and Dawnbringer had visibly improved even in the previous forty-eight hours.

Philemon asked once more who her wielder had been. Dawnbringer seemed to turn toward the sunlight, and after a long pause she began to weep in Thorin's mind. At last she said, “Dynaphyse.” Dynaphyse had been a servant of Lathander, the Morning Lord, and Dawnbringer's first and true wielder. Brysis of Khaem had killed Dynaphyse and, as Dawnbringer explained, “buried me with her own corpse.” Dawnbringer flickered on and off for several seconds, then admitted, “I feel a lot better after saying that.”

Philemon handed Thorin a note:

> “Lathander is the god of second chances. She has just had hers. This is her giving yours.”

Her fear of the dark was gone. In thanks, she bound a blessing to Thorin called Lathander's Dawn, so that once between long rests, when he would fall, she would flare with the light of dawn and leave him standing with a single breath of life. When Thorin asked what he could do for her, she answered that he had already done so much—then demanded that no one mention the crying.

> “I've got a reputation to maintain.”

Thorin promised, “I didn't see any tears.”

> “Excellent. That's exactly what… that's the story, and we're sticking to it. One does not… one does not become the triumphant vengeance of the Morning Lord by being. Weepy.”

Philemon, who catalogued sentient artifacts, could not resist asking whether Dynaphyse was spelled with an “S” or a “Z.” Dawnbringer answered that it was an “S.” Philemon then recalled a line in the Netherese temple scrolls concerning “a Lathanderite woman of the Dawn,” struck from the record and lost with Khaem. He had never known what Khaem had taken.

When Thorin admitted he did not understand the reference, Dawnbringer gave him the metaphorical look of a partner whose loved one had failed to pay attention to something deeply important. She reminded him that he was young, and explained that the Netherese were an ancient civilization from four or five thousand years ago, lost after attempting to break the Weave. Dawnbringer's story had survived as an obscure fragment within that history. Philemon was delighted, wished them well, and suggested that Thorin—with his surprising insight and empathy—might consider therapy as a second career.

Gyrgum went to Brother Vareth, who greeted him eagerly. Vareth asked whether Gyrgum had marked up the papers he had previously been given. Gyrgum claimed he had been unable to find a red pencil and returned the papers untouched. Vareth nevertheless took them back as if they were sacred relics merely because Gyrgum had handled them. Vareth's once-obscure research into orcs, Tiamat, Bahamut, Stroud, and the “question of the age” had become suddenly prominent because Gyrgum had participated in world-changing events.

Just then the slow tortle scholar Pizwog shuffled past the doorway and stared at Gyrgum's satchel. Gyrgum pulled out his tedious, sprawling, syncretic paper—which he had never actually read—and began pointing out its supposed best passages to Vareth. He improvised the praise well enough that the already eager Vareth believed him and promised Pizwog they would find a place for his research in the Pantheon. Gyrgum reflected, “Gotta be nice to the little people,” while the gesture made Pizwog's next century.

Vareth led Gyrgum through a museum-like hall of artifacts to station nine, which held a record of the speech given by the dragon Protanther after his defeat at chess by Stroud:

> “For 400 years, I have played and replayed this game in my head. I have waited for Ortho to finish the game for 400 years. I thought… I had thought, I had thought, I thought I had thought of every position, every eventuality that I could not lose. But I… did, because there was one thing I'd missed. One move. If my ally was this kin of Ortho, I might have won today.”

Vareth explained the three established schools of interpretation. The Stroudite school called the speech diplomatic theater for the Tiamat alliance and held that Stroud won through mortal initiative. The Draconic school said Protanther had deliberately let Stroud win as part of a larger plan to bring the dragons into the alliance. The Bahamutian school held that Bahamut had intervened so Protanther could not perceive the decisive move, and that the dragon's silence afterward was reverence.

Gyrgum supplied a fourth interpretation: Stroud was going to win, so there had been nothing to say.

> “There was hardly even any point in playing. We already knew the result.”

Vareth immediately wrote down the “Gyrgumite School” position: “The game was already decided. So what was there to say?” Gyrgum added, “'Twas already written in the Book of Fate.”

Vareth then showed him the back of one of Stroud's earliest post-match letters. In a different hand and different ink, written within the previous three years, was the sentence:

> “He let you win, old friend.”

Nobody knew who had written it, and Stroud himself had remained uniquely silent. The Stroudites dismissed it as irrelevant nonsense; the Bahamutians attributed it to Protanther under Bahamut's prompting; and the dragons believed it came from Protanther or one of his allies. Gyrgum decided that “he” was a metaphorical reference to fate itself. Vareth declared the solution brilliant because it rendered the writer's identity irrelevant: if fate had ordained the result, then of course the sentence had to be written.

The resulting fixed-point theory held that Stroud's victory was inevitable, but the behavior surrounding it was not. Protanther could accept the loss, rage against it, or denounce the game; fate determined the fixed event but preserved freedom in what came before and after. Vareth was overjoyed and declared this a complete theory of the question of the age.

He renamed his project the Gyrgumite Foundation, dedicated to the fixed-point theory of fate. Gyrgum immediately wondered how he might skim some research funds from it. Vareth, almost reading his thoughts, offered a small and dignified honorarium in exchange for occasional duties, including an annual colloquium and a lecture. Gyrgum agreed after ensuring that the foundation would also sponsor his travel. With no plan beyond doing something big, he had founded a school of thought—and potentially a future employment program, faculty position, and new cultic interest in the gods of fate.

Zalthir and Glabbagool went to the House of Mechanus. They found Spanner, the head of the house, deeply distraught. The Avowed had told him that his tools were not the “dust” named in Alaundo's riddle, so he believed he had not participated in the great events at all.

> “Do you know what it's like to know that you could have been this close to being a participant in the events that involved Alaundo and it not happening?”

Zalthir assured him that the tools had worked and that Spanner's insights had made the partnership between Zalthir and Glabbagool possible.

> “You were clearly a participant. You were of significant aid to our success.”

Spanner went instantly from despair to delight. Thirteen Modrons joined him in a synchronized celebration, after which they reverently disassembled the returned tool piece by piece as a blessed artifact.

Then the promised procedure began: a survey with 169 steps, thirteen observers, and thirteen positions for each observer, expected to last until evening. Zalthir attempted the first few movements but struggled to understand the purpose of the exercise. Spanner soon sighed that it would take forever and proposed doing the remaining 166 steps by correspondence, handing Zalthir a form.

A Modron stood opposite Zalthir and mirrored his movements. Asked to demonstrate a movement, Zalthir shadow-stepped without warning. Thirteen pens immediately began scratching and revising notes while Spanner observed, “The subject does not announce the move.” The unannounced teleportation had thrown the perfectly ordered observation into confusion.

The Modrons then turned to Glabbagool and found a new problem: “subject B” had no axis of symmetry. Glabbagool tried to compress himself into a square approximating a Modron. After a stunned silence, all thirteen declared together:

> “Symmetrical within Tolerance.”

The room visibly calmed after the chaos of Zalthir's shadow step. The Modrons next asked to see Glabbagool's “corrosive embrace” without the acid. Glabbagool found the request awkward and asked Zalthir if it was the right thing to do. One Modron volunteered because it was its turn, and Zalthir cautioned Glabbagool not to hurt it too much.

Glabbagool grappled the volunteer successfully, carefully restraining his corrosive effect. Even so, the contact lifted a fine-grained film from the Modron's faceplate. Spanner immediately stopped the survey, caught the film on a clipboard, and exclaimed:

> “My goodness, it is dust of Mechanus, the first ever taken without a Modron dying. What a spectacular blessing.”

He gave the party one pinch of Dust of Mechanus. He then asked whether the rest of the party would participate in an asymmetric baseline. Zalthir protested that everyone was busy—“One of them's in therapy”—but Spanner promised the procedure would take only thirty seconds. Gyrgum agreed after hearing that Associate Reader Gyrgum would receive author credit.

Daz sat down with Sister Yvenne, who was spectacularly unimpressed. He had walked out with an arcane book bearing the eye of an evil lich, killed two of Manshoon's simulacra, and worn Manshoon's robes, but his citation list was too short. Daz insisted she was mistaken and cast Minor Illusion, adding a mass of plausible but fabricated citations modeled on works he had previously seen in Candlekeep. Yvenne failed completely to see through the deception and apologized:

> “Oh, well, I did not realize that you were such an accomplished academic, Mr. Daz.”

Daz replied, “I wouldn't expect you to pay that attention.” Yvenne admitted that his work had apparently been in a different part of the literature.

She then turned to the attack by House T'sarran in the fungal cavern. It had been a real deployment of house blood, a whole drow patrol, and house mages—not a random or minor commitment. A matron mother did not invest such resources in extracting a nobody. The operation suggested that House T'sarran was personally curious about who had paid for Daz's protection, rather than merely seeking to kill him.

Yvenne handed Daz a sealed tube. With his exceptional eye for detail, he immediately noticed that the wax seal had been broken and recently resealed in a different color. The tube was empty. Brother Hilmin Thaer had pulled it three years earlier, then died of natural causes eighteen months ago, only weeks after a private audience with a visitor whose name was never logged.

Yvenne next produced a redacted survivor list from a formally destroyed house. Such a house was not supposed to have any survivors, yet the record showed two dated and verified as deceased, with one location unknown and the corresponding records purged. Daz recognized that the ink composition was wrong for the document's apparent age. The redacted block merely looked old at a glance. Its formatting was anachronistic, and the oxidation pattern of the standardized drow iron-gall ink dated the alteration to years rather than decades. The ink was younger than Yvenne's career. Someone was still actively maintaining the seal and erasing the trail.

Yvenne said she had once suspected Daz might have been an infant of Eilistraee, but she would have known if he were one of theirs, and he was not. What she saw instead was a long-term, patient operation using cut-outs and layered resources, with enough reach to penetrate Candlekeep and erase material that was not meant to be read. She would not tell Daz what he was—that was for him to decide whether to know—but what was happening was not random.

> “Someone with a stake in a bloodline has decided you are worth keeping alive at considerable cost. That is a kind of inheritance, whether or not you ever meet the person who is paying for that price.”

Daz concluded that a house which once held the fourth seat and used the name Daz'issin had been formally annihilated; one of its three survivors remained alive somewhere; and someone with current institutional reach was still actively erasing the trail. He compared himself to “the Fresh Prince of Bel-Air, but underground,” though Yvenne's evidence did not establish that he was the lost heir—only that someone might believe he was. Daz understood that some people were protecting him, while House T'sarran wanted to know who would expend such resources to protect “a nobody.” His name and possible heritage had placed him in the middle of a larger drow intrigue.

Yvenne gave him material about drow culture so he could begin understanding that heritage. She also warned him about the book bearing Vecna's eye. Fheminor had been very glad to see it leave, and Yvenne thought Daz should ask himself why a book had wanted him. The warning carried additional weight because it was after Daz flipped through the Book of Vile Darkness that he had been prompted to take the Vecna book.

Last, Zalthir remembered the question that had nagged him: who was Jimjar? He gathered the party and went to Brother Khell-Vire in the Echoing Hall. Khell-Vire warmly congratulated them on defending Candlekeep before leading them through a deity-identification ritual.

The ritual revealed that Jimjar was Callarduran Smoothhands, the Deep Brother and god of the svirfneblin. He had been in the Abyss during a long quarrel with Yeenoghu, Demogorgon, and Zuggtmoy over their depredations against his children, the deep gnomes. When the same planar intrusion that released the demons tore through the barriers, Callarduran was dragged to the Material Plane with them. He disguised himself so the demons would not recognize him.

Khell-Vire explained Jimjar's sudden disappearance:

> “Gods who walk among us do not leave when the work is done. They leave when they are seen. Whatever he came out of the dark to do, he has not finished.”

Jimjar had vanished because someone had recognized his divinity. His work remained incomplete.

The gambler's knucklebone, which Jimjar had tossed onto Glabbagool so he could speak to and calm the oozes, was a god's marker and a debt Callarduran intended to pay:

> “The holder of the gambler's bone will be heard once and only once and will have to choose what to say. It's a God's marker, a debt he means to pay.”

Jimjar had previously pretended that the bone held no special significance. In truth, it promised repayment for the party's role in saving the deep gnomes. Khell-Vire read the signs to mean Jimjar would return once more, though neither the timing nor the length of his stay was known. Daz wondered whether Callarduran was helping him because the god was angry with whoever wanted Daz dead—the enemy of his enemy.

As dusk approached, the party could feel the teleportation magic gathering. Tadric reminded them that they had never told Sylvira what had happened to her son. Tadric had been forced to explain it himself, and Sylvira was angry. Thorin, newly recognized for his extraordinary empathy, volunteered to approach her with Dawnbringer and explain what had happened before the party departed for Gauntlgrym.

## NPCs

### Eldeth Feldrun

A dwarf warrior who has spoken to the lords of Mithral Hall and the lords of Gauntlgrym. They have believed her for two months but were waiting for confirmation from people whose word carried more weight than that of a soldier. She arranges the party's teleportation to Gauntlgrym at dusk, warns that the forge has been cold long enough, and ends her summons with, “Do not make me a liar.”

### Master Archivist Philemon

An Avowed monk and specialist in sentient artifacts who facilitates the therapy session between Thorin and Dawnbringer. He prepares a special stand for Dawnbringer's scabbard, carefully leads her toward naming her true wielder, and eagerly documents every breakthrough for future scholarship. He connects Dynaphyse to ancient Netherese temple scrolls, asks precise questions about the spelling of her name, and concludes that Thorin possesses unusual insight and empathy suited to a possible second career in therapy.

### Dawnbringer

A sentient sun sword who communicates telepathically and emits radiant light. Thorin had understood Brysis to be her former wielder, but during her session with Philemon he realizes that Brysis was actually her captor. Her first and true wielder was Dynaphyse, a servant of Lathander whom Brysis of Khaem killed before burying Dawnbringer with Brysis's own corpse. Naming Dynaphyse allows Dawnbringer to grieve, overcome her fear of the dark, and grant Thorin Lathander's Dawn. Fiercely protective of her reputation, she orders Thorin never to mention that she cried.

### Dynaphyse

A servant of Lathander, the Morning Lord, and Dawnbringer's first and true wielder. Brysis of Khaem killed her and stole Dawnbringer. An obscure Netherese temple record appears to preserve part of her story, referring to a Lathanderite woman of the dawn who was struck from the record and lost with Khaem.

### Brysis of Khaem

The figure on whose corpse the party discovered Dawnbringer. Previously believed to have been Dawnbringer's wielder, Brysis was actually her captor. Brysis killed Dynaphyse, stole Dawnbringer, and was ultimately buried with the sword.

### Brother Vareth

An enthusiastic scholar deeply invested in the dossier surrounding Stroud's chess victory over Protanther. His previously obscure work becomes prominent after Gyrgum participates in major historical events. He eagerly accepts Gyrgum's improvised interpretations, records the new Gyrgumite position, and founds the Gyrgumite Foundation around the fixed-point theory of fate. He offers Gyrgum an honorarium, funded travel, and an annual speaking engagement.

### Pizwog

A slow-moving tortle scholar who authored a long, tedious, and syncretic academic paper. He watches for Gyrgum to acknowledge his work. Although Gyrgum has not read it, he successfully improvises praise in front of Vareth, who promises to find his research a place in the Pantheon. The recognition is expected to make Pizwog's next century.

### Protanther

An ancient dragon who famously lost a chess match to Stroud. He claimed to have replayed the game for four hundred years and to have accounted for every possible position except one decisive move. His cryptic post-game speech and permanent silence afterward support four competing interpretations: Stroudite, Draconic, Bahamutian, and now Gyrgumite.

### Stroud

The victor in the famous chess match against Protanther. The established Stroudite school credits his mortal initiative, while the new Gyrgumite school holds that his victory was a fixed point ordained by fate. Stroud has remained uniquely silent about the mysterious note, “He let you win, old friend.”

### Spanner

The administrator of the House of Mechanus and supervisor of its Modron librarians. He is initially devastated because the Avowed distinguish his “tools” from the “dust” in Alaundo's prophecy, apparently excluding him from the foretold events. Zalthir's assurance that his contribution was invaluable transforms his despair into ecstatic joy. Spanner directs the 169-step survey and identifies the residue from Glabbagool's grapple as the first Dust of Mechanus obtained without killing a Modron.

### Modrons

Thirteen mechanical beings from the plane of absolute neutrality who staff the House of Mechanus. They act in perfect synchronization, mirror research subjects, and record observations with obsessive precision. Zalthir's unannounced shadow step disrupts their procedure, while Glabbagool's square form reassures them that he is “Symmetrical within Tolerance.” One volunteers to receive Glabbagool's restrained corrosive grapple because it is that Modron's turn.

### Sister Yvenne

A stern Candlekeep academic who interviews Daz about the forces protecting him. She is initially more concerned by his inadequate citations than by his encounters with evil liches and Manshoon simulacra. After being deceived by Daz's illusory references, she treats him as an accomplished scholar. She uncovers House T'sarran's major commitment of resources, the tampered empty tube, and the recently altered survivor list. She theorizes that someone with a stake in a bloodline is spending considerable resources to keep Daz alive and warns him to ask why Vecna's book wanted him.

### Brother Hilmin Thaer

The Candlekeep brother who pulled the now-empty sealed tube three years ago. He died of natural causes eighteen months before the session, only weeks after a private audience with an unidentified visitor whose name was never entered in the records.

### Jimjar

The party's gambling companion, now revealed as Callarduran Smoothhands in disguise. He had been trapped in the Abyss during a long quarrel with demon lords over their attacks on the deep gnomes. He escaped during the planar rupture by disguising himself among the demons and later vanished when his divine nature was recognized. His work remains unfinished, and he owes the party a divine debt represented by the gambler's bone.

### Callarduran Smoothhands

The Deep Brother and deity of the svirfneblin. He opposed Yeenoghu, Demogorgon, and Zuggtmoy because of their depredations against his children. Brother Khell-Vire identifies him as Jimjar's true identity and interprets the gambler's bone as his promise to hear its holder once and only once.

### Brother Khell-Vire

A monk in Candlekeep's Echoing Hall who performs the deity-identification ritual that reveals Jimjar's true identity. He explains Callarduran's escape from the Abyss, the reason for Jimjar's disappearance, and the divine significance of the gambler's bone. He reads the signs to mean that Jimjar will return one more time.

### Tadric

A lieutenant who reminds the party of its social obligations as the teleportation magic gathers. Because the party never personally told Sylvira what happened to her son, Tadric was forced to deliver the news. He warns them that Sylvira is angry.

### Glabbagool

An intelligent gelatinous ooze accompanying the party. He participates in the House of Mechanus survey, struggles to provide the axis the Modrons request, and reshapes himself into a square to satisfy the Modrons' need for symmetry. His carefully restrained grapple against a volunteer Modron produces the rare Dust of Mechanus. Jimjar once tossed his gambler's bone onto him so he could communicate with and calm other oozes.

### Fheminor

Fheminor Scrivenbark, a Great Reader of Candlekeep. Sister Yvenne believes Fheminor was very glad to see the book leave, contributing to her suspicion that Daz should ask why the book chose or wanted him.

### Sylvira

The mother whose son's fate the party failed to explain to her personally. Tadric had to deliver the news in their place, leaving her angry. Thorin volunteers to approach her with Dawnbringer before the party departs.

## Locations

### Candlekeep

A great library fortress where the party has rested, celebrated its recent victory, consulted scholars, and uncovered secrets about its past, its artifacts, and the divine nature of its former companions. During their final day before Gauntlgrym, separate members of the party complete personal business involving Dawnbringer, the Gyrgumite theory, the House of Mechanus, Daz's heritage, and Jimjar's identity.

### Gauntlgrym

An ancient dwarven city and home to the Great Forge. Eldeth's note says the forge has been cold long enough that someone will need to relight it. The lords of Mithral Hall and Gauntlgrym have believed Eldeth for two months but have been waiting for the party's authoritative testimony. A teleportation effect connected to Eldeth's note is prepared to carry the party there at dusk.

### Mithral Hall

A dwarven stronghold whose lords, like the lords of Gauntlgrym, have heard Eldeth's account and have believed her for two months.

### Philemon's Reading Salon

A comfortable, quiet space within Candlekeep where Master Archivist Philemon studies sentient artifacts and conducts Dawnbringer's ritualized therapy. For this session, he prepares a custom stand for Dawnbringer's scabbard beside Thorin's seat.

### Vareth's Exhibit Room

A museum-like room within Candlekeep filled with stations displaying artifacts and research materials. At station nine, Vareth presents Protanther's post-match speech. Elsewhere in the exhibit he displays one of Stroud's early letters bearing the mysterious recent inscription, “He let you win, old friend.”

### House of Mechanus

A highly ordered facility within Candlekeep run by Spanner and staffed by thirteen Modron librarians. It houses carefully catalogued tools and supports a 169-step observational survey of Zalthir and Glabbagool. The interaction between Glabbagool and a volunteer Modron produces the first nonfatal sample of Dust of Mechanus.

### The Echoing Hall

The area within Candlekeep where Brother Khell-Vire performs rituals and interprets divine signs. Here the party learns that Jimjar was Callarduran Smoothhands and that his gambler's bone represents a divine debt.

### The Abyss

The plane where Callarduran Smoothhands was caught in a prolonged quarrel with Yeenoghu, Demogorgon, and Zuggtmoy. A rupture in the planar barriers pulled him toward the Material Plane alongside the demons, allowing him to escape among them in disguise.

## Items

### Eldeth's Note

A written summons from Eldeth Feldrun urging the party to come to Gauntlgrym at dusk. It says the dwarven lords have believed Eldeth for two months, that the party's word carries the authority needed to answer their remaining questions, and that the cold forge must be relit. The note is part of the travel arrangement: whoever holds it will be teleported to Gauntlgrym when dusk arrives.

### Dawnbringer

A sentient sun sword that communicates telepathically and emits radiant light. During her therapy session with Philemon, she reveals that she belonged to Dynaphyse, a servant of Lathander. Brysis of Khaem killed Dynaphyse, stole Dawnbringer, and was later buried with her. Naming her true wielder allows Dawnbringer to overcome her fear of the dark and grant Thorin Lathander's Dawn.

### Netherese Temple Scrolls

Ancient records referenced by Philemon that preserve an obscure line about a Lathanderite woman of the dawn who was struck from the record and lost with Khaem. Philemon connects that passage to Dynaphyse and Dawnbringer, linking the sword's history to events from four or five thousand years ago.

### Protanther's Recorded Speech

An artifact at station nine of Vareth's exhibit. It preserves Protanther's statement that he replayed his chess match for four hundred years, believed he had considered every possible position, and nevertheless lost because he missed a single move. The meaning of that admission and Protanther's silence afterward are central to the competing schools of interpretation.

### Stroud's Post-Match Letter

One of Stroud's earliest letters following the chess match with Protanther. On its back, written in a different hand and ink within the previous three years, is the mysterious note, “He let you win, old friend.” Its writer is unknown, and Stroud has never explained it. Gyrgum interprets “he” as fate itself.

### The Blessed Tool

One of the devices Spanner lent to Zalthir and Glabbagool. After Zalthir confirms that it was invaluable to the party's success, Spanner declares it blessed and has the Modrons reverently collect and disassemble it piece by piece.

### The Correspondence Form

A document Spanner gives Zalthir after concluding that the full 169-step observational survey will take too long. It is intended to let Zalthir complete the remaining 166 steps by correspondence.

### Dust of Mechanus

A rare, fine powder lifted from a volunteer Modron's faceplate during Glabbagool's carefully restrained grapple. Spanner calls it the first Dust of Mechanus ever taken without a Modron dying and a spectacular blessing. The party receives one pinch. Each pinch is spent on use: on a mechanism it is the most perfect of all lubricants, and the next attempt to operate or disable that mechanism succeeds; thrown on a creature, it stops the creature from changing shape for a short time.

### The Gambler's Bone

A knucklebone Jimjar tossed onto Glabbagool to let him communicate with and calm other oozes; Gyrgum has carried it since. Brother Khell-Vire reveals it to be a divine marker from Callarduran Smoothhands, whom the party knew as Jimjar. It represents a debt the god intends to pay. Its holder will be heard once and only once and will have to choose what to say.

### The Empty Tube

A container presented to Daz by Sister Yvenne. Its original wax seal was broken and recently replaced with wax of another color, and its former contents are missing. Brother Hilmin Thaer pulled it three years earlier, then died eighteen months ago shortly after meeting an unidentified, unlogged visitor.

### The Redacted Survivor List

A survivor list for a formally annihilated drow house. It records two survivors as deceased and leaves a third survivor's location unknown, with the relevant records purged. Daz determines that the redaction is recent: the ink composition is wrong for its claimed age, the formatting is anachronistic, and the oxidation pattern of the drow archive's standardized iron-gall ink dates it to years rather than decades. Someone with current institutional access is actively maintaining the erasure.

### Tome of the Stilled Tongue

An arcane book bearing the eye of an evil lich that left Candlekeep with Daz. Sister Yvenne believes Fheminor was pleased to see it depart and warns Daz to ask why the book wanted him. Daz was prompted toward it only after he had flipped through the Book of Vile Darkness.

### Yvenne's Drow Materials

Educational material Sister Yvenne gives Daz so that he can begin learning more about drow identity, history, and culture while investigating his possible connection to the destroyed fourth-seat house.

## Spells

### Lathander's Dawn

A divine blessing manifested by Dawnbringer following her emotional breakthrough in therapy. Once per long rest, when Thorin drops to zero hit points, Dawnbringer flares with Lathander's dawn and leaves him at one hit point instead. Philemon frames the blessing as Dawnbringer giving Thorin the second chance that Lathander has just given her.

### Minor Illusion

Cast by Daz during his interview with Sister Yvenne to add a visual display of fabricated academic citations based on references he had previously seen in Candlekeep. Yvenne completely fails to recognize the deception, apologizes for overlooking his scholarship, and begins treating him as an accomplished academic.

### Shadow Step

Used by Zalthir without warning during the House of Mechanus survey. Because the observing Modron expected to mirror announced movements, Zalthir's sudden disappearance and reappearance causes all thirteen observers to scratch out and revise their notes while Spanner records that the subject failed to announce the move.
