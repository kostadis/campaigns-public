# Chapter 027

Date: 2025-09-01

## Summary
The party stood in the sweltering industrial heart of Gracklstugh, surrounded by the roar of forges and the clang of hammers, staring down the heavily barred gates that separated them from the Darklake and their freedom. They had to find a way through one of three gates. They debated their options: bluffing their way through, presenting themselves openly, or some other desperate scheme. Thorin suggested pole-vaulting over the gates, and Gyrgum pointed out that sneaking is hard in a city full of invisible people. Then the distant sound of massive wings cut through the smoky air. A young red dragon, voluminous and ungainly, came dive-bombing toward them out of the cavern heights. It was Themberchaud, the Wyrmsmith of Gracklstugh, and he had spotted them during their fight. The party had already handed off his egg, so they had nothing on them to explain the visit. They reasoned that if he wanted them dead he would simply breathe on them from a distance. Gyrgum chose to talk to him while standing near a market entrance he could duck into.

He landed with a shuddering, graceless impact, his belly wobbling as he righted himself with all the dignity he could muster, like a fat bird or a turkey in flight. Themberchaud addressed the party as his personal agents, expressing grand frustration with the inefficiency of the Keepers of the Flame and their petty checkpoints. Before the party could fully process what was happening, the dragon grandly declared that he would provide them with his "express service" to wherever they needed to go next, and invited them to climb aboard, as though it were the greatest honor he could bestow. Not wishing to question the will of a dangerous dragon, the party scrambled onto his back. Gyrgum called the ride "dangerous and stupid" but agreed because it was a dragon ride.

The flight over Gracklstugh was anything but graceful. The party worked to steer Themberchaud toward the docks of the Darklake. At first he scoffed that his agents had better things to do than "loiter among the common rabble and petty traders." Gyrgum's Persuasion roll was a middling 13, and the help-granted reroll came out worse. When Gyrgum asked Themberchaud where he thought they should investigate, it became obvious the dragon had no idea. The party then argued that the docks were a place his agents had not yet checked:
- Daz suggested dropping them there.
- Gyrgum promised to "find the devil in the details."
- Zalthir pointed to the recent attack near the docks as a source of fresh intelligence.

He was persuaded, but as he banked into a turn, his excess weight and lack of recent exercise made for a chaotic, lurching maneuver. It called for DC 13 Athletics or Acrobatics checks, and several party members slid off his back. Zalthir, newly level five, sprouted wings and caught the falling Thorin, whose natural 3 and bonus d10 still fell short. Daz vanished in a burst of silvery mist and reappeared beside the flying Zalthir. Jimjar, ever the quick thinker, hurled a rope to the tumbling Gyrgum, who had cried out to Bahamut. Gyrgum caught it with a 19 on Acrobatics and was hauled back up by the combined effort of his companions.

Themberchaud, seemingly oblivious to the chaos on his back, showed off his realm with a series of fly-bys through the foul, forge-choked air. He then made a proud detour to the Forge of Thrazgad, where the party roped themselves together just in case. He hovered and delivered a booming speech about his own greatness before breathing a torrent of fire to light the forge below. The duergar below rolled their eyes at his ego. Then someone noticed the riders, and the citizens of Gracklstugh looked up in stunned terror, not just at the dragon but at the figures on his back. Daz rolled a natural 20 on Insight and worked out what the crowd believed: the Ember Vanguard, the legendary and feared agents of the Drow Empire, were riding the Wyrmsmith himself. The entire city fell into a panic. The party realized their reputation, however fabricated, was working in their favor. In rumor, Daz is an 18th-level mage able to cast Meteor Swarm.

Themberchaud deposited the party at the docks with a thunderous, ungainly landing. He roared for good measure and used his frightful presence to terrify the locals. Gyrgum promised to "search the docks far and wide," and the dragon flapped off toward his lair, convinced his agents were on a vital mission. A lone Duergar guard stepped forward, trembling but dutiful, and raised his hand to halt them in the name of the Deepking. Gyrgum's attempt to pass "in the name of Themberchaud" failed, rolling an 8 and then worse. The guard pulled out a wanted poster and recognized the party's faces as those of the Ember Vanguard. Thinking quickly, the party leaned into the deception, claiming they were undercover agents impersonating the Ember Vanguard to lure out the real threat. When Thorin suggested deputizing him, Gyrgum solemnly produced a caltrop from his pack and pressed it into the guard's hand as a secret symbol of the conspiracy, officially "deputizing" him. He then helpfully advised him to file down the sharp points. The guard suggested the party wait there to meet the real Vanguard, and Gyrgum said they would hide on the boat to spring the trap. The guard, enormously relieved that he would not have to fight anyone, rushed off to inform Captain Errde. That gave the party just enough time to seize the boat and push off into the Darklake.

On the water, the DM took stock of the party's standing:
- Hgraam is the one person they have not betrayed, and the Stone Giants are good with them.
- The Derro High Council and the Gray Ghosts probably want them dead.
- The Deepking hates the Ember Vanguard, which the party only resembles.

Thorin concluded, "We better be as powerful as they think we are if we come back."

On the Darklake, the party weighed their routes. Blingdenstone was the known exit, and the tomb lay on the way; the tomb was four days' travel by boat. Zalthir proposed a compromise: raid the tomb first for a demon-slaying treasure, then deal with the troubled Myconid circle. Jimjar made a new wager, betting an empty shell gem that the Neverlight Grove's "circle of joy" would reveal itself to be a circle of "unwilling vows" before the party ever left it. The Neverlight Grove is the Myconid home where Stool and Rumpadump longed to return. Gyrgum took the bet.

Three days into their voyage across the vast, dark subterranean waters, the party was ambushed by a swarm of stirges that descended from the cavern ceiling above. They were hideous things resembling a cross between an oversized bat and a monstrous mosquito, with AC 14, 2 hit points each, and a 1d4+3 piercing bite. The fight went like this:
- Zalthir unleashed a 15-foot cone of searing fire that incinerated three of the creatures mid-air, then punched down another.
- Daz conjured a shimmering barrier of force to deflect one that dove straight at him.
- Thorin drew his longsword and cut down two more in rapid succession.
- Gyrgum struggled to pry one loose that had latched onto him and begun to drain his blood.
- Daz finally ended the encounter by firing a volley of unerring magical bolts that struck the last of the creatures, leaving a bloody mess across the deck.

The rest of the journey passed without incident. The party arrived at a desolate, lonely beach that Sarith identified as the path toward the Lost Tomb of Khaem. They had heard about the tomb from Fargas Rumblefoot, the gnome they rescued in the spider webs long ago.

Before heading inland, the party took precautions to hide their arrival. Thorin asked whether there was anyone to sell the boat to. Sarith looked at the empty beach and said no. He pointed out they might want the boat later and proposed sinking it, then fixing it with Mending. The party scuttled their own boat, letting it sink beneath the dark waters with the plan to use magic to repair it upon their return. With the boat gone and their tracks as hidden as they could manage, Sarith led the group away from the shore and back into the winding, lightless tunnels of the Underdark, toward the tomb and whatever waited within.

## NPCs
### Themberchaud
The Wyrmsmith of Gracklstugh, a somewhat out-of-shape young adult red dragon who serves as a glorified lighter for the city's forges. He is vain and egotistical, styling himself "father of the flame" and "bringer of the flame," and he believes the party to be his personal agents. He offered them a ride across the city, showed off his realm, and lit the Forge of Thrazgad mid-flight. He knows little about where his "vast network" should be looking. He deposited the party at the docks, terrifying the locals with his frightful presence.

### Jimjar
A deep gnome companion traveling with the party who is prone to making bets. He saved the falling Gyrgum during the dragon flight by throwing him a rope, then wondered whether letting go would cost him the bet. He wagered an empty shell gem that the Neverlight Grove would prove to be a place of 'unwilling vows'; Gyrgum took the bet.

### Errde Blackskull
The captain of the Stone Guard in Gracklstugh. Not present in this session. The party had been working for her. The dock guard recognized the party and threatened to warn her. After the bluff he ran off to tell her the party was at the docks, and she is expected to discover their deception and pursue them.

### Duergar Guard
A terrified sentry at the Gracklstugh docks who recognized the party from a wanted poster as the Ember Vanguard ("one drow, one dragonborn"). He was tricked into believing the party was on a secret undercover mission. He accepted a caltrop as the symbol of the conspiracy, was very relieved not to have to fight, and ran off to inform Captain Errde.

### Eldeth Feldrun
The dwarf who has travelled with the party since Velkynvelve. At the landing beach she works to hide the party's tracks.

### Sarith
A Drow companion traveling with the party who feels a strange, possibly demonic compulsion to visit the Neverlight Grove. He guided the party across the Darklake and identified the hidden beach as the path toward the lost tomb. He rejected selling the boat, pointed out they might want it later, and proposed sinking it and repairing it with Mending.

### Stool
A young Myconid sprout traveling with the party who wishes to return home to the Neverlight Grove, and who insists all is under control there. Being a fungus, he was ignored by the blood-sucking stirges during the ambush on the Darklake.

### Rumpadump
A Myconid companion of Stool who is also seeking to return to the Neverlight Grove. Like Stool, he lacks blood and was therefore unaffected by the stirge attack.

## Locations
### Gracklstugh
A massive cavern city in the Underdark, home to the Duergar and the red dragon Themberchaud. The atmosphere is filled with the heat of forges and the sounds of heavy labor, and the air is foul enough to trouble an asthmatic. Multiple guarded gates control movement between districts. The cavern is huge but connects onward only through small tunnels.

### Laduguer's Furrow
The upscale industrial district of Gracklstugh, home to the city's most prominent factories and forges.

### Forge of Thrazgad
A major industrial forge in Gracklstugh that Themberchaud ceremoniously lit with his dragon breath during the party's flight over the city.

### Darklake Docks
The waterfront district of Gracklstugh where the party landed via dragon-back, bluffed a guard, and secured a boat for their escape into the Darklake.

### Darklake
A vast, dark subterranean body of water within the Underdark that the party navigated for four days. The DM described it as a three-dimensional "Swiss cheese" of passages. It is home to dangerous flying predators like stirges lurking in the cavern ceiling above.

### Neverlight Grove
The home of the Myconids Stool and Rumpadump, rumored to be suffering from a demonic influence. Sarith feels a strange compulsion to go there.

### The Hidden Beach
A lonely, desolate stretch of shore on the Darklake that Sarith identifies as the landing point for the party's trek toward a mysterious tomb. The party's boat now lies sunk offshore here.
