# Chapter 69 One Manshoon Dead, at least one or two more to go.

Date: 2026-09-08

## Summary
Combat resumes beside the basalt tower above the magma. Manshoon is already thirteen points down from Daz's Phantasmal Killer and protected by Mirror Image. Daz stands at the beginning of the standing anti-magic band on the bridge, and Edvaldo remains nearby.

Thorin charged forward with Dawnbringer raised high, landing a powerful blow against the robed figure before them. Manshoon fought back with cunning, protected by illusory duplicates that absorbed some of the party's strikes, sending fists and blades through false images rather than flesh.

Zalthir moved with terrifying precision, shifting his magical darkness over Manshoon and activating the eldritch power bound into his tattoo. Tendrils of living darkness reached fifteen feet through the gloom, struck the wizard, and seized him. Zalthir then dragged Manshoon bodily into the anti-magic barrier and battered him with further attacks. Manshoon, desperate, used a legendary burst of movement to wrench himself free and reposition near the tower. After the party clarified exactly how far Zalthir could have dragged him, Manshoon still had to run to escape, allowing Daz to slash him with a short sword as he passed.

Manshoon retaliated with Ward Eater, a devastating wave of magical energy affecting creatures outside the anti-magic field. Zalthir and Daz were protected by their position inside the field, but Gyrgum, Thorin, and Edvaldo were caught. Thorin and Edvaldo suffered the full force of the effect and were immobilized, while Manshoon siphoned vitality from his victims and regained twenty-five hit points. Edvaldo's continued survival immediately looked wrong: by straightforward calculation, the Avowed researcher should have been dead.

Gyrgum answered by summoning a floating spectral sword at fifth level. The weapon struck Manshoon for a heavy blow and remained hovering nearby for the rounds that followed. Seeing Edvaldo howling in pain despite having survived impossible punishment, Gyrgum called reassuringly across the battlefield, “Don't worry, if you die, I can bring you back.”

Daz left the anti-magic field long enough to cast *Fireball*, centering and shaping the blast so that it caught Manshoon and Edvaldo without striking Gyrgum or Thorin. Manshoon partially evaded the flames but was left with only a sliver of strength; immobilized Edvaldo took the full blast and still did not fall. Thorin, unable to move but still able to fight, hurled a javelin at Manshoon. The wizard's remaining duplicate spared him from death. Thorin considered throwing Dawnbringer next, but dwarf and sentient sword shared a moment of mutual judgment before he chose a dart instead—and missed.

Zalthir pressed the assault. Manshoon raised a magical *Shield* to deflect the first blow, bringing his defenses just high enough to survive it, but the protection bought him only a moment. Zalthir's next strike landed true, and the great Manshoon died—only to revert instantly into snow and melt in the heat of the magma chamber. An Arcana check confirmed the truth: the party had been fighting a simulacrum, a partially real construct of ice and snow with Manshoon's abilities but only a fraction of his vitality.

The party turned upon the suspicious Edvaldo. He insisted, “I am Edvaldo! I am this lowly Candlekeep!” and protested that he was an innocent victim. Zalthir struck and grappled him, then dragged him toward the edge of the walkway, where dropping him into the magma remained an immediate possibility. Asked to prove his identity, Edvaldo spoke warmly, fluently, and at length about the Endless Chant and the Word Lattice. Daz scrutinized the performance closely and found it convincing enough to stay Zalthir's hand, although Edvaldo's impossible survival and earlier inability to state the tower's precise height continued to trouble the party.

Glabbagool earnestly asked why Zalthir had not simply thrown Manshoon into the lava. His hero-worshipping delivery made the tactical criticism especially difficult to ignore. Zalthir explained that the party had needed to give the wizard a chance because killing him before he cast a single spell would not have been “sporty,” then asked Glabbagool to remind him in the future whenever he overlooked an available instant-death trap.

The winged owlbear watching from nearby also offered cheerful color commentary: “This was much better than the last one. The last one didn't even stop to say hello.” It was referring to the Manshoon simulacrum the party had just destroyed.

Faint speech drifted through the stone floor from somewhere above, in a part of the tower where no one was expected to be.

Gyrgum considered climbing the stairs stealthily, then chose a more direct magical approach. He cast *Locate Creature*, initially considering whether to seek the real Manshoon or a simulacrum. Since Manshoon had supposedly been dead for 170 years yet continued to appear “like a bad penny,” Gyrgum specified a Manshoon simulacrum. The spell, with its thousand-foot range, confirmed that another duplicate was upstairs.

Edvaldo begged to be left out of another confrontation with a powerful spellcaster “killing people left, right, and center.” Gyrgum suggested leaving Glabbagool to watch him, prompting Edvaldo to reconsider and request second authorship on the eventual paper about the combat. Zalthir agreed on the condition that Edvaldo not resist, then bound him thoroughly and left him subdued within the anti-magic zone while Glabbagool accompanied the party.

With Edvaldo bound, a disembodied magical voice spoke in the cadence of prophecy: “A dwarf will return to the Hall of Stone with the names of demons in her mouth.” The returning dwarf appeared to be Eldeth; the later giant-raised dwarf was Thorin, and the shadow-walker was Zalthir. The prophecy also mentioned the lords of the dwarves and an unlit forge. Daz recognized that the voice was conjured rather than human because of its slight fluctuation in timbre and its lack of breathing between enunciations.

The party ascended into a plain circular chamber. Dawnbringer was the principal source of illumination. Shelves rose from floor to ceiling along the eastern wall, holding a hundred of the most dangerous books in the world. Each showed the faint visual distortion of a magical glyph. A desk stood in the middle of the room with a note upon it, and an open velvet tray on the far wall held ten prismatic gemstones, four of them dark.

At the far end stood a door of black stone with no handle, keyhole, or visible seam. Eleven scorch marks formed a rough circle at chest height, evidence of repeated unsuccessful attempts to force it open. Before the shelves stood another Manshoon, his hands clasped behind his back. He looked exactly like the simulacrum the party had just killed, but his gray robes were noticeably cheaper.

This Manshoon reminded the party that the chamber contained hundreds of *Glyphs of Warding*. An explosion could trigger a catastrophic cascade among the warded books. He had already solved part of the door's mystery but could do nothing with it: “I've been sitting here since then with 100 books I cannot read and a door I cannot open, hoping somebody would come. And you have.”

Then a recorded echo of Alaundo's voice filled the chamber: “To the last door I gave no lock, for a lock is a promise that a thing can be stolen. I gave it a question instead.” The party recalled the decoded instructions from their earlier discoveries: those well-versed in Candlekeep's lore might fearlessly pass the Obsidian Door; the answer concerned something consumed to make light; it had to be spoken in the dark, offered as a gift rather than a demand, and said only once. Because Gyrgum had answered the guardians' questions and been verified as the reader, he was the person who would need to speak it.

The second Manshoon proposed a bargain and pointedly asked the party to notice that it was not a threat. He lifted one of the prophetic stones and explained the exchange: “If you want the dwarf's prophecy, I want a door. These are not the same object. Your cleric says one word, the door opens. You take the tray, I take the book.” Manshoon offered the tray in exchange for opening the door. The implied threat was that Manshoon could destroy the prophecies if the party refused.

Gyrgum connected the chamber to the previously decoded line stating that those well-versed in Candlekeep's lore could fearlessly pass the Obsidian Door. The party considered bringing Edvaldo upstairs, though Zalthir remained suspicious that the researcher might somehow be the real Manshoon. They compromised by returning downstairs and asking the bound scholar the riddle directly.

Edvaldo first blurted, “Books!” before changing his answer to candles: “Candles are what's consumed to make light. You're in Candlekeep. How obvious could it be?” His flailing did not convince the party that he actually understood the door. They continued considering candles, wax, and darkness, but no answer felt sufficiently certain to risk speaking the required phrase only once.

The party returned to the problem of the darkened chamber, with Dawnbringer providing the sole light and Gyrgum established as the verified reader. They considered asking Manshoon, the owlbear, or even the dragon for help. The session ended with the Obsidian Door still sealed and the answer hovering just beyond their grasp.

## NPCs
### Manshoon (Simulacrum)
A magical duplicate of the ancient and powerful wizard Manshoon, created from ice and snow. The first duplicate fought the party with spells and legendary maneuvers, using *Mirror Image*, *Shield*, Ward Eater, and extraordinary movement. Ward Eater damaged and immobilized Thorin and Edvaldo while restoring twenty-five of the simulacrum's hit points. Zalthir finally destroyed it, causing it to revert to snow and melt instantly.

The party learned that a simulacrum is partially real, has only half the original creature's maximum vitality, cannot learn or regain expended spell slots, and turns back into snow at zero hit points. Manshoon himself was reportedly killed 170 years ago but has continued to reappear “like a bad penny.”

A second simulacrum in cheaper gray robes was found waiting patiently in the upper tower chamber. It had spent six hours surrounded by warded books it could not read and an Obsidian Door it could not open. This second duplicate proposed a bargain: Gyrgum opens the door; the party receives the prophetic gemstones; Manshoon enters and takes one book. Although he insisted the proposal was not a threat, his handling of the gemstones made the danger to the prophecies clear.

### Edvaldo
A man presenting himself as the Avowed researcher Edvaldo, who accompanied the party into the tower because his claimed research concerned Candlekeep lore. His commoner token appeared as a “Babushka lady,” causing some initial confusion about his identity.

Edvaldo survived damage that straightforward calculation suggested should have killed him, raising the party's suspicions. He had also behaved strangely earlier when he knew the tower was approximately five hundred feet high but could not provide a precise measurement.

When Zalthir attacked and grappled him, Edvaldo pleaded innocence and demonstrated knowledge of Candlekeep lore, speaking fluently about the Endless Chant and the Word Lattice. Daz's investigation found his account convincing, but the party remained suspicious. Edvaldo later asked to avoid further magical violence, then agreed to remain involved if he received second authorship on the eventual paper about the combat. He was bound and left in the anti-magic zone.

Asked what is consumed to make light, Edvaldo first answered “Books!” and then “Candles,” insisting the latter should be obvious in Candlekeep. The party concluded that he did not actually know how to open the Obsidian Door.

### Glabbagool
An intelligent gelatinous ooze who accompanies the party and projects the earnest, hero-worshipping energy of the main character from *Kung Fu Panda*. Glabbagool questioned why Zalthir had not simply dropped Manshoon into the magma, forcing the party to explain that immediately eliminating the wizard would not have been “sporty.”

Zalthir instructed Glabbagool to point out any forgotten instant-death traps in the future. Glabbagool was also highly impressed by the speed and thoroughness with which Zalthir bound Edvaldo.

### The Flying Iron Owlbear
A hovering winged iron guardian encountered before the party entered the tower. It previously questioned the adventurers to confirm their identities and later watched their fight with Manshoon.

After the simulacrum melted, the owlbear provided color commentary: “This was much better than the last one. The last one didn't even stop to say hello.” Its “last one” was the Manshoon duplicate the party had just destroyed.

### Alaundo
A legendary, long-dead seer whose recorded voice provides the final chamber's instructions. Daz recognized the voice as artificial or conjured because it did not breathe and showed a slight fluctuation in vocal timbre.

Alaundo's voice explained that the Obsidian Door has no conventional lock because “a lock is a promise that a thing can be stolen.” Instead, he gave the door a question in place of a lock. Alaundo also left ten prismatic gemstones in a velvet tray, apparently containing prophecies, including one concerning a dwarf, the Hall of Stone, demons, an unlit forge, a giant-raised dwarf, and a Shadow Walker.

### Dawnbringer
A sentient magical sword wielded by Thorin. She served as the primary light source in the darkened upper chamber of the tower and visibly twitched in response to the presence of numerous *Glyphs of Warding*.

While immobilized, Thorin briefly considered throwing Dawnbringer as a ranged weapon. The sword's presumed response—“I'm a sword, not a spear”—and the silent look exchanged between wielder and blade persuaded him to use a dart instead.


## Locations
### Magma Chamber
A perilous subterranean chamber reached after passing through a shaft containing a permanent magical free-fall effect. A thirty-foot basalt walkway and an extendable mechanical bridge cross a pool of molten magma toward the tower.

A section near the tower is enveloped in a standing anti-magic zone where magical effects are suppressed. The field begins at the marked edge of the bridge and covers the final approach to the basalt platform. Anyone relying on magical flight to cross would lose that protection and fall into the magma.

The chamber became the site of the battle against the first Manshoon simulacrum and the subsequent interrogation of Edvaldo. Zalthir repeatedly used the anti-magic field to neutralize spellcasting and the nearby lava as an implicit threat.

### The Tower Library
A room within the basalt tower filled from floor to ceiling with every manner of evil and powerful tome imaginable. The books include materials capable of improving extraordinary abilities and contain knowledge of enormous value to an academic such as Manshoon.

The collection is heavily protected by magical wards and *Glyphs of Warding*. The party understood that mishandling the books could produce multiple explosions, potentially triggering a catastrophic cascade throughout the library.

### The Tower Chamber
A plain circular room in the upper tower. Shelves cover the eastern wall from floor to ceiling and contain roughly one hundred of the world's most dangerous magically warded books. A desk occupies the middle of the room with a note upon it.

An open velvet tray on the far wall holds ten prismatic gemstones, four of them dark. A second Manshoon simulacrum waits with his back to the shelves and his hands clasped behind him, unable to read the warded books or open the final door. The chamber is dark except for Dawnbringer's light.

### The Obsidian Door
A door of black stone with no handle, keyhole, or visible seam, located within the upper tower chamber. Eleven scorch marks form a rough circle at chest height, showing where someone—apparently Manshoon—repeatedly tried and failed to force it open.

It is protected by a riddle associated with the legendary seer Alaundo. Earlier decoded instructions state that those well-versed in Candlekeep's lore may fearlessly pass it. The answer concerns what is consumed to make light and must be spoken in darkness by the verified reader, as a gift rather than a demand, only once.

## Items
### Tool of Mechanus
A specialized device procured by the party and used to activate an extending mechanical walkway across the magma pit. Its bridge-like mechanism was compared to an extending walkway “straight out of Star Wars.”

The device allowed the party to cross the anti-magic section safely without relying on magical flight, which would have failed and dropped them into the magma.

### Empowered Tattoo
A magical tattoo on Zalthir's body that allows him to manifest tendrils of eldritch darkness. The tendrils can strike from fifteen feet away and grapple a target.

Zalthir used the tattoo from within his magical darkness to seize Manshoon, inflict force damage, and drag the wizard into the anti-magic zone. Zalthir carries two tattoos, though only the empowered tattoo's tendrils were central to this encounter.

### Prismatic Gemstones
Ten gemstones held in an open velvet tray within the tower chamber, four of which are dark. They contain prophecies placed there by Alaundo, including one apparently concerning Thorin and Eldeth.

The second Manshoon simulacrum offered the entire tray to the party in exchange for opening the Obsidian Door and allowing him to claim a book beyond it. By picking up one of the stones during the negotiation, he also made clear his ability to threaten the prophecies if the party refused.

## Spells
### Phantasmal Killer
A terrifying illusion cast by Daz to strike at Manshoon's mind at the start of the encounter. Manshoon made his later saving throw, limiting the damage to thirteen points rather than suffering the spell's full continuing effect.

The wizard had considered using his reaction and a spell slot on *Counterspell* but chose to risk the saving throw instead.

### Mirror Image
A defensive spell used by Manshoon to create illusory duplicates of himself, causing several of the party's attacks to hit false images instead of the wizard.

The duplicates diverted one of Thorin's attacks and later prevented his javelin from delivering the killing blow. Attackers first had to determine whether they struck Manshoon or one of his remaining images before other defenses, such as *Shield*, were resolved.

### Glyph of Warding
Magical traps placed upon the books in the tower library, designed to trigger destructive magic if the tomes are handled improperly. The party compared the danger to a book carrying a separate *Fireball* glyph on every page.

Hundreds of these glyphs line the upper chamber. Manshoon warned that an explosion there could trigger the other wards and cause a catastrophic chain reaction, making combat in the library dangerous for everyone.

### Spiritual Weapon
Gyrgum cast *Spiritual Weapon* at fifth level, summoning a floating spectral sword. Its initial strike hit Manshoon for nineteen damage, and it remained present during the following rounds and the party's ascent.

The spectral weapon resembled a sword, though the spell would have allowed Gyrgum to choose another form.

### Fireball
Daz unleashed a third-level *Fireball*, centering and shaping the blast so that it engulfed Manshoon and Edvaldo while sparing Gyrgum and Thorin.

Manshoon made his saving throw and took fifteen points of fire damage, leaving him at two hit points. Immobilized Edvaldo failed his save, took the full thirty-one damage, and remained alive despite having suffered fifty-three points in total.

### Shield
A reactive magical barrier cast by the Manshoon simulacrum when Zalthir landed what would otherwise have been a killing attack. It increased Manshoon's armor class by five and lasted until the start of his next turn.

The spell turned Zalthir's first hit into a miss, but it could not stop the follow-up blow that destroyed the simulacrum.

### Simulacrum
A complex spell that creates a partially real duplicate of a beast or humanoid from ice or snow. The duplicate uses the original creature's statistics but has only half its maximum vitality, is treated as a construct, cannot learn or grow more powerful, and cannot regain expended spell slots.

When reduced to zero hit points, a simulacrum reverts to snow and melts instantly. The party discovered the first Manshoon's true nature only after killing him. A second, more cheaply dressed simulacrum was subsequently found waiting in the upper tower chamber.

### Locate Creature
Cast by Gyrgum to sense the presence of another Manshoon within a thousand-foot range. The party first had to decide whether Gyrgum was seeking the real wizard or one of his magical duplicates.

Because Manshoon had reportedly been dead for 170 years yet continued to appear, Gyrgum specified a Manshoon simulacrum. The spell successfully located another duplicate upstairs and confirmed that the party needed to continue its ascent.
