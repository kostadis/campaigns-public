## Identity
- Protanther is an ancient dragon who famously lost a chess match to Stroud [ch 072 / entry].
- He claimed to have replayed the game for four hundred years and to have accounted for every position except one decisive move [ch 072 / entry; ch 072 / 072.03].
- Vareth said that Stroud found a move in a four-hundred-year-old game that Ortho had abandoned mid-move, and that Protanther's acceptance of that outcome is why the controversy matters [ch 057 / 057.01].
- He has never appeared on screen. Every item shows him only through discussion, a statue, or a recorded speech. His only direct words are the recorded post-defeat speech [ch 072 / 072.03].
- The summaries do not say his alignment, his form, or where he lives. Those points are left out.
- His "permanent silence" after the match is stated in the ch 072 entry [ch 072 / entry].

## Personality and Motivations
The evidence shows Protanther only through his recorded speech and other people's interpretations. Nothing shows him acting in the present.

- **From his own speech:** he says he replayed the game for four hundred years, waited for Ortho to finish it, and believed he had considered every eventuality. He says one move he missed cost him the game. He adds that if his ally "was this kin of Ortho, I might have won today" [ch 072 / 072.03].
- **Competing readings of his motives.** These are interpretations by scholars and factions, not established fact:
  - Draconic school: he honored Stroud's move because he is honorable, not because Bahamut compelled him [ch 057 / 057.01]. In ch 072 the same school holds that he let Stroud win as part of a plan to bring the dragons into the alliance [ch 072 / 072.03].
  - Stroudite school: the speech was diplomatic theater for the Tiamat alliance, and Stroud won through mortal initiative [ch 072 / 072.03]. In Mirabar a Stroudite polemicist rejected the idea that Protanther simply let events unfold [ch 055 / 055.03].
  - Bahamutian school: Bahamut kept him from perceiving the decisive move, and his silence was reverence [ch 072 / 072.03]. Triboar Bahamutians called him and Stroud "merely agents" of Bahamut [ch 055 / 055.05].
  - Gyrgumite school: Stroud was going to win, so there was nothing to say [ch 072 / 072.03].
- **Fixed-point theory:** Stroud's victory was inevitable, but Protanther was free to accept the loss, rage against it, or denounce the game [ch 072 / 072.03].
- **The "Protanther Doctrine":** a Triboar listener called Gyrgum's remark "heretically close to the Protanther Doctrine." The content of the doctrine is not given [ch 055 / 055.04].
- His real motives are not established in the summaries.

## History with the Party
- At Candlekeep, Vareth said his research on orc converts to Bahamut had gone from a quiet area to "a flood" because of the "Stroud-Protanther events". Protanther was spoken of as part of those events. [ch 054 / 054.06]
- In Mirabar, a Stroud School polemicist rejected both the Bahamutian hypothesis and the idea that Protanther had simply allowed events to unfold. Gyrgum came away sensing factionalism in Bahamutian religious culture around Stroud and the events around Protanther. [ch 055 / 055.03]
- In Triboar, Gyrgum's remarks drew Stroudites, Protanthians and Bahamutians who wanted his position on "the only question of the age". The Bahamutian faction said Protanther and Stroud were "merely agents" of Bahamut. [ch 055 / 055.04; ch 055 / 055.05]
- Also in Triboar, one listener called Gyrgum's remark "heretically close to the Protanther Doctrine". Gyrgum answered that the differences between Bahamut and Protanther are Legion. [ch 055 / 055.04]
- In Waterdeep's Castle Ward, the party saw a bronze statue of the Stroud–Protanther chess match, with an inlaid marble board and the plaque *This is the position. The move is yours.* Gyrgum said that if Protanther had moved his Rook, all could have gone another way. This drew a crowd of strangers eager to debate. [ch 055 / 055.05; ch 055 / 055.06]
- Vareth described Stroud finding a move in a four-hundred-year-old game that Ortho had abandoned mid-move, and said Protanther's acceptance of that outcome made the controversy matter. [ch 057 / 057.01]

## Relationships
- **Stroud:** his opponent in the chess match. Stroud found the decisive move in a game Ortho had abandoned [ch 057 / 057.01; ch 072 / entry]. A statue in Waterdeep's Castle Ward shows the two at the board, with the plaque *This is the position. The move is yours.* [ch 055 / 055.05].
- **Ortho:** the player who abandoned the game mid-move. Protanther says he waited four hundred years for Ortho to finish it [ch 057 / 057.01; ch 072 / 072.03]. The ally he mentions who might have been "this kin of Ortho" is not identified [ch 072 / 072.03].
- **Gyrgum:** he has not met Protanther. Gyrgum's remarks about Protanther at the Waterdeep statue and in Triboar drew debate [ch 055 / 055.04; ch 055 / 055.05]. His fourth reading was recorded as the "Gyrgumite School" [ch 072 / 072.03].
- **Vareth:** a Candlekeep scholar who treats the Stroud-Protanther events as the source of the "flood" in his research field [ch 054 / 054.06]. He keeps Protanther's recorded speech and the note on Stroud's letter [ch 072 / 072.03]. He renamed his project the Gyrgumite Foundation [ch 072 / 072.03].
- **Bahamut:** the Bahamutian faction holds that Protanther and Stroud were "merely agents" of Bahamut [ch 055 / 055.05]. Gyrgum answered that the differences between Bahamut and Protanther are Legion [ch 055 / 055.04].
- **The dragons:** the Draconic school holds that he let Stroud win as part of a plan to bring the dragons into the alliance [ch 072 / 072.03]. The dragons attribute the "He let you win" note to him or an ally [ch 072 / 072.03].
