## Identity
- Vareth, also called Brother Vareth, is an elderly, frantic, officious half-elf scholar and member of the Avowed. His specialty is orc conversions to the worship of Bahamut. [ch 054 / entry]
- He has kept his corner of the Theology Library (the Immortal Chambers) at Candlekeep for forty-one years. His narrow specialty is why a half-orc would convert to Bahamut. [ch 057 / entry; ch 057 / 057.01]
- His field was obscure and ignored until the Stroud-Protanther events, after which it became "a flood" of interest, with funding requests and conference invitations. [ch 054 / 054.06]
- Bookwyrm, the First Reader, assigned him as Gyrgum's mentor. [ch 056 / 056.04]
- He is not shown to be a Great Reader or to hold any other formal title in the evidence.

## Personality and Motivations
- **Eager and excitable.** He burst in furious that no one had told him a cleric of Bahamut had arrived. He grabbed Gyrgum's hand and dragged him toward his office. [ch 054 / 054.06]
- **Opportunistic about his research.** He called Gyrgum a "living data point" and said he could not wait to take advantage of the news about Stroud's Gambit. [ch 054 / 054.06; ch 054 / moment]
- **Passionate about the Question of the Age.** He described how heated the arguments had become, including three duels at Candlekeep. [ch 057 / 057.01]
- **Eager to find a new school.** Gyrgum's fixed-point proposal electrified him. He labelled it the "strong-weak hypothesis" and rushed off to begin his own research. [ch 057 / entry; ch 057 / 057.01]
- **Easily persuaded by Gyrgum.** He took the returned papers "as if they were sacred relics" and believed Gyrgum's improvised praise of Pizwog's paper. He accepted Gyrgum's improvised readings of the chess speech and the "He let you win, old friend" note, calling the latter brilliant. [ch 072 / 072.03]
- **Irritated but respectful** during Pizwog's roughly five-minute tract handoff. [ch 057 / 057.01]
- **Generous with institutional backing.** He renamed his project the Gyrgumite Foundation and offered Gyrgum an honorarium, an annual colloquium and lecture, and funded travel. [ch 072 / entry; ch 072 / 072.03]
- The summaries never show that he suspects Gyrgum is improvising.

## History with the Party
- Vareth is introduced as an elderly, frantic, officious half-elf scholar and member of the Avowed. His specialty is orc conversions to the worship of Bahamut. [ch 054 / entry]
- Vareth burst in on the First Reader, furious that he had not been told a cleric of Bahamut had arrived. [ch 054 / 054.06]
- Vareth acted on Gyrgum at first contact. He grabbed Gyrgum's hand, practically dragged him toward his office, and called him "a real living orc cleric of Bahamut" and a "living data point." [ch 054 / entry; ch 054 / 054.06]
- Vareth said he had secured a record of the legendary chess game. When Gyrgum named it "Stroud's Gambit," he said he could not wait to take advantage of the news. [ch 054 / entry; ch 054 / 054.06]
- Vareth's previously obscure field had become "a flood" of interest since the Stroud-Protanther events. The GM noted he was now getting conference invitations and funding requests. [ch 054 / 054.06]
- Bookwyrm, the First Reader, assigned Gyrgum to Vareth when she assigned the party's mentors. In that scene Vareth was named only in the assignment and did not act. [ch 056 / 056.04]
- Brother Vareth has kept his corner of the Theology Library at Candlekeep for forty-one years. [ch 057 / entry; ch 057 / 057.01]
- Brother Vareth led Gyrgum to the Immortal Chambers (the theology library) and into his side office. [ch 057 / 057.01]
- Vareth laid out three schools of thought on the Question of the Age: Stroudite, Bahamutian and Draconic. He also described how heated the arguments had become, including three duels at Candlekeep. [ch 057 / 057.01]
- Gyrgum proposed a fourth view, fixed points where choice is constrained. Vareth was electrified and labelled Gyrgum a "weak Stroudite and strong Bahamutian around fixed points." He called it the "strong-weak hypothesis." [ch 057 / entry; ch 057 / 057.01]
- Vareth gave Gyrgum Stroudite notes and papers, and Gyrgum promised to return them marked up. [ch 057 / 057.01]
- Vareth was irritated but respectful during Pizwog's roughly five-minute handoff of a tract to Gyrgum. [ch 057 / 057.01]
- At the Refectory dinner, the summary says Vareth had previously explained that Alaundo's prophecies are recited continuously and removed once fulfilled. Vareth was not shown present at the dinner. [ch 057 / 057.02]
- During the murder investigation, Vareth unveiled the fourth option, turning the Gyrgumite triangle into a quadrangle. This drew most scholars and Avowed to an emergency disputation in the Oval Theatre. [ch 060 / entry; ch 060 / 060.01]
- The migration to the Oval Theatre left Candlekeep's corridors empty and let the party's minder, Fembris Lancer, leave to attend. [ch 060 / 060.01]
- After the party emerged from the vault, Vareth was ecstatic. He said Gyrgum's theory had turned a three-legged model into a four-legged quadrangle, and he read the prophecies' fixed points as support for the "Gyrgumite school." [ch 071 / entry; ch 071 / 071.09]
- Vareth wanted to discuss future collaborations and research. Gyrgum promised to put him high on his list of potential collaborators. [ch 071 / 071.09]
- Gyrgum returned the papers to Vareth unmarked, claiming he could not find a red pencil. Vareth took them back as if they were sacred relics. [ch 072 / 072.03]
- Vareth believed Gyrgum's improvised praise of Pizwog's paper and promised Pizwog a place for his research in the Pantheon. [ch 072 / 072.03]
- Vareth took Gyrgum to station nine, which held a record of Protanther's speech after losing the chess game. He explained the three established schools. [ch 072 / 072.03]
- Gyrgum offered a fourth interpretation: the result was already decided, so there was nothing to say. Vareth wrote it down as the "Gyrgumite School" position. [ch 072 / 072.03]
- Vareth showed Gyrgum a note on the back of one of Stroud's early post-match letters: "He let you win, old friend." The writer was unknown. Gyrgum read "he" as fate, and Vareth called that brilliant. [ch 072 / 072.03]
- Vareth renamed his project the Gyrgumite Foundation, dedicated to the fixed-point theory of fate. He offered Gyrgum a small honorarium, an annual colloquium and lecture, and funded travel, which Gyrgum accepted. [ch 072 / entry; ch 072 / 072.03]
