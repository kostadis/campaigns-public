## Identity
Spanner is a gnome librarian who oversees the House of Mechanus in Candlekeep and directs thirteen modrons in their mechanical tasks [ch 067 / entry; ch 067 / 067.05]. He is methodical in his approach to librarianship and maintenance of the House [ch 067 / entry].

## Personality and Motivations
Spanner values the integrity of his mechanical staff above immediate utility, declaring that killing a modron to obtain Dust of Mechanus is "absolutely unacceptable" [ch 067 / 067.05]. He is methodical and inclined to lengthy, carefully structured explanations [ch 067 / entry]. He requires specific compensation for lending valuable modron-created tools, after initially asking for collateral, seeking to study the "mechanical symmetry" of Zalthir and Glabbagool [ch 067 / entry; ch 067 / 067.05]. He is susceptible to being stunned by interruptions or unconventional suggestions, such as when Gyrgum suggested "asking politely" during a carefully structured explanation [ch 067 / entry; ch 067 / 067.05]. He is practical enough to accept logical arguments for temporary borrowing rather than permanent retention of items, as demonstrated when Thorin clarified that the party only needed to borrow the tools [ch 067 / 067.05].

## History with the Party
- Spanner is a gnome librarian who oversees the House of Mechanus in Candlekeep and directs thirteen modrons in their mechanical tasks [ch 067 / entry; ch 067 / 067.05].
- He explains to the party that Dust of Mechanus is "the most perfect of all lubricants" and confirms that killing one of his modrons to obtain it is "absolutely unacceptable" [ch 067 / 067.05].
- Spanner agrees to lend the party specialized tools created by the modrons on the condition that Zalthir and Glabbagool submit to a future study of their mechanical symmetry after the expedition [ch 067 / entry; ch 067 / 067.05].
- The party utilizes the tools borrowed from Spanner to activate a mechanical bridge mechanism across a lava chamber in the dungeon [ch 068 / 068.02].

He communicates with them in a mechanistic language, eliciting synchronized physical responses [ch 067 / 067.05].
