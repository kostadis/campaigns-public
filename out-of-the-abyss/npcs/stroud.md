## Identity
- Stroud is known in the evidence only through other people's accounts. No item shows him present, acting or speaking.
- He is the victor of the famous chess match against the dragon Protanther, called "Stroud's Gambit" [ch 054 / 054.06; ch 072 / entry].
- Vareth described the game as four hundred years old, left abandoned mid-move by Ortho. He said Stroud "knew no chess yet found a move" in it. Protanther's acceptance of that outcome is what made the controversy matter [ch 057 / 057.01].
- The events of Stroud and Protanther are treated as legendary. They made Vareth's once-obscure research on orc conversions to Bahamut "a flood" [ch 054 / 054.06].
- Stroud's actions caused controversy across the realms, including what orc interest in Bahamut means for the orc pantheon [ch 054 / 054.06].
- His name and image are everywhere in the North:
  - a bronze bust at the Smiths Guildhall in Mirabar and a tavern called The Knight Beneath the Knight [ch 055 / 055.03];
  - a bronze statue of the Stroud–Protanther match in Waterdeep's Castle Ward, with the plaque *This is the position. The move is yours.* [ch 055 / 055.05];
  - a framed Milo Goodbarrel painting at Rishaal's Pageturners that shows Stroud, Milo, Malfire and Whistler [ch 055 / 055.06];
  - a love song about the chess game, sung by a bard in Beregost [ch 056 / 056.03].
- Race, class, origin and age are not stated in the supplied evidence.

## Personality and Motivations
- No item shows Stroud's personality, speech or motives. The evidence describes the Stroudite school, not the man.
- The Stroudite school holds that mortal choice is sovereign. Vareth gave its example as Tiamat being denied her power because a dwarf chose [ch 057 / 057.01]. The school credits Stroud's mortal initiative for the win [ch 072 / 072.03; ch 072 / entry].
- A Stroud School polemicist in Mirabar rejected both the Bahamutian hypothesis and the idea that Protanther let events unfold [ch 055 / 055.03].
- Stroud himself "has remained uniquely silent" about the note on the back of one of his earliest post-match letters [ch 072 / entry; ch 072 / 072.03]. The summaries give no reason for the silence. UNSUPPORTED: any motive for it.

## History with the Party
- At Candlekeep, Vareth said his obscure specialty in orc conversions to Bahamut became "a flood" after "these Stroud-Protanther events". The First Reader's scene describes this as the hottest field in academia following the legendary events of Stroud and Protanther. [ch 054 / 054.06]
- Vareth said he had secured a record of the chess game known as Stroud's Gambit, and Gyrgum confirmed he was familiar with it. [ch 054 / 054.06]
- The GM explained that Stroud's actions caused controversy across the realms, including what the sudden orc interest in Bahamut means for the orc pantheon and whether it was part of Bahamut's plan. [ch 054 / 054.06]
- In Mirabar, Stroud was ubiquitous: a bronze Stroud bust at the Smiths Guildhall and a tavern named The Knight Beneath the Knight. [ch 055 / 055.03]
- A traveling Stroud School polemicist in Mirabar tried to recruit Gyrgum to the "human agency hypothesis". Gyrgum answered that he stood for "strength and luck", and the polemicist gave him the pamphlet *Stroud and the Human Agency Hypothesis: A Critique — Dragons and Gods Do Not Rule*. Gyrgum kept it. [ch 055 / 055.03]
- Gyrgum sensed factionalism in Bahamutian religious culture around Stroud and Protanther. [ch 055 / 055.03]
- In Triboar, local Stroudites, Protanthians and Bahamutians pressed Gyrgum on where he stood on "the only question of the age". He replied "Are we not all the playthings of fate?", which won over the Bahamutian faction. [ch 055 / 055.05]
- In Waterdeep's Castle Ward the party saw a bronze statue of the Stroud–Protanther chess match with an inlaid marble board. Its plaque read *This is the position. The move is yours.* Gyrgum's remark about Protanther's Rook drew a crowd of strangers eager to debate. [ch 055 / 055.05; ch 055 / 055.06]
- At Rishaal's Pageturners, a framed Milo Goodbarrel painting depicted Stroud, Milo, Malfire and Whistler. Rishaal said it "saved the print run". [ch 055 / 055.06]
- In Daggerford, a monument to Travis Houlin carried a Dwarven inscription, "The move is yours", which Daz recognized as mirroring the Castle Ward statue associated with Stroud. [ch 056 / 056.01]
- The Daggerford priest said the monument was funded by the Metalworkers Guild, possibly with Stroud-school sponsorship. He called himself "a pure Bahamutian" and rejected the Stroudite heresy, but let the inscription stay because it was a gift. [ch 056 / 056.01]
- At the Burning Wizard Inn in Beregost, Gyrgum asked the bard for a song about Stroud. She asked whether he was Bahamutian or Stroudite, and Gyrgum said he was Bahamutian with sentimentality for Stroud. She then sang a love song about the famous chess game. [ch 056 / 056.03]
- Vareth described the Stroudite school as holding that mortal choice is sovereign, and that Tiamat was denied her power because a dwarf chose. [ch 057 / 057.01]
- Vareth said Stroud "knew no chess yet found a move" in the four-hundred-year-old game Ortho had abandoned mid-move, and that Protanther's acceptance of that outcome made the controversy matter. [ch 057 / 057.01]
- Gyrgum proposed a fourth school based on fixed points, and Vareth classified him as a "weak Stroudite". [ch 057 / 057.01]
- Vareth gave Gyrgum a collection of Stroudite notes and papers. [ch 057 / 057.01]
- Vareth also said the Stroudite pamphlets had crossed the Sea of Swords and that three duels had been fought at Candlekeep over the polemic shelves. [ch 057 / 057.01]
- At station nine in Candlekeep, Vareth showed Gyrgum a record of Protanther's speech after losing to Stroud. The Stroudite school reads the speech as diplomatic theater and credits Stroud's mortal initiative. [ch 072 / 072.03]
- Gyrgum's fourth reading was that Stroud's win was inevitable, which Vareth recorded as the "Gyrgumite School". [ch 072 / 072.03]
- Vareth showed Gyrgum a note on the back of one of Stroud's earliest post-match letters, in a different hand and ink, written within the previous three years. Its author is unknown, and Stroud himself has remained uniquely silent about it. [ch 072 / 072.03; ch 072 / entry]
- The Stroudites dismiss the note as irrelevant. The Bahamutians attribute it to Protanther under Bahamut's prompting. The dragons attribute it to Protanther or an ally. Gyrgum read "he" as fate. [ch 072 / 072.03]
- Gyrgum's fixed-point theory treats Stroud's victory as inevitable but leaves free the reactions around it. Vareth renamed his project the Gyrgumite Foundation. [ch 072 / 072.03; ch 072 / moment]
- Stroud is recorded as the victor over Protanther. The Stroudite school credits mortal initiative, and the new Gyrgumite school holds that the victory was a fixed point ordained by fate. [ch 072 / entry]

## Last Observed State
- His last appearance in the evidence is in Chapter 072, scene 072.03, as a subject of discussion. He did not act or speak there.
- At station nine in Candlekeep, Vareth showed Gyrgum a record of Protanther's speech after losing to Stroud [ch 072 / 072.03].
- Vareth also showed Gyrgum the back of one of Stroud's earliest post-match letters. A different hand, in different ink, wrote on it within the previous three years: "He let you win, old friend." The author is unknown [ch 072 / 072.03].

  - The Bahamutians attribute it to Protanther under Bahamut's prompting.
  - The dragons attribute it to Protanther or an ally.
  - Gyrgum read "he" as fate [ch 072 / 072.03].
- The Gyrgumite School now holds that Stroud's victory was a fixed point ordained by fate [ch 072 / entry; ch 072 / moment].
- Status not established in the summaries. Whether Stroud is alive, where he is, and whether he has contact with the party are not stated.
