# Chapter 37 The Necromancer's Thank You Note

Date: 2026-08-23

## Summary
The session opened with the DM sharing a new in-world recap document, *The Ledger of Outstanding Business*, "Kept by Zephyr, Chief of Operations, the Obsidian Edge, because somebody has to, and the cleric has declared bookkeeping beneath him." The ledger tracks what is owed to management, finished business, the party's own business, and "things nobody's paying for us." Any mistakes in it are to be blamed on Zephyr, who "takes bad notes." The DM then read the previous session's summary aloud before play resumed.

The party stood at the threshold of a ruined reception hall, its walls adorned with faded murals of new arrivals being welcomed to the temple. Those welcomes had long since turned to ruin. Battered couches, chairs, and stands lined the walls. Skeletons and gnawed skulls and bones of humans and other humanoids lay scattered across the floor. None of the cressets or wall sconces were lit, so the only light was what the party carried in. At the center of the room, beside a shallow pool with a stopped fountain, sat what appeared to be a filth-covered decorative statue of a Minotaur upon a throne. Sequoia noticed first that the creature was not stone at all, but very much alive. He quietly pulled the door shut before it could spot them, and the party huddled together to decide their next move. The DM reminded them that, as the new "big bosses," the Minotaur nominally worked for them now. He also pointed out that these were chaotic evil creatures, and that they could always kill it and take its treasure instead.

The Minotaur's own tongue was Abyssal. Nobody in the party spoke it, Zephyr's Undercommon was no help, and not even the fire salamander knew it. The group chose a bold gambit anyway. They would walk in with Calmer dressed as the Earth Cleric, and Zephyr would announce himself as the new management. The Minotaur, it turned out, harbored a deep and violent hatred for the temple's leadership, and the announcement sent it into a rage. It caught Zephyr and Sequoia by surprise. It charged 10 feet and gored Zephyr recklessly for 7 and then 10 damage. Zephyr rolled a natural 1 on the DC 14 Strength save and was hurled to the ground. The Minotaur then stepped in to loom over him with its double-headed axe. Zinnia struck the creature for 12 damage against its AC of 19, though his follow-up attack missed. Calmer stepped forward next, rolling a 21 on persuasion with a calm voice, and somehow gave the beast pause. It kept its axe raised and pointed at Zephyr with a clear indication that it viewed him as "eating material." Battered and prone on the cold stone floor, Zephyr ("Protect me, please") made his case with Zinnia's help. Rolling with advantage on his +11 persuasion, he convinced the beast that the new management was different. When the party offered it food from their packs, the Minotaur, grumbling and suspicious, began to eat.

With its hunger momentarily satisfied, the creature explained in broken speech that "Men in black cloaks" had been coming and attacking it. It agreed to terms. It would guard the reception hall in exchange for regular food and an end to the black-cloaked bandits' raids. It declared the room its own, then turned its gaze back to Zephyr and asked, with surprising casualness, "No hard feelings?" The truce came with a pointed warning: "If bandit come attack, I find you, I kill you, I eat you." Zephyr promised. Calmer wanted to step in and ask for something, insisting the creature understand that there was a new clerical order under "the High Lord Master Calmer." Zephyr pulled him aside to stop him, arguing that the Minotaur "is not very smart, and he will want to eat us." The party accepted the arrangement and quietly noted that they now had a very large, very hungry guard on their payroll.

Before they left the hall, Calmer began to reveal a troubling shift in his thinking. He asked where the Minotaur's food would come from. Sequoia suggested leftover bodies from the battles, and Zephyr said they would bring people into the temple. Calmer happily took it to mean sacrifices: "Excellent, I think that's a fantastic idea." Zinnia rolled a 20 on insight and sensed how strange that was from a cleric of St. Cuthbert, a fanatically lawful good god. Sequoia asked whether Calmer was feeling all right. Calmer reflected openly on his disillusionment with the church of St. Cuthbert. When he had died, he explained, it was not his order that came to save him. It was a tiefling, a spy with a scarred face, and a rogue who paid the price to bring him back, and Prefect Terjon of Hommlet had been willing to let him stay dead. That loyalty, he said, had put a very different spin on the religious doctrine he had been raised to follow. He insisted he was not yet an evil man. He did say the Earth Temple's teachings put a different perspective on the endless prattle of St. Cuthbert.

The party pressed on, passing a stairway leading up and the remains of a recent battle, now a pile of water and steam. The DM reminded them that the Greater Temple had forces deployed somewhere on this level. Its troops had appeared from all sorts of locations when the earlier battle ended. The party then discovered a series of storerooms that revealed just how well-supplied the temple truly was. One room held stacks of firewood near its entrance, planks and beams, and large lumpy sacks. A second room's lock was no match for Sequoia's thieves' tools; he rolled a 33. Inside was a remarkable cache of equipment: chainmail and plate armor, backpacks, holy water, lanterns, lockpicks, mirrors, parchment, pouches, ropes, sacks, spikes, and tinderboxes. The DM noted that being the big bad at the end of the game also meant handling supplies and boring things like equipment. Zephyr resolved to "get some other guys to do this" and to put somebody in charge of it.

Down the hall, Zephyr recalled the three prisoners still held nearby; the party had freed only one of the four earlier. Calmer observed that they had a feeding problem: the Minotaur needed food, and they had prisoners. That was two birds, one stone. "Freeing good people," he added, "is not exactly something we would be doing." Sequoia asked whether he was trying to get his cleric powers taken away. Calmer replied that at a certain level of power a cleric's power becomes intrinsic, and he assured them that he and St. Cuthbert had agreed his integration into the temple was part of the plan to defeat it. Even Frostbrand, the party's sentient sword, chimed in to remind Sequoia that their plan had always been to remain discreet. The DM warned out of character that the more days Calmer spent as boss, the more likely bad things would happen. Zephyr concluded that they should attack as soon as possible. Calmer then surveyed the rotting remains of what had once been a food storage area and declared it "absolutely unacceptable." He said they would have to talk to Lucius about cleaning it up, then settled on Dren Halveth and the Broken Blades to do the work; Zephyr assured him that Dren was "fond of manual labor." He then proposed assigning individual storerooms to each of the four elemental temples as part of the new management structure. He miscounted the temples and named the Water Temple twice. Sequoia jabbed that somebody didn't pass school, and Calmer blamed the St. Cuthbert educational system. When Sequoia asked how the Earth Temple would fix St. Cuthbert's schools, Calmer pulled out a scrap of scroll and began sketching a new educational system.

Their exploration was cut short when a massive, demented ghoul lurched out of the shadows of a corridor, moaning. It fixed its gaze on the living warmth of the party: "you are living. He is undead. This is an opportunity." Zinnia recognized that a necromancer had been at work. It was the same one responsible for the undead that had plagued the temple before, and he was now raising far more dangerous creatures. The battle was brutal. Sequoia went first, hiding around a corner and loosing a sneak-attack arrow for 29 damage. Zephyr followed with his pistol and his assassin's bonuses for 31. The ghoul sank its teeth into Calmer for 21 damage, and the cleric failed his save and crumpled, paralyzed by the creature's touch. Zinnia rolled a natural 20 to weather the creature's foul, poisonous stench and delivered blows of 19 and 21 damage. Sequoia and Zephyr both failed their saves against the stench and were poisoned. Sequoia lost his sneak attack to disadvantage but kept pressing the attack with his dagger. Zinnia finally put the ghoul down for good with two strikes of 9.

When the party searched the corpse, they found something unexpected: a note, addressed directly to them. It was written by a necromancer who called the temple his home and the party interlopers and thieves. He thanked them, with cold sarcasm, for the harvest of death they had left in their wake. Every corpse they had created was a resource he intended to use against them. The note went on to mock Calmer specifically, calling him a disgrace and unqualified to head the Earth Temple, claiming he could see through him, and declaring his approach to the rituals entirely wrong. Calmer read it aloud with an expression of mild offense ("my god, this person's busy"), then folded it carefully and tucked it away.

On the insight or investigation check the DM called for, Zinnia rolled a 21 and Zephyr a natural 20, and together they began to understand the shape of the threat. Zinnia remembered Romag muttering that the undead on the first level no longer reported to him, which implied they had once listened to someone else. Barkinar had once led the Earth Temple before being promoted to command the Greater Temple's troops below. When Romag had taken his place, someone else had apparently expected the position. Perhaps Romag never cleaned up his own mess; perhaps the necromancer escaped. Either way, that necromancer had been passed over and now viewed the party's occupation of the temple as a personal insult. He had been quietly raising the dead from the shadows, and the party's "dumpster fire of dead bodies" had only given him more material to work with. "We will have to be on our toes," Zephyr concluded. "We'll find this guy, somehow." As the DM put it, being the big bad at the end of the game is a little painful, because there are lots of other big bads.

The group's final discovery of the day came in an oddly shaped chamber bisected by a low stone wall holding back a deep pool of cold water. Water trickled in from the east, south, and west walls, and the overflow ran to floor drains. Zephyr recognized it as the water supply of the upper temple. Calmer observed that the last time they thought they had found a water supply, it turned out to be a creature from hell. Sequoia took a few steps back, having jumped into acidic pools one too many times. Zephyr stepped inside to investigate, rolled an 8 on investigation, and immediately slipped on the algae-slicked, sloping floor, sliding hard into the far wall. Calmer threw him a rope while Zinnia let Calmer handle it. Once steadied, Zephyr peeked over the wall and spotted a large, round object resting at the bottom of the pool beneath the slime. He tied the rope around himself, took a deep breath, and dove. His first sleight-of-hand attempt (11) failed, leaving the object dislodged just enough that a second miss would lose it entirely. On the second dive he rolled a 13, seized it, and wrenched it free just before his lungs gave out. Zinnia helped Calmer haul him back to the surface. When the slime was wiped away, the object revealed itself to be a finely crafted shield +1. Calmer accepted it with great satisfaction ("That was very well done."). As the party gathered themselves to move on, the Minotaur's earlier words drifted back to them. It had mentioned black-cloaked bandits attacking its territory, and now they understood that some of those bandits had not been alive at all. The Minotaur could eat the undead, but it prefers fresh meat.
