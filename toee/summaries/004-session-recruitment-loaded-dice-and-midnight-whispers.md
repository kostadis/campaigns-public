# Chapter 04 Recruitment, Loaded Dice, and Midnight Whispers

Date: 2024-12-14

## Summary

The party left the Moathouse and returned first to Thorne (the Duke) and the frogs. The Duke was surprised to see all four adventurers return alive so quickly, remarking that expeditions usually came back with one or two members dead. When he asked whether they had found anything interesting, the party—mindful that nearly everyone in the supposedly friendly village seemed to have a private agenda—claimed they had found nothing of interest.

Back in Hommlet, a series of meetings with the town’s various power brokers awaited them. Calmer, ever eager to cast himself in a heroic light, insisted on reporting first to Terjon, the stern chief cleric of the Church of St. Cuthbert. The party allowed Calmer to present the expedition in his own self-aggrandizing fashion. He declared the mission a success and dramatically produced the stone bearing a painted eye as proof that the Temple was “alive and well.” Terjon stared at the stone with barely concealed contempt, pointing out that all they had brought him was a rock with some ink on it and that they had failed to bring back their prisoner for questioning. He ordered Calmer out of his sight until he had something useful.

Terjon nevertheless wanted to retain the rock. Zephyr cleverly argued, “If he thinks it’s such a trivial rock, then I don’t—why would he be interested in it? We could just keep it.” Terjon paused, conceded, “You’re right. This is of no value,” and handed it back.

Barely out the door, the party was intercepted by members of the town guard and told that Burne and Rufus wished to see them. The two officials were waiting over drinks at the Inn of the Welcome Wench. Rufus wore his usual flowing magical robes, displaying far too much chest hair, while Burne greeted the party as his “most recently deputized troops.” Calmer excused himself to return to his church, pray, and rest.

Burne and Rufus listened carefully as the party described the bandits in the Moathouse and presented the eye stone. Then Rufus leaned forward with a revelation of his own: the keep’s architectural blueprints had briefly vanished. His architect knew nothing about their disappearance, and although the plans later returned, traces of charcoal dust suggested that someone had copied them. Rufus had moved the plans to a more private part of the tower, but he now suspected a saboteur within the work encampment. The eye rock proved nothing by itself, but it convinced him that greater caution was necessary. He asked the party to identify the spy, while Burne stressed that the investigation must remain “between the four of us, five of us.” The officials paid for everyone’s drinks before departing.

Ostler then approached the party and observed that Burne and Rufus apparently regarded them as good people. He wanted to know what Jaroo thought of them and hinted that, if the druid approved, he might have work of his own. He also explained the inn’s policy: adventurers paid three days in advance, and anyone missing for longer than three days was presumed dead, after which anything left in their room would be sold to the highest bidder.

The party made their way to Jaroo’s grove. The elderly druid examined the eye stone and praised their work, confirming that evil was indeed stirring in the Moathouse. Nevertheless, he insisted that the stone was not the evidence he needed: “You must go deeper into the Moathouse. You must find what is actually going on.” Before they left, Jaroo asked whom they had already told. Knowing that news traveled through Hommlet almost instantly, the party answered honestly. Jaroo’s expression softened with genuine approval. He declared himself pleased that they had not insulted his intelligence, invoked the blessings of Beory upon them, and presented them with a potion of healing.

Their next stop was the fortified estate of the Village Elder, a retired farmer whose home stood behind an eight-foot-high stone wall and heavy gate. The Elder lived there with his wife, four grown sons, two daughters-in-law, three grandchildren, two servant girls, and a hired farmhand. His sons and the farmhand served in the militia, and the household followed the Old Faith.

The Elder welcomed the party warmly, noting that his sons had seen them returning from the Moathouse, but he wasted little time making his expectations clear. Hommlet had both a militia and a ruling council, and henceforth all information concerning the Moathouse was to be presented to the entire council at once rather than parceled out to individual members. He listed the council’s members: himself, Jaroo, Terjon, Elmo’s father the militia captain, Ostler, the miller Mytch, Burne, and Rufus. He also teased Zinnia about how quickly the Church of St. Cuthbert had acquired a convert. Satisfied that everyone agreed to his reporting procedure, he clapped his hands and had a generous meal brought out, which the party accepted.

Back at the Welcome Wench, Ostler pulled the party into the kitchen with a conspiratorial air. He believed Furnok was cheating his customers and offered three nights of free lodging in exchange for any proof. Furnok possessed nicked cards and loaded dice. Zephyr moved in quietly to lift the evidence from Furnok’s person but came away empty-handed, though Furnok never noticed the attempt. Zephyr then made a table fall, creating a scene that drew Furnok’s attention. While Furnok stood and angrily demanded to know what Zephyr had done, Zinnia slipped in and palmed his dice. The moment he touched them, he could tell they were loaded.

The weighted dice were all the proof Ostler needed. He publicly accused Furnok of stealing from his customers and ordered him out. Furnok denied everything, but Elmo rose from his seat and warned him, “Furnok, just get the fuck out of here, man. You’re just like, this is just bad news. You don’t want me to get my dad and the militia.” Furnok protested that he was merely trying to make a living, prompting Ostler’s retort: “A dishonest living.” Furnok departed, and the party received three free nights, a modest discount on food, Ostler’s approval, and a useful new source of local information. Spugnoir watched the affair with a raised eyebrow, visibly impressed.

With Furnok gone, Elmo called the party over. Though he appeared to be something of a drunk, he proved friendly and was revealed to be a ranger. He praised them as “good guys,” while Zephyr answered, “We’re good guys when money’s involved, you know?” Elmo noticed that the group seemed one person short of the conventional four-member adventuring party and offered to accompany them on future hunts in the woods, including a possible expedition after the mysterious creature near the Moathouse.

The party also gathered two rumors. A farmer’s daughter named Lysia was eager to explore the ruined Moathouse but lacked both equipment and companions. They also heard that a secret passage beneath the inn led to extensive chambers belonging to a long-dead wizard. Spugnoir laughed at the second rumor and insisted that the only thing beneath the inn was “smelly, stinky food.” He then revealed that he did know the location of a genuine lost wizard’s laboratory near Emridy Meadows, where the forces of good had fought the Temple of Elemental Evil ten years earlier. He offered to guide the party and split the treasure, provided he received first choice of any arcane scrolls or spellbooks. The party accepted, though Spugnoir suggested waiting until morning because it was already late.

Later that evening, an apprentice to the local Braumeister approached the party with a worried look. On several nights during the previous week, he had seen two cloaked figures lurking behind the brewery around midnight. With rumors spreading that the Temple was not as dead as everyone believed, he feared that the figures might be spies.

The party agreed to investigate and hid among the trees near the brewery after dark. Two cloaked figures duly appeared behind the building. Zephyr crept closer but initially could not hear their conversation, forcing him to approach again while Zinnia watched for an ambush and Sequoia followed. The terrifying cult operatives proved to be two local teenagers, Marek and Althea, secretly making out. They spotted Zephyr and panicked. Marek begged the party not to tell their parents and explained that Althea’s father, Filliken, would never permit the relationship because the two teenagers belonged to different religions: one followed the Church of St. Cuthbert, while the other followed the Old Faith of Beory. The party advised them to be more discreet, and the two fled into the night.

The following morning, the party weighed their many open threads: the spy in the work encampment, Spugnoir’s lost wizard’s laboratory, the creature rumored to lurk in the Gnarley Forest, and the young dragon Airgid, who was still waiting to be taken home. They chose to honor their obligation to Airgid first and recruited Calmer to accompany them.

While planning the trip, the party briefly joked about making a dragon reward them before reconsidering the wisdom of second-level adventurers attempting to intimidate an adult dragon. The journey into the Kron Hills was remarkably uneventful. After roughly a day’s travel, Airgid caught sight of the distant Lortmil Mountains and cried, “Home! I can see home. I’m gonna go fly now, and I promise you, mommy’s gonna give you a really nice present.” Delighted, Airgid hugged everyone and gave each of them a slobbering, tongue-heavy kiss. The party called goodbye as she launched herself into the sky and flew toward her mountain home.

## Locations

### Hommlet

A seemingly friendly village where various factions and leaders maintain their own agendas and carefully control the flow of information. News travels so quickly that nearly everyone learns about a meeting within minutes. The arrival and growing influence of the Church of St. Cuthbert have also created tension with followers of the Old Faith.

### Inn of the Welcome Wench

The party’s base of operations and a bustling local tavern where they meet with Burne and Rufus, expose Furnok’s cheating, gather rumors, meet Elmo, and negotiate with Spugnoir. Adventurers must pay three days in advance; those absent for more than three days are presumed dead, and possessions left behind are auctioned. The party earned three nights of free lodging and a food discount by helping Ostler.

### The Moathouse

A ruined structure near Hommlet where the party previously discovered bandits, captured a prisoner, and found a stone bearing an eye symbol. Jaroo believes genuine evil is active there but requires the party to venture deeper and recover stronger evidence.

### The Grove

The peaceful residence of Jaroo the Druid, where the party reports on the Moathouse, receives spiritual guidance, and is rewarded for telling the truth. It is associated with the Old Faith and the worship of Beory.

### Village Elder's House

A prosperous, defensible residence surrounded by an eight-foot-high stone wall and secured by a heavy gate. It houses the Elder, his wife, their four grown sons, two daughters-in-law, three grandchildren, two servant girls, and a hired farmhand. The family follows the Old Faith, and the sons and farmhand serve in the militia.

### The Brewery

A local establishment in Hommlet where two cloaked figures were reported lurking around midnight. The party’s stakeout revealed not cult spies but Marek and Althea, two teenagers using the secluded area for secret meetings.

### Emridy Meadows

The site of a major battle ten years ago between the forces of good and the Temple of Elemental Evil. Spugnoir knows of a lost wizard’s laboratory nearby and has offered to guide the party there in exchange for first choice of arcane writings.

### Kron Hills

A region of rolling hills crossed by the party while escorting Airgid toward the Lortmil Mountains. Their roughly day-long journey through the region was remarkably uneventful.

### Lortmil Mountains

The mountainous region where Airgid’s home and mother are located. Airgid recognized the mountains from a distance, joyfully embraced the party, and flew the rest of the way alone.

## NPCs

### Terjon

The stern chief cleric of the Church of St. Cuthbert in Hommlet. He is unimpressed by Calmer’s dramatic presentation of the eye rock, condemns the failure to bring back a prisoner for questioning, and dismisses Calmer until he can produce something useful. Though he initially tries to keep the stone, Zephyr persuades him to return it.

### Burne

A magic user and member of the village council who, along with Rufus, has deputized the party to investigate threats to Hommlet. He greets them as his “most recently deputized troops,” helps commission the investigation into the copied plans, insists on secrecy, and with Rufus pays for the party’s drinks.

### Rufus

An associate of Burne and member of the village council. He wears flowing magical robes that display a conspicuous amount of chest hair. After discovering that the keep’s missing blueprints were returned with charcoal dust on them, he concludes that someone probably copied the plans and suspects a saboteur in the work encampment.

### Calmer

A cleric of St. Cuthbert who accompanies the party. He habitually frames events to make himself appear heroic, presenting the Moathouse expedition to Terjon as a triumphant success. Terjon is openly disgusted by his inadequate evidence. Calmer later accompanies the party when they escort Airgid toward home.

### Ostler

The innkeeper of the Welcome Wench and a member of the village council. He asks the party to prove Furnok is cheating and rewards them with three free nights and a food discount. He becomes a friendly source of information and possible future work after the party exposes the gambler.

### Jaroo

An elderly druid of the Old Faith who is pleased to see the party and repeatedly praises their work. He confirms that evil is present in the Moathouse but demands stronger evidence from deeper inside. When the party honestly identifies everyone it has already consulted, he praises them for not insulting his intelligence, invokes Beory’s blessing, and gives them a potion of healing.

### The Village Elder

A wise retired farmer who heads Hommlet’s ruling council and lives with his large family in a fortified estate. He expects the party to report all future Moathouse intelligence to the complete council at once. He follows the Old Faith, has heard positive reports from Jaroo, and hosts the party for a generous meal after securing its agreement.

### Elmo

A local man who appears disheveled and somewhat drunk but is also a ranger. He helps drive Furnok from the Welcome Wench by threatening to summon his father and the militia. He approves of the party, shares Zephyr’s pragmatic attitude toward paid heroism, and offers to serve as a fourth adventurer on future hunts in the woods.

### Furnok

A rogue and dishonest gambler who uses loaded dice and nicked cards to swindle patrons at the Welcome Wench. After Zinnia steals his loaded dice, Ostler exposes him publicly and Elmo threatens to involve the militia. Furnok protests that he was only trying to make a living before being driven from the inn.

### Spugnoir

A self-important but knowledgeable aspiring wizard staying at the Welcome Wench. He dismisses rumors of a magical complex beneath the inn and complains that nobody pays attention when he speaks. He does know the location of a lost wizard’s laboratory near Emridy Meadows and proposes an expedition in which the treasure will be shared, provided he receives first choice of arcane scrolls and spellbooks.

### Marek

A local teenager caught meeting Althea behind the brewery at night. Terrified when Zephyr discovers them, he begs the party not to tell their parents. He explains that Althea’s father would oppose their relationship because the teenagers come from different religions.

### Althea

Filliken’s daughter, who meets Marek secretly behind the brewery. Their relationship is forbidden because one belongs to the Church of St. Cuthbert and the other follows the Old Faith of Beory. She and Marek flee after the party advises them to be more discreet.

### Airgid

A young dragon the party has been protecting. She is playful and intensely affectionate. Upon seeing the Lortmil Mountains, she excitedly recognizes her home, promises that her mother will give the party a very nice present, hugs everyone, delivers a series of slobbering kisses, and flies away.

## Items

### Eye Rock

A simple stone with an eye painted on it in ink, found in the Moathouse. Calmer presents it as proof that the Temple is active, while Terjon dismisses it as no more than “one rock with some ink.” Jaroo agrees that it is insufficient evidence, though both its existence and the party’s report concern Burne and Rufus. Zephyr persuades Terjon to return it by arguing that something so trivial should be of no interest to him.

### Keep Blueprints

Architectural plans for the keep that mysteriously disappeared and later returned. Rufus’s architect did not know where they had gone, and charcoal residue on the returned pages suggests that someone copied them. Rufus moved the plans to a more private location and tasked the party with identifying a suspected spy in the work encampment.

### Potion of Healing

A magical draught gifted by Jaroo after the party honestly tells him whom it has already consulted. He presents it as a reward both for the party’s service to the village and for refusing to insult his intelligence with a lie. Zinnia carries it in his inventory.

### Loaded Dice

A pair of weighted gaming pieces used by Furnok to cheat customers at the Welcome Wench. Zinnia steals them while Furnok is distracted by Zephyr’s fallen-table ruse and recognizes their altered weight by touch. The dice provide the evidence Ostler needs to banish Furnok.
