# Chapter 08 A Deal with Lareth, a Deal with Rufus, and Sheernobb

Date: 2025-03-30

## Summary

The party stood in the office of Lareth the Beautiful, a man of almost unsettling physical perfection whose charm barely concealed the darkness beneath it. He tasked them with traveling to the gnome settlement of Sheernobb, where a group of bandits had been preying on travelers and had stolen a valuable art chest. He instructed them to retrieve the chest, eliminate the bandits, and — perhaps most pointedly — to keep the entire affair secret from anyone outside the room. He also prevented his guard sergeant from accompanying them, explaining that the party were professionals and an escort would only get in their way. Though Zephyr's insight told him Lareth was withholding something, the party agreed to the mission and resolved to remain especially vigilant.

Emerging from the Moathouse, the party found the Duke still waiting by a campfire, growing cold and impatient. His first reaction was, “Oh, you're alive,” followed by awkward assurances that he was not sorry to see them. He pressed them for evidence of cult activity or treasure, but the party played it carefully, claiming they had found signs of brigands, torture chambers, zombies, and goblins but nothing connecting the ruins to a larger temple plot. With the Duke none the wiser, they made their way back to the village of Hommlet to report their findings to more trusted allies.

At the tower of Burne and Rufus, the party laid out what they had discovered. The two pressed them to distinguish genuine servants of the Temple of Elemental Evil from impostors using its reputation as cover. One of them explained that the temple had been a profoundly destabilizing force a decade earlier, prompting Verbobonc to extend its military presence into the Kron Hills and creating tensions with neighboring powers. In his view, enemies of Verbobonc might invoke the temple to undermine treaties and regional stability.

When the stolen art chest was mentioned, Zinnia quietly pieced together the truth: the chest had been crafted as a diplomatic gift from the Viscounty of Verbobonc, intended for the isolationist elven Kingdom of Celene as part of a long-running effort to open trade relations. Celene's borders were so tightly controlled that non-elves were normally forbidden entry, confined to border trading posts, or blindfolded and put to sleep before being transported to approved meeting places. Zinnia kept his deductions to himself rather than revealing his awareness of government affairs.

The party trusted Burne and Rufus enough to admit that they had met Lareth. Rather than condemning them, the two leaders were delighted by the arrangement. By posing as Lareth's friends, the adventurers had induced him to pay them to attack a rival bandit faction. Any resulting bloodshed would appear to be infighting rather than an operation by Verbobonc, weakening the brigands without costing the government money or exposing its involvement.

The party then visited the druid Jaroo, who launched into a passionate and lengthy tirade about the imperialist tendencies of the Viscounty, its disregard for the natural order, and the missionary zeal of the Church of St. Cuthbert. He directed much of his disapproval toward Zinnia, whose conversion to St. Cuthbert made the lecture feel distinctly personal. Between his complaints, he filled in the history of the chest: it had been selected at a grand art festival as the most profoundly beautiful and natural work the Viscounty could produce, then sent toward Celene as an offering. Its disappearance on the road had become a considerable embarrassment. Jaroo suggested that the wisest course would be to recover the chest and present it to the elves directly on behalf of the druids of the region, an outcome that would redirect any resulting prestige away from the Viscount.

The party chose Calmer over Spugnoir as their companion. The following morning, Calmer arrived at the Inn of the Welcome Wench loudly announcing their supposedly secret journey to Sheernobb, complete with conspicuous winks. Zinnia quickly recognized that Calmer was the sort of person who might walk into a covert operation and proudly announce that he was there to betray everyone. Ostler Gundigoot also stopped the party before departure to ask whether he should hold their rooms or rent them to someone else. They released the rooms, and Ostler warned them not to complain if no fine accommodations remained upon their return — though his gestures suggested that extra money might solve the problem.

The party made the one-day journey to Sheernobb and chose to present themselves as swords for hire: armed adventurers looking for problems requiring the pointy end of a blade. At the village gates, gnome guards halted them and demanded their business. While they were stopped, Zinnia shared what his elven sources had taught him: the Kingdom of Celene views such gifted art pieces as horrible and unfinished — by elven standards, anything taking less than a hundred years to make is incomplete — and the pieces it accepts are taken only to be burned, though the elves do appreciate the effort behind them. Whether that intelligence represented the whole truth, Zinnia could not say.

The guards found Zephyr's appearance especially perplexing. Zephyr insisted he was actually human and merely wore a fake prosthetic. The guard deadpanned an explanation for him — a polymorph spell, or the wrong potion drunk by his mother at his birth — which Zephyr eagerly accepted. Calling it a likely story but choosing to believe it, the guard waved them through. He recommended the Honey Haven Inn for humans and elves, noted that Sequoia was of a more proper stature than his “freakish” oversized companions, and directed anyone seeking authority to Headgnome Glennddarc at the Moot Hall.

The Honey Haven Inn proved to be a warm and whimsical establishment, its green awnings and ivy surrounding a hand-painted sign of a bee perched on a flower. Inside, gnome-sized elmwood furniture with horsehair cushions crowded the room. Fireflies glowed inside hanging glass globes, bright tapestries covered the walls, logs crackled in an oversized fireplace, and the air smelled of roasting potatoes and fresh ale. The gnome at the bar, Mari Clee, greeted the newcomers with a cheerful smile — pausing only to pull a live hedgehog out of her drinking mug and pat it on its way before offering rooms, refreshments, bathing, and laundry.

Zephyr declared himself the group's leader, secured a private room for himself while leaving the others in shared accommodations, and condescendingly ordered porridge for “the peasants” while requesting chicken for himself. Mari handed him a menu and challenged his manners. When Calmer selected the expensive Moonbeam Pie, Zephyr tried to stop him on the authority of being leader, but the others insisted Calmer could spend his own money. Zephyr ultimately paid for everyone's food, announcing that a leader provided for his poor citizens. Mari made it clear that the inn extended no tabs: payment was cash up front.

A gnome bard named Gurdy soon sidled up to the table, trading information about the town's bandit troubles for a mug of mead. He explained that raids had grown increasingly brazen over two years and were disrupting local trade. The gnomes preferred defending their villages and farms to pursuing the brigands, while previous attempts to investigate had ended disastrously. Gurdy mentioned a gnome who had vanished while investigating and then reappeared weeks or months later — the same prisoner the party had previously rescued. He recommended consulting the Moot Hall for further intelligence and offered to accompany the party with inspirational music and a quick blade in exchange for a proper share of the treasure.

Calmer rejected Gurdy's proposal immediately, declaring the group full and the bard unwelcome. When Zephyr suggested they might reconsider, Calmer became anxious that he was already being replaced. Zephyr tried to reassure him by describing Calmer as part of the “starting five” and Gurdy as a possible second-team member, prompting Calmer to insist that he was a valiant and courageous adventurer.

Before the night was out, the party had accumulated a small pile of local quests. Glennon, the inn's owner, asked them to find barrels of mead stolen from the cellar, with suspicious tracks leading into the forest; the reward would be a week of free lodging and a small keg of enchanted Honey Haven mead. The tavern cook, Quinna Poul, asked them to hunt a rare, elusive, and dangerous gnarled boar from the Gnarley Forest for the annual harvest festival. Her promised feast would grant those who ate it five additional hit points for a day.

Captain Gnonsbim of the local militia then formally hired the party to locate the coordinated brigands' camp somewhere northeast of Sheernobb — perhaps in the Gnarley Forest or nearer the fens by Hommlet — eliminate or capture its leader, and bring back proof. He offered 25 gold for the camp's location, 10 gold for every bandit captured or killed, 50 gold for the leader, and a custom-forged piece of equipment from local artisans if they brought the leader back. He also recommended interviewing merchants and survivors or examining the site of a recent attack before setting out. His definition of useful proof was direct: “proof, evidence, violence, dead bandits.”

The captain quietly admitted that he was up for re-election. To receive the custom equipment, the party would need to endure a public speech and agree that the victory had been made possible by his leadership. Once the deal was struck, he bought them another round of Honey Haven mead. Zinnia drank despite already feeling tipsy, while Calmer — whose religious upbringing had exposed him only to poor beer — eagerly reached for his own. Sequoia warned him that drinking so much unfamiliar alcohol was unwise. Captain Gnonsbim agreed and had Calmer's portion set aside in a special barrel for later. The session ended with the party planning to investigate the missing mead barrels first, carrying a growing list of obligations into a very busy tomorrow.

## Locations

### Lareth's Office

A room within the Moathouse dungeon where the party negotiates with Lareth the Beautiful and receives their mission. Lareth dismisses the possibility of sending his guard sergeant with them and insists that the assignment remain confined to those already present.

### The Moathouse

A ruined site where the party exits the dungeon and finds the Duke waiting by a campfire outside. The DM describes its torture rooms as holding zombies and goblins. Beyond them, secret doors lead to Lareth's operation, which the party conceals from the Duke.

### Hommlet

The village where the party returns to report their progress and gather intelligence from local leaders including Burne, Rufus, and the druid Jaroo. The party's return attracts notice, giving them a choice between confiding in local authorities or allowing the village to speculate.

### Burne and Rufus's Tower

The headquarters of the local militia leaders in Hommlet where the party discusses the political implications of the stolen chest and their mission. Burne and Rufus interpret the arrangement with Lareth as an opportunity to weaken hostile factions without visible government intervention.

### Inn of the Welcome Wench

A local establishment in Hommlet run by Ostler Gundigoot where the party maintains their lodgings before departing for Sheernobb. Rooms are rented by the fortnight, and the party allows Ostler to rent theirs to other guests during the journey.

### Kron Hills

A regional area known as gnome territory, home to the town of Sheernobb and the surrounding forests and fens. Verbobonc extended its military influence into the region following the rise of the Temple of Elemental Evil, creating political friction with neighboring peoples and governments.

### Kingdom of Celene

An isolationist elven kingdom with closed borders. The stolen art chest was originally being sent there as a diplomatic gift from the Viscounty of Verbobonc. Non-elves generally cannot enter, and even approved visitors may be restricted to border trading posts or blindfolded and put to sleep before transportation into the kingdom. According to Zinnia's sources, Celene regards artwork created in less than a century as unfinished and may burn the diplomatic gifts it accepts.

### Sheernobb

A charming gnome settlement nestled within the lush greenery of the Kron Hills, known for its burrow homes with intricately carved doors and the famous Honey Haven mead. Dense forest surrounds its winding approach, while the village air carries the scents of earth, roasted potatoes, and fresh ale. Guards closely monitor newcomers.

### Honey Haven Inn

A cozy, gnome-sized tavern in Sheernobb adorned with green awnings and flowering ivy. Its exterior sign shows a hand-painted bee perched on a flower. Inside are elmwood furnishings, horsehair cushions, woven tapestries, an oversized fireplace, and hanging glass globes illuminated by freely housed fireflies. The inn is famous for its locally cultivated Honey Haven mead. Glennon owns the establishment, while Mari Clee works the bar.

### Sheernobb Moot Hall

The largest mound in the village of Sheernobb, serving as the administrative center where Headgnome Glennddarc can be found. The guards and Gurdy recommend it as the best place to seek official intelligence about the bandits.

### Gnarley Forest

A nearby woodland area rumored to be the home of a rare gnarled boar and a potential hiding place for the bandits plaguing the region.

He treats Sequoia as sensibly proportioned and the taller party members as freakish giants.

### Quinna Poul

The enthusiastic cook of the Honey Haven Inn, preparing for Sheernobb's annual harvest festival. She asks the party to hunt a rare, dangerous, and elusive gnarled boar in the Gnarley Forest. If successful, she promises to prepare an extraordinary feast that grants five additional hit points for a day.

## Items

### Stolen Art Chest

A profoundly beautiful piece of art chosen as the finest and most natural work at a grand art festival held in the Viscounty of Verbobonc. It was intended as a diplomatic gift to the Kingdom of Celene to improve relations with the isolationist elves, but was stolen by brigands along the way, causing significant political embarrassment. Acceptance would ordinarily bring prestige to its artist. Lareth the Beautiful has tasked the party with retrieving it, while Jaroo suggests giving it directly to the elves on behalf of the druids. According to Zinnia's elven sources, Celene may regard the chest as an unfinished monstrosity and burn it if accepted.

### Honey Haven Mead

A famous and exclusive brew cultivated by the gnomes of Sheernobb and served at the Honey Haven Inn. It is potent enough that a second round leaves drinkers tipsy. Zinnia continues drinking, while Sequoia and Captain Gnonsbim prevent the inexperienced Calmer from overindulging. Glennon offers a small keg of enchanted Honey Haven mead as part of the reward for recovering the stolen barrels.

### Moonbeam Pie

A specialized and somewhat expensive dish served at the Honey Haven Inn. Calmer orders it enthusiastically because he is hungry and finally has money to spend, despite Zephyr's attempt to forbid the expense.

### Stolen Mead Barrels

Several barrels missing from the Honey Haven Inn's cellar. Suspicious tracks lead from the cellar into the forest, and the party chooses their recovery as its first local assignment.

### Custom-Forged Equipment

A piece of equipment to be crafted by Sheernobb's local artisans and ceremonially presented by Captain Gnonsbim if the party brings back the bandit leader or equivalent proof. Claiming it requires enduring a campaign speech and publicly crediting Gnonsbim's leadership.
