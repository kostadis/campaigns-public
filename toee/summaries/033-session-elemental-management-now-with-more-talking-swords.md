# Chapter 33 Elemental Management: Now With More Talking Swords

Date: 2026-06-14

## Summary
Before play resumed, the DM and Sequoia's player found out that Sequoia, an Arcane Trickster, had barely been using spells. The DM remarked: "It says a lot about this adventure and its paranoia, that using spells was not one of the things we wanted to do." Sequoia set up his spells and took Silvery Barbs, Sleep, and Detect Magic. The DM also announced that he had "finally read the Nick rules." The weapon mastery does not grant a third attack. It moves the light-weapon attack from the bonus action into the Attack action, which frees the bonus action for something like Disengage. He said the table would not change how it had been playing retroactively, but players should know the rule. The DM then read the previous session's recap, and the fight resumed with the salamanders acting first.

The battle against the fire salamanders raged on within the heart of the Fire Temple's central pit. One of the serpentine creatures coiled around Zephyr with terrifying speed. Zephyr failed the Strength save by a single point (14 against a needed 15), and the constriction left him restrained and grappled. With advantage, the salamander drove its flaming spear into him with devastating force. Zephyr took 18 from the constriction and 21 from the spear and its fire, 39 points in total. Though Zephyr's quick instincts let him deflect the worst of the physical blow, the searing heat that washed over him was inescapable. Uncanny Dodge applied only to the attack and not to the fire, and he ended up taking 33.

The second salamander sized up Sequoia, then turned its ranged weapon on Calmer, whose frost shield was still up. Both strikes sailed wide.

Sequoia carved into a salamander with a series of precise cuts and then wisely pulled back from the front lines. The DM had coached him to open with his Vex weapon so the follow-up attack would have advantage and sneak attack. His first swing was a critical miss, but he rerolled it with Lucky and hit for 9, then landed a second blow for 19 before disengaging. Zinnia then charged across the chamber and dealt a killing blow of 11 to one of the wounded creatures. At the DM's prodding ("You don't want to attack the other guy who's fully healed?"), he pivoted to strike another for 8.

Calmer was already holding a concentration spell, so he cast Toll the Dead, but the salamander made its Wisdom save. Then, just as the party braced for another brutal exchange, the last remaining salamander did something wholly unexpected. It knelt, lowered its weapon, and waited.

None of the party spoke the creature's language, so the parley had to be conducted "the miming way." Sequoia led an Insight/Investigation check with Calmer's help and rolled an 18. Through careful observation and intuition, the party came to understand that the creature had no desire to die for a cause already lost and might be open to an arrangement. It was a fire salamander named Vurakhal, and it recognized the party as the new masters of the Fire Temple.

Zephyr communicated through gesture and expression that the party now ruled the temple ("I want to tell them that we are the new kings here"), and Vurakhal acknowledged it, stood, and pointed to the fire pit. A second reading, a 22 with help, made his meaning clear: the treasure in the pit belonged to the new bosses.

The salamander then planted his spear point-down and produced a magical contract. Its text shifted into a readable language the moment it was touched, and it offered to transfer his loyalty to the party in exchange for continued service as the temple's guardian. As a tiefling, Zephyr recognized the boilerplate. He read the document carefully and struck out several clauses that would have claimed pieces of their souls; under those clauses, Vurakhal would also have owned a part of the signer. Vurakhal simply shrugged at the edits. He wasn't trying to pull a fast one; "he is what he is." The amended terms were accepted. Zephyr cut himself to sign in blood ("always, you know, in style," per the DM).

Vurakhal agreed to guard the Fire Temple against intruders and to fight anyone from the Water Temple still alive. He hinted that, if the business relationship proved fruitful, he had knowledge about the Greater Temple and "what really is going on" that he would be willing to share. When Zephyr moved to shake his hand, the DM warned him that it would cost 2d6 damage.

Calmer voiced quiet concern about forging alliances with elemental evil creatures while maintaining their cover. Zinnia, drawing on his deep experience as a spy, warned him plainly that he was going in too deep and reminded him that he was not a real Earth Temple cleric. Calmer took the caution to heart, telling Zinnia, "You are a professional, and I respect professionals." At the end of the session, the DM awarded Zinnia an Inspiration point for playing his spy background so well in this exchange.

With the battle concluded and an unlikely ally secured, the party turned their attention to a trapped chest hidden within the fire pit itself. First, Calmer bristled at Zephyr giving orders and asked that he now be called "the Prophet Calmer." Sequoia, with a +11 Sleight of Hand that came up as a 26, carefully worked the lock and narrowly avoided a blade that sliced through the air as the chest sprang open.

Inside, they found two vials of fire-resistant potion, a ring imbued with similar protective magic, and a short sword that was, impossibly, cold to the touch despite having been stored inside a fire pit. The DM noted this was why the house was called "the Gygax House": the treasure of the monster you're trying to kill holds the things you needed to kill it. Sequoia, holding the party's best Arcana bonus at +3, examined the blade and noticed only the unnatural cold.

The party regarded the sword with deep suspicion. Sequoia asked about using a glove, then about using sticks, and finally flipped the whole chest over to dump its contents onto the floor. Rather than risk touching the sword directly, Sequoia conjured a spectral hand to lift it. The sword responded by letting out an audible cough.

What followed was one of the stranger conversations the party had experienced in their time beneath the Temple of Elemental Evil. The sword introduced itself as Frostbrand, a sentient weapon of considerable power and even more considerable opinions. It immediately complained about how deeply unpleasant it was to have been locked in a box.

Frostbrand declared that it could only be wielded by those of good alignment and surveyed the party with an almost imperious air. It pointed itself at Zephyr (neutral) and said "no." It deemed chaotic good Zinnia and neutral good Sequoia possibilities, and it pronounced lawful good Calmer "exactly what I was looking for." In the end, it settled on Sequoia as a worthy bearer.

Frostbrand then revealed that it had been held prisoner since at least the Battle of Emridy Meadows, taken as a trophy, with Vurakhal serving as its jailor. The revelation prompted the sword to demand satisfaction from the salamander at once. The two exchanged heated words in the salamander tongue before a grudging peace was brokered, largely thanks to Zephyr's persuasive intervention ("All is forgiven"), which reached a 24 with Sequoia's help.

The sword also recognized Calmer as one of St. Cuthbert's and was scandalised to learn he was "pretending to be a member of the Earth Temple." Zinnia took a moment to explain the party's deep undercover mission to the sword, keeping the details vague. Frostbrand found the plan genuinely admirable ("Well, I like that! Yes, that is an excellent plan!"). Zephyr could not use the sword because he was not good, and Zinnia did not really use weapons. So Sequoia claimed the blade, and the newly freed weapon declared, with great enthusiasm, that together they would sneak through the shadows and defeat the evil of the Temple of Elemental Evil. Vurakhal looked thoroughly bored and rolled his eyes. Zephyr ordered him to protect the temple ("I expect loyalty"). As they left, the sword muttered an insult, Vurakhal slapped at it, and the two cursed each other.

The party pressed further into the Fire Temple through a secret door to the north, which Zephyr opened, to Calmer's satisfaction at seeing him take the lead. There they discovered a lavishly decorated chamber dominated by a great lacquered wooden throne in red and gilt, with a gold skull on its armrests. Seated in it was a tired, wounded man: Varek Solain, the former commander of the Fire Temple's forces.

Zephyr opened gently, promising no harm and even offering healing. The commander recognized the party immediately as the Obsidian Edge, the interlopers who had killed Alrrem, destroyed his temple, and massacred his forces, and he asked whether they had come to finish the job. Zephyr said yes. The commander drew his sword, vowing not to go down without a fight, and Zephyr at once said he had lied. He assured the commander that no harm was intended, that a new order had simply taken hold, and that he could go on being a Fire Temple soldier so long as he didn't interfere.

Calmer formally declared himself the new Chief Cleric, introduced the others as "my loyal henchmen," and informed the commander that he now answered to Dren Halveth, Supreme Commander of the Earth Temple Forces. Zinnia and Sequoia were content to let the man keep his gold. Zephyr asked for tribute, and when Calmer scoffed "just two?", Zephyr explained, "What I demand is not money, but respect." The bewildered man, outnumbered and outmaneuvered, paid a small tribute of two gold coins for his life. When Zephyr told him "I will not forget this," he answered, "Nor will I."

Deeper still, the party entered a chamber draped in scarlet and gold, its ceiling painted with scenes of demons and humans in lewd revelry. There they found Maeris Dorn, a fire cleric, frantically looting the room before making her escape. Zephyr's opening line, "Why are you fleeing?", did not land. The cleric turned on them with fury, her grief over the destruction of her temple written plainly across her face.

Rather than draw weapons, the party took Zephyr's suggestion ("Let's all go hug him [sic] together to surprise him [sic]"). They surprised her entirely by physically swarming her in a group grapple and holding her in place until she had no choice but to listen. Zephyr rolled a natural 20, Zinnia followed with a 21, and Sequoia piled on. Calmer refused to grapple members of the Fire Temple.

Zephyr offered her a simple choice: leave the temple freely, or stay and serve. Calmer helpfully added death as a third option and was startled when Zephyr refused it ("I thought you were an assassin!"). Calmer also insisted that any who remained must acknowledge the supremacy of the Earth Temple over the Fire Temple. The cleric looked at the three people surrounding her and the dagger beside her throat. After a long pause, she admitted that she had never particularly liked Alrrem anyway and accepted the terms. She confirmed that no other fire clerics survived. The party appointed her as the new head cleric of the Fire Temple and sent her off to find the captain of the Fire Guard, organize the remaining troops, and restore order.

Calmer, "feeling particularly emboldened," took stock of their gains. They had a loyal new captain, the Fire Temple was subordinate to Earth, and its cleric had acknowledged their power. "What more could we ask for?" he asked, then added, "one of the most important things in power is to recognize when you have it." After a short rest, the party made their way to the Water Temple.

The Water Temple was a grand hall of polished azurite and malachite that shimmered with a soft, eerie luminosity, as though the entire chamber lay fathoms beneath the sea. A hideous bronze fish-head relief poured a constant stream of water into a series of four tiered basins along the western wall. The largest basin never overflowed, which implied a drain somewhere. At the center of the hall stood a round bronze altar, four feet high and ten feet across, its shallow basin filled with coins, gems, and seashells. An eight-foot serpentine stone idol of writhing marine creatures loomed in an alcove to the south, with a curtain behind it, and four stone gargoyles perched atop pillars throughout the chamber.

Zephyr moved to investigate the great bronze sheet on the wall and knocked on it to see whether it was real. The idol began to rotate, and the gargoyles shuddered to life, their stone eyes fixing on the intruders with predatory hunger. The DM explained the party had "improperly touched" the temple. Zephyr's first thought was to flee. Calmer, rather than retreat, insisted on staying for the treasure ("This is what we're here for! I want to plunder!") and declared with relish that he wanted every last one of them dead. He also took the chance to needle Zephyr: at least *he* had only poured water, "but you had to go poke everything with your stick."

The party surged into action. Zephyr and Sequoia both rolled 27 for initiative, while Zinnia rolled a 5, worse even than Calmer. Zephyr struck with the speed and precision of a trained assassin, landing a 26-point sneak attack that included his assassin's +8. Sequoia delivered a series of powerful blows, 21 on his first strike, before pulling back to safety. Calmer called upon a 4th-level host of divine spirits that swirled around him like a storm, instantly destroying one gargoyle and leaving two others badly wounded and sluggish with 21 and 20 damage. As the spirits continued to batter the remaining creatures, the session ended mid-fight, the Water Temple's treasures still waiting to be claimed.
