The party's escape from the Hall of Verdigris was a harrowing affair. A triton's signal had cut across the water, a desperate plea that came with a warning: a kraken would be loosed if no rescue came. Zephyr did not stop to question it. He took the bait of that mental suggestion and threw himself into what he believed was a pool of water. A sentient, acidic intelligence seized him and locked him in place as acrid smoke curled off the surface.

Sequoia tried to lash a rope around his waist to haul Zephyr free. He stood only three feet tall against a four-foot-deep pool, so the effort was doomed from the start, and Calmer was quick and unsympathetic in pointing this out. Zinnia ultimately took up the rope, tied it off and leapt in without hesitation. The water made no grab for him. He dragged Zephyr out alive, though coated head to foot in acid.

Shaken, the group turned to leave. Brother Eelrich named the place unwelcoming and said as much, though he agreed to follow Brother Calmer's lead. Calmer looked to Zephyr, who told them plainly to get out. Sequoia, sure the danger was over and not to be outdone in poor decisions, jumped in a second time. He realized his mistake only once the acid was burning him, and scrambled clear.

Calmer pieced together the most unsettling truth of all: the thing in the water could plant suggestions in their minds. The party fell back behind a curtain just outside the hall, and as they caught their breath, their thoughts turned quickly from survival to opportunity. Brother Eelrich Vane's chambers awaited, now claimed in the name of the Prophet of Earth, and the party wasted no time:

- From a desk they recovered a collection of powerful scrolls.
- From locked chests in the bedroom they pried free ritual texts on elemental water, a ceremonial bronze mace, a gold ring set with pearl, and potions of water breathing.

As they leafed through the ritual texts, an unsettling suspicion took root. The writings hinted strongly that Vane had known all along about the sentient, living nature of the water elemental, and had said nothing. Before leaving, the party made their position crystal clear to the defeated priest: the next surprise found in these halls would be meant for him.

The party returned to their lair on the first level to rest and recover, and Calmer took the opportunity to formalize the new order:

- **Zinnia:** head of all intelligence operations.
- **Zephyr:** chief of day-to-day operations.
- **Sequoia:** responsible for ensuring valuables found their way into the right hands.
- **Calmer himself:** spiritual matters of the elemental gods. Petty operational details were entirely beneath him, a declaration delivered with the full gravity of a man who had just forced a priest to call him Lord Master.

A knock at the door soon interrupted their rest. Dren reported the arrival of a soft-looking grey man who smiled far too much and claimed to have been sent by Barkinar. The man had laughed when Hessa Claymoor, one of Dren's Broken Blades, patted him down for weapons. Dren found that deeply suspicious, though he admitted the visitor appeared harmless enough.

The man introduced himself as Lucius Graeme: a facilitator, a middleman, a carrier of things from clever people to clever people. By his own account, the new management was the cleverest thing to happen to the temple in a year.

He arrived bearing gifts. He produced a cask of genuinely excellent wine and presented each member of the household with a potent healing potion, all courtesy of Barkinar and the temple's new supply chain. When the party asked about magical wares, Lucius snapped his fingers and had Dren bring in a chest of enchanted goods. Dren had nervously warned that some chests had a habit of turning into mimics. The chest held an assortment of items available for purchase.

Sequoia and Zephyr each walked away with finely enchanted blades. The items that truly captured the party's imagination, however, carried price tags that dwarfed everything they had accumulated. Lucius offered to hold both of those items in reserve for a deposit. The party declined, and was left instead with a powerful and very practical motivation to plunder every last secret the temple had to offer.

## Summary

Before play resumed, the DM distributed magic items that had been overlooked. Calmer claimed a Ring of Free Action. Sequoia's player agreed: "We don't want our hero getting paralyzed." Zinnia confirmed he had a Ring of Protection and attuned to it. A Mace of Smiting and a suit of Half Plate +1 went into the party inventory. The party, now level 9, had just finished a long rest.

The DM framed the situation plainly: it is good to be the big bad at the end of the game, because people show up with magic items and you just pay for them. The problem was that the party did not have enough money.

Before setting out, Dren raised the matter of the trolls still lurking somewhere in the temple's depths. He was decidedly not equipped to handle them. Picking at something with a stiletto, he explained that a troll regenerates, is very strong, and is "a pain in the ass." He added that trolls need a steady supply of flesh, which the party could not easily provide. Zephyr suggested they simply kill them, and Dren agreed that he didn't trust trolls either.

On a 16, Zephyr realized that regeneration was the key and asked how to deal with it. The only reliable way to put a troll down for good, Dren said, was fire. Zephyr compared the trolls to the Hydra of Greek myth. Dren dismissed that as "some abyssal stuff I don't understand." He then reminded them that they already had an ally who fought with fire, their salamander, and the party went to the second floor to pay him a visit.

Vurakhal does not speak Common, so he communicated through Frostbrand, Sequoia's sentient short sword +1 and a committed foe of the Temple of Elemental Evil. Frostbrand was dismayed to be back among the forces of fire. Sequoia pointed out that you can't simply stop going to places you own. Frostbrand then decided it was all part of a master plan to overthrow the temple from inside, and asked whether he should "put on my evil face." When he declared himself "Frostbrand, the evil sword" as part of the party's cover story, the performance landed with all the menace of a damp cloth.

Vurakhal agreed to join the hunt as soon as Zephyr asked. He first wanted to be clear about one thing: did he work for the Fire Temple's new leadership or for the party? The arrangement they settled on was that he works for Zephyr but reports temporarily to the Fire Temple. Vurakhal was therefore relieved to learn that he did not have to tell the Fire Temple about this job.

Captain Varek Solain, who seemed to have been listening at the door, arrived and supplied the trolls' history and their location.

On their way to the troll lair, the party passed through a southwestern chamber. There they came face to face with an owlbear that was very large, bigger than they were used to, very hungry, and very chained. Sequoia offered it food, and Zinnia approached with calm confidence. With a second helping of food and an animal-handling roll of 19, the creature's fury gradually subsided into wary tolerance.

Sequoia drew on hard-won experience from his days as a rogue: owlbears make lousy pets unless you get them young, and this one looked like the meanest of the nastiest. Releasing it would be an unpredictable gamble at best. The party left it chained and moved on, Sequoia openly relieved to avoid "another owlbear pet situation."

Calmer declined to scout, delegating the job to his operational subordinates. Sequoia and Zephyr crept ahead to the troll lair. They found two of the creatures, dressed in colorful tribal garb, feasting on the remains of fallen soldiers. The soldiers might have been Fire Temple or Earth Temple; it was impossible to tell. The party launched a devastating surprise assault, with Sequoia and Zephyr striking hard and fast before disengaging.

The trolls proved their reputation. One of them manifested grotesque additional limbs and made four attacks against Sequoia, dealing 52 of his 66 hit points. Vurakhal strode into the fray bellowing "THIS IS WHAT I WAS BUILT FOR!" His searing heat aura cut off the trolls' ability to regenerate, and he drove his flaming spear through one of them with lethal efficiency. Zinnia battered the survivor down to its last few hit points. It turned on the salamander and hit him three times.

Calmer attempted to mend Sequoia's wounds with divine healing, Sequoia finished off the second troll, and Calmer tried again. The two attempts restored 14 and then 15 hit points, results so underwhelming that the party could barely contain their disbelief. The two trolls carried nothing of value; their chieftain had presumably taken everything.

Pressing east through the door beyond the troll lair, the party discovered a chamber that could only be described as a monument to depravity:

- walls plastered with excrement and bones, arranged into patterns and shelf-like ledges;
- skulls of humans, dwarves and various humanoids leering from those ledges;
- a large armchair and a crude table, both fashioned from bone.

The biggest, ugliest troll the party had seen in a long time rose from a heap of torn rags and tapestries in the corner, enormous and oblivious to the party's presence. Barkinar didn't care whether it ended up allied or dead. Dren felt threatened by it and wanted it gone. Sequoia settled it: "Let's just kill it."

The group coordinated a stealthy approach, bringing Vurakhal forward to neutralize the creature's regeneration. Zinnia's stealth roll fell short, but the salamander came in clutch with a 22. Sequoia and Zinnia tore into the troll with a surprise assault before it could react, for 41 and 47 damage. Zephyr tripped the chieftain and sent him crashing to the ground. Sequoia then stepped in and delivered a critical strike, the "one stab" that dealt 66 damage. It finished a 154-hit-point creature that the party had already cut down to its last few hit points, before it could take a single action. The whole party stood in stunned silence for a moment before the reality of what had just happened sank in.

The chieftain wore bone armor, and his remains yielded a handsome reward: a ring worth 1,700 gold pieces, a little bag of 80 platinum pieces, a potion of greater healing, and a curious magical wood totem. Calmer suggested the totem be tested on the orc or the desk rather than on anyone important. It turned the desk a vivid shade of pink. The change was permanent and random in color. The totem works on objects but not on living creatures, and can be used once a day. The party claimed it immediately as a novelty too useful to leave behind.

Turning back toward the Water Temple, Zinnia's passive perception of 17 caught a secret door. It opened north onto a long hallway, with a door along it into the Air Temple, home of the party's new allies, however far they could be trusted. The party chose to loot what remained of the Water Temple before Eelrich decided the looting should stop.

Continuing their exploration, the party pushed into a room that had once belonged to the Water Temple. There they found four bugbears dressed in the colors of the Air Temple. Kelno had been busy. The border between the water and air territories had quietly shifted while the party was occupied elsewhere, and the bugbears made it clear they answered to the Air Temple now.

Calmer drew himself up to his full height and announced that he was Lord Master Calmer. He fumbled his companions' names, then demanded tribute as the new ruling authority of the area. The bugbears stared at him in confusion, and the intimidation landed very badly until Zephyr stepped in with a roll of 19 to back him up: this was their master, and he could have them dead in a second. The bugbears paid twenty gold pieces in tribute, five apiece, and the party conceded the area to Kelno and moved on.

Vurakhal, injured and with his job done, headed back to his lair. At the end of the long hallway Sequoia rolled a natural 1 on the lock of a heavy door. His halfling luck turned it into a natural 20, and the party stepped into what had once been a grand reception chamber, 40 by 50 feet. Murals on the walls depicted new arrivals being treated to the delights of the temple. The room itself was now a ruin:

- battered couches, chairs and stands along the walls;
- scattered skeletons, mostly human;
- unlit sconces, with no torches left;
- a shallow pool whose fountain had stopped.

At the room's center a dirty, decorated statue of a minotaur sat on a throne, dull and motionless. Sequoia studied it carefully and rolled a 20. He realized, with a cold certainty, that it was not a statue at all.

The session closed with the party staring down a living minotaur, poised on the edge of yet another deadly encounter in the depths of the temple they now called home. The DM ended play there because it was 10:30, and play resumes in two weeks because he was flying out to India for work. He told the table this was the most fun he had ever had running the adventure, out of five playthroughs.

## Locations
### Hall of Verdigris
A dangerous area containing a pool of sentient, acidic water about four feet deep. It can trap intruders and plant mental suggestions in their minds. Its lure in Chapter 31 was a triton's plea warning of a kraken. A curtain hangs just outside the hall, and the party fell back behind it.

### Brother Eelrich Vane's Chambers
A set of rooms including a bedroom with locked chests and a desk area. The party claimed scrolls, ritual texts on elemental water, a ceremonial mace, a pearl ring, and potions there.

### The Party's Lair (First Level)
The party's base of operations on the first level. They rest, recover, receive guests and conduct administrative business here.

### Second Floor of the Temple
The level where the party meets with their fire salamander ally Vurakhal and coordinates with Fire Temple leadership, Captain Varek Solain in particular.

### Owlbear Chamber
A southwestern room containing a large, chained owlbear. It was hungry and agitated until Sequoia fed it and Zinnia calmed it. The party left it chained.

### Troll Lair
A grim area where two trolls in tribal garb were found feasting on the remains of fallen soldiers from the recent temple wars, possibly Fire or Earth Temple troops. It holds nothing of value. A door leads east to the chieftain's chamber.

### Troll Chieftain's Bone Chamber
A foul, disused room that someone took "considerable pains" to decorate. Its features:
- walls plastered with excrement and bones, forming patterns and shelf-like ledges;
- a floor of bones and ordure;
- a bone armchair and crude bone table in the northwest corner;
- shelves lined with humanoid skulls;
- a heap of torn rags, leather scraps, tapestries and rugs in the southeast corner, used as a bed by the chieftain.

### Water Temple Border
A room once belonging to the Water Temple. Bugbears loyal to Kelno's Air Temple now occupy it, revealing a significant territorial shift. It is reached through a secret door that opens onto a north-running hallway, which has a door into the Air Temple.

### Reception Chamber
A 40-foot by 50-foot room decorated with murals depicting new arrivals being hosted to the temple's delights. It holds battered couches, chairs and stands, skeletal remains, unlit sconces, and a dry fountain with a "much dirtied" minotaur-on-a-throne that conceals a living minotaur disguised as a statue. Reached through a locked door at the end of the north hallway.

## NPCs
### Brother Eelrich Vane
A defeated priest of the Water Temple who is forced to submit to Calmer's authority. In Chapter 31 he called the Hall of Verdigris unwelcoming but agreed to follow "Brother Calmer's" lead. The party discovers evidence suggesting he knew far more about the sentient nature of the water elemental than he ever revealed, casting doubt on how much he can be trusted. The party is looting the Water Temple's remains quickly "before Eelrich decides that it's time for him to stop the looting."

### Dren Halveth
Captain of the Broken Blades, the mercenary company holding Earth Temple territory, whom the party installed as commander of the Earth Temple forces.
- He reports the arrival of Lucius Graeme, whom Hessa Claymoor of his Broken Blades patted down for weapons, and whom he found suspicious. He warned that chests can turn into mimics.
- He briefs the party on trolls, picking at something with a stiletto as he talks, and calls them "a pain in the ass" that need a steady supply of flesh.
- He tells them fire is needed to stop troll regeneration and reminds them of their fire salamander ally.
- He dismisses Zephyr's Hydra comparison as "abyssal stuff."
- He feels threatened by the troll chieftain and wants it gone.

### Lucius Graeme
A soft-looking grey man who smiles constantly and arrived claiming to be sent by Barkinar.
- He is a facilitator and middleman who calls the new management "the cleverest thing to happen to the temple in a year."
- He laughed when Hessa Claymoor patted him down.
- He brought gifts of wine and healing potions, and presented a chest of enchanted items for sale.
- He offered to hold two coveted items for a deposit.

### Barkinar
Commander of the Greater Temple's Troops, and the authority who handed the Upper Temple to the party after four prophets died in a single day.
- He sent Lucius Graeme as their quartermaster and conduit for supplies and magical wares.
- He does not care whether the troll chieftain ends up allied or dead, so long as it is one or the other.
- The one-week deadline he set for the party to organize the temple is still running through this session. Out of character, the DM described it as "five days to plunder the place before anybody starts asking questions."

### Vurakhal
A fire salamander ally who does not speak Common. He is contracted to the party ("Mr. Zephyr") but temporarily reports to Captain Varek Solain and the Fire Temple's clerical lead. He was pleased to confirm that he need not tell them about this job.
- He agrees to help eliminate the trolls ("I would be delighted to"), and entered battle crying "THIS IS WHAT I WAS BUILT FOR!"
- His fire aura proves vital in preventing troll regeneration, and he wields a flaming spear with deadly efficiency.
- His group-stealth roll of 22 won the party its surprise round on the chieftain.
- He is injured by the end of the troll encounters, having taken 37 damage from one troll, and returns to his lair afterward.

### Varek Solain
The Captain of the Fire Temple, who arrives during the party's meeting with Vurakhal as if he had been listening at the door.
- He informs the party about the trolls' history. He names **Oohlgrist**, whom both Alrrem and Belsornig had tried to recruit, and describes how the trolls refused both sides, waited out the war, and scavenged the bodies afterward.
- He says Alrrem "offered them so much power and they betrayed him."
- He provides their location and says he will be glad to see them gone.

### Frostbrand
A sentient, opinionated short sword +1, committed to defeating the Temple of Elemental Evil, that serves as a translator between the party and Vurakhal.
- At first he is dismayed to be working with the fire element again.
- Once Sequoia reminds him that the party owns the place, he enthusiastically embraces the cover story of overthrowing the temple from within, "so the betrayal will be more complete."
- He even attempts to adopt an "evil face," which comes off badly.

### Air Temple Bugbears
Four guards dressed in the colors of the Air Temple who have moved into the Water Temple's former territory on Kelno's orders. They declare themselves "loyal clerics… loyal to Earth Air Temple." They are confused by Calmer's demand for tribute, and pay twenty gold pieces, five apiece, once Zephyr backs him up.

### Kelno
Kelno the Prefect, leader of the Air Temple and the party's ally. He took their deal rather than fight them in Chapter 27, and is now the party's conditional number two. That alliance is what makes this session's discovery pointed: his bugbears have quietly moved into the Water Temple's former holdings while the party was occupied elsewhere, annexing territory the party had just taken. The party chose to concede the area to him.

### Oohlgrist, the Troll Chieftain
A massive, particularly ugly troll who decorated his lair with the skulls and bones of his victims and wore bone armor. He had 154 hit points. Varek Solain named him earlier in the session: **Oohlgrist**, the troll both Alrrem and Belsornig had tried to recruit. His name was not spoken again at the chamber. He was caught completely off guard when he rose from his rag heap, and Sequoia slew him before he could take a single action.

### Minotaur
A creature disguised as a dirty, decorated statue sitting on a throne in the reception chamber. Sequoia's investigation roll of 20 revealed it just as the session ends.
