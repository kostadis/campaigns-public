# Chapter 38 The Sleeper in the Iron Coffin

Date: 2026-08-30

## Summary
The party took stock. They had more or less satisfied Barkinar's requirement to re-establish the temple's leadership, and they had dealt with the orc and troll problems. All that remained was to explore the rest of their domain. When they reached the area where the prisoners had been held, after leaving them without food for several days, they found the captives gone. Zephyr's Investigation (22) showed they had not escaped: they had been killed and their corpses taken, the chains torn apart. The party suspected their "friendly neighborhood necromancer" had added them to the bodies he controlled. Zephyr proposed posting a notice demanding that anyone who found the necromancer report to the party. Calmer called it "a fantastic idea," handed responsibility for drafting it to Zephyr as Chief of Staff, and promised to sign it. He then turned to Zinnia, his Chief of Security, who endorsed the plan. Sequoia, a self-declared completionist, pushed the party to explore the last unexplored areas before heading back. They found a refuse-filled chamber that had once been a place of relaxation but was now a heap of rotting garbage, dung, bones, and skulls, with parts of a bronze-bound coffer visible in the central pile. Worried the necromancer would use the remains, the party decided to burn the entire room. They discovered that Calmer had no fire spells, which he admitted was "a mistake," and that none of them had Meteor Swarm, at which Calmer laughed. Zephyr and Sequoia went to the Fire Temple through a dark corridor they had never used before, while Zinnia stayed behind with Calmer, who could turn undead, to "watch what he does."

Captain Varek Solain of the Fire Temple, no longer defeated in demeanor, received them. He was puzzled that they wanted to burn the trash, since the temple simply threw its garbage there and "it kind of gets eaten away." When Zephyr explained that a necromancer was quietly taking control of the skeletons, Varek swore and filled in the history. A necromancer had once worked for Barkinar. When Romag got the job instead, he "got pissed, and disappeared." Varek had warned Alrrem about him, but Alrrem didn't listen. Asked to hunt the necromancer down, Varek protested that he had no army, food, troops, or money, because "You took all my money and resources, dude!" What he needed was for someone to go back to Nulb and sort out the supply chain. Varek handed over a limited supply of fire arrows and explained the technique: throw a flask of oil at the target, then ignite it with a burning arrow. Zephyr then gave the Fire Temple the northern food supply depot to guard. Varek's pleasure was obvious, and when asked whether this was his preferred outcome, he replied, "He who controls food controls everything." Zephyr corrected him that the party controlled the food and the Fire Temple was simply guarding it. Varek accepted this as a sign of trust in the Fire Temple, then could not resist declaring, "And fire shall burn again!" Zephyr privately resolved to plant an Earth Temple agent to keep the Fire Temple in check.

When the party returned and set the refuse ablaze, a horrific, furious creature emerged from the trash and desperately tried to douse the flames. The party was determined to protect their territory and engaged with surprise on their side, while the fire burned the creature for 5 damage each round. Calmer, who rolled a very high initiative, cast Hold Monster and paralyzed it, so every hit within 5 feet counted as a critical. Zinnia struck for 27 and 19 using a focus point. Zephyr landed a sneak attack crit for 52 and disengaged. Sequoia rolled twelve dice on his critical and totaled only 36, cursing his luck, then added 8 more and cursed Frostbrand. Calmer added a Toll the Dead ("this new spell of mine is very effective"), and Zinnia struck again for 32. Zephyr finished it with a 57-point sneak attack, and Calmer was very pleased with himself. The party stepped outside and let the room burn, which would take a few hours.

Next, the party passed through a storage area of broken containers, recently stripped of foodstuffs. The temple's food supplies were running low, and nobody was currently running this food supply. They then entered an oddly shaped chamber crammed with barrels, crates, and bales of black cloth and finished garments. Zinnia's Insight (24) identified it as Greater Temple equipment, the black cloaks. Everyone failed their Arcana checks. Then Sequoia, half as a joke, looked up at the ceiling "because the answer is always on the ceiling" and, with an Investigation of 21, spotted four nightmarish creatures clinging upside down by their prehensile tails. They were gaunt, four-armed predators with ash-gray hide, hooked black claws, three alert eyes, and violet psychic energy pulsing around their skulls. The creatures attacked with psychic blasts that stunned Sequoia and battered the party's minds. Calmer surrounded himself with Spirit Guardians and twice unleashed Word of Radiance, while his new Shield +1 pushed his AC to 21 and turned aside nearly every claw. Zephyr and Zinnia focused their strikes and killed two of the creatures. The remaining two disengaged into a corner and hissed at the party. Zephyr warned them, "You know where the boss is, okay?" His attempt to communicate in Undercommon was met only with sneers before they curled up into the shadows.

An exhausted, flustered Dren Halveth arrived just then with a scroll for a notepad and a deadpan summary of the temple's cascading administrative disasters: the Minotaur had food for only three days, the men had pay for only four, and the dead had started leaving without permission. The supply chain ran through Nulb, so the party resolved to go there and fix it. Their orders were to feed usable bodies to the Minotaur and burn the rest, which Dren proposed doing with the Fire Temple salamander's help. The armor depot went to the Water Temple and the firewood depot to the Air Temple. The party would leave the next morning, "for whatever tomorrow morning looks like in this place." Before departing, they told Dren to keep everyone fed and keep order. Dren was left in charge, with Sequoia's pointed reminder: "I trust you, but if you betray, you're dead."

That evening, as the party relaxed and Calmer savored being in charge ("I like being in charge"), an illusory image of a hooded man appeared before them. "Forgive the theatrics," he began. The party came to understand him as Falrinth, a wizard hiding from the Greater Temple whom Barkinar had said they could talk about later. He warned them about Lucius Graeme, a flattering man with golden eyes and soft hands who had already visited them with gifts and wine and asked what they were short of. "He collects the ones he gives you." Falrinth claimed to possess the Orb of Golden Death and said he would not give it to Hedrack, to Senshock, to any of the "holy gentlemen" who wanted him dead, or to the party. He warned Calmer that his scarab with the four letters would be known on sight by Lucius and by Senshock, who would each use it against the party without saying they had noticed. He called Lareth a friend and thanked the party for handling him with discretion, but warned that "not all of them are as patient as I am." He urged them to stall if the flattering man returned, and said that "A candle in the West" would reach him. His most chilling revelation came last. On the level beneath their feet lay a deep, forgotten room where "the holy gentleman" kept a sleeper in an iron coffin, and waking it would bring Verbobonc and all its banners down on the temple. "Ask your golden friend what he's so afraid you'll find, then signal me." The illusion faded, and the party realized it had been a one-way message that had predicted some of their questions. It left them with far more questions than answers.

The next morning, the party traveled to Nulb dressed discreetly, choosing not to announce their new status as lords of the temple. Zephyr summed it up as "Power and subtlety," and Calmer approved: "This is why I have made you Chief of Staff." Smoke lay low over the river, the same barges leaned at the same rotten piers, and men at the tavern doors still argued over which elemental prophet would win a war that had ended beneath their feet two days before. "Nulb does not change. That is how you know nobody told it." A child of the Rhennee river folk the party had helped recognized them and ran shouting, "FAMILY HAS RETURNED!" A dock hand pressed a folded note into Sequoia's hand from Skole, the Nulb bartender who had once warned them that Tolub was telling everyone they were dead. It carried a simple, pointed update: Captain Tolub, who had tried to kill them, was still telling people the party was dead. The game, it seemed, was far from over.

## NPCs
### The Minotaur
Installed in the previous session (Chapter 33) as guard of the ruined reception hall, in exchange for regular meals and protection from black-coated bandits, some of whom were actually undead. Its own tongue is Abyssal, though it understands and speaks broken Common. It can eat the undead but prefers fresh meat, and it warned the party: "I find you, I kill you, I eat you." This session it is a logistics problem rather than a presence on stage: per Dren, it has food for only three days, and the party orders usable bodies fed to it.

### The Necromancer
An unseen antagonist whose mocking note, found on the ghoul's corpse in the previous session (Chapter 33), claims the temple as his home and vows to use every corpse the party creates against them. It specifically mocks Calmer as unqualified to lead the Earth Temple. Varek Solain reveals he was formerly under Barkinar and disappeared after Romag got the promotion instead. He is suspected of taking the prisoners' corpses and of raising the dead who have "started leaving without permission."

### Varek Solain
The Captain of the Fire Temple guard. No longer defeated in demeanor, he reveals that a disgruntled necromancer formerly under Barkinar is likely responsible for the undead infestation, and that Alrrem had been warned but failed to act. He complains that the party took all his money and resources, and asks for someone to fix the supply chain through Nulb. He provides the party with fire arrows and oil, and is given the food supply depot to guard. A cultist at heart, he believes "He who controls food controls everything" and cannot help proclaiming "And fire shall burn again!"

### Alrrem
The late head of the Fire Temple, mentioned by Varek Solain as having been warned about the necromancer threat but failing to act on it.

### Vurakhal
The party's bound salamander retainer, guarding the Fire Temple altar. The party considered asking it for help burning the garbage room before taking Varek's arrows instead, and Dren proposed using "the Fire Temple salamander" to burn bodies.

### Barkinar
The party's superior, who formerly led the Earth Temple before promotion to Commander of the Greater Temple's Troops. He had demanded the party re-establish the temple's leadership, which they have done. The necromancer once served under him, and he is still waiting to see what the party does with the chest of Rao. He previously said they could talk about Falrinth later.

### Romag
Barkinar's successor at the Earth Temple. He had muttered that the first-level undead no longer reported to him, and earlier told the party the Orb of Golden Death could summon monsters and destroy anything if fully possessed.

### Dren Halveth
An exhausted and flustered administrator who manages the temple's day-to-day logistics and is said to be fond of manual labor. He reports that the Minotaur's food supply is dwindling, the men are unpaid, and the undead have begun wandering off without permission. He is left in charge of the temple while the party travels to Nulb, with orders to keep everyone fed and keep order, and a warning about betrayal.

### Psychic Predators
Four gaunt, four-armed ape-like creatures with ash-gray hide, three eyes, hook claws, powerful fanged jaws, and prehensile tails. They clung to the ceiling of the Greater Temple storeroom and attacked with both slashing claws and violet psychic blasts that force Wisdom saves and can stun. Two were slain; two retreated into the shadows.

### Refuse Creature
A horrific, furious creature that burst from the garbage pile when the party set it ablaze, trying to douse the flames. It had AC 15 and a high Wisdom, but was held by Calmer's Hold Monster and killed while paralyzed.

### Falrinth
A wizard who communicated via an illusory hooded projection, unable to risk sending a runner. He claims to possess the Orb of Golden Death and refuses to surrender it to any temple faction. He warned the party about Lucius Graeme, cautioned Calmer about his scarab, called Lareth a friend, and revealed the existence of a sleeper in an iron coffin on the level below. He can be reached via "A candle in the West."

### Lucius Graeme
Described by Falrinth as a flattering man with golden eyes and soft hands who arrives bearing gifts and wine, secretly cataloguing what he gives in order to collect favors and information later. He has already visited the party, and Falrinth predicts he will come again.

### Senshock
Named by Falrinth as one of those who would like him dead and who would recognize Calmer's scarab on sight.

### Hedrack
Named by Falrinth as one of those to whom he will not surrender the Orb of Golden Death.

### Lareth
Called "a friend" by Falrinth, who thanked the party for handling him with discretion.

A captain who previously attempted to kill the party and is currently spreading rumors in Nulb that they are dead, as reported by the informant Skole.

An informant in Nulb who passed a covert note to Sequoia via a dock hand, revealing that Captain Tolub is still telling people the party is dead. "He thought you might enjoy the update."
