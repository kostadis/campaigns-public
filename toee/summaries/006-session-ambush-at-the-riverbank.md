# Chapter 06 Ambush at the Riverbank

Date: 2025-01-18

## Summary

As night fell over the quiet countryside, the party shadowed a wiry, rail-thin commoner named Hestort “Tort” Flintshine through an open field toward the river. Four men waiting near the shore rose and waved to him. When the party chose to continue watching rather than intervene, the men suddenly shot Tort and left him crumpled and bleeding on the ground. He was not quite dead, but the party had only moments to save him before the bandits finished the job.

The party sprang into action. Zinnia closed the distance and unleashed two precise strikes against the bandit captain, reasoning that he likely possessed the most useful information and should not be allowed to escape. One of the bandits rushed toward Tort to finish him, leaving the party with only one round to intervene. Sequoia engaged that attacker while Zephyr sprinted to Tort’s side and forced a healing potion down his throat, pulling him back from the edge of death. Saving Tort turned the skirmish into what the DM called “a real fight”: another bandit attacked Zephyr, a crossbowman tried and failed to shoot Tort, and the bandit captain retaliated against Zinnia with two scimitar strikes and a dagger thrust, inflicting sixteen points of damage and leaving him with a single hit point.

Zinnia landed a critical hit and used a focus point to disengage to safety. Sequoia then cut down the bandit who had wounded him with a devastating fourteen-point blow, snarling, “Don’t mess with me, boy.” Zephyr followed with a sneak attack against another of Tort’s would-be killers, dealing fourteen points of damage and killing him outright. Zinnia hurled a spear from a safer distance, Sequoia wounded the last ordinary bandit, and Zephyr struck the captain with his shortbow. With two crew members dead and the last bandit already fleeing, the captain abandoned the fight and disappeared into the darkness.

Tort was astonished to be alive, with the party standing over him, rather than in the afterlife. His first frightened questions were, “Am I in hell? Am I in the abyss?” Sequoia answered, “No, don’t worry, but you will be if you don’t answer a question.” Tort immediately begged for protection. He introduced himself as Hestort Flintshine—“but my friends call me Tort”—and asked why they had saved him. Zephyr’s attempted reassurance was blunt: “Let’s just say we’re not your saviors. They kidnapped you, but we wanted to kidnap you first.”

After Zephyr persuaded him to cooperate, Tort initially claimed, “Terjon. I work for Terjon. The cleric of St. Cuthbert.” The story did not sound right, and Zephyr invoked Thaumaturgy to lend supernatural force to his accusation: “I know that’s a lie.” The intimidation broke Tort completely. Having barely escaped death once, he believed he was about to die again and promptly wet himself before spilling the truth. He did not know the identity of the ultimate mastermind, but the man he had been working with was known as “the Duke.”

The Duke had offered Tort money to gather information about the castle. Tort also knew that the Duke worked with shady local bandits and answered to somebody else in the village. He did not know who that superior was and had deliberately avoided learning more, explaining, “Between you and me, I don’t want to know who the Duke works for.” If he knew, he feared that person would kill him. Tort believed that merely planning to speak about meeting Zephyr had already provoked the attempt on his life. He declared that he was leaving town and that the party would never see him again.

The party recognized the Duke as the same man hired by the merchant Rannos Davl to guard their camp and accompany them around the area. Tort’s confession also established who had stolen Burne and Rufus’s maps, but the party decided not to tell Burne and Rufus yet. Zephyr cautioned everyone not to behave strangely around the Duke, and the party began wondering whether Rannos and the other traders were entangled in the conspiracy as well. When Zephyr asked, “Is everyone evil in this village?” the DM suggested a better question: “Does everybody have an agenda in this village?” The party disposed of the dead bandits by dropping their bodies into the river and watching the current carry them away.

The next morning, the party gathered at the Inn of the Welcome Wench for breakfast with Ostler. Calmer and Spugnoir stood waiting to learn which of them would accompany the party that day: the cleric or the magic user. The party selected Calmer, who responded with an enthusiastic, “Excellent. Excellent, excellent, excellent.” Calmer innocently described his restful night and asked how the party had slept, unaware of their battle and interrogation by the river.

The Duke arrived and asked whether the party intended to visit the Moathouse or go elsewhere. When Zephyr turned the question back on him, the Duke replied that he had been paid to protect their belongings and did not care whether he guarded them at the inn or accompanied the party to the Moathouse: “You guys are in charge.” Although Zephyr felt uneasy about leaving their possessions with him, Sequoia reasoned that outright theft was probably not the Duke’s business—otherwise, someone would already have discovered it. The party concealed their suspicions and proceeded as though nothing had changed.

Back at the Moathouse, the party descended into the chambers where they had previously been driven off by a large pack of undead. Five zombies remained. Confident in Calmer’s divine abilities, Zephyr told him, “You better have killed five zombies or you’re fired from the squad.” Calmer tried to be brave while the party promised to stand behind him “in case” he died. Raising his holy symbol, he cried, “By the power invested in me by the great Saint Cuthbert, be gone, zombies!” Four of the five recoiled under his Channel Divinity and fled, leaving only one unaffected.

Zinnia struck the resisting zombie twice for fifteen total damage, and Sequoia followed with a crushing attack that overcame its unnatural resilience and destroyed it. Rather than allow the four turned zombies to recover together, the party exploited the divine compulsion and attacked them one at a time. They carefully chose targets according to the initiative order so that nearly the entire group could strike before each newly freed zombie had a chance to retaliate.

Zinnia and Sequoia destroyed the next zombie, with Sequoia landing a critical hit. Zephyr then assaulted another with repeated dagger strikes and sneak attacks. The creature fell to zero hit points, lurched back upright with one hit point, and finally succumbed after another blow. Zinnia wounded the next zombie before Sequoia’s critical strike bypassed its undead fortitude entirely. Against the final zombie, Zephyr considered Chill Touch but abandoned the idea after being reminded that it dealt necrotic damage and undead creatures were generally resistant to it. He instead drove his dagger into the creature over and over. It repeatedly collapsed and clawed its way back to a single hit point before finally surviving long enough to slam Zephyr for four points of damage. Calmer then invoked Sacred Flame and killed it, extinguishing the last undead threat and leaving the Moathouse chamber silent.

## Scenes

### 006.01 Ambush at the Riverbank

#### The party tracks a commoner through a field at night, only to witness him being ambushed by a group of bandits near the river.

- The day passes uneventfully. At nightfall the commoner goes to his encampment hut, and the party watches him from the forest. Late at night, he begins walking across an open field toward the river.

- Four men lying near the shore stand and wave to the commoner as he approaches.

- Asked whether they want to intervene or continue watching, the party elects to continue. The four men immediately shoot the commoner and leave him critically wounded.

- The commoner is not yet dead, but the party has only two rounds to stabilize him or extract any information before he expires.

- Zephyr initially offers to hold off the attackers while the other two rescue the victim, only to learn that there are four enemies rather than two.

- Zinnia wins initiative and targets the bandit captain. A successful History check reminds him that bandit captains rarely fight to the death and are likely to run once their followers fall, but he still prioritizes the captain because he believes the captain has more information and wants to prevent his escape.

- Zinnia rushes the captain and lands two melee attacks for seventeen total damage.

- One bandit moves toward the fallen commoner and tries to finish him, reducing the party’s rescue window to a single round.

- Sequoia engages the bandit threatening the victim. His first attack narrowly misses, but a second strike deals five points of damage.

- Zephyr reaches the commoner and administers a Potion of Healing rather than risk a Medicine check. The potion restores him and removes the immediate threat of death.

- The DM warns, “You know, now you’ve turned this into a real fight,” after Zephyr saves the intended victim.

- A bandit swings a scimitar at Zephyr and misses. Another fires a crossbow at the rescued commoner but also misses completely.

- The bandit captain decides “he’s going to teach you a lesson” and attacks Zinnia twice with his scimitar and once with his dagger. The assault inflicts sixteen points of damage, leaving him with only one hit point.

- Zinnia lands a critical hit against the captain, expends a focus point to disengage as a bonus action, and retreats from melee.

- A bandit strikes Sequoia for five points of damage. Sequoia immediately retaliates for fourteen points, killing him in one blow and declaring, “Don’t mess with me, boy.”

- Zephyr attacks a bandit threatening Tort. Because the rescued commoner counts as an ally beside the enemy, Zephyr qualifies for sneak attack and deals fourteen points of damage, killing the bandit.

- After seeing two of his followers fall, the captain backs away rather than leading from the front and fires a crossbow, but misses completely.

- With only one hit point remaining, Zinnia keeps his distance and throws his spear. The attack hits the captain for five points of damage.

- The final ordinary bandit runs. Sequoia wounds him for eight points, but he remains alive and continues his escape.

- Zephyr uses his shortbow to deal another five points of damage to the captain.

- With two crew members dead and the last one fleeing, the bandit captain also runs into the darkness.

- The rescued commoner, very surprised to be alive, sees the party standing over him and asks, “Am I in hell? Am I in the abyss?”

- Sequoia replies, “No, don’t worry, but you will be if you don’t answer a question.”

- The commoner begs, “You gotta protect me, man. You gotta protect me.”

- He identifies himself: “My name’s Hestort Flintshine, but my friends call me Tort.” He is five feet tall and as thin as a rail.

### 006.02 Interrogating Hestort Flintshine

#### The party interrogates the commoner they saved, uncovering a web of lies and a connection to a mysterious figure known as the Duke.

- Tort is baffled by his rescue and asks, “I know. You saved my life. Why did you save my life?”

- Zephyr answers, “Let’s just say we’re not your saviors. They kidnapped you, but we wanted to kidnap you first.”

- Sequoia offers a simpler line: they saved Tort because they need to ask him some questions. Zephyr’s Persuasion check convinces him to cooperate.

- Asked who employs him, Tort claims, “Terjon. I work for Terjon. The cleric of St. Cuthbert.”

- The party recognizes Terjon as Calmer’s superior. An initial attempt to read Tort suggests he is telling the truth, but his story still does not sound right.

- Zephyr invokes Thaumaturgy to enhance his intimidation and tells Tort, “I know that’s a lie.”

- Tort is so terrified by Zephyr’s magically amplified threat that he wets himself. Having just survived the bandits’ attempt on his life, he believes the party may kill him instead.

- Tort admits that he does not know the ultimate mastermind, but the man he has been dealing with is called “the Duke.”

- The party recognizes the name. The Duke is the man hired by Rannos Davl, the merchant. The Duke protects the party’s camp while they explore the Moathouse and has been following them around.

- Tort says the Duke approached him with a job: gather information about the castle and deliver it in exchange for a large payment.

- The DM explains that this confession tells the party who stole Burne and Rufus’s maps.

- Tort does not know the identities of the men who tried to kill him, but he knows the Duke works with “some shady bandits around here.”

- Tort also says the Duke works for somebody else in the village, although he does not know who that person is.

- When pressed about the Duke’s superior, Tort says, “Between you and me, I don’t want to know who the Duke works for.”

- He explains, “Because if I knew, that guy might kill me.”

- Tort believes the mere fact that he intended to speak about meeting Zephyr was enough to trigger the assassination attempt: “And as it is, the fact that I was going to go talk to him about meeting Zephyr over here almost got me killed.”

- Tort resolves to flee: “So as far as I’m concerned, I’m leaving town and you will never see me again.”

- The party concludes that the Duke, who regularly accompanies them and guards their belongings, is not someone they should trust.

- Zephyr advises the others, “But let’s not act weird around him.”

- The party also remembers that Rannos Davl and the traders hired the Duke in the first place, raising the possibility that they may be involved in the same conspiracy.

- Zephyr asks, “Is everyone evil in this village?” The DM reframes it: “Does everybody have an agenda in this village? Is the better question?”

- The party decides not to tell Burne and Rufus who stole the maps. Zephyr argues that they should be the first to learn everything and notes that Burne and Rufus might be evil as well.

- The party chooses to conceal the battle by dumping the dead bandits into the river. The current flows away from the area, carrying the bodies downriver.

### 006.03 Morning at the Welcome Wench

#### After a night’s rest, the party prepares for their next move and chooses their companions for the day.

- The party returns to the Inn of the Welcome Wench, sleeps, and gathers for breakfast the following morning.

- Ostler greets them while Calmer and Spugnoir stand waiting to learn which of them will be selected for the next expedition.

- The choice is framed as taking either the cleric or the magic user. The party chooses Calmer.

- Calmer responds enthusiastically: “Excellent. Excellent, excellent, excellent.”

- Unaware of the party’s nighttime activities, Calmer says, “Actually, I had a wonderful night’s sleep last night. It was very relaxing and very calming. How did you guys sleep?”

- The party claims to have slept “great,” “very well,” and “very nice,” concealing the riverbank battle and Tort’s interrogation.

- The Duke arrives and asks, “So where are we going today? Where are we going today? For the Moathouse, somewhere else?”

- Zephyr tests him by asking, “Where do you want to go, Duke?”

- The Duke replies, “I’m paid to protect your shit. I don’t care where I go. You want me to stay here and protect your shit? I’ll protect your shit here. Want to go to the Moathouse? We can go to the Moathouse. You guys are in charge.”

- The party decides to return to the Moathouse, remembering that their previous expedition ended when a large group of zombies nearly slaughtered them.

- Zephyr admits, “I don’t feel too good with the Duke having all their stuff.”

- Sequoia reasons that the Duke is probably not in the business of stealing possessions outright, since that behavior would already have been discovered.

- The party keeps the Duke in his familiar role and avoids revealing that Tort has exposed his connections to the bandits.

### 006.04 Return to the Moathouse — Confronting the Zombies

#### The party returns to the Moathouse to deal with the undead threat they previously fled from, using Calmer’s divine powers to gain the upper hand.

- The party descends into the Moathouse chamber where they had previously encountered more than five zombies.

- Five zombies remain in the area.

- Zephyr reminds Calmer of his usefulness against undead. Calmer declares, “The power of St. Cuthbert will protect us against the zombies.”

- Zephyr warns him, “You better have killed five zombies or you’re fired from the squad.”

- After scouting and confirming that there are exactly five zombies, Zephyr decides, “I think Calmer can deal with that.”

- Calmer attempts to appear brave while Zephyr reassures him, “I’ll be behind you in case you die.”

- Calmer raises his holy symbol and shouts, “By the power invested in me by the great Saint Cuthbert, be gone, zombies!”

- Calmer uses Channel Divinity to turn four of the five zombies. The party is warned that attacking a turned zombie will immediately end the effect on that particular creature.

- Zinnia attacks the sole zombie that resisted the turning, landing two blows for fifteen total damage.

- Sequoia follows with a powerful melee attack. After dealing fifteen points of damage, he adds another seven, and the zombie fails to endure the assault.

- The four turned zombies flee. Rather than let them all recover, the party decides to attack them one at a time, exploiting the initiative order to focus their attacks before each target can retaliate.

- Zinnia chooses one of the fleeing zombies and deals fifteen total damage over two hits. Sequoia lands a critical hit, and the zombie dies.

- Sequoia jokes about the timing of the turning effect after the DM says it ends “the minute” a zombie is attacked: “Yeah, but a minute is 10 rounds.” The DM replies, “That is a good pun. Well done.”

- Zephyr targets another turned zombie with his dagger. His first sneak attack deals sixteen points of damage, and a second hit brings the total to twenty-two.

- The zombie falls but immediately returns with one hit point due to its unnatural fortitude. Zephyr uses a third attack and finally destroys it after forcing another resilience check.

- Calmer tries to involve the party in choosing his next target, asking whether he should attack zombie four or zombie five, before casting Sacred Flame.

- Zinnia attacks the next active zombie twice for thirteen total damage but conserves his remaining focus point rather than using it for extra strikes.

- Sequoia lands a critical hit for ten damage. Because the attack is a critical, the zombie’s undead fortitude cannot save it.

- Against the last zombie, Zephyr initially announces, “This time I’m going to use chill touch.”

- After being reminded that Chill Touch inflicts necrotic damage and undead creatures are generally resistant to it, Zephyr switches back to his dagger.

- Zephyr hits repeatedly, including a sneak attack. The zombie drops to one hit point, is struck again for eight damage, and then returns to one hit point yet again.

- The revived zombie finally receives a turn. The DM voices its lurching exhalation as “Brains!” before clarifying that zombies do not literally say the word; the sound is merely air being expelled from their lungs and body.

- The zombie slams Zephyr for four points of damage.

- Calmer casts Sacred Flame and kills the final zombie, ending the encounter.

## Locations

### The Field near the River

A dark, open field adjacent to a river where the party tracked Hestort Flintshine after he left his encampment hut. Four ambushers had been waiting near the shore and initially rose to wave to him before shooting him. The party fought a bandit captain and three ordinary bandits here, rescuing Tort moments before his death. Afterward, they dumped the dead bandits into the river and watched the current carry the bodies away, concealing evidence of the fight.

### Inn of the Welcome Wench

A local inn where the party rests for the night and reconvenes over breakfast the following morning. Ostler hosts them, while Calmer and Spugnoir wait to discover which of them the party will take on its next expedition. The Duke also meets the party here and offers either to guard their possessions or accompany them to the Moathouse.

### The Moathouse

A ruined fortification where the party had previously been nearly overwhelmed by a large group of zombies. The party returns to the underground chamber with Calmer and finds five zombies still present. Calmer’s Channel Divinity turns four of them, allowing the party to eliminate the undead methodically, one at a time.

## NPCs

### Hestort 'Tort' Flintshine

A commoner, five feet tall and thin as a rail, who was ambushed and nearly killed by bandits near the river. On seeing the party standing over him, he wondered whether he was in hell or the Abyss and immediately begged for protection. He initially claimed to work for Terjon, but Zephyr’s Thaumaturgy-enhanced intimidation terrified him into confessing.

Tort was hired by the Duke to gather information about the castle in exchange for money. He knew that the Duke associated with shady local bandits and worked for another person in the village, but he had deliberately avoided learning that superior’s identity. Tort believed that his intention to talk about meeting Zephyr had prompted the attempt on his life. After revealing what he knew, he declared that he would leave town and never return.

### Bandit Captain

A powerful and self-preserving leader of the ambushers who wields a scimitar, a dagger, and a crossbow. He endured Zinnia’s opening attacks, then retaliated with two scimitar strikes and a dagger thrust for sixteen points of damage, leaving him with one hit point. He subsequently withdrew from close combat and attempted a crossbow shot rather than leading from the front. Once two crew members were dead and the last surviving bandit had fled, he also escaped into the darkness.

### The Duke (Thorne)

A man hired by the merchant Rannos Davl to protect the party’s camp and follow them on their adventures. Hestort’s interrogation reveals that the Duke paid him to gather information about the castle, has been working with shady bandits, and answers to an unknown benefactor in the village. This casts serious doubt on his trustworthiness and may implicate the traders who hired him.

The morning after the revelation, the Duke behaves normally and offers either to guard the party’s possessions or accompany them to the Moathouse. The party conceals what it has learned and avoids acting suspiciously around him.

### Ostler

The proprietor of the Inn of the Welcome Wench who receives the party after its nighttime activities and is present when it gathers for breakfast the next morning.

### Calmer

An enthusiastic cleric of Saint Cuthbert and subordinate of Terjon. The party chooses him over Spugnoir for the return to the Moathouse. He is eager but visibly trying to be brave when asked to confront five zombies.

Calmer channels the power of Saint Cuthbert to turn four of the five undead creatures, giving the party a decisive tactical advantage. He attempts to participate in the group’s decisions before attacking and ultimately destroys the final zombie with Sacred Flame.

### Spugnoir

A magic user who waits alongside Calmer at the Inn of the Welcome Wench, hoping to be chosen for the day’s excursion. The party selects Calmer instead. When Zephyr asks about “some magical cave somewhere,” the DM reminds him that was Spugnoir’s lead, and the party has already done it.

### Terjon

A cleric of St. Cuthbert and Calmer’s superior. Hestort initially and falsely claims to work for him, using a plausible connection to the local church as a cover story before Zephyr’s intimidation exposes the lie.

## Items

### Potion of Healing

A magical healing draught carried by Zephyr and administered to the critically wounded Hestort Flintshine during combat. Tort was only moments from death, but the potion restored him immediately and prevented the bandits from completing their assassination.

### Spear

A thrown weapon used by Zinnia after the bandit captain’s attacks left him with only one hit point. By keeping his distance and throwing the spear from twenty feet away, he struck the captain for five points of damage without exposing himself to another melee assault.

### Magical Dagger

A special blade noted to be in Zinnia’s possession and discussed as a possible thrown weapon during the riverbank fight. Zinnia initially says, “I don’t think I have a magical dagger,” but is told that he does. He ultimately chooses to throw his spear instead.
