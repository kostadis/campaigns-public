# Chapter 26 Harpies, Ghouls, and Earth Temple Rules

Date: 2026-02-21

## Summary

The battle began with Zephyr attacking a harpy that had charmed Sequoia. The DM ruled that he couldn't add his sneak attack bonus because Sequoia counted as the harpy's ally for the moment. Zephyr struck the harpy three times for 24 damage in total. Zinnia then joined in with a pair of strikes for eighteen damage and killed it, which freed Sequoia from the enchantment. The respite was brief. Two more harpies sat thirty-four feet up in the rafters and began their haunting songs, forcing a DC 11 Wisdom save. One by one the party succumbed. Sequoia rolled a natural one, Calmer rolled a three, and Zephyr resisted the first song but failed against the second harpy's song with a five. "Oh, boy. This could be bad," the DM observed. Only Zinnia remained free, his will strong enough to resist the supernatural compulsion.

The DM reminded the table that any damage lets a charmed creature re-roll its save. With his companions entranced, Zinnia struck his own allies to break the enchantment. He hit Zephyr first, and the blow snapped him back to reality. He then turned to Sequoia and finally to Calmer, spending a focus point to keep his attacks going, each strike meant to free them from the harpies' control. Sequoia came out of it on 5 hit points and remarked "I'm about to die." Everyone was "kind of like very happy to be alive." The harpies howled and raged.

As the party regained their senses, three ghouls burst through the southern door, their rotting forms adding to the chaos. One ghoul ignored the living entirely and went to feed on the dead harpy. The undead attacked with claws and teeth, and one paralyzed Zephyr with its touch despite his first successful resistance.

The harpies swooped down from their perches to attack Calmer, but the cleric proved a difficult target. Each time a harpy tried to flee, Calmer struck it with his mace of smiting. One of those blows was a critical hit for ten damage, delivered with the cry "die, creature of the dark." Calmer was also invaluable as a healer: "have no fear. I am here." He restored nineteen hit points to Sequoia and twenty-three to Zinnia. Sequoia fought with remarkable ferocity, leaning on his halfling luck to cut down ghouls. Zinnia moved through the battlefield with deadly precision, using his focus points for extra strikes.

The situation grew more dire when four more ghouls threw open the northern door with a cry of "Foo! Foo!" Zinnia charged straight at them, since as an elf he had no fear of their paralysis. He was soon beset by several ghouls whose claws raked him for nineteen damage. One ghoul paralyzed Calmer with its touch, leaving the cleric briefly helpless, but he shook off the effect. Zephyr kept failing his DC 10 Constitution saves; the DM asked "How many times can you miss a DC?" The DM then realized he had misread the rules: the paralysis lasts only a turn, not until a successful save. Zephyr was freed and immediately landed a sneak attack. The party worked together to eliminate their foes, and Sequoia delivered especially devastating blows. One of them dealt 24 damage, "four points off for my theoretical max."

As the battle neared its end, only a single harpy remained. It perched in the rafters and snarled defiantly at the party below. Zephyr fired his pistol into the air to frighten it away, but the creature only snarled back. The DM warned that "If you attack it again, you're going to have to kill it." When Zephyr attacked it anyway, the harpy struck back and tried to flee. Zinnia and Sequoia struck at the escaping harpy, and Calmer called on Sacred Flame to scorch it. Finally, Sequoia took aim with a ranged weapon and brought the creature down from the rafters. In the DM's words, "it falls down dead it just got sniped."

With the battle won, the party explored the rooms that had served as the harpies' lair. The first chamber had no other exits and reeked of carrion and filth. Bones were scattered across the floor, and old cloth marked where the creatures had nested. Among the refuse they found elven chain mail hanging from the wall. The party weighed whether anyone should wear it. Zephyr was happy with his AC of 16, Sequoia's armor class relied on his Dexterity, and the DM raised concerns about stealth disadvantage. Calmer already wore plate +1. The party decided to keep the chain mail in the party inventory to sell later for "a pretty penny."

In another abandoned room they found a broken chest. Zephyr tried to get Calmer to open it, in case it was trapped. The cleric refused: "i'm not a rogue you're a rogue either open the chest or don't open the chest." Zephyr then pushed Sequoia to open the chest instead, and the rogue agreed. With help, Sequoia rolled a 28 to search for traps and found none. Inside lay a modest treasure of gold, platinum, silver, and copper coins. Sequoia distributed it among the party, but he rolled a 26 on Stealth and quietly pocketed a slightly larger share for himself, Calmer, and Zinnia without Zephyr noticing. Zephyr's player tried to have Zephyr pickpocket Sequoia anyway. The DM shut it down with "No, you don't know that happened, man," and warned that Zephyr's habit of sending his allies to open things meant "Eventually, they're going to start turning on you."

Next came an antechamber with plastered walls covered in obscene paintings and the remnants of once-fine furnishings. When they opened the next door, they found two ghasts. "The ghasts look at you and they are aghast," the DM said. The ghasts challenged the party's presence and claimed the territory as their own. One of them boasted of seizing the area from its previous occupant: it picked up a gnawed bone, guessed "Maybe an elf," crunched it, and decided "No, definitely human." When Zephyr threatened violence and asked for their last words, the ghasts proposed a deal instead. Zephyr offered to let them work under him, and when they asked who the party worked for, the party named the Earth Temple.

The ghasts reacted at once and with enthusiasm, shouting "Hail the Earth Temple!" The more perceptive party members saw that the creatures were only playing along to survive. The party accepted the arrangement anyway, claiming the ghasts' territory as a subsidiary holding under the Earth Temple's banner. Zephyr declared himself "The greatest deal maker," and the DM noted that they had "added to the glory of Romag's Empire." They smashed open the wood-and-steel gate with a hammer and claimed the whole explored area. Zephyr then started wondering aloud when they would get paid, and even floated a coup. Sequoia pointed out "We don't have the people." After a short rest, they returned to Romag's office to report their success.

Romag sat in his office looking very pleased. The party told him about their territorial expansion and the defeat of the ghouls and harpies that had long refused to bow to his authority. He complained that ever since his predecessor was elevated to the Deep Temple, the undead had refused to bow down to him. The DM corrected the party's claim that the ghasts were dead, since only the ghouls and harpies had been killed. Romag was delighted anyway: "You got rid of their power base. You weakened them." The Earth Temple leader launched into a five-minute speech about the power of the earth to crush all things to dust, and he praised the party for weakening the ghasts and bringing them under the temple's control.

When the party asked about payment, Romag explained that the arrangement worked differently than they expected. He provides the base and they go raiding. They were to bring treasure to the temple and pay him a portion, not receive payment themselves.

The conversation took a significant turn when the party asked for more power, official titles, and higher ranks within the Earth Temple hierarchy. Romag considered the request and agreed, but said such advancement would require a demonstration of loyalty. He instructed his subordinate Hartsch to prepare a ceremony. When Zephyr nervously told Calmer to be ready to die, the cleric pulled him aside to say he was tired of this and that he also had an opinion. Zephyr's reassurance that they would resuscitate him did not help.

Romag overheard and clarified that the party members were too valuable to sacrifice. Instead, prisoners would be used for the ritual, which would involve ripping their bodies apart as an offering to the "earthen, Elder Elemental, earthen god." Calmer, a little nauseous, had asked Romag what kind of ritual it was; once Romag answered, Calmer turned to the others and asked whether they were really going through with it. Zephyr said yes and gave him a look. The session ended with Romag postponing the ceremony until the following day. The party knew that the prisoners they had freed earlier would be discovered missing, which could disrupt Romag's plans and force them to blame the Fire Temple for the disappearance. The DM promised only that "something will happen."

Out of game, the DM announced he would be running a marathon in Napa Valley the following weekend but might still run a Saturday-morning session. The table closed with a chat about George's basketball season and Giannis Antetokounmpo.

- The party enters an antechamber with plastered walls covered in obscene paintings and remnants of upholstered furnishings.
  - The DM: "This inner room was evidently the antechamber of some minor functionary."
  - The furnishings are a couch and four chairs, with "old and battered pewter dishes" heaped on a small table. There are doors in the east, west, and south walls.
  - Zephyr: "I think we're good here." Sequoia: "Explore everything," and he points out a room beyond.
- The party opens a door and encounters two ghasts who question their presence.
  - "The ghasts look at you and they are aghast... They are kind of pissed off."
  - Offered the choice to attack or walk away, Zephyr says "I'll let them be... I'll ask them what they're doing."
- The ghasts claim the territory as their own, stating they seized it from previous occupants.
  - The second ghast: "This is our territory. We seized it."
- Zephyr asks the ghasts what they are doing, and the ghasts respond that this is their territory.
  - Ghast: "What are you doing here?" Zephyr: "Oh, just exploring." Ghast: "This is our territory, so you can stay if you want, but just don't attack us."
- The ghasts reveal they seized the territory from a human or elf, picking up a gnawed bone to demonstrate.
  - Zephyr asks, "From this person, was he a human?" The ghast answers, "I think so. Maybe an elf."
- One ghast crunches on the bone and confirms it was human.
  - "He pauses, puts the bone in his mouth, crunches. No, definitely human."
- Zephyr threatens to kill the ghasts for killing someone, asking for their last words.
  - Zephyr: "Well, now you killed someone, so I'm gonna have to kill you. Any last words?" Sequoia: "Wait, are we fighting?" Zephyr: "Unless you leave this place right now."
- The ghasts propose arriving at a deal instead of fighting.
  - Ghast: "why would I leave this place? Couldn't we arrive at some deal?"
- Zephyr asks if the ghasts will work under the party, and the ghasts ask who the party works for.
  - Zephyr: "Okay, do you want to work under me?" Ghast: "Who do you work for?"
- The party claims to work for the Earth Temple.
- The ghasts immediately declare allegiance to the Earth Temple, shouting 'Hail the Earth Temple!'
  - "Yes, he goes, we are all hail the Earth Temple." Zephyr echoes: "Oh, hail the Earth Temple."
- An insight check reveals the ghasts are willing to go along with the fiction of being members of the Earth Temple for the moment.
  - Zephyr rolls 17 and Sequoia rolls 14.
- The party agrees to this arrangement, effectively claiming the ghasts' territory as a subsidiary of the Earth Temple.
  - Zephyr: "The greatest deal maker."
  - The DM marks the room on his map ("these in room seven") and adds: "You have now added to the glory of Romag's Empire."
- The DM reminds the party that they still haven't found a way to open the gate, which is "made of wood and steel." The party smashes it open with a hammer.

- The party decides to make it official that they own the newly explored territory.

  - Zephyr: "Yeah, when do we start getting money for this?" The DM: "You should go ask Romag that question." Sequoia: "You know, that's a great question."

  - Sequoia interjects a correction: "The southeast."

  - Romag: "Oh, no, no, no, no. We're not going to kill you two. You two are far too valuable. We have prisoners for that."
  - Zephyr: "Yeah, you see?... It was all a joke." Calmer starts to crack up.

  - Romag: "Why, an earth temple ritual. I know, Calmer. I know you wish to show your loyalty to the earthen, Elder Elemental, earthen god. Together we shall rip their bodies apart."

  - Calmer: "Are we really gonna go through with this?" Zephyr: "Uh, yes."

## NPCs

### Harpies

Winged creatures that use enchanting songs to charm adventurers. They position themselves thirty-four feet up in the air and attack with claws. When their plans are thwarted, they become furious and swoop down to attack party members.

- Their armor class is 11; the DM calls it "very low AC."
- The song forces a DC 11 Wisdom save. Taking damage lets a charmed creature re-roll.
- They follow hit-and-run tactics: they swoop down to attack, then fly away and provoke reactions and attacks of opportunity.
- They target whoever looks vulnerable, going after Zephyr once Calmer is paralyzed ("ooh, tasty").
- One taunts Calmer: "Yeah! You can't hit me."
- The last survivor sat in the rafters snarling and ignored the party until Zephyr attacked it.

### Ghouls

Undead creatures that attack with claws and bites, possessing the ability to paralyze their targets with a DC 10 constitution save. They have an armor class of twelve. Three initially emerged from a southern door, and four more later entered from the north. One ghoul initially focused on eating a dead harpy before joining combat.

- Their base initiative is 12. The northern group of four had 22 hit points.
- One bite dealt 7 damage, 3 of it necrotic.
- The table discussed elves being immune to the paralysis. As the DM finally ruled it, the paralysis lasts only a turn rather than until a successful save.
- The four from the north announced themselves with "Foo! Foo!"

### Calmer

A cleric and party member who wields a mace of smiting and uses Sacred Flame in battle. He provides crucial healing during combat, restoring nineteen and twenty-three hit points to party members. He expresses discomfort and weariness with Zephyr's decisions and is deeply troubled by Romag's planned ritual involving prisoners.

- He wears plate +1 and is the party's only real tank, though his Dexterity is "so awful."
- He conserves resources: "I'm not going to burn a third-level slot on these guys."
- His battle cries include "have no fear. I am here," "die, creature of the dark," and a threat to "roll my Spirit Guardians."
- He refused to check the chest for Zephyr: "i'm not a rogue you're a rogue."
- He was charmed early in the fight and later briefly paralyzed by a ghoul.

### Ghasts

Two territorial undead creatures encountered in an antechamber with obscene paintings. They claim to have seized the territory from a human or elf, demonstrating this by crunching on a gnawed bone. They feign allegiance to the Earth Temple when confronted by the party, though an insight check reveals they are merely playing along for their own benefit.

- They first offered coexistence: "you can stay if you want, but just don't attack us."
- When threatened, they bargained rather than fought: "Couldn't we arrive at some deal?"
- They were not killed. Romag believes they have been weakened by the loss of their ghoul and harpy power base.

### Romag

The leader of the Earth Temple who sits in his office looking very pleased with the party's territorial expansion. He delivers passionate tirades about the power of the earth crushing everything to dust. He operates on a system where the party brings treasure to the temple and pays him a portion rather than being paid directly. He proposes a loyalty ritual involving prisoners where bodies will be ripped apart, and instructs Hartsch to prepare for this ceremony.

- He resents that the ghasts, ghouls, and harpies "ever since my predecessor was elevated to the deep, the deep temple, have refused to bow down to me."
- He considers the party "far too valuable" to sacrifice: "We have prisoners for that."
- He frames the ritual as loyalty to "the earthen, Elder Elemental, earthen god."
- He scheduled the ceremony for "tomorrow," which will need "a major preparation."
- The DM refers to the party's holdings as part of "Romag's Empire."

### Hartsch

Romag's subordinate in the Earth Temple. Romag turns to him to prepare the ceremony, then tells him to prepare the prisoners for the ritual.
