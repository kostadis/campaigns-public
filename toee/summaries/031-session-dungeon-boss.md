# Chapter 31 Dungeon Boss

Date: 2026-05-24

## Summary
The session opened with the DM reading the recap of Chapter 26, "Attrition": the sealed Greater Temple door, the wraith and its specters, Hartsch's betrayal of the party's two-pronged plan, the rescue of Captain Dren Halveth's Broken Blades, Calmer's triumphant turning of the undead ("Who's the man? … Who is the man?"), Sequoia cutting down the bribed bugbear, and the gnoll ambush at the newly exposed secret door. Before play resumed, the DM reminded the table how the war worked. The Greater Temple wanted the fighting to end, and since the party's goal was to destroy the temple, "having them slaughter each other is not a bad outcome." These were chaotic evil cultists driven by power, so "pretty much all you have to do is let them keep fighting and periodically stab one in the back."

With Calmer badly wounded and carried off to safety by Captain Dren Halveth and his mercenaries, the remaining members of the party slipped through the secret door that had been exposed during their skirmish with the gnolls and descended into the Air Temple. Those three were Zephyr, Sequoia, and Zinnia. Inside the door were stairs leading down, which the DM warned probably led to the barricaded lower temple, and a passageway leading off to the southwest. One of Dren's mercenaries came along as backup with orders to protect the boss. Because the mercenaries were "anything but stealthy," the party left him guarding the doorway against anyone coming in from behind.

The passage ended at a curtain. Zephyr peeked through, and it opened into a sparse, gray-carpeted room lit by dull metal lamps. A crock half-filled with mead sat on the table, a narrow bed stood in the northeast corner, and the shelves were lined with scrolls, books, foodstuffs, and human and demi-human skulls. There, seated in one of two upholstered chairs beside a small table, was an old man. He was ancient even by the standards of evil cultists, and he looked up at them with tired, cunning eyes and said simply, "Come, sit down. I've been waiting for you." The party walked in and sat down, and when he began to talk they sat "silently and menacingly." The old man appreciated the menace.

The man introduced himself as Kelno, the leader of the Air Temple. He wasted no time laying out the situation with the precision of someone who had been rehearsing the conversation for weeks. He explained that the party's arrival and promotion of Hartsch had triggered a war between the prophets, and that the war would only end when two of the four prophets were dead. Kelno pointed out that the party had to choose who to keep alive. He revealed that Belsornig had already sent assassins to Hartsch's antechamber: "By tonight, your puppet is meat." He also said Alrrem was already dying. He explained what nobody had told them: when the war ends, the great doors open, and whoever is standing on top becomes the Greater Temple's new partner up here. "You never asked what the rules were, did you?" The last competition had been won by the former head of the Earth Temple (by the registry's succession ruling, Barkinar).

As the weakest of the prophets, Kelno offered no negotiation: he would clearly be their number two. "So you can kill me. I have the worst guards in the dungeon," but then they would walk into the Greater Temple alone with no Air Temple, no goblin eyes in the dark, and no bribery network. He tapped a note on the table. It was a message from Belsornig himself, detailing the assassination plot against Hartsch, and it contained a chilling instruction: if the group known as the Obsidian Edge appeared, his agents were to withdraw and not engage. Kelno's offer was blunt and practical. If the party killed Belsornig, Kelno would serve as their subordinate, offering his goblins, his informants, and his network in exchange for his life and his office.

Asked whether he was for or against the temple, Kelno smiled and swore loyalty to the power of air: "I am for chaos, but I'm also for living."

Seeing the choices in front of them, the party decided the best option was to let Hartsch and Alrrem die and to kill Belsornig. They agreed to the deal not a moment too soon. Zephyr's terms were "We won't kill you for his news." A delighted Kelno replied, "I think this is the beginning of a wonderful relationship." He also handed over a Dagger of Venom, which he had been saving for Belsornig, and Zephyr passed it to Sequoia.

After the deal was struck, the party searched his quarters under the pretext of a formality between new partners. They found a ledger of payments on his desk and, more tellingly, a note reading "set out the second chair." It showed that Kelno had been waiting for them for two weeks, having anticipated their arrival from the very moment they first appeared in the temple. The DM explained that Kelno had seen them as a wild card. He was the weakest of the four, the one everyone else had always written off and expected to die, and he had realized "there is a way I could survive." Zephyr asked Kelno to post guards so that no one would interrupt their business at the Water Temple.

Captain Dren then arrived with urgent news. He had first deposited Calmer upstairs ("oh my god, that guy whines a lot"). His report was that Hartsch was finished and Belsornig's forces were at his door. Belsornig himself was pulling his treasury out through the south route: "slow, heavy, vulnerable." The DM stressed that Dren was "the only person in this temple who might be loyal to you." The party therefore ordered Dren to remain behind with his mercenaries to guard Calmer, and then they moved toward the Water Temple.

The Water Temple was a breathtaking and unsettling place. Its massive hall stretched forty feet wide and ninety feet long beneath a vaulted ceiling thirty feet overhead, where buttresses and arches formed a shadowy tracery. Its walls and floors were covered in polished azurite-malachite that swirled with deep blue and green. Some enchantment had been placed upon the stone, causing a soft, cloudy, greenish luminosity to seep from the walls and floor, so that the entire chamber felt as though it lay at the bottom of the sea. A great sheet of bronze covered the middle of the west wall. At the center stood a great bronze altar sculpted with marine creatures, its concave top forming a shallow basin filled with water, coins, gems, and seashells. In an alcove nearby loomed a horrific serpentine idol depicting a writhing, amorphous mass of fish, eels, octopi, and other marine monsters. It stood eight feet high and nearly as broad, with a drapery of seaweed and water covering the alcove's back wall. Four bronze gargoyle statues coated in verdigris watched from atop corner plinths. The air smelled of the sea, and the silence was heavy. The party touched nothing, since this was not the time and place to go after treasure.

The party moved through the hall without a sound and reached a door to the west. Zephyr chose it because "I'd rather not go into many different rooms." Their attempt to smash it open failed: Zinnia, the strongest of the three, bounced off the door twice. The guards within were alerted and demanded, "You're not supposed to be here? Who is this?" Sequoia's first answer was "Housekeeping!" Rather than panic, the party improvised and claimed to be messengers bearing news of an assassination. The guards, believing them to be allies, let slip their own plans: "All that remains is for us to defeat those pompous asses known as the obsidian Edge." Then they opened the door. The moment it swung wide, the party struck. The DM granted them surprise "given how well you role-played this."

Sequoia and Zinnia cut down the first cult fanatic in a rapid, brutal exchange. Zephyr then landed a critical blow on the second, using the assassin's automatic critical against surprised foes, and Sequoia finished him. The way to Belsornig's inner sanctum was open.

Moving with exceptional care, the party crept into the chamber where Belsornig stood. He was preoccupied and completely unaware that his guards were already dead. The party's stealth rolls were 26, 20 and 18. Kelno had previously entrusted the group with a Dagger of Venom he had been saving for exactly this purpose, and now the moment had come. The party fell upon Belsornig in a coordinated storm of strikes, with Sequoia, Zinnia, and Zephyr working in lethal tandem; the DM called it "a staggering amount of damage in one round." Before the Prophet of Water could mount any real resistance, Zephyr drove the poisoned blade home. At the table Zephyr's player wavered over spending the poison, since Belsornig was already down to a single hit point ("It's useless to use it"), and the killing stroke landed for 8 damage.

As Belsornig fell, a deep gong resonated through the entire level. The party moved to get back to their quarters on the first level, heading to the Air Temple. Zephyr's reasoning was "Well, we don't know, but we should be there." As they were about to climb up the stairs, a group of bugbears dressed in black arrived from multiple directions, coming through the previously barred secret door, the eastern door, and the western door. They gruffly announced that Barkinar wished to speak to the new rulers of the Upper Temple. When Zephyr said they would go see him, he was corrected: "he will see you." The sheer number of bugbears surrounding them made the party realize the true power of the Greater Temple in an instant. The fact that the bugbears came through the eastern and western doors reinforced the notion that the Greater Temple had forces on the second level as well. On an Insight roll of 26, Zephyr concluded they had come from areas the party had not yet explored. Zephyr's attempt at hospitality was to ask the bugbears, "Would you like some meat?"

The bugbears asked where the cleric was ("The cleric is in charge, of course"), and the party assured them he was injured but would recover. Then a voice called out from the stairs leading down. It was cold, composed, and unmistakably powerful: "That was efficient. Come down, bring your puppet master if he is alive. We should talk." Barkinar waited below. The bugbears brought the injured Calmer down to join them, and together the party entered a large, heavily guarded room at the foot of the stairs leading down toward the Greater Temple. It was a room full of tables and doors where, the DM made clear, a fight would not be survivable. Barkinar stood there flanked by ogre bodyguards and a hill giant, and Kelno stood beside the party.

Barkinar took Calmer for the party's leader, the "puppet master" he had told them to bring. He looked at them with something approaching genuine admiration and said, "Romag. Hartsch. Alrrem. Belsornig. Problems I had on a list yesterday morning, and four problems gone by sundown. You will forgive me if I'm impressed." He declared the upper temple theirs and outlined the work ahead:
- leaderless cultists in the Fire Temple
- trolls on contract in the Water Temple
- a treasury to account for
- mercenaries in need of a chain of command
- a chest previously delivered to Romag that the Greater Temple had a particular interest in recovering

None of it was urgent "in the sense that it will burn you alive tomorrow," he said, and the DM noted that the word "tomorrow" carried real threat. All of it was urgent "in the sense that the upper temple should look organized when I come back through… these steps." He warned them to be civil toward the Lord Wizard Senshock, who was ambitious and paranoid but useful, and to let him underestimate them. A wizard named Falrinth, hiding from the Greater Temple for reasons of his own, was mentioned as a matter to be discussed later. "For now, organize your house. We'll be in touch."

Then, without waiting for a response, Barkinar turned, spoke quietly to one of his ogre bodyguards, and walked back down into the dark. He left the party standing in the heart of the very temple they had come to destroy, now its rulers, with a week before the next check-in.

The DM congratulated the table on "definitely the most surprising" outcome of the roughly five times he had run the adventure ("Nobody ever figures out how to start a war"). He welcomed them to management and warned that they now had to worry about murder-hobo adventurers. Everybody gained a level.

The leader of the Broken Blades mercenaries who remained loyal to the party. He deposited the injured Calmer safely upstairs and delivered urgent tactical updates on the status of the other temple prophets before being ordered to stand guard. He complained that Calmer "whines a lot" and deferred decisions to the party ("You tell me"). The DM called him the only person in the temple who might be loyal to the party. One of his mercenaries accompanied the party into the Air Temple and was left guarding the doorway.

### Alrrem
The Prophet of Fire, reported by Kelno to already be on his pyre and not expected to survive ("he won't last until the morning"). He does not appear directly in this session.

### Kelno
An old, cunning, and tired-looking cultist who leads the Air Temple. He had been anticipating the party's arrival for two weeks, viewing them as a wildcard he could leverage for his own survival. He explained that the party's promotion of Hartsch triggered a war that only ends when two prophets are dead. Kelno pointed out he was the weakest prophet and offered no negotiation: if the party killed Belsornig, he would serve as their clear number two, offering his goblins and informant network in exchange for his life and his office.

He admitted he had "the worst guards in the dungeon" and very few troops. The DM called him "a quintessential survivor" who "knows a lot about what's going on, because nobody pays any attention to him." Asked where his loyalties lay, he swore to the power of air and said, "I am for chaos, but I'm also for living." He handed the party a Dagger of Venom to use on Belsornig. He was present beside the party when Barkinar addressed them.

### Belsornig
The Prophet of Water and the party's primary target this session. He had sent assassins to kill Hartsch and was, by Dren's report, pulling his treasury out the south route. The party found him preoccupied, ambushed him and killed him in his inner sanctum using the Dagger of Venom.

His note shows his orders that the Obsidian Edge "are not to be killed." His statistics at the table were AC 16 and initiative 14. He was dropped to 1 hit point within the surprise rounds before the final 8-damage blow.

### Cult Fanatics
Two guards of the Water Temple who had pre-cast a spiritual weapon in anticipation of trouble. They were tricked into opening their door by the party's bluff before being swiftly and brutally eliminated.

At the table they had AC 13 and initiative 12. Before opening the door they gloated that, with Alrrem dying, "All that remains is for us to defeat those pompous asses known as the obsidian Edge."

### Senshock
The Lord Wizard of the Greater Temple, described by Barkinar as ambitious and paranoid but useful. Barkinar said he "has opinions about new arrivals." The party was warned to be civil and let him underestimate them.

### Falrinth
A wizard mentioned by Barkinar as someone who has been hiding from the Greater Temple for his own mysterious reasons, to be discussed with the party at a later time ("We could talk about Falrinth later").

### Barkinar
The Commander of the Greater Temple's Troops, who commended the party for eliminating the elemental prophets. He took Calmer for the party's leader.

He first spoke as a voice from the stairs ("That was efficient. Come down, bring your puppet master if he is alive. We should talk."). He received the party in a room held by a hill giant and ogre bodyguards. He laid out the upper temple's outstanding business and gave them a week before he would "come back through… these steps." Then he left after a quiet word with an ogre bodyguard.

### Greater Temple Bugbears
A large force of bugbears dressed in black who emerged from the secret door, the eastern door and the western door after the gong sounded. They announced that Barkinar wished to speak to the new rulers of the Upper Temple, corrected Zephyr ("he will see you"), and asked after the cleric, assuming "The cleric is in charge, of course." They brought Calmer down to the meeting.

## Locations
### Air Temple
A section of the temple complex accessible via a secret door, featuring stairs leading down to the lower levels and a passageway to the southwest. Kelno's quarters within are characterized by gray carpeting, plaster walls, Spartan furniture, and shelves lined with skulls, scrolls, and foodstuffs.

The quarters are entered through a curtain. They are dimly lit by grayish metal lamps and furnished with a pair of upholstered chairs, a table bearing dishes and a crock half-filled with mead, and a narrow bed in the northeast corner. The stairs down are among the doorways the DM identified as leading toward the Greater Temple. The meeting with Barkinar took place in a large room at the foot of the stairs.

### Earth Temple
The area where the party previously sought refuge and where Hartsch's forces were stationed. The party used it as a base of operations before pressing into the Water Temple.

### Water Temple
A grand 40-by-90-foot hall with 30-foot arched ceilings and walls of polished azurite-malachite that emit a soft greenish glow, giving the impression of being deep underwater. The air smells of the sea. It contains a large bronze altar filled with water, coins, and gems, a horrific serpentine idol, and four bronze gargoyle statues atop corner plinths. It served as the final stronghold of Belsornig.

Its buttresses and arches hide the true height of the ceiling. A great sheet of bronze covers the middle of the west wall. Its bronze doors and fountain are coated in verdigris. Doorways lead off to the east and west, and the party took the western door toward Belsornig's guards. Kelno noted it was lightly guarded at the time of the assault.

### Belsornig's Inner Sanctum
The private chamber of the Prophet of Water, located past the main Water Temple hall, where Belsornig was cornered and killed by the party. It lies beyond the guard room of the two cult fanatics behind the western door.

### Greater Temple
The power below the upper temple, whose bugbears wear black. After the upper temple prophets fell, the party was summoned down the stairs toward it, to a large room held by a hill giant and ogre bodyguards.

The Greater Temple stays sealed during the lesser temples' war and opens its doors when the war ends. The DM described the room as full of tables and doors, and made plain that a fight there would not be survivable. Insight revealed that its forces also occupy unexplored parts of the second level.

## Items
### Belsornig's Note
A strategic message from Belsornig shown to the party by Kelno, detailing the assassination plot against Hartsch and containing instructions for his agents to withdraw and not engage if the 'Obsidian Edge' appeared.

A book found on Kelno's desk containing records of payments made to various individuals throughout the temple, revealing the extent of his financial network.

### Serpentine Idol
An eight-foot-high statue of serpentine stone in the Water Temple depicting a writhing, amorphous mass of fish, eels, octopi, and other marine monsters, standing near the bronze altar. It stands in the mouth of a 20-foot alcove whose back wall is covered by a drapery of seaweed and water.

### Bronze Sheet
A great sheet of bronze set into the middle of the Water Temple's west wall. It was noted by the DM and left unexamined.

### Dagger of Venom
A magical poisoned dagger provided by Kelno to the party, which he had been saving for use against his enemies. It was ultimately used by Zephyr to deliver the killing blow to Belsornig.

Kelno said, "I've been saving this to kill those Belsornig for some time." Zephyr initially handed it to Sequoia. The DM did not specify the strength of its poison.

### Chest of Rao
A chest previously delivered to the Prophet of Earth, Romag, that Barkinar specifically mentioned as something the Greater Temple has a significant interest in recovering. He listed it among the upper temple's practical matters, alongside the unaccounted treasury.
