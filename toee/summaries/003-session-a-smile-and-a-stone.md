# Chapter 03 A Smile and a Stone

Date: 2024-11-22

## Summary

At the Moathouse, the fight with the giant frogs from last session went on, with two frogs left. Zinnia moved with lethal precision, killing one wounded frog outright and badly injuring another. Calmer, the war cleric of St. Cuthbert, rushed forward with his mace in a display of courage but completely missed. The remaining frog tried to bite and swallow the small Sequoia, but missed; Sequoia then delivered the killing blow, briefly clearing the way to the drawbridge.

Calmer insisted on taking the lead across the bridge. As a cleric of St. Cuthbert, he reasoned that his place was at the front, where others could see and believe in him. Thorne (the Duke), the man-at-arms supplied by Rannos and Gremag, remained outside with the horses. Just as the party crossed, three more frogs landed on the drawbridge. The combined mass of adventurers and monsters caused the rotten structure to collapse, plunging Calmer and Sequoia into the water and injuring them. Their movement was hampered in the moat while the frogs attacked in their natural element.

A frog bit Calmer and knocked him unconscious almost immediately. Zinnia jumped down from the wreckage, killed one frog, and later abandoned his attack to stabilize the cleric after Calmer failed two death saves. Zephyr fought through the remaining creatures with short-sword strikes, while Sequoia dealt fifteen points of damage to the final frog. Zephyr finished it with a natural twenty. Thorne looked over the carnage and remarked, “So I guess we're taking a short rest.” The group did exactly that, and Calmer used divine magic to restore himself.

Thorne’s sympathy proved limited. He expressed the hope that the party would be “more successful” from then on, with no more people being knocked unconscious or eaten alive by frogs. His tone left the party with the distinct impression that he was something of a jerk. Calmer initially suggested that someone quieter should lead next, but when encouraged to remain in front, he reclaimed his religious resolve: “St. Cuthbert would not approve if I was to take a step back at this time. Always forward. Always forward. Always looking forward.”

Inside the Moathouse, the party moved with careful silence through a ruined courtyard. A door lay to the southwest and stairs to the northeast, while the entire place appeared long abandoned. Zephyr examined the steps and found signs of human, animal, and possibly other traffic. Dirt and rubble obscured the route, but someone had unquestionably been using it. Calmer urged caution and suggested that the rogues scout ahead while he guarded against an attack from behind.

The scouts entered what had once been a grand audience chamber. Tattered banners and tapestries hung from the walls; destroyed furniture, heaps of rotting cloth, leaves, dirt, and cobwebs filled the room. Broken beams and chunks of stone protruded from above, showing that the upper stories were largely destroyed and impassable to anything much larger than a rat. Despite the devastation, Sequoia discovered a concealed trail leading directly to a door. Whoever used the path had invested considerable effort in hiding their movements. Listening at the door, he heard the muffled sounds of a group within.

Zephyr proposed hiding nearby and using magic to create a sound beside the door. The door’s recent repairs and heavy reinforcement made it clear that it could not simply be opened by magic, but the illusory noise drew out one of the occupants. A dirty, rough-looking brigand in tattered clothes opened the door to investigate. The party could not make out what he said before he withdrew and shut it again, after which they heard increased movement inside. The bandits might be preparing to attack or flee, and every moment of delay gave them more time to do either.

The party chose stealth over a frontal charge. Zephyr slipped through the door and found eight bandits inside, hurriedly packing their belongings to escape. Four wore ordinary leather armor, while several others had better equipment—including studded leather, shields, and heavier protection. The toughest and best-equipped man stood near the door and was plainly their leader. Zephyr squeezed inside while the bandits were distracted, struck the leader with a devastating sneak attack for twelve points of damage, and killed him instantly. He then wounded another bandit and darted back through the door, narrowly avoiding a clumsy swing from the man’s club.

Zinnia entered next and finished the bandit Zephyr had wounded with a powerful strike to the chest before withdrawing from the room full of armed men. Sequoia followed, cutting down a weaker bandit with his short swords and daggers before retreating. With their leader dead and their companions falling to attackers who repeatedly vanished through the doorway, the survivors broke. Three fled through an arrow slit that had been widened into an escape route. Sequoia killed another bandit, then moved to block the remaining man’s escape. Zinnia closed in and used unarmed strikes to knock the final survivor unconscious rather than kill him.

Calmer entered after the fight and announced, “I think it's important that we should all calm down,” prompting immediate groans from the others.

The captured bandit was revived and questioned. The combined effect of Sequoia’s gruff charm, Zephyr’s tiefling presence, and Calmer’s supposedly calming influence caused him to break down in tears. He identified himself as Voss and insisted that he was only a common bandit. He described losing his property, running out of money, and falling into drinking and gambling before being taken in by the gang. He regretted the stealing and robbery, he said, but “a guy's got to eat and live.”

Voss reacted to questions about the Temple of Elemental Evil with genuine confusion: “The what?” He claimed the bandits had occupied the Moathouse precisely because people avoided the place due to its historical connection to the Temple. “But we don't have any affiliation with them. I mean, I don't even know who they are. This is just where we are.” When Sequoia told him that he had nevertheless been working indirectly for the Temple, Voss grew deeply worried and pleaded that, since the connection was indirect, perhaps they could simply say he had not known. Zephyr carefully assessed him and concluded that he was telling the truth: Voss honestly believed the ruins were abandoned and knew nothing about a cult presence.

A search of the bandits uncovered a flat river rock inscribed with an eye and a tear. The sight of it made Voss panic. Calmer’s attempt to calm him with a lecture about righteousness failed, but Sequoia coaxed out an explanation. Voss said, “Look, I don't know what this symbol is, but apparently it was the symbol that the big boss gave us.” Zephyr judged that Voss was simply too low in the organization to know more. The symbol’s meaning remained unknown, but it provided the first tangible suggestion that the seemingly ordinary bandits answered to someone beyond their dead local leader.

## Locations

### The Moathouse

An abandoned and ruined fortification surrounded by stagnant water, historically associated with the Temple of Elemental Evil. The site’s ominous reputation keeps ordinary travelers away, making it attractive to bandits seeking an isolated refuge. Giant frogs lurk in and around its moat.

Although much of the structure appears long deserted, hidden traffic through the courtyard and great hall proves that humans, animals, and perhaps other creatures have been using it. Its current occupants have repaired and reinforced at least one door, carefully concealed their tracks, and widened a broken arrow slit into an escape route.

### The Moathouse Drawbridge

A rotting wooden bridge spanning the stagnant moat at the entrance to the ruins. Three giant frogs landed on it while the party crossed, and the combined weight caused the entire structure to collapse. Calmer and Sequoia were dumped into the water and injured, while Zephyr and Zinnia initially remained atop the wreckage. The collapse turned the moat into a confined battlefield where movement was difficult and the frogs held the advantage.

### The Great Hall

A ruined audience chamber that was once richly appointed. Tattered banners and tapestries cling to the walls, while destroyed furniture and heaps of rotting cloth fill the corners. Leaves, dirt, and cobwebs cover the interior. Broken beams and chunks of stone protrude through the ceiling, revealing that the upper floors have collapsed and are likely impassable to anything larger than a rat.

Despite its despoiled appearance, Sequoia found a deliberately concealed path of recent foot traffic leading across the hall to a heavily repaired and barricaded door. Beyond it, eight bandits used an adjoining room as a refuge and staging area. A widened arrow slit provided them with an emergency exit.

## NPCs

### Thorne (the Duke)

A man-at-arms provided by Rannos and Gremag who accompanies the party to the Moathouse but remains outside with the horses. After the drawbridge disaster, he dryly observes, “So I guess we're taking a short rest.”

Thorne says he has the horses and other practical matters under control while the party proceeds. His hope that there will be no more people knocked unconscious or eaten by frogs sounds more mocking than encouraging, leading the group to conclude that he is something of a jerk.

### Calmer

A war cleric of St. Cuthbert who believes his faith obliges him to lead visibly from the front. He crosses the Moathouse drawbridge first, only to fall into the water and be knocked unconscious by a giant frog. After failing two death saves, he is stabilized by Zinnia and later heals himself with divine magic.

Calmer favors caution once inside the ruins, recommending that the rogues scout ahead while he protects the rear. He is fond of wordplay based on his own name, entering the bandit room to declare, “I think it's important that we should all calm down.” His initial suggestion that someone quieter take the lead is short-lived; he decides that St. Cuthbert would disapprove of retreat and recommits himself to “Always forward.”

### Voss

A low-ranking bandit captured by the party. Voss claims to have lost his property and money before falling into drinking, gambling, and eventually robbery. He presents himself as a desperate man accepted by a gang when he had nowhere else to turn.

Voss knows the Moathouse was once the Temple of Elemental Evil’s place, but denies any tie to the Temple and insists the bandits occupied the Moathouse only because its reputation kept others away. He becomes frightened when Sequoia says the gang may have served the Temple indirectly. He knows that the inscribed river rock was a symbol supplied by the bandits’ “big boss,” but claims not to understand its meaning. His reactions are judged sincere, and Zephyr concludes that he is too low-ranking to possess useful knowledge of the larger organization.

### Bandit Leader

The toughest and most heavily armored member of the brigand group occupying the Moathouse. His superior equipment and position near the door mark him as the local leader. While he and his men are distracted by preparations to flee, Zephyr slips into the room and kills him instantly with a twelve-point sneak attack. His sudden death contributes heavily to the collapse of the remaining bandits’ morale.

## Items

### Inscribed River Rock

A flat river rock recovered while searching the bandits, marked with the image of an eye and a tear. Voss panics when questioned about it and eventually explains, “Look, I don't know what this symbol is, but apparently it was the symbol that the big boss gave us.”

Voss does not know the symbol’s meaning, and the party judges him too low in the hierarchy to identify the person or organization behind it. The stone nevertheless hints that the bandits answered to someone above their own leader.
