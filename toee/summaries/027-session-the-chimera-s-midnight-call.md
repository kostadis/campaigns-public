# Chapter 27 The Chimera's Midnight Call

Date: not stated in chapter
Session date: 2026-03-11

## Summary

The party's attempt at a long rest was shattered in the dead of night when Zinnia's sharp senses caught the unmistakable sound of dragging chains and a haunting, animalistic chorus echoing from beyond their southern door: the guttural roar of a lion twisted together with the frantic, deranged bleating of a giant goat. He roused his companions just in time, for moments later the door exploded inward as a monstrous Chimera came crashing through, its three heads snarling with demonic fury. Behind it, a Fire Temple warlock gripped the beast's chain and bellowed a declaration of war. He jeered that Romag hid behind his new walls and his new champion, and proclaimed that Alrrem, the all-consuming, had brought the strength to tear those walls down: break their bones, and leave nothing but meat for the earth. The creature was a horrifying amalgamation of the hindquarters of a great goat, the powerful forequarters of a lion, and the leathery wings of a dragon. Each head was driven by its own savage instinct to hunt, hoard, and destroy. The party had only just agreed to let the theft of Romag's prisoners be pinned on the Fire Temple, and the attack seemed to confirm that the Fire Temple now considered them a serious enemy. As Zephyr put it, the Fire Temple's arrival would "help with blaming this on the fire temple."

The battle that followed was desperate and brutal. Sequoia charged the beast head-on, but alone at its flank he could not land a sneak attack. He was quickly overwhelmed, battered into unconsciousness by the combined fury of the Chimera's attacks and the warlock's crackling magical blasts, even with Uncanny Dodge blunting one blow. Calmer called upon divine power to mend Sequoia's wounds and bring him back to his feet, even as the warlock continued to goad the beast forward. It was Zephyr who turned the tide. Reading that the warlock was the one driving the creature, he slipped past the rampaging monster and ripped the control chain clean from the warlock's hands, leaving the man crying out, "What have you done?" Zinnia darted in to deliver a series of precise strikes before retreating to safety. The Chimera, now free of its handler, thrashed wildly, its chain whipping through the air as the warlock scrambled desperately to reclaim it.

With the beast unbound, chaos reigned. A bugbear tried to steer the Chimera toward the warlock by pointing his weapon at it. The Chimera unleashed a devastating breath of fire upon both the warlock and the bugbear, scorching them badly, then raked the warlock again with its claws. The warlock, badly burned and barely standing, screamed, "Are you mad? It'll kill us all." Zephyr then managed to snatch up the fallen chain once more in a bold attempt to dominate the creature, and Zinnia silenced the warlock permanently with a flurry of devastating blows. The Fire Temple's warlock lay dead on the stone floor. The Chimera, however, was far from finished, and it turned its full, unhinged fury upon those who remained.

Zephyr bore the brunt of the beast's wrath, suffering a savage onslaught of bites and claws that left the rogue crumpled and unconscious. Calmer first made a show of asking Sequoia whether he should bother healing Zephyr at all. Muttering something about having only walking band-aids to offer, he then called upon his divine gifts once more to pull Zephyr back from the edge of death. He made sure everyone remembered it was done "for the greater glory of the Earth Temple." A successful animal-handling read told Zephyr he would need to seize the chain and intimidate the beast, but the Chimera wrenched it out of reach. Sequoia, having recovered and hidden himself behind a chest, loosed a precise shot that left the Chimera at a single hit point. The enraged beast charged him before he could finish it and knocked him unconscious with a series of brutal strikes. It was Calmer who ended it, invoking the tolling resonance of necrotic power that rang out like a funeral bell and dropped the Chimera dead upon the cold stone floor. Afterward, Calmer remarked that if not for the grace of the earthen elemental god, they would all be dead.

In the quiet aftermath, the party took a short rest. Zinnia began to wonder whether Calmer's devotion to the Earth Temple was still an act. Sitting down with Calmer, he concluded that Calmer was on the border, and that if the party kept treating him like dirt, he might actually cross the line. Then he got Calmer a glass of mead. The party then searched the warlock's charred remains and uncovered a trove of revealing items. A wax-sealed ledger bore the crest of a crimson ship. It was a bill of sale from Captain Vesrek of the Crimson Trader, detailing the purchase of "one tri-headed exotic asset" paid in full by the Fire Temple. Alrrem, it seemed, was secretly importing monsters to make up for his lack of actual troops. More damning still, Hartsch would later recognize Vesrek as an ally of Belsornig, the prophet of the Water Temple. That suggested the Water Temple had orchestrated the whole affair as a calculated win-win: either the party died, or the Fire Temple bankrupted itself. The warlock also carried several other items:

- a heavy gold amulet bearing the eight-pointed symbol of the Fire Temple;
- a potion of fire resistance he had never managed to reach for in the chaos;
- a pouch of platinum and gold;
- a masterwork whip of alchemically treated red leather, which Sequoia claimed.

The heavy iron collar and chains used to control the beast were stamped with the marks of Nulb's river pirates. Someone in Nulb had been paid to deliver the monster to the temple.

The party brought their findings to Romag. Before they could finish, he thumped his fist down, declared that the Fire Temple had stolen the prisoners, and demanded an explanation. He seized on the news of the chimera attack: Alrrem had lost his werewolves and now his chimera, and "must be quaking in fear." Romag declared that the moment to strike against Alrrem had arrived and ordered the party to deal with the Fire Temple's weakened leader. He would not lead the army himself; he likened himself to the king in a game of chess. But it was a private meeting with Hartsch, Romag's sub-general, that proved far more illuminating. Hartsch laid out the political landscape of the temples in stark detail:

- **Alrrem and the Fire Temple.** Alrrem's power had been waning since the catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Emridy Meadows a decade prior. He had reportedly banished a usurping relative down to the Deep Temple. He had chased exotic monsters instead of mass troops. The loss of his werewolves, followed by the ruinous cost of the chimera, had left him nearly broken.
- **Kelno and the Air Temple.** The Air Temple under the prophet Kelno was dismissed outright as a farcical remnant, reduced to a handful of rooms and no meaningful power.
- **The Water Temple.** The Water Temple, however, remained a formidable rival. Hartsch called it the only other temple with the power to contest the Earth Temple, and warned that with the Fire Temple gone, its opportunity would be tremendous.

It was during this meeting that the party's ambitions crystallized into a bold and dangerous plan. Sequoia proposed provoking a three-way battle between the temples so that none came out on top, then slipping away to the temple's lower depths. The DM reminded the party of the coordinating force somewhere beneath the temple. Zinnia, using carefully chosen words and a disarming manner, drew out Hartsch's true feelings about Romag's passive, erosion-like style of leadership. Hartsch made no effort to hide his belief in the destructive, earthquake-like power of the earth, nor his desire to wield it. When Zinnia pressed further, Hartsch replied with thinly veiled intent: if a position of leadership were to suddenly open itself, he would absolutely consider himself worthy of it. The party recognized the signal for what it was: Hartsch wanted Romag removed, and he wanted them to do it. A plan took shape to assassinate Romag and install Hartsch as the Earth Temple's new leader. The ultimate aim was to engineer a three-way war between the Earth, Fire, and Water temples and destroy all three factions from within. The party quietly agreed that the time for subtlety was nearly over, with detailed planning left for the next session.

## Scenes

### 027.01 A Rude Awakening

#### While the party attempts to take a long rest, Zinnia hears strange noises outside their door, leading to a sudden and violent ambush.

- Before resting, the party settles on letting Romag discover that the prisoners are missing and blame the Fire Temple. Zephyr: "Yeah, fire temple did it."
- Passive perception decides who hears the noise. Zinnia has 16, Sequoia 11, and Calmer 10. Zinnia rolls a 17 and hears the noises; the rest of the party sleeps on.
- Zinnia wakes the party after hearing the sound of dragging chains and animalistic roars outside the southern door.
- A Chimera, controlled by a warlock from the Fire Temple, smashes through the door to attack the party.
- The warlock commands the beast to tear the party apart as retribution for their actions against the Fire Temple. He declares that Romag "hides behind his new walls and his new champion."
- The party identifies the attackers as Fire Temple warlocks. The DM notes they could have come up the stairs and around to the door.
- Zephyr sees the attack as useful cover: "since they're here in the fire temple, we will help with blaming this on the fire temple."
- The DM frames the attack: the Fire Temple has declared the party a serious enemy and "sent their big bad to kill you". He calls that "not entirely a bad outcome."
- Zephyr considers sending Calmer around to flank from the back, but it would take him four rounds, so he stays.
- The DM describes the chimera's origins:
  - Chimeras arose when mortals summoned Demogorgon, the Prince of Demons, to the world; unimpressed with the creatures around it, it twisted them into multi-headed monstrosities.
  - Its dragon head drives it to raid and hoard.
  - Its lion nature compels it to hunt and kill.
  - Its goat head gives it a stubborn streak that compels it to fight to the death.
- Sequoia goes first in initiative. Zinnia repositions near the broken secret door.
- Sequoia rushes the Chimera but is quickly overwhelmed by the beast's ferocity and the warlock's magical blasts.
  - With no ally adjacent to the beast, Sequoia's sneak attack does not apply. The first strike deals only 5 damage and the second misses.
  - The warlock holds the chain in one hand and casts Eldritch Blast with the other, landing two hits for 16 damage.
  - The enraged Chimera bites Sequoia. Uncanny Dodge halves the biggest attack, after Zephyr reminds Sequoia of it, but Sequoia still drops.
- Calmer looks at the situation and says, "this, as they say, is not good."
- Calmer uses divine magic to harm the Chimera and heal the fallen Sequoia.
  - Toll the Dead deals 6 damage; the DM says, "I rolled very badly."
  - A second-level Healing Word brings Sequoia back up to 12 hit points.
- Zephyr's insight check (13) reveals the warlock is holding the creature and goading it. Killing the warlock might make the creature stop attacking.
- Zephyr declares, "I bum rush the warlock." Offered a choice, he goes for the chain rather than the man, since the warlock has too many hit points to drop in one attack.
- Zephyr maneuvers past the beast to confront the warlock directly. His Acrobatics roll of 12 beats the warlock's 9, and he rips the control chain from the warlock's hands.
- The warlock is left terrified as he loses control of the monstrous Chimera: "What have you done?"
- Zephyr uses his bonus action to disengage.

### 027.02 The Chimera's Rampage

#### A chaotic battle ensues as the party attempts to neutralize a Chimera that has broken free from its Fire Temple handler.

- Zinnia strikes the Chimera and retreats to a safe distance.
  - The Chimera's AC is 14.
  - Zinnia deals 9 damage, then 11 with the extra attacks, for 19 in total, then moves away.
  - Sequoia disengages to join the others.
- The Chimera breaks free from its chain, terrifying its warlock handler.
  - It roars and sends the chain flying in a circle.
  - The warlock spends his action trying to grab the chain back and fails.
- A bugbear attempts to distract the Chimera by pointing a weapon at it, hoping it will attack the warlock. The beast unleashes a devastating breath attack on both the warlock and the bugbear (23 damage), then follows with a bite that misses and a claw that deals another 7 to the warlock.
- The warlock screams, "Are you mad? It'll kill us all."
- Zephyr successfully snatches the dragging chain from the ground while remaining stealthy. To get advantage, the DM requires a stealthy Sleight of Hand grab rather than Acrobatics; Zephyr succeeds on a 14.
- Zinnia delivers a lethal blow to the Fire Temple warlock before turning his attention back to the Chimera.
  - The warlock wears studded leather (AC 14). Zinnia deals 22 damage, killing him.
  - As an elven monk with 40 feet of movement, Zinnia uses Flurry of Blows to keep fighting rather than disengaging.
  - Zinnia hits the Chimera for 24 more; the DM: "That was a good choice."
- Sequoia's ranged attack misses.
- Zephyr asks, "Y'all want to do anything or just let them duke it out?" Sequoia suggests slipping out through the secret door: "Yeah, what if we just dip?"
- The Chimera focuses its fury on Zephyr, unleashing a flurry of bites and claw attacks that leave the rogue unconscious.
  - It makes three hits against AC 16.
  - Uncanny Dodge halves one attack, but Zephyr still takes 32 damage against a maximum of 28 hit points.
- Zephyr: "I'm dead."
- The bugbear flees the fight.
- Calmer uses divine magic to heal Zephyr, bringing him back into the fight.
  - Calmer first asks Sequoia whether he should heal Zephyr. Sequoia says yes, and Calmer reminds everyone it was done "for the greater glory of the Earth Temple."
  - Zephyr comes back up to 17 hit points.
  - Calmer follows with another Toll the Dead for 10 damage, and the creature is down to 51.
- Zephyr attempts to use the chain to intimidate and dominate the beast, but the Chimera thwarts the effort. An animal handling check of 20 tells him he needs to grab the chain and then intimidate the creature, but his Acrobatics roll of 12 fails to wrest it away.
- Zephyr and Zinnia fall back through the secret door. The DM notes the Chimera can in fact fit through it.
  - Zinnia hits for 25.
  - Zinnia uses Flurry of Blows again to disengage.
- Sequoia complains: "Wait, you guys can just abandon me and not give me a sneak up attack. Love it."
- Sequoia hides behind a chest and launches a precise ranged attack, leaving the Chimera on the brink of death.
  - Sequoia rolls a 23 on Stealth, and the beast cannot find him.
  - The arrow deals 25 damage and leaves the Chimera at 1 hit point.
- Sequoia: "No! … God damn it!"
- The Chimera charges Sequoia, knocking the halfling unconscious with a series of brutal strikes.
  - It bites, claws, and gores with its horns. All three would hit AC 14, and the first two drop Sequoia from 12 hit points.
  - The remaining claw swings at Calmer but misses his high armor class.
- During the charge, the table bickers over whose fault this is (see Memorable Moments).
- Calmer invokes necrotic power, finally slaying the Chimera as it stands over the fallen party members. He casts Toll the Dead, the bell tolls, and the Chimera "lies dead on the ground."
- Calmer: "well, that was a bit awkward." Zephyr: "We handled him easily."

- The party begins to consult with Hartsch about the layout of the temple and Alrrem's location.
  - Hartsch notes that "after 10 years, everybody knows where everybody lives" and produces a map, revealing the temple's second level.

- The DM points out that the party does have a cleric "having second thoughts about his religious upbringing."
- Zinnia agrees: "Because Romag doesn't do much. I feel like his officers would be not very satisfied."

### 027.05 Subverting the Earth Temple

#### The party meets privately with Hartsch to gauge his loyalty and ambition, and begins plotting to overthrow Romag.

- The party moves to their private quarters to meet with Hartsch away from Romag's ears.
  - Hartsch arrives a little later and asks, "What can I do for you?"
  - Zinnia opens by asking about plans for the raid.
- Hartsch sits down: "Oh, this was just to discuss the plans to the attack, of course. What else could it possibly be?"
- The party shows Hartsch the ledger.
- Hartsch reveals the history of Alrrem and the Fire Temple's recent financial struggles following the loss of their werewolves and the chimera.
  - Alrrem led the temples at the Battle of Emridy Meadows ten years ago. That defeat and the loss of the great artifact have haunted his rule.
  - Rumor holds that a sibling or relation tried to usurp Alrrem and was sent down to the Deep Temple, fate unknown.
  - While the Earth Temple invested in large numbers of troops, Alrrem's "vain views on war" led him to collect exotic monsters, "never thinking that perhaps mass counts for something." The DM likens these to wonder weapons instead of a large army of tanks.
  - The chimera "probably almost bankrupted him… this creates an opportunity for us."
- Hartsch explains that Vesrek, an ally of Belsornig the prophet of the Water Temple, was the one who arranged the chimera purchase. That suggests the Water Temple manipulated the situation as a win-win.
  - Hartsch on Vesrek: "Who is Vesrek is not important. What Vesrek is important."
  - Hartsch's reading: either the party died, or the Fire Temple went bankrupt and the Earth Temple would then attack it.
  - "The Water Temple is the only other temple that has the power to … contest us for power. With the Fire Temple gone, the opportunity for the Water Temple would be tremendous."
- Hartsch dismisses the Air Temple and its prophet Kelno as a joke with very little remaining power or territory.
  - Hartsch laughs: "He was a joke once, and he's an even bigger joke now. Nobody takes them seriously."
  - The map shows Kelno reduced to a few rooms, "a farcical joke of power." His only hope is that everyone else kills each other and doesn't bother to crush him "as a giant swats a fly."
- Asked whether to attack the Fire Temple, Hartsch replies that the high prophet Romag "has already given his command. We must attack the fire temple."
- Zinnia reads Hartsch.
  - Zinnia's insight is rolled with advantage after a 16 perception and comes to 21.
  - Zinnia gathers that Hartsch is "not exactly the world's biggest fan of Romag" and likely wants to be the Temple of Earth's chief cleric, though it's only an impression.
- The party proposes a plan to trigger a three-way war between the Earth, Fire, and Water temples to weaken all factions.
  - Sequoia outlines it: ally with the Water Temple to gang up on the Fire Temple, then convince the Earth Temple to strike the Water Temple early in the battle. The aim is that "we don't want a temple to come out on top."
  - In the chaos, the party would "dip from the temple" and head down to its lower parts.
  - The DM calls this "an entirely reasonable strategy," but reminds the party of "some kind of coordinating force beneath the temple that may get in the way". That force has not interfered yet.
- Zinnia asks whether Romag has always led from the shadows. Hartsch looks at Calmer and replies, "I prefer to say that he's always led from under the earth."
  - Zinnia says, "Well, I personally don't like that," and the party agrees.
  - Hartsch neither agrees nor disagrees.
- Zinnia uses deception to suggest to Hartsch that he would be a superior leader to Romag: "Well, I feel like you would probably make a better leader, you know." The Deception roll is 22.
- Hartsch expresses his belief in the 'vengeance power of the earth' and a preference for a destructive, earthquake-like leadership style over Romag's slow erosion approach.
  - Hartsch: "There are some who believe that the earth will slowly erode, and there are others who believe in the power of earthquakes to destroy."
  - Hartsch adds: "But Romag remains in charge. And as long as he remains in charge, I must do as he commands."
- Hartsch implies he would be willing to take command if a position were to 'suddenly open itself', signaling to the party that he wants them to remove Romag.
  - Zinnia's second Deception roll is 24.
  - Hartsch adds that until the earth god "decides that my style of leadership is the one he wants, I must let him go through his chosen vessel, Romag."
  - Zephyr: "He wants us to kill him."
- The DM names the coup as another option. When Zephyr asks how they could carry out Sequoia's plan without controlling the Earth Temple, the DM asks: "if you killed Romag and Hartsch, what cleric do you have that suddenly could control the Earth Temple?"
- The DM lays out the options: a coup, or the three-way war. He warns that doing both at once would make the lower temple start questioning the party, since each of the three sides must actually want to win.
- Hartsch adds that Romag "is too passive and … has sat there and accumulated power without using it."
- The party decides to move forward with a plan to kill Romag and allow Hartsch to take over, believing he will be more aggressive in attacking the other temples. Zephyr: "Let's just kill Romag and let this guy take over… He seems like he's gung-ho about the idea."
- Detailed planning is held for next session.

## NPCs

### Fire Temple Warlock

A servant of Alrrem who led a Chimera on a chain to assassinate the party, announcing that Alrrem had "brought the strength to tear those walls down." He held the chain in one hand and cast Eldritch Blast with the other, and commanded the beast until Zephyr disarmed him of the chain. He wore studded leather armor (AC 14). He carried a potion of fire resistance but panicked and tried to retake the chain instead. He was burned by the Chimera's breath, screamed, "Are you mad? It'll kill us all," and was ultimately slain by Zinnia.

### Chimera

A horrific multi-headed monstrosity with the features of a lion, a goat, and a dragon. The Fire Temple imported it at great expense to destroy the party; the ledger lists it as a "tri-headed exotic asset." Per the DM's lore, chimeras descend from creatures twisted by Demogorgon, the Prince of Demons, after mortals summoned it into the world. Its goat head gives it a stubborn streak that drives it to fight to the death. It went on a mindless rampage after being freed from its chains and turned its breath on its own handler. It downed Sequoia twice and Zephyr once, and was ultimately slain by Calmer's necrotic magic at 1 hit point.

### Bugbear

A guard or observer who attempted to intervene in the chaos by pointing a weapon at the Chimera, hoping it would attack the warlock. He was caught in the beast's devastating breath attack and later ran away.

### Romag

The leader of the Earth Temple who prefers to lead from behind, viewing himself as a chess king who sends others to fight his battles. He had already concluded that the Fire Temple stole his prisoners. He ordered the party to deal with Alrrem after hearing of the chimera attack, declaring "The moment is now." Hartsch describes him as having "always led from under the earth." The party now plans to kill him.

### Hartsch

Romag's sub-general and speaker who provided the party with tactical information and maps, including the temple's second level. He revealed the political landscape of the temples and identified Vesrek as Belsornig's ally. He subtly signaled his desire for the party to remove Romag from power so he could take command. He sees himself as an earthquake rather than erosion, the power of the earth "to destroy and rend and crack," and considers Romag "too passive."

### Alrrem

The leader of the Fire Temple, called "the all-consuming" by his warlock. He is currently weakened after losing his werewolves and his expensive chimera asset, and is likely nearly bankrupt. He was originally the leader of the temples in the great battle of Emridy Meadows and has been struggling to hold power since. He favors exotic monsters over mass troops, and reportedly banished a relative who tried to usurp him down to the Deep Temple.

### Vesrek

Captain of the Crimson Trader and an ally of Belsornig, the Water Temple prophet. The ledger's bill of sale names him as the seller of the chimera, paid in full by the Fire Temple; Hartsch read his name as Belsornig's hand in the deal. The chimera's chains bear the marks of Nulb's river pirates, tying the delivery to someone in Nulb.

## Locations

### Party's Quarters

The room where the party attempted to take a long rest, featuring a southern door and a secret door. The Chimera smashed through the southern door here, leading to a desperate struggle. The attackers likely came up nearby stairs and around to the door. The broken secret door connects to an adjoining room with a barrel and a chest, where the party retreated and Sequoia hid. The room later served as a private meeting place with Hartsch, away from Romag's ears.

### Romag's Chambers

The command center for the Earth Temple where Romag and Hartsch coordinate their forces. The party reported the chimera attack here and received orders to deal with Alrrem. Hartsch revealed a map of the temple's second level here. The party judged it the wrong place to talk about subverting Romag.

## Items

### Chimera Chain

A heavy iron collar and dragging chains used by the Fire Temple warlock to control and goad the Chimera into battle. Zephyr managed to rip the chain from the warlock's hands during the fight, later snatched it up stealthily, and then lost it to the beast on a failed Acrobatics roll. The chains were later found to be stamped with the marks of Nulb's river pirates, revealing that someone in Nulb was paid to deliver the monster. The party removed the chain to sell to the local blacksmith in Nulb.

### Warlock's Ledger

A document found on the Fire Temple warlock, containing a heavy wax-sealed receipt bearing the crest of a crimson ship. It is a bill of sale from Captain Vesrek of the Crimson Trader detailing the purchase of "one tri-headed exotic asset," the chimera, paid in full by the Fire Temple. The sum was clearly enormous. Hartsch, reading it, named Vesrek as an ally of the Water Temple's prophet Belsornig.

### Fire Temple Amulet

A heavy gold amulet and chain depicting the eight-pointed symbol of the Fire Temple, found on the warlock. Its craftsmanship and gold weight make it worth 100 gold pieces, split 25 gp to each party member.

### Potion of Fire Resistance

A magical elixir found on the warlock, intended to protect the user from heat and flames. The warlock had it on his person but panicked and never reached for it during the chaos of the chimera's rampage. It was added to the party's shared loot.

### Masterwork Whip

A high-quality whip woven with alchemically treated red leather, found among the warlock's belongings. While not magical, it is of fine craftsmanship. It has a 10-foot reach, can slow an enemy's movement by 10 feet, and deals 1d4 damage, with a sale value of 25 gp. Zephyr declined it ("a good rogue doesn't have a whip"), and Sequoia kept it.

### Coin Pouch

A small pouch on the warlock containing 12 platinum pieces and 35 gold pieces, worth an additional 25 gp to each party member.

## Spells

### Eldritch Blast

Beams of crackling energy fired by the Fire Temple warlock at Sequoia, dealing significant damage early in the fight. The warlock cast it with one hand while holding the chimera's chain in the other, landing two hits for 16 damage.

### Healing Word

A quick divine incantation used by Calmer on two separate occasions during the battle. The first, cast at second level, restored Sequoia to consciousness with 12 hit points. The later one brought Zephyr back from the brink of death with 17 hit points.

### Toll the Dead

A necrotic spell cast by Calmer that manifests as the sound of a melancholy tolling bell. It was used multiple times during the fight: 6 damage on the first casting and 10 on the second. On the third casting, "the tolling bell of the necrotic," it delivered the final, fatal blow to the Chimera, which was down to its last hit point.
