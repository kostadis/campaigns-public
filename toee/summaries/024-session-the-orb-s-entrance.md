# Chapter 24 The Orb's Entrance

Date: 2026-01-18

## Summary

The session opened with the DM reading back last week's recap and a pun ("What is a cleric's favorite tea?" "Divinity."). With this area of the dungeon cleared of its ghouls and ghasts, Zephyr asked whether the party could lay claim to it. The DM laid out the choice. They could claim the land for themselves and "report to no one," which would make them everyone's enemy. Or they could go to Romag as his "golden boys, or earthen boys." Zephyr chose to report to Romag.

At Romag's chambers, the party approached Romag to report their success and lay claim to the cleared area. Sequoia asked what the temple's actual goal was. Romag, pleased with the party, admitted he had "not done proper ideological training of you to understand the true purpose. So sit!" He began an ideological lecture, and the DM warned the table that they were "obviously… getting an Earth Temple view of the story." Romag explained the temple's founding belief: the true nature of the universe is constant chaos between the elements, with earth destined to reduce all things to dirt. The temple drew those who saw this inevitability as "something not to be feared, not to be fought against, not to be denied, but to be embraced." Its purpose was to bring about the blessed moment when nothing would exist but a wasteland of earth.

When the party asked why it was called the Temple of Elemental Evil and not simply the Temple of Earth, Romag gritted his teeth in irritation. He explained that at the founding it was decided the Earth Temple alone could not fight everyone who opposed it. So the four elemental temples (earth, fire, water, and air) aligned to destroy their enemies together, planning to settle their differences only in the final battles.

Asked why the temples were fighting each other now, Romag answered: "That is because of what happened 10 years ago!" The temple's forces had united and marched out to conquer, but were defeated at the Battle of Emridy Meadows by the Archcleric of Veluna and the Kingdom of Furyondy. The Circle of Eight, including powerful wizards like Bigby, Tenser, and Mordenkainen, had then locked the temple itself. That cut off the central driving power that gave them their energy. Since then they had sat and plotted, searching for the key to unlocking the heart of the temple.

The DM then spelled out what the party could piece together:
- Four chaotic-evil factions that hate each other could only be held together by something more powerful.
- That power sits behind the locked doors with the magical symbols that they could not approach.
- Even locked away, it is strong enough to keep influencing events.
- The previous head of the Earth Temple had been "promoted" into that greater organization.
- The merchant Rannos Davl in Hommlet had revealed the existence of a deeper temple. The DM confirmed that the "deeper temple" is literally deeper in the dungeon.
- Killing everyone in the temple without destroying that power would accomplish "nothing." Sequoia countered: "It might buy more time."

Sequoia also floated a scheme: seize the key and tell each temple that the other three have it.

The party asked Romag about this key. He weighed their loyalty: they had cleared territory, acquired new ground for him, and attended a ritual where he killed somebody and cheered. Then he pulled out some brandy and told them, "drink!" Sequoia checked it for poison with a Nature check (21). It was not poisoned, "although, it isn't very good brandy." Romag revealed that the key was an orb, the only object capable of forcing the chaotic elements to submit to the stability of earth. It was "the shackle that binds the storm and flood," the tool to impose "the discipline of stone upon the reckless factions of Alrrem and the vicious machinations of Belsornig" (the fire and water temples). Whoever held the orb would hold the mandate of the temple and have the power to supersede the bureaucratic authority of the Greater Temple.

Romag said that with the orb he could flood the dungeon with earth elementals and gorgons, crush all opposing temples, "summon a power, and rule!" The orb was also the key to the vault that opened the deep places within the temple. As the DM clarified, it is essentially a magical device that lets its holder control what is in the vault. When asked what was in the vault, Romag said, "That is information that you need to earn." He did not yet trust them enough. The DM cautioned that Romag "can't be trusted." However, the party's perceptions had been sharp enough to notice that he had never deliberately lied; he had only omitted details. Sequoia guessed the vault held "some god." Zephyr resolved to keep doing "Romag stuff, so he'll tell us more."

To earn more trust, Romag tasked them with recruiting people to man the newly captured areas. Dick Rentsch had sent word of a company of professional mercenaries called the Broken Blades currently being recruited in Nulb. Dick's success in hiring "higher-level operators" like the party had shown Romag that hiring whomever and relying on force of numbers was not the only path. "Hiring gnolls and bugbears is good, but we need something more." Romag was tired of unreliable gnolls and unruly bugbears. He wanted disciplined, brutal soldiers loyal to gold to guard the Earth Temple's critical checkpoints: former soldiers and deserters from the Wild Coast. The Water Temple was also trying to recruit them, so the party had to secure the mercenaries first. When the players asked whether Romag would fund the recruitment, the DM explained that it comes out of the party's own money: "If Romag hires it with Romag's money, it's not ROMAG's gang." Romag would happily lend them money, but Sequoia declined: "We're not gonna go into debt, bro."

The party traveled to Nulb and arrived discreetly at the Waterside Hostel around 9 AM, when the nocturnal town is asleep. The building is run-down, with a sign reading "Rooms are cheap, lives cheaper." Inside the smoke-filled tavern room, Zephyr whispered their purpose into Dick Rentsch's ear. Dick took them to his back room to talk privately because "The walls have ears here." He poured drinks and told them that Romag was very pleased, adding, "with your work, we're going to rule over all of the temples in no time." He explained that Dren Halveth, captain of the Broken Blades, was taking bids. There were three ways to recruit him: outbid the Water Temple, defeat him in a fistfight, or complete a task for the company. Sequoia judged the third "probably our best bet," and the party chose it.

Dick took them up to the hostel's second floor and arranged a meeting with Dren Halveth. Dren is a half-orc with broad shoulders, scars, and one ruined white eye that "stares as if seeing something else entirely." His crew hung back behind him, "relaxed in the way only killers can be," fingers drifting near weapons. Dren demanded to know who sent them, what they wanted in Nulb, and which gods they prayed to "when the knives come out." The party explained they had gained territory in the temple for the Earth Temple.

Dren laughed off the offer of doing him a favor: "You're going to hire me by proving to me that you could be my errand boys?" The party reframed it: the Earth Temple could help him if he helped them, and they wanted people like him on their side. The DM called that "a better strategy." Dren then revealed that a shipment of weapons his company had been counting on had been stolen by the Crimson Reapers, who occupied the room next door. He tasked the party with retrieving the weapons without bringing the wrath of the temple down on everyone. Calmer asked why the Blades didn't just knock and ask for it. Brakka Vusk laughed and explained that they had enough enemies already, and there was no profit in stealing it back if you die doing it.

The party decided to try negotiating with the Crimson Reapers rather than stealing the chest. Zephyr suggested simply buying it ("Dren doesn't have to know about this"), since that might be cheaper than outbidding the Water Temple. Calmer protested being sent to knock: "the last time I knocked on a door, I was killed." He went anyway while the rest of the party hid nearby in the shadows. A tattooed and scarred Crimson Reaper opened the door. Calmer glimpsed a tattoo on the man's chest that suggested a powerful evil entity. The Reaper became angry when Calmer asked about the chest and drew a blade. When Calmer revealed he worked for the Earth Temple, the Reaper explained that the chest was temple property that Dren had stolen. He said Dren had until midnight to join the temple, or he and his crew would be dead. The party got the impression that the Crimson Reapers worked for a deeper organization behind the four elemental temples, not for one of the four directly. Zephyr peeked inside and saw only three guards and the chest.

Returning to Dren, the party explained the situation: he must either join the temple or be killed by midnight. Dren was skeptical. The Water Temple was offering a large sum of money, and he had heard that the Earth Temple "just uses dumb recruits to get slaughtered in pointless battles." Sequoia countered brilliantly. He argued that the Water Temple relied on propaganda and lies, while the Earth Temple showed real commitment by coming to recruit them in person. "Have you really met any of these water… somebody from the Water Temple, or have you just seen a large sum of cash?" When one of Dren's men asked who they would be working for, Zephyr answered "the Earth Temple, but then also us." The party emphasized they had just claimed new territory and were giving it to the Broken Blades. Impressed by these arguments, Dren agreed to join the Earth Temple in exchange for one hundred gold pieces of walking-around money to help set up their new business. Sequoia paid. The DM admitted, "This was not the outcome I was expecting, but well done."

As the party prepared to leave the hostel with the Broken Blades, Zinnia (Perception 16) noticed that Wat, the barkeep, looked very unhappy about the outcome. The party took this as confirming their suspicion that he worked for the Water Temple. Sequoia wanted to ignore him. Zephyr taunted him instead, inviting him to join "the winning side, Earth Temple." When Calmer questioned Dick Rentsch about keeping Wat on his payroll despite his apparent loyalty to other factions, Dick responded that until they had secured complete power, everyone had to work together. The DM noted that there are powers in Nulb also invested in keeping the peace. As they departed, Wat approached Dren and whispered something to him, but Dren shoved him away and Wat stumbled off.

The party successfully brought the Broken Blades back to the temple, where Romag was extremely pleased with their success. He announced that the new territory would be given to the mercenaries, who would report to the party. However, he claimed another portion of the cleared territory for himself. About a day later Romag declared, "tonight we shall drink!" He hosted a celebratory drinking party, the first banquet in the newly cleaned banquet hall, which still carried the stench of cadavers and dead creatures mixed with food. Romag seemed to relish having his own banquet hall. Leaders from various areas of the temple attended the celebration. Romag proposed a toast to Sequoia, Zephyr, Zinnia, and Calmer for their contributions to increasing the Earth Temple's power. He specifically highlighted Calmer's conversion from a cleric of St. Cuthbert to a follower of the Elder Elemental God. Sequoia's player seized on the name ("So that's what's hidden away"), and the DM answered only, "Perhaps, and perhaps not." The party had positioned themselves deeper within the temple's hierarchy, continuing their dangerous mission of espionage and sabotage from within the enemy stronghold. After the session, the DM checked that the players were happy with the balance of social play and combat. Zephyr's player answered, "I trust your mix."

## NPCs

### Romag

The high cleric of the Earth Temple. He is pleased with the party's success in clearing dungeon territory and recruiting mercenaries.

Romag reveals the Temple of Elemental Evil's philosophy about earth's supremacy, opening with "So sit!" after admitting he had neglected the party's "proper ideological training." He also recounts the temple's defeat ten years ago at the Battle of Emridy Meadows. He grits his teeth in irritation when asked why it isn't just the Temple of Earth.

He describes a powerful orb that serves as the key to the temple's vault and can control the elemental factions. He scorns the "reckless factions of Alrrem and the vicious machinations of Belsornig." He withholds what is in the vault: "That is information that you need to earn." The DM warns he "can't be trusted," though he has not deliberately lied, only omitted details.

He expects the party to fund their own recruits, though he is happy to lend money. He shares brandy with the party and hosts a celebratory banquet, toasting their achievements and specifically noting Calmer's conversion from St. Cuthbert to the Elder Elemental God. He keeps part of the cleared territory for his own appointee.

### Dick Rentsch

The proprietor of the Waterside Hostel in Nulb and a contact for the Earth Temple. He first recruited the party into the Earth Temple and sent Romag word of the Broken Blades.

He greets the party with a charming smile and facilitates their mission to recruit the Broken Blades. He takes them to his back room to discuss business privately, noting that 'the walls have ears.' He explains that Romag is very pleased with their work: "with your work, we're going to rule over all of the temples in no time." He lays out the three recruitment options and helps arrange a meeting with Dren Halveth, knocking on Dren's door himself.

When questioned about keeping Wat on his payroll despite suspected Water Temple loyalty, he states that until complete power is secured, everyone has to work together.

### Dren Halveth

The half-orc captain of the Broken Blades, a company of veteran mercenaries and deserters from the Wild Coast. He has broad shoulders, scars beneath a worn leather coat, and one good eye, with the other ruined and white. He speaks low and sharp, like steel scraping through bone. His crew hangs back behind him, relaxed in the way only killers can be.

He is currently taking bids from both the Earth and Water Temples for his company's services. He mocks the party's first pitch as an offer to be his "errand boys," then tasks them with retrieving stolen weapons from the Crimson Reapers as a test. He has heard that the Earth Temple wastes "dumb recruits" and that the Water Temple is the more elite organization.

He is ultimately convinced to join the Earth Temple by the party's arguments about territory and genuine support, accepting 100 gold pieces to help set up his new business. He shoves Wat away when the barkeep tries to whisper to him.

### Brakka Vusk

A silver-tongued, crossbow-loving gambler who is a member of Dren Halveth's Broken Blades. He laughs when Calmer asks why Dren's crew doesn't retrieve the weapons themselves. He explains that they "have enough enemies as it is," and that there's "no profit in stealing it if you're going to die."

### Crimson Reaper

A member of the Crimson Reapers, a particularly ruthless gang of evil cultists, described by the DM as "somewhat on the loony side." He has tattoos and scars on his face and a particular tattoo on his chest suggesting allegiance to a powerful evil entity.

He opens the door when Calmer knocks and becomes angry when asked about the stolen chest. He pulls a blade and demands to know who Calmer is. Once Calmer names the temple, he says Calmer could have the chest "as long as you first bring me Dren's head," then breaks off to explain Dren's crime.

He reveals that the chest is temple property stolen by Dren and states that Dren has until midnight to join the temple or be killed. He doesn't care which temple Dren joins. He appears to work for the deeper organization behind the four elemental temples rather than one of the four temples directly.

### Wat

The barkeep at the Waterside Hostel. He is seen preparing the bar and scrubbing vomit off the floors, arranging tables in the morning. He eyes the party suspiciously when they come downstairs. He looks very unhappy when the party successfully recruits the Broken Blades, leading the party to suspect he works for the Water Temple. Zephyr taunts him to join "the winning side." He later tries to whisper to Dren and is shoved away.

### Dala

A worker at the Waterside Hostel who is seen cleaning tables during the party's visits.

## Locations

### Temple of Elemental Evil

An ancient multi-level dungeon serving as the base for four elemental cults. Ten years ago, it was locked by the Circle of Eight after the temple's forces were defeated at the Battle of Emridy Meadows. Locked doors bearing magical symbols the party could not approach seal off the powerful something within.

The party has cleared a section of the first level of the dungeon and claimed it as new territory for the Earth Temple. They reached that level by the central pit and entered through a white secret door. The first-floor map "looks like a rocket ship," with four circles and the pit. The "deeper temple" lies literally on deeper floors, and "the deeper you go," the worse it gets.

### Nulb

A town where recruits flow through to the temple; "all recruits flow through NULB." It is known for its nocturnal activity, with most residents sleeping during the day and conducting business at night. Arriving at 9 AM means "everybody's asleep." The party travels here to recruit mercenaries. Unnamed powers in Nulb also work to keep the peace between factions.

### Waterside Hostel

A run-down establishment in Nulb with a field stone foundation, patched timber walls, lean-to sheds, and a rickety gallery facing the river. The roof line sags under too many winters. A sign scratched onto the door reads 'Rooms are cheap, lives cheaper.'

Inside, the tavern room has low ceilings, thick smoke, and lantern light the color of old butter. Sound "stacks under the beams, whispers carry farther than you think." Dick Rentsch keeps a private back room off the tavern.

It serves as a base for Dick Rentsch and houses both the Broken Blades and the Crimson Reapers. Their rooms are next door to each other on the second floor.

### Banquet Hall (Dungeon)

A newly claimed area within the temple dungeon that has been cleaned up for use as a banquet hall. Despite cleaning, it retains the stench of cadavers, dead creatures, and food, "a very kind of creepy combination." Romag hosts its first banquet here, a celebratory drinking party with leaders from various areas of the temple.

## Items

### The Orb

A powerful magical device described by Romag as the key to the temple's vault and the deep places within. It is the only object capable of forcing the chaotic elements of fire, water, earth, and air to submit to the stability of earth. It serves as the shackle that binds the storm and flood, and the tool to impose "the discipline of stone" on the fire and water factions.

Whoever holds the orb holds the mandate of the temple and can supersede the bureaucratic authority of the Greater Temple. In the DM's gloss, it is "a magical device that allows you to control the things in the vault." With it, Romag claims he could flood the dungeon with earth elementals and gorgons, crush all opposing temples, and "summon a power, and rule."

### Stolen Weapons Chest

A chest containing a weapons shipment the Broken Blades were counting on, which Dren says the Crimson Reapers stole from them. The Crimson Reapers claim it is temple property and that Dren committed a crime by stealing it. The chest is guarded by three Crimson Reapers in their room at the Waterside Hostel.

### Brandy

Alcohol offered by Romag to the party during their discussion about the temple's secrets. The party checks if it is poisoned (Sequoia, Nature 21) and determines it is not, though it is not very good brandy.

### 100 Gold Pieces

A sum of money paid by Sequoia to Dren Halveth and the Broken Blades as 'walking around money' to help them set up their new business after agreeing to join the Earth Temple.

Alcohol brought out by Romag for a celebratory drinking party in the newly claimed banquet hall within the dungeon.
