# Chapter 02

Date: 2024-11-09

## Summary

The adventurers settled into the Inn of the Welcome Wench, mingling with the locals and loosening tongues with six silver pieces’ worth of food and drink. They heard praise for the inn’s fine food and reasonable prices, unsettling tales that the trees of the Gnarley Forest came alive at night to thwart attempts to harvest its timber, and a chilling rumor about a mysterious, forlorn herdsman named Black Jay who supposedly warned local bandits whenever a promising target passed through Hommlet. The DM added that not all of these rumors were true.

After deciding to spend the night, the party negotiated lodging with Ostler Gundigoot. Zephyr declared, “Look, I only sleep in the finest places,” and claimed the inn’s sole five-gold private suite, furnished with a huge feather bed, chairs, and a closet. Sequoia and Zinnia each chose a smaller private room costing five electrum pieces. The group spent more money trying to draw further rumors from the patrons, but their attempt to ply the locals with beer went nowhere.

Seeking knowledge beyond what the townsfolk could offer, the adventurers made their way to the Druid Grove. The grove was a serene and carefully tended place of worship: its trees were neatly pruned, its grass well maintained, and a line of bluish stones led toward a rock cairn adorned with flowers, nuts, berries, and garlands of leaves. A narrow path continued to a low-roofed wooden building beneath the great boughs of the central oaks.

There they met Jaroo Ashstaff, a druid of the Old Faith sent by the druids of the Gnarley Forest to monitor the region. Jaroo pointedly indicated the place where visitors were expected to leave donations, remarking that he had heard the party was staying in a private suite and therefore assumed they were people of means. He regarded Zephyr with visible unease because some druids of the Old Faith considered tieflings violations of the natural order. Zephyr tried to reassure him by saying that he also loved nature, then donated one hundred copper pieces. Although this amounted to one gold piece, Jaroo interpreted the conspicuous pile of copper as an insult, and his opinion of Zephyr fell. Sequoia repaired the social damage by contributing two gold pieces; Jaroo, already favorably disposed toward halflings as creatures of the natural order, immediately became much more welcoming.

The party told Jaroo that they had infiltrated a bandit fort and learned from its leader that the Temple of Elemental Evil was funding the operation. They admitted that their evidence was not concrete, but the news disturbed him. Jaroo promised to send an unnamed agent in the forest to investigate the locations they described and agreed to compartmentalize the information so that the party’s involvement would remain secret.

Jaroo explained that, before its defeat ten years earlier, the Temple of Elemental Evil had projected its influence outward from its principal temple through smaller towers and outposts arranged in expanding circles. One of its most prominent satellite strongholds had been a ruined moathouse near Hommlet. If the temple was active again, Jaroo believed evidence would be found there. He charged the party with investigating the ruins and returning with proof, while also advising them to inform Burne and Rufus, the local officials charged with protecting the area.

Before the party departed, Jaroo mentioned what he called a “pathetic rumor”: supposedly, ordering the venison stew at the Inn of the Welcome Wench signaled a willingness to join the local thieves’ guild. Jaroo dismissed the story as nonsense and assured them that the stew was simply quite good. The rumor nevertheless troubled the group when they remembered that Furnok the Fabulous—the charming rogue who had warned them that not everyone in Hommlet could be trusted—had been eating that exact dish.

The party made its way to the unfinished castle and guard tower, passing the village hall, blacksmith, stonemason, and a large encampment of construction workers. Only the tower appeared complete. It stood approximately fifty-five feet tall, with a smaller tower rising inside it, arrow slits, battlements, and an entrance reached by a curving stone staircase. Its outer door could lower to bridge the gap to a landing ten feet above the ground, while two men-at-arms watched from the roof.

At the gate, Zinnia initially claimed that the armed adventurers were “New construction workers,” but the guard found their weapons inconsistent with that story. The party then honestly explained that it had arrived with a caravan from Narwell, encountered bandits on the road, and possessed information for Burne and Rufus. Inside, the adventurers were received by Rufus, a battle-hardened, tough-as-nails fighter, and Burne, a fabulously groomed man with perfect hair who seemed far more interested in his experiments than in regional security. A perceptive glance made it immediately apparent that Burne and Rufus were partners in life as well as leadership.

Burne reacted to the report of bandits with, “Oh, not that again,” and tried to leave Rufus to dispose of the problem. Rufus challenged the party’s claim that the Temple of Elemental Evil could have reappeared beneath his nose after being defeated by the combined armies of the archcleric and the kingdom of Nyrond. Recognizing the threat such a revelation posed to his reputation, he neatly transferred responsibility by deputizing the party to investigate the Moathouse. He ordered them to report any proof directly to him—and to no one else—and expressed relief that they had come to him before accusing him of incompetence elsewhere.

The party accepted Rufus’s command, then falsely claimed that they had never spoken to Jaroo. Rufus called the druid a busybody and specifically asked them to keep him out of the matter. As the adventurers left, they noticed that the castle’s construction had barely progressed, the workers appeared unhurried, and the completed tower was richly appointed. Burne and Rufus seemed to have found a comfortable home and little incentive to finish the project for which Verbobonc’s government was paying them.

The party next visited the Church of St. Cuthbert, but Calmer informed them that High Cleric Terjon would not return until the following morning. With time to spare, they visited the village smithy. The short, brawny blacksmith recognized them as the party that had accompanied the latest caravan and offered to sell or build something for them. When Zephyr asked instead for “Information,” the smith first asked whether they had visited Jaroo and made a donation. Zephyr claimed it was “A hefty one,” and the smith said he hoped they weren’t lying.

The blacksmith warned that some great beast was stalking travelers along the high road and urged the party to travel cautiously. He also shared the darker rumor that Y’dey, the former head cleric of the village, had disappeared mysteriously. Terjon was extremely close-mouthed about the matter, and the smith suspected that he had played “no small role” in her disappearance before assuming control of the church. Finally, the smith explained that he sold all his goods through Rannos and Gremag, reinforcing the party’s impression that the two traders controlled nearly all of Hommlet’s manufacturing and commerce.

The following morning, the adventurers returned to the Church of St. Cuthbert. Calmer waited anxiously in armor that was presented as shiny, although it was “not really that shiny.” Terjon greeted Zinnia as a new convert and expected an enthusiastic display of devotion to St. Cuthbert of the Cudgel. When Zinnia complied, Terjon congratulated Calmer for converting a visitor on the spot and granted the cleric his blessing to accompany the party, reasoning that there were always more people to convert.

Terjon attached a condition to his approval. He knew the party had spoken to Burne and Rufus and declared that the pair had failed to protect Hommlet or make acceptable progress on the castle. Since Canoness Y’dey’s disappearance, he had been investigating their activities and had become convinced that something shady was happening. Terjon demanded that any evidence of the Temple of Elemental Evil’s return be brought first to him rather than to Burne and Rufus. The party recognized that Verbobonc’s government was dominated by the Church of St. Cuthbert, that Terjon considered himself the rightful authority in Hommlet, and that he appeared to want Burne and Rufus removed from office.

With Calmer officially in tow, the party declined an opportunity to question the village elder or other residents about what had happened when the Moathouse was originally attacked. Zephyr decided, “I think it’s funner if you go blind,” and the group set out without gathering further intelligence.

The two-mile journey followed a track choked by thorns, thistles, weeds, shrubs, fallen branches, and trees. Considerable hacking and clearing was necessary, and traces of animal traffic were only faintly visible after the first mile. As the trail turned north, the land sank into a bog thick with cattails, tamaracks, and tall marsh plants. The party’s hired man-at-arms remained available to guard their camp, packs, and any animals while they entered the ruins.

The jagged silhouette of the Moathouse eventually appeared to the left. A narrow, raised path crossed the wetland toward its entrance, but the crumbling embankments made travel near the edge dangerous. The bog stank, and although its vegetation was dense and prolific, it also looked diseased. Creepers and vines cast strangling loops over living bushes and dead saplings alike, while strange bird calls, croakings, and other unwholesome sounds drifted across the fen. Unlike the earlier bandit fort, the Moathouse gave no obvious sign of habitation; if servants of the temple were hiding there, they had concealed themselves well.

At the drawbridge, the party debated marching order. Calmer initially began pontificating about his courage while maneuvering toward the rear, but when Sequoia offered to lead, Calmer reversed himself: “No, no, I am the leader. And since I am the leader, I shall be in front.” His insistence reinforced the party’s growing impression that their newly recruited cleric suffered from delusions of grandeur.

As the adventurers approached the ruins, three enormous frogs erupted from the bog and attacked with their massive tongues and jaws. All three missed during the initial surprise assault, including one spectacular failure against Zephyr. A frog then bit Sequoia for four points of damage, but the halfling resisted its attempt to grapple him.

Sequoia retaliated with his short sword and dagger, landing two attacks before a final strike went wide. Zephyr attacked the frog engaged with him and Sequoia, missing first with his short sword but then driving his dagger home for ten points of damage and killing it. Zinnia struck another frog twice for a total of seventeen points of damage. The session ended with two frogs still in the fight and the Moathouse’s dark secrets waiting beyond the drawbridge.

## Locations

### Inn of the Welcome Wench

A local tavern and inn known for fine food and reasonable prices, where the party gathers rumors and spends the night. Ostler Gundigoot offers accommodations ranging from a one-silver common dormitory bed to smaller private rooms and a five-gold suite. Zephyr takes the sole suite, which contains a huge feather bed, chairs, and a closet, while Sequoia and Zinnia rent private rooms for five electrum pieces each. The inn’s venison stew is rumored to serve as a signal to the local thieves’ guild, though Jaroo dismisses that claim as nonsense.

### The Druid Grove

A well-tended sanctuary of the Old Faith with neatly pruned trees and carefully maintained grass. Bluish stones line the path to a rock cairn where flowers, nuts, berries, and garlands of leaves are offered. Beyond the shrine stands a low-roofed wooden building beneath the boughs of the grove’s central oaks. Although peaceful, the grove lacks the polished grandeur of the government-supported Church of St. Cuthbert, leaving Jaroo eager for donations to improve it.

### The Guard Tower

An approximately fifty-five-foot stone structure containing a smaller inner tower. A curving flight of stone stairs leads to a landing ten feet above the ground, and the tower’s outer door lowers to bridge the remaining gap. The fortification includes numerous arrow slits, a splayed base, battlements, pierced merlons, and roof positions watched by two men-at-arms. It is the only substantially completed portion of Burne and Rufus’s long-delayed castle project and is richly appointed compared with the languishing construction around it.

### The Smithy

A shed marked by three horseshoes nailed to the faces of a post. Inside are a forge and bellows where Hommlet’s short, brawny blacksmith works iron. The smith sells all his production through Rannos and Gremag, evidence of the traders’ broad control over local commerce. The party gathers rumors here about a beast on the high road and Y’dey’s disappearance.

### Church of St. Cuthbert

A massive and comparatively new religious building in Hommlet, constructed with the support of Verbobonc’s government. Its wealth and polish contrast with Jaroo’s more modest grove of the Old Faith. The party first recruited Calmer here, returned to seek Terjon’s formal permission for him to travel, and received Terjon’s demand that all evidence concerning the Temple of Elemental Evil be reported to the church before Burne and Rufus.

### The Moathouse

A ruined stronghold approximately two miles from Hommlet that once served as one of the Temple of Elemental Evil’s most prominent satellite outposts. It stands beyond an overgrown trail in a stinking, boggy fen filled with sickly vegetation, strangling vines, dead saplings, strange bird calls, and unwholesome croaking. A narrow raised path with crumbling edges leads to its drawbridge. Three giant frogs ambushed the party from the surrounding water before the adventurers could enter.

## NPCs

### Spugnoir

A famous mage known across many dimensions who was previously encountered at the Inn of the Welcome Wench. During the session, the group discovered that his name had been misread and mispronounced for decades; the previously used surname “Balkof” had also been an addition rather than part of his original name.

### Furnok the Fabulous

A rogue encountered at the Inn of the Welcome Wench who warned the party that not everyone in Hommlet is trustworthy and suggested consulting the Druid Grove. He was notably eating venison stew, the dish rumored to signal a willingness to join the local thieves’ guild. The party already chose not to recruit him because it has two rogues, but the stew rumor raises new questions about his allegiances.

### Ostler Gundigoot

The cheerful innkeeper of the Inn of the Welcome Wench. He offers accommodations ranging from a one-silver dormitory bed to private rooms and the inn’s only five-gold suite. He is delighted when the party chooses to stay, providing Zephyr with the lavish suite and Sequoia and Zinnia with smaller private rooms.

### Calmer

An eager cleric of St. Cuthbert recruited by the adventurers. He cannot join them without Terjon’s permission and waits anxiously for their return in armor that is supposedly shiny but “not really that shiny.” Terjon is particularly pleased that Calmer converted Zinnia. At the Moathouse, Calmer calls himself the party’s leader and insists on marching in front once Sequoia volunteers, giving the group the impression that he has delusions of grandeur.

### Terjon

The head cleric of the Church of St. Cuthbert in Hommlet. He expects conspicuous religious fervor from Zinnia and congratulates Calmer for converting a newcomer. Terjon gives Calmer permission to accompany the party but demands that any evidence of the Temple of Elemental Evil’s return be brought first to him. He believes Burne and Rufus have neglected the town’s defenses and the castle’s construction, regards himself as Hommlet’s rightful authority, and appears to want the pair removed from office. The blacksmith suspects that Terjon may have played a role in Y’dey’s disappearance.

### Rannos

A local trader who speaks with a slow accent. Along with Gremag, he appears to control most of Hommlet’s trade and manufacturing. The blacksmith sells all his goods through the pair, and their employee serves as the man-at-arms hired to guard the party’s camp and belongings.

### Gremag

A trader who speaks with a nasal, aristocratic voice. Along with Rannos, he controls much of the village’s commerce and manufacturing. Their employee accompanies the expedition to guard the party’s camp.

### Black Jay

A mysterious and forlorn herdsman rumored to be in league with local bandits. According to tavern gossip, he watches for promising travelers and warns the bandits whenever a likely target passes through Hommlet. The truth of the rumor remains unverified.

### Jaroo Ashstaff

A druid of the Old Faith who maintains Hommlet’s grove. The druids of the Gnarley Forest sent him to monitor the region after the Temple of Elemental Evil’s defeat. Jaroo is visibly averse to Zephyr because he considers tieflings unnatural, while his view of halflings makes him warmly disposed toward Sequoia. He offers to send an agent into the forest to investigate the party’s report, promises to compartmentalize their identities, directs them toward the Moathouse, and instructs them to alert Burne and Rufus.

### Rufus

A strong, experienced, tough-as-nails fighter who shares command of the guard tower with Burne and serves as the locally appointed military magistrate. He is skeptical that the Temple of Elemental Evil could have resurfaced beneath his nose and is deeply concerned that such a revelation would make him look incompetent. Rufus deputizes the party to investigate the Moathouse, demands that it report directly to him, and asks it to exclude Jaroo from the matter. He and Burne appear to be partners in both leadership and life.

### Burne

Rufus’s refined and fabulously groomed partner, described as having perfect hair. Burne is annoyed by renewed reports of bandit activity and would rather conduct his experiments than address threats to the region. He asks Rufus to send the party away, and Rufus later dismisses the adventurers because the pair have “important material to discuss and activities to pursue.” He and Rufus appear to be enjoying a comfortable, richly appointed life while their government-funded castle project languishes.

### The Blacksmith

A short, brawny man who works vigorously at Hommlet’s forge. He recognizes the party from the latest caravan and initially offers to sell or build equipment before Zephyr requests information instead. He warns of a great beast stalking the high road, claims Terjon may have played a role in Y’dey’s disappearance, and confirms that Rannos and Gremag control the sale of his goods.

### Y’dey

The former canoness and head cleric of Hommlet who disappeared mysteriously. Terjon describes her disappearance as a tragedy, while the blacksmith suspects that Terjon played “no small role” in it before taking charge. Her fate remains unknown.

### Giant Frogs

Large predatory amphibians dwelling in the bog surrounding the Moathouse. Three leap from the water and surprise the party near the drawbridge, attacking with massive tongues and powerful bites. Their initial assault fails completely, but one later bites Sequoia and tries unsuccessfully to grapple him. Zephyr kills one frog, Zinnia heavily wounds another, and two remain in combat when the session ends.

## Items

### Short Sword

A light blade wielded by both Sequoia and Zephyr during the fight at the Moathouse drawbridge. Sequoia attacks with both his short sword and his dagger, while Zephyr’s short-sword attack misses before he finishes the frog with his dagger.

### Dagger

A small blade used as part of Sequoia’s multi-weapon assault and by Zephyr against the giant frogs. Zephyr lands a ten-point dagger strike that kills the first frog defeated in the battle.

## Memorable Moments

> “Look, I only sleep in the finest places.”
> — Zephyr

*Zephyr immediately claims the Inn of the Welcome Wench’s only private suite, complete with a huge feather bed, chairs, and a closet.*

> “We’re really turning into tourists.”
> — Sequoia

*After spending money on rooms, food, drinks, and local gossip, Sequoia recognizes what the party’s investigation of Hommlet has become.*

> “Although I heard you’re staying in the private suite, so I would like to think that you are men of—you are people of means.”
> — Jaroo Ashstaff

*Jaroo points the newly arrived adventurers toward the grove’s donation area and makes it clear that he already knows about Zephyr’s expensive accommodations.*

> “Well, I tell him that I’m also, I also love nature.”
> — Zephyr

*Receiving the Old Faith druid’s unmistakable side-eye, Zephyr tries to establish that being a tiefling does not prevent him from appreciating nature.*

> “I’ll give him 100 copper.”
> — Zephyr

*Zephyr presents what is technically a one-gold donation in the least impressive possible denomination. Jaroo interprets it as an insult, and his opinion of Zephyr falls.*

> “Yes, thank you so much. How may I help you, sir?”
> — Jaroo Ashstaff

*Jaroo’s demeanor changes immediately after Sequoia, a halfling he considers part of the natural order, donates two gold pieces.*

> “I will compartmentalize the information.”
> — Jaroo Ashstaff

*When Sequoia asks Jaroo not to reveal the party’s involvement, the druid responds with the language of an experienced intelligence handler.*

> “There’s this pathetic rumor. It’s a rumor, I tell you, that ordering the venison stew at the Inn of the Welcome Wench signifies a willingness to join the local thieves guild. I can assure you that is nothing but nonsense. The venison stew is quite good.”
> — Jaroo Ashstaff

*Jaroo’s attempted dismissal only makes the rumor more suspicious—especially when the party remembers that Furnok the Fabulous was eating the venison stew.*

> “New construction workers.”
> — Zinnia

*Standing outside Burne and Rufus’s tower while visibly armed for adventure, Zinnia offers the guard an improvised explanation that does not survive even casual scrutiny.*

> “Oh, not that again.”
> — Burne

*The impeccably groomed local leader reacts to news of bandits and a possible cult resurgence as if the party has interrupted him with a tiresome recurring chore.*

> “And you claim that that information proves that the temple of elemental evil, which the combined armies of the archcleric and the kingdom of Nyrond defeated 10 years ago, has somehow magically under my nose reappeared. Well, that would make me look like a fool now, wouldn’t it?”
> — Rufus

*Rufus quickly identifies the most alarming consequence of the temple’s possible return: the damage it could do to his reputation.*

> “Because as the local appointed magistrate of this area in charge of the military defense of the region, I deputize you to go investigate the Moathouse and prove your allegations. And when you prove them, you will come directly to me and to nobody else.”
> — Rufus

*Rather than investigate the report himself, Rufus transforms the adventurers into his official agents and orders them to control the flow of evidence.*

> “I will not have people accuse me of incompetence behind my back. I am glad you came to me first.”
> — Rufus

*Rufus makes his priorities explicit while congratulating the party for approaching him before spreading the story elsewhere.*

> “Yeah, we never talked to him.”
> — Sequoia

*After spending considerable time telling Jaroo everything, the adventurers instantly deny knowing him when Rufus asks that the “busybody” druid be excluded.*

> “Now, please leave. Burne and I have got important material to discuss and activities to pursue.”
> — Rufus

*Rufus dismisses the newly deputized adventurers so that he and Burne can return to their more private priorities.*

> “What can I sell you? What can I build you?”
> — The Blacksmith
>
> “Information.”
> — Zephyr

*The party enters the smithy seeking rumors rather than ironwork, and Zephyr gets directly to the point.*

> “Y’dey, the head cleric of the village, has disappeared mysteriously. The priest Terjon is awfully close-minded about—closed-mouthed about the situation. I bet he played no small role in her disappearance. He’s in charge now.”
> — The Blacksmith

*The blacksmith lays out Hommlet’s darkest piece of clerical gossip just before the party seeks Terjon’s blessing.*

> “My goodness, a bad walks in and you convert him on the spot. Well done, Calmer. Well done.”
> — Terjon

*After Zinnia is prompted to display sufficient enthusiasm, Terjon congratulates Calmer on the speed of his conversion work.*

> “I want you, if you get any information that the temple is alive, I want it brought to me first, not to Burne and Rufus.”
> — Terjon

*The party receives a second—and directly conflicting—order about who must receive evidence from the Moathouse first.*

> “I think it’s funner if you go blind.”
> — Zephyr

*Offered the chance to learn more about the Moathouse’s history before entering it, Zephyr chooses surprise over preparation.*

> “No, no, I am the leader. And since I am the leader, I shall be in front.”
> — Calmer

*The newly recruited cleric asserts command of the expedition as soon as Sequoia volunteers to take the dangerous lead position.*

> “Three giant frogs leap out of the water.”
> — DM

*The party’s first encounter at the Moathouse begins when enormous amphibians erupt from the fetid bog beside the drawbridge.*

> “All three of them missed. Okay. The surprise attack was a failure.”
> — DM

*Despite catching the adventurers unaware, every frog fails to connect during the opening ambush.*

> “High froggy.”
> — Sequoia

*Sequoia greets the giant frog attacking him moments before it bites him for four points of damage.*

> “You killed the frog. The frog dies.”
> — DM

*After missing with his short sword, Zephyr lands a ten-point dagger strike and drops the first giant frog of the fight.*
