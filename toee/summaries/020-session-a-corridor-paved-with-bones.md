# Chapter 20 A Corridor Paved with Bones

Date: 2025-12-06

## Summary

The party stood over their captured gnoll, bound and snarling at their feet. It was the last one left alive after their ambush in the forest. Calmer, the cleric of St. Cuthbert, burned with righteous fury at the sight of the creature. To him it was a spawn of demon powers, born by bursting from the bodies of hyenas and "gross beyond all human recognition." Calmer is not usually the most bigoted of clerics, but gnolls fill him with righteousness. When Zephyr attempted to politely question the prisoner, Calmer suggested a more forceful approach "with a little bit more oomph," while reminding everyone that he no longer does face duties. Zephyr slapped the gnoll and demanded information, rolling a 21 on Intimidation with advantage. The creature only hissed and threatened to tear them apart. Zephyr concluded, "I think he wants to die," and with the interrogation clearly failing, Calmer said "excellent" and swiftly ended the gnoll's life. His hatred for these demonic beings overrode any further attempt at diplomacy.

Calmer suggested there might be treasure on the bodies. Searching them, the party found a scrap of hairy hide and a thin piece of reddish leather covered in symbols. On inspection it proved to be a first-level scroll of Hunter's Mark. They also found three ornate gold buttons and symbols marking the dead as servants of the Temple of Fire.

With their grim task complete, they made their way back to Nulb and the Waterside Hostel, where their contact Dick Rentsch awaited them. Everyone in the common room turned to look as they entered. Dick, standing at the bar, gestured for them to follow him into his back office. Once they confirmed the gnolls were dead, he said he had arranged a meeting with Romag, a powerful figure within the temple they sought, and asked them to sit tight while he got things ready. Calmer, ever cautious and weary of being killed yet again, insisted on taking precautions while they waited. With the party's help he moved a heavy chest to barricade the door, and he positioned Zephyr to watch the window. He muttered about his growing exhaustion with making deals with evil powers that wanted them all dead. Zinnia urged everyone to stay alert so they would not be surprised.

When Dick Rentsch returned, he led them out of Nulb along a rutted, weed-choked road toward their destination. The path was spotted with rank vegetation: thistles, burrs, thorns, and nettles. The local constable had forbidden anyone to come here, yet clearly people did. The road appeared little-used, but Zinnia noticed (passive Perception 16, then a 25 on the follow-up check) that many creatures of widely varying sizes had recently traveled this way, and that someone was working hard to disguise the heavy traffic. That made the party wonder exactly who was being recruited.

As they approached, the landscape grew increasingly disturbing. Dead trees stood like skeletons, and twisted, unnaturally colored vegetation surrounded them. On Survival checks (Zinnia 25, Zephyr 18), the party spotted skulls and bones gleaming white among the weeds, some covered in strange fungus none of them had ever seen before.

The Temple of Elemental Evil loomed before them, a massive structure far larger than they had imagined. This was the temple they had been trying to find for months. A ruined tower jutted from the northeast corner of shattered walls, beside a grove of stunted, unhealthy trees. The temple itself stood like a leprous gray monument to darkness. Its arched buttresses were obscene, covered in climbing vegetation, and every surface was carved with leering faces and writhing forms depicting unspeakable obscenities. Crushed stems in the courtyard gave off a foul stench or raised angry welts on bare skin.

A pervading sense of fear hung over the entire area like a tangible cloud of vileness. Sounds seemed distorted, and movements flickered at the edges of their vision. A maze of narrow paths wound through the weedy courtyard. The wind moaned through hundreds of apertures in the temple walls, built to sing like doomed souls, while ravens croaked and flapped among the ruins.

The main entrance featured four pairs of massive bronze doors, twenty-three feet high and twenty feet wide, held fast by huge iron chains, with every crack sealed with soft iron. Graven upon these doors were glowing runes that burned with silvery radiance, making their eyes water to look upon. Dick Rentsch carefully avoided these runes, leading them instead to a side entrance. When Zephyr said he hoped Dick knew where he was going, Dick shot back that they had better hope so, "because otherwise you won't be alive for very long." The party recalled a story from Spugnoir in Hommlet about the Circle of Eight, the wizards who try to maintain the balance of power in Greyhawk. These included the legendary Mordenkainen and Bigby, who had come here and performed mighty magic to seal this place.

Dick Rentsch pulled them inside through the side door into a vestibule. The floor was covered in reddish-brown slate-like tiles, each about two feet square. The plastered walls were painted with horrifying scenes of killing, torture, enslavement, robbery, and unspeakable acts. Evil was flaunted and lionized. Dim light filtered through stained glass windows, casting revolting colors across the floor. Pinkish mineral pillars shot through with warm-colored veins supported arches decorated with vile reliefs; this area was probably reserved for the lowliest of worshippers.

Dick Rentsch led them deeper into the temple, to a chamber containing a central altar of pinkish-white marble. It was carved with a disturbing human-shaped depression, stained dark with old blood. North of the altar yawned a circular pit twenty feet in diameter, surrounded by shards of broken crystal vessels.

Dick Rentsch seemed disturbingly at ease here. Insight checks (Zephyr and Zinnia, 18 each) read the place as his happy place. When Zephyr asked why the temple was so empty, he explained that once the Earth Temple destroyed the other false prophets, this place would be filled with supplicants who would kill and slaughter and bury alive all who opposed them, "But that is a promise for another day."

He then led them down into the pit itself. Zephyr investigated first (22) and found a narrow ledge, one foot wide, beginning fifteen feet below the mouth of the shaft. It spiraled down the northern face to steep steps below. As the party prepared to descend, Calmer asked if they could use rope for safety. Dick Rentsch sighed in exasperation, muttering that he thought they were getting better recruits this time, but allowed them to tie themselves together before beginning the long descent down many, many steps.

At the bottom, Dick Rentsch fumbled at the wall and knocked, and a secret door opened to reveal a corridor at least one hundred feet long and twenty feet wide. The entire passage was covered in bones: a terrible jumble of human and humanoid skeletons, many skulls gnawed, many still wearing sundered armor or lying beside broken weapons. A hundred, perhaps two hundred, had met their deaths here in a great battle. Dick Rentsch warned them not to disturb the dead as he picked his way through the grim remains. Zephyr followed but kept a lookout.

As they followed him eastward, the party failed to notice the danger (Investigation 11 from both Zinnia and Sequoia). Several gnoll skeletons suddenly animated and rose from the bone pile to attack, three to the east and three to the south. Dick stood back and watched to see what the party would do.

The party fought desperately against the undead creatures. Sequoia went first and struck down the first skeleton with a powerful blow. The remaining skeletons attacked, wounding both Sequoia and Zephyr. Zephyr struck back and then disengaged, while Zinnia delivered a devastating critical blow to another skeleton. Calmer raised his weapon to the sky, crying "By the power of St. Cuthbert!" Two skeletons failed their saves and took 8 damage each, and one of them fell; the other two were unaffected. The party quickly finished off the remaining undead. Zephyr's sneak attack and Calmer's mace both proved decisive against foes vulnerable to bludgeoning.

Dick Rentsch urged them to move quickly before more skeletons could animate. He warned that this was where the various temples' factions fought their battles, and not a place to stand around. They ran through the rest of the corridor, emerging into a guarded area. Along the way the party was getting the sense that this place was huge.

At a door, Dick knocked in a particular pattern. Zinnia rolled Insight with advantage, "since you're a spy," and noticed the pattern without learning it. Four guards stationed in a torch-lit hall questioned Dick Rentsch, who explained that the party were the new recruits he had promised to Romag. The hall held a bench, a stool, pegs bearing a brown cloak, a quiver of nine arrows, and a handaxe, along with a wire alarm system. The guards expressed their loyalty to the Earth Temple ("FOR THE EARTH!") and their hatred for the Fire Temple. They boasted that once they killed enough gnolls, Alrrem, the head of the Fire Temple, would be theirs. Sequoia recognized the name on a History check of 15.

The party passed through a barracks area and a lounge decorated with scenes of drinking and debauchery. At the lounge door Dick knocked and called, "It's me, I've got the new recruits!" They finally arrived at a well-furnished chamber where two commanders stood guard. Dick Rentsch asked whether he needed to confiscate their weapons or whether they were smart enough not to try anything stupid. The party assured him of their cooperation, though there was some lighthearted banter about cutting off Zephyr's tiefling horns. Zephyr had jokingly offered up Sequoia's weapons, calling him a loose cannon, and Sequoia did not take kindly to Zephyr calling himself the boss.

They were brought before Romag, the High Priest of the Earth Temple, who sat waiting for them. Romag greeted them warmly, pleased that Dick Rentsch had finally brought him something worthy of his time, and bade them sit. He told them they had done the Earth Temple a great service. He asked for the Chest of Rao, and the party presented it to him. Before handing it over, the DM reminded the players of their strategy: to ally with one temple and play the factions off against each other. Zephyr likened it to being "an arms dealer" and proposed to "feed the fire" by giving up the chest.

When the party seemed uncertain about the temple's scope, Romag laughed heartily at their ignorance. Zephyr flattered him as "a real insider" whose expertise might help. Romag explained that the temple had been constructed almost fifteen years ago. He said the surface temple was merely the beginning, and that the true power lay in the deeper levels descending into the very pit of evil itself. The first two levels were divided among the Earth, Fire, Water, and Air temples, all locked in constant conflict. Below that lay the deeper temple and deeper secrets, knowledge reserved for those who had proven their worth.

Romag's mission for them was clear: destroy the other temples so the Earth Temple could seize ultimate power. Specifically, the Fire Temple was plotting with gnolls on the lower level, and he needed them to deal with it. Dick Rentsch provided them with a map showing where the gnolls could be found. He explained that they would need to navigate through territory controlled by multiple hostile factions, starting by going back down the hole they had come through. When Zephyr asked whether they would be recognized as temple members, Romag promised that once they proved they could operate within the temple by completing this mission, they would earn the right to wear the badge of Earth. Zephyr pledged to do it quietly, which sparked a debate about who, if anyone, led the party.

The party descended once more, running quickly past the skeleton-filled corridor to avoid another encounter. They arrived in a large octagonal room fifteen feet below the normal dungeon floor, beneath a ceiling vaulting forty feet high. It was illuminated by an eerie milky radiance emanating from the ceiling, walls, and floor. The polished gray stone floor, shot through with glittering mica, was partially obscured by swirling, glowing mist. In the center stood a great pierced bronze square, and a circular shaft opened above a pit of the same diameter. South of the pit sat an alabaster block bearing two knives and a crystal bowl, while crystal braziers suspended from silver chains emitted sickly sweet perfume smoke. Bronze doors to the north were sealed with iron chains and warning runes identical to those on the main entrance above.

Zephyr jumped into the pit first, and immediately a wraith materialized before them, a terrifying undead spirit of malevolent power. Zephyr struck first. Knowing undead are immune to necrotic damage, he chose his dagger over Chill Touch and landed blows with it. The wraith retaliated with devastating force. Its spectral touch drained Zephyr's very life essence, not only wounding him but reducing his maximum vitality in a way that could only be restored through extended rest or powerful healing magic. On his turn Calmer stated grimly that he was beginning to think Dick hadn't told them everything about what awaited them here. He called upon St. Cuthbert's divine power once more, causing the wraith to recoil in fear and suffer holy damage, and with the wraith frightened, he moved away.

The party regrouped, realizing with growing concern that they were standing in what was clearly the Air Temple's power source. Dick Rentsch had not simply sent them down a hallway. He had deposited them directly into enemy territory belonging to a rival elemental faction. Zephyr suspected it was a test to see whether they would survive. Whether that was all it was, or something more sinister, remained unclear. One thing was certain: they were now deep within the Temple of Elemental Evil, surrounded by ancient evil and warring factions, with only their wits and weapons to see them through the trials ahead.

## NPCs

The party's contact for the Earth Temple who guides them through the Temple of Elemental Evil. ## Locations

### Nulb

A town where the party returns after killing gnolls and where they meet their contact, Dick Rentsch, at the Waterside Hostel. The road from Nulb to the temple is rutted and weed-choked; most traffic avoids it so as not to leave a discernible path.

### Waterside Hostel

A hostel in Nulb where the party meets Dick Rentsch in a back room. This is where Dick Rentsch arranges their meeting with Romag and where the party had earlier seen the bandit chief who was escorting the gnoll recruits. The back room is Dick's office. Calmer barricaded its door with a chest and posted Zephyr at its window.

### Temple of Elemental Evil

A massive, ancient temple that the party has been trying to find for months. It is surrounded by dead, twisted vegetation, skulls, and bones. The temple exudes a palpable sense of dread and vileness. Its main entrance features huge bronze doors sealed with glowing runes that were placed by the Circle of Eight. The temple extends deep underground with multiple levels controlled by warring elemental factions.

- Additional features of the grounds: a stunted grove at the north end of the former compound, the stump of a tower at the northeast corner of the shattered wall, and a maze of narrow paths through the courtyard.
- Wind moans through hundreds of apertures "built to sing like doomed souls."
- The approach road has been deliberately disguised to hide heavy traffic by creatures of many sizes.
- The local constable has forbidden travel there.

### Temple Vestibule

The entrance area of the temple accessed through a side door. It features reddish-brown slate-like floor tiles and plastered walls painted with scenes depicting evil acts like killing, torture, enslavement, and robbery. Dim light filters through stained glass windows, and pinkish mineral pillars support arches leading to side aisles. The nave lies to the north, and the area was probably reserved for the lowliest worshippers.

### Central Altar

A chamber within the temple featuring white marbled pillars veined with ugly red. It contains a disturbing pinkish-white marble altar with a human-shaped depression stained dark, and a large circular marble-lined pit twenty feet in diameter to the north. Shards of broken crystal vessels are scattered about. A one-foot ledge begins 15 feet down the pit's northern face and leads to steep steps descending toward the secret door.

### Corridor of Bones

A 20-foot-wide corridor accessed through a secret door from the central pit, at least 100 feet long and covered with the skeletal remains of hundreds of humans and humanoids. Many skeletons still wear sundered armor or hold broken weapons, testifying to a great past battle. Gnoll skeletons animate here when disturbed. Many of the bones appear gnawed. Dick Rentsch describes it as a battleground of the rival factions, "not a place you should be standing."

### Guard Post

A 10 by 20 foot hall lit by two torches, containing a bench, a stool, and an alarm system with wire running through eyebolts. Four guards loyal to the earth temple are stationed here, expressing rivalry with the fire temple. Recently added pegs hold a brown cloak, a quiver of nine arrows, and a handaxe. The door to it answers to a patterned knock.

### Barracks

A 20-foot square plastered room lit by torches and a lantern, converted into barracks with four double bunks on each side, a mantle, brown cloaks on pegs, and eight small chests under the bunks. The lantern is fixed to shine on the south door, and a jug and several cups sit on the table.

### Lounge Area

A plastered room illuminated by four wall cressets, displaying scenes of drinking and debauchery. It contains eight double bunks, a long table with benches, three barrels (two with liquids, one with lumpy objects), hanging smoke sausages, and a cabinet with dishes. Its far door is answered by a voice that admits Dick's "new recruits."

### Commanders' Room

A once lavishly decorated room on the way to Romag, where Dick Rentsch asked whether he needed to take the party's weapons. It features two plush couches, a wardrobe, a small table with inlaid wood top, two padded armchairs, a large chest, dented pewter dishes, and an unlit candle. Two fighters who appear to be commanders are stationed here.

### Octagonal Room (Air Temple)

A large octagonal room 15 feet below the normal dungeon floor, clearly part of the Air Temple's power source. It is illuminated by a milky radiance from all surfaces and features polished gray stone with glittering mica, swirling glowing mist, a great pierced bronze square in the center, a circular pit twenty feet wide and five feet deep, an alabaster block with two knives and a crystal bowl, and two crystal braziers emitting sickly sweet perfume smoke. Bronze doors to the north are sealed with runes identical to those on the main entrance. The ceiling vaults to 40 feet, short broad stairways descend to the floor, and a wraith materializes when anyone drops into the pit.
