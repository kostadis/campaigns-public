# Chapter 40 Beware the Eastern Tomb

Date: 2026-10-11

## Summary

In Nulb, the party stood before Mother Screng, whose disguise they had already seen through. Otis stood beside her.

Zephyr’s ledger of outstanding business closed on his mood: “We came here to find out who was running the temple. We have reached the point where people ask us.”

As the party prepared to leave, Mother Screng gave them her last words: “You can walk where I cannot. Bring him out alive. Do not trust what the temple shows you when you find him.” The prisoner, she revealed, was Prince Thrommel, a Paladin Lord, Grand Marshall of Furyondy, and Provost of Veluna. He had vanished ten years earlier at the Battle of Emridy Meadows. His marriage was intended to unite Furyondy and the Archclericy of Veluna, strengthening both realms against the Horned Society and the demigod Iuz, who had escaped imprisonment in Greyhawk and had since grown ominously quiet.

Zinnia pressed Mother Screng on her sources, and she admitted, “My intelligence is all secondhand.” No one else would risk entering a dungeon to investigate a rumor about a prince missing for a decade. Otis coughed and supplied his own account: “During the Battle of Emridy Meadows, his guard failed him, and he disappeared.” He did not believe that so great a man would have allowed himself to be captured or could have remained imprisoned for ten years, though Mother Screng insisted her evidence was credible. She rolled her eyes at his skepticism. Zephyr answered simply, “We shall investigate.”

Sequoia then laid out four puzzle pieces taken from the body of his dead sister. Mother Screng fitted them together and revealed the emblem of a crown and crescent moon, Thrommel’s own personal sign. The pieces had always admitted several possible arrangements, so no one had known whether any were missing. Once assembled correctly, however, the image was unmistakable, and Mother Screng exclaimed that this was the emblem. When Sequoia had first asked her about it, she had called the emblem proof and said that she knew it was “precisely his emblem, his personal emblem.”

When Mother Screng asked how Sequoia had acquired it, he told her it had belonged to his sister. Asked whether the woman might still be alive, he answered, “Well, judging by the fact I took it off her corpse, yeah.” Mother Screng apologized for prying, offered to pray for his sister and wish horrors upon her killer, and wondered whether the emblem had been a secret gift from Thrommel. Otis concluded, “It would seem that Sequoia’s family has some relationship to the prince.”

The party agreed that once they found Thrommel, they would entrust him to Otis rather than seek a ransom or carry him openly to Verbobonc or Hommlet. Otis promised, “Very well, we can commit to a safe house and discreet transport. But you must get him to Nulb.” The party would have to penetrate the temple, identify a prisoner whom Falrinth claimed was hidden in some fashion, and extract him without trusting what they saw.

Their errands in Nulb finished, the companions returned to the temple. Dren met them in the corridor with his jaw set and his voice low. “Barkinar is in your quarters. I told him you were away. He said he would wait.” Dren had admitted him because the party had never told him whether to “start a war in the doorway” when a superior temple officer demanded entry.

Inside, Tolub’s severed head sat on a plate atop the table. Selentis had answered the party’s wish to have the captain gone, and Barkinar explained, “Lucius suggested I present this to you.” Sequoia understood why Tolub was dead but objected to the presentation: “I am happy he is dead, but this is unsanitary. I sometimes eat food off this table.” After a pause, Barkinar conceded, “You know, you’re absolutely right, Sequoia. I don’t know what I was thinking,” and moved the head to a side table. He then offered to put it on a shelf, which Sequoia accepted.

Barkinar praised the party for restoring order and reopening trade between Nulb and the lower temple. He asked where matters stood with the necromancer and the Chest of Rao. Zephyr reported that the necromancer was still being sought and described the chest as “a work in progress,” adding, “I’m confident we are close.”

Turning to Calmer, Barkinar praised the Earth Temple’s new supremacy and reminisced about his own time as its head. He then ordered Calmer to perform the required ceremony: “All you have to do is take one of these prisoners and have the earth elemental slaughter him and bathe in its blood. It’s not that big a deal.” Barkinar had noticed the open ritual book and initially demanded the sacrifice within two days.

He then remembered a hamlet a few miles away whose inhabitants refused to recognize the temple as their masters. Barkinar decided the party should raid it for supplies, reestablish the temple’s authority, and seize a victim for Calmer. The expedition extended the ritual deadline to five days. Calmer looked terrified, but Sequoia answered for him: “Yeah, that sounds great.”

Barkinar added that Lord Hedrack, supreme commander of the lower temple, had noticed their work and wished to meet them the next day. Before leaving, Barkinar looked at Tolub’s head and remarked, “I never liked the man.” Tolub had mocked Barkinar’s efforts to recruit him into the Earth Temple and boasted that Belsornig and Water would rule the universe. Barkinar found it gratifying that an Earth Temple cleric had brought him down and congratulated Calmer. As Barkinar departed, he clicked his fingers, and several black-clad human guards—members of the lower temple forces whom the party had not noticed—fell in behind him.

Once Barkinar was out of earshot, Dren demanded clearer standing orders: “When someone I’ve never met before shows up here and says they outrank me, whose permission do I need to keep them out?” The party named Hedrack, though they had no direct way to contact him until he contacted them.

Calmer then excused himself to research the ritual. Zinnia took Zephyr and Sequoia into private chambers, deliberately excluding Calmer, and discussed the intelligence surrounding Thrommel. Zinnia resolved not to alert his spymaster until after the prince was found. The others agreed that Zinnia would receive credit for discovering Thrommel, a feat that could turn him into a legendary spy. The others reasoned that Thrommel’s emblem might carry them to the top of the temple, where they could dismantle it from within.

The party rested rather than immediately descending into the lower level or contacting Falrinth. The next morning Calmer summoned them, excitedly declaring, “I have figured it out, how we are going to get out of killing someone.” During his explanation, he thanked Zinnia for warning him that he was going too deep. He had reconsidered whether being an evil overlord was truly who he was, though he had not reembraced what he called “the insane, cult-like behavior of the Church of St. Cuthbert.”

Calmer then remarked that Zephyr’s appearance reminded him of “what the hellish brutes look like.” Sequoia immediately challenged him and demanded sincerity. Calmer admitted, “I’ve been a little bit full of myself, haven’t I?” He confessed that he had been reading too many evil manuals and had found the promise of power and control seductive. Now he understood that no one was truly in control while another evil overlord stood above him, and that the temple’s leaders readily forgot allies as soon as those allies died.

He remembered that the party had surrendered something precious to bring him back from the dead when his own church had not. “Just because you look like a demonic presence doesn’t mean you ARE a demonic presence,” he told Zephyr. Terjon looked like a saint but had been willing to let Calmer die. Calmer apologized directly to Zephyr and thanked Sequoia for serving as his conscience. Zephyr accepted. Zinnia answered Calmer’s thanks only with a steady stare.

Calmer then described the ritual in full. A victim would be placed atop the Earth Temple pyramid and torn apart by four earth elementals. The Earth cleric would bathe in the victim’s blood and sprinkle it over the faithful. The rite would bless those followers temporarily and increase the cleric’s ritual power. Calmer observed that this benefit raised the question of why Romag had not performed it before going to war, but “Romag was never the smartest of the bunch.”

Calmer had discovered an alternative: the Chest of Rao could be sacrificed instead of a person. Yet he warned that the chest was a powerful magical object and that the temple’s senior leaders seemed unusually determined to see it destroyed. He suggested asking Hedrack why before committing it to the ritual. He left the ultimate choice—perform the ritual with the chest or sacrifice an innocent—to Zephyr, Sequoia, and Zinnia, explaining that he no longer trusted his judgment after his infatuation with evil.

Dren then arrived with his report. The corpses had been burned, the soldiers paid, and the food stores replenished. The necromancer, however, remained active. Dren reminded Sequoia that his troops had only been ordered to gather and burn bodies to reduce the necromancer’s future army, not attack the necromancer directly. He could send troops, but doing so would cost money and lives, and a victory by the party would better demonstrate their power.

Dren’s patrols had lost a couple of soldiers while sweeping the territory. His investigation led him to a massive bronze door near an old crypt. Brother Eelrich Vane had been uncooperative and received a bruised eye for it. Dren offered to kill him, but Calmer argued that Eelrich had been terrorized enough and that further deaths might make Barkinar suspicious just as the party had reestablished its authority. Kelno’s informants also confirmed that the necromancer had once been an Earth Temple cleric who became furious when Romag was promoted and disappeared.

At a sealed northern door, Zephyr heard nothing and found no obvious way through. Zinnia’s inspection was equally fruitless, but Sequoia immediately saw that the visible handle was a decoy. Pulling or pushing at it would create noise; pressure applied at a particular spot on the opposite side opened the door silently. The construction was relatively new, and claw marks scarred it.

Sequoia and Zephyr slipped through unseen. They entered a ruined storeroom littered with smashed flasks, shattered pottery, glass shards, broken shelves, herbs, spices, chemicals, and unidentifiable mixtures. Six ghouls manipulated the surviving materials like grotesque laboratory assistants while a hulking ghoul abomination directed them. Dead bodies, bones, and partially eaten remains covered the floor.

The scouts continued into a meditation room plastered with disgusting earth-cult scenes and adages, with a huge painted eye glaring from the ceiling. Padded benches and chairs showed that the chamber was still used. A powerful ghast and two lesser ghasts occupied it, stretching and chewing on something the scouts preferred not to identify. Sequoia and Zephyr withdrew without being detected.

At the massive bronze door, the party found a great lock and runes that might identify what lay beyond or serve as a warning. Calmer identified them as glyphs of warding and cautioned that touching the door would cause damage. None of the others had Dispel Magic, but Calmer did. He proposed neutralizing the wards, allowing the rogues to pick the lock, and launching “one dastardly, full-power throttle battle.” The party postponed the assault and explored south instead.

They entered a chamber finished in polished black stone. Thirty feet overhead, phosphorescent points formed miniature constellations across a domed ceiling. A broken obsidian altar stood in the center, and a tall arch to the south bore the Common inscription “Exhaulted Chamber of High Augury.”

Beyond it lay another polished black chamber, this one with blue-tinged lights forty feet overhead. A circular depression, one foot deep and ten feet across, occupied the center. Low stone seating surrounded it, suitable for halflings or for humans sitting inward with their feet toward the hollow. Eight small drawers were hidden beneath the benches. Seven were empty; the last contained pieces of bone, ivory, rock, and wood shaped into cubes, rods, cones, and other forms.

When Zephyr lifted the pieces, he felt an overwhelming compulsion to throw them into the central depression. Their configuration appeared able to answer simple questions. After conferring, the party asked where Prince Thrommel could be found. The oracle answered, “Beware the Eastern Tomb.” Asked whether their current location was that tomb, it replied, “Unlikely.”

The party now knew the likely position of the necromancer’s base, the strength of some of his servants, and the means by which the bronze door might be opened. They decided not to rush the lair. Their next priority would be the meeting with Hedrack, followed by the hamlet expedition, while the warning about the Eastern Tomb hung over the search for Thrommel.

### Prince Thrommel

A Paladin Lord, Grand Marshall of Furyondy, Provost of Veluna, and heir apparent who disappeared ten years ago at the Battle of Emridy Meadows. His planned marriage was intended to unite Furyondy and the Archclericy of Veluna against northern threats such as the Horned Society and Iuz. He is rumored to remain alive beneath the temple and may be Falrinth’s sleeper in an iron coffin. His crown-and-crescent personal emblem was found in four pieces on the corpse of Sequoia’s sister. The divination chamber warns anyone searching for him to “Beware the Eastern Tomb.”

### Falrinth

A wizard who previously told the party of a sleeper hidden in an iron coffin on the level below their quarters. He claimed that awakening the sleeper could bring Verbobonc’s banners down upon the temple. Falrinth also claims to possess the Orb of Golden Death but explicitly refuses to surrender it. He can be contacted by placing a candle in the western wall and has asked for word once the sleeper awakens. He warned the party that Lucius turns gifts into obligations and that Lucius and Senshock would recognize Calmer’s “TZGY” command key.

### Dren

The party’s subordinate commander within the temple. He manages supplies, troop pay, food, corpse disposal, and local security. He lets Barkinar into the party’s quarters because he has no standing order to begin a war in the doorway against anyone claiming superior rank. His patrols locate the northern undead chambers and the bronze door near the crypts, though a couple of soldiers are lost in the process. He roughs up Brother Eelrich Vane for withholding information and obtains further intelligence from Kelno. Dren argues that the party should handle the necromancer personally to preserve troops and demonstrate their strength.

### Barkinar

A senior temple commander and former head of the Earth Temple. He waits uninvited in the party’s quarters beside Tolub’s severed head, which Lucius suggested he present as a trophy. Barkinar praises the party’s success in restoring the lower temple and reopening trade with Nulb, questions them about the Chest of Rao and the necromancer, and orders Calmer to perform the Earth Temple blood ritual. He proposes raiding a resistant hamlet for supplies and a victim, extending the ritual deadline to five days. He also summons the party to meet Lord Hedrack. Barkinar departs with several black-clad lower-temple guards who had remained unnoticed during the meeting.

### Tolub

A boastful captain in Nulb who mocked the party and corresponded with the Water Temple. The party asked Selentis to make him “gone,” and she complied literally. His severed head is presented on a plate in the party’s quarters. Barkinar remembers that Tolub mocked his attempt to recruit him into the Earth Temple and boasted that Belsornig and Water would rule the universe.

### Lord Hedrack

The supreme commander of the lower temple. The party’s recent accomplishments have drawn his attention, and he requests a meeting for the day after their return. The party intends to ask him why the temple’s leaders, as Calmer sees it, are so concerned with destroying the Chest of Rao. Dren is also told that Hedrack’s authority would determine whether other high-ranking temple figures could be barred from the party’s quarters.

### Calmer

A cleric of St. Cuthbert serving as the head prophet and self-styled Lord Master of the Lesser Temples. He has recently been seduced by the authority promised in the Earth Temple’s evil manuals but reconsiders after Mother Screng’s questions and Zinnia’s warning that he was going too deep. After insulting Zephyr’s tiefling appearance, he sincerely apologizes, acknowledges that outward appearance does not determine evil, and contrasts Zephyr’s loyalty with Terjon’s saintly appearance and willingness to let him die. He thanks Sequoia for serving as his conscience. Calmer discovers that the Chest of Rao can replace a human victim in the Earth ritual, identifies the glyphs of warding on the bronze door, and offers to cast Dispel Magic.

### Brother Eelrich Vane

A provisionally useful temple subordinate previously implicated in the acid-pool incident. He proves uncooperative when Dren questions him about the necromancer and receives a bruised eye. Dren offers to kill him, but Calmer advises keeping him alive to avoid drawing Barkinar’s suspicion.

### Ghoul Abomination

A large and powerful undead creature occupying the ruined storeroom. It supervises six lesser ghouls as they manipulate chemicals, herbs, and surviving laboratory materials, apparently conducting research for the necromancer. The party recognizes it as a more powerful form of a creature encountered previously.

## Locations

### Nulb

A dilapidated and treacherous village where the party meets Mother Screng and Otis, restores its supply arrangements, and learns that Prince Thrommel may still live beneath the Temple of Elemental Evil. Otis can provide a safe house and discreet transport here if the party successfully extracts the prince.

### The Party’s Quarters

A room within the temple where Barkinar waits uninvited. Tolub’s severed head is initially displayed on the dining table before Sequoia’s hygiene complaint persuades Barkinar to move it to a side table. The meeting here establishes the five-day ritual deadline, the hamlet raid, and the party’s appointment with Hedrack.

### Private Chambers

The personal quarters where Zinnia, Zephyr, and Sequoia discuss Thrommel away from Calmer. Here they agree to conceal the intelligence until the prince is found, allow Zinnia to claim credit for the discovery, and consider using Thrommel’s emblem in their campaign to dismantle the temple from within.

### Earth Temple Pyramid

The ritual structure where a victim is traditionally placed atop the pyramid and torn apart by four earth elementals. The presiding cleric bathes in the blood and sprinkles it over the followers, temporarily blessing them and strengthening the cleric’s rites. Calmer proposes substituting the Chest of Rao for the living victim.

### The Necromancer’s Storeroom

A wrecked chamber filled with smashed flasks, shattered pottery, glass shards, destroyed furniture, herbs, spices, chemicals, bones, and partially eaten corpses. Six ghouls operate as laboratory assistants under the direction of a ghoul abomination.

### The Meditation Room

A former Earth Temple retreat plastered with disgusting cult scenes and elemental adages. A huge painted eye glares from the ceiling, and padded benches and surviving chairs line the walls. A powerful ghast and two lesser ghasts currently use the room for rest and feeding.

### The Bronze Door

A massive portal near the old crypts, fastened by a heavy lock and covered in runes. Dren’s investigation points to it as the entrance to the necromancer’s inner sanctum. Calmer identifies its markings as glyphs of warding and offers to dispel them before the rogues attempt the lock.

### Exhaulted Chamber of High Augury

A pair of polished black stone chambers beneath domed ceilings filled with phosphorescent constellations. The outer chamber contains a broken obsidian altar and a southern arch bearing the location’s name. The inner chamber has blue-cast lights, a shallow circular depression, low inward-facing seats, and eight hidden drawers. Its divination objects answer the party’s inquiry about Prince Thrommel with “Beware the Eastern Tomb” and indicate that the chamber itself is unlikely to be that tomb.

## Items

### Prince Thrommel’s Emblem

A personal heraldic device depicting a crown and crescent moon. Sequoia found it as four puzzle pieces on his sister’s corpse, but their multiple possible arrangements concealed the intended image until Mother Screng assembled them. She identifies the result as precisely Thrommel’s personal emblem and speculates that it may have been a secret gift from the prince to Sequoia’s sister. Otis takes it as evidence of a relationship between Sequoia’s family and Thrommel. The party hopes it may provide proof of identity or leverage within the temple.

### Tolub’s Head

The severed head of the boastful Nulb captain whose removal the party requested from Selentis. Lucius staged its presentation on a plate atop their dining table, and Barkinar presented it to them. After Sequoia objects that he eats there, Barkinar moves it to a side table and offers to put it on a shelf. For Barkinar, the head is also proof that an Earth Temple cleric finally brought down a man who had mocked Earth’s power.

### Chest of Rao

A powerful artifact of great good that the party previously surrendered to Romag to gain entry and must now locate again. Brother Eldin said that it belongs with the Archclericy of Veluna and warned that evil powers could use it as a magical battery. Barkinar wants it recovered or dealt with, while Calmer discovers that it could replace a human victim in the Earth Temple ritual. Because Calmer believes the temple’s senior leaders are unusually eager to destroy it, the party intends to question Hedrack before deciding its fate.

### Bronze Door

A massive portal in the temple crypts secured by a heavy lock and covered in magical runes. Dren believes it guards the necromancer’s lair. Calmer identifies the runes as glyphs of warding and proposes dispelling them before Sequoia or Zephyr picks the lock.

### Divination Objects

Bits of bone, ivory, rock, and wood shaped into cubes, rods, cones, and other geometric forms. They are found in the only occupied drawer beneath the benches of the Exhaulted Chamber of High Augury. Anyone lifting them feels compelled to cast them into the central depression. Their configuration can answer simple questions; when asked where Prince Thrommel could be found, they warn, “Beware the Eastern Tomb.”

## Spells

### Glyph of Warding

A protective magical trap inscribed upon the bronze door leading to the necromancer’s suspected inner sanctum. Calmer warns that touching the door would cause damage. The party leaves the runes undisturbed.

### Dispel Magic

A spell Calmer can cast to neutralize the glyphs of warding on the bronze door. His proposed plan is to dispel the runes, allow the rogues to pick the massive lock, and attack whatever lies beyond at full strength. The party postpones the attempt while it prepares to meet Hedrack.
