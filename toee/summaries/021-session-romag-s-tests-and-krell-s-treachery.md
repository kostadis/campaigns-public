# Chapter 21 Romag's Tests and Krell's Treachery

Date: 2025-12-14

## Summary

The party stood in the eerie octagonal room deep beneath the Temple of Elemental Evil, where the previous session had left them. With the wraith still frightened by Calmer's divine power, they had only a few minutes to slip out.

Following the map Dick Rentsch had provided, the party made their way through a 20-foot-wide processional hallway painted with scenes of gross evil, death, and destruction, including parties of worshippers and their sacrificial captives, each led by a cleric. Great doors of polished brass and bronze lined the southern walls, their surfaces carved with vile scenes and runes, with huge rings hanging from their centers. A religion check (Zephyr rolled a 17) told the party this was where clerics would drag their sacrificial captives during religious ceremonies, the procession branching toward the Air Temple in one direction and another temple in the other—part of the web of elemental temples that warred for dominance in this cursed place.

When they reached the designated door and knocked, they entered a room that felt entirely different from the rest of the temple. The floor was white marble, the walls and vaulted ceiling sheathed in pearly alabaster, creating a right feel—a place of safety in a storm of evil. The room was bare and clean, and as they looked around, a sheet of cloudy crystal substance set into the northern portion of the east wall began to brighten, its radiance lighting the chamber. Two man-like creatures with wings of snowy feathers and radiant faces appeared amidst glowing, cloud-tinted billows, robed in purest white with a pale blue sky visible between them.

The brighter of the two radiant figures peered at the party and spoke in dulcet tones, asking what fair and just folk were doing in this foul place of pain and base wickedness. When the party explained they were on a mission to find gnolls and defeat the Temple of Elemental Evil—Zephyr insisting, "We were not evil, we are quite the opposite"—the figures seemed delighted. The brighter figure then made an unusual request: the party should place every weapon, holy symbol, magic item, silvered item, and magical herb before the crystal window, then step out of the west door and count slowly to seventy-seven while the figures sent the Ray of Just Might through the room to bless their equipment, doubling its efficacy for seven hours. Any flesh left in the room, they warned, would be blasted to oblivion, and any mortal eye beholding the ray would be forever blinded.

The party hesitated, their instincts warning them something was amiss. Zephyr's Insight check came up a mere 7, leaving him unable to read the figures. Zephyr offered only his short sword, Zinnia gave a single dagger ("I don't use weapons anyways"), and Sequoia reluctantly parted with one dagger as well. When the radiant figures pressed them to surrender more weapons, insisting they surely wouldn't refuse such a divine blessing, the party stood firm in their refusal—Zephyr protesting that he had already given his most powerful weapon. Disappointed, the angelic figures declared they would reserve their blessing for someone else and vanished, leaving the party to wonder what trap they had narrowly avoided.

As they exited through the eastern door, Zinnia's keen eyes (passive Perception 16) spotted a secret entrance. Opening it discreetly, they discovered a bare 20-foot-square room permeated by a feral odor, with nesting heaps of cloth and pillows, bones and litter covering the floor, a large cupboard holding dirty pewterware, wine, and cooking herbs, and rust-red cloaks hanging on pegs. Inside were two werewolves who snarled at the intruders, their eyes gleaming with predatory intelligence. One of them laughed darkly, saying this could have been much easier if they had just dropped all their weapons and come in unarmed—they supposed they would have to do it the hard way for the Fire Temple.

The battle was fierce and desperate. The party quickly realized their weapons were ineffective against the lycanthropic creatures, their blades seeming to pass through flesh without causing proper harm—and, to the DM's surprise, heard the players say none of them carried a magic weapon. ("Wow. I'm really stingy with the magical stuff, aren't I?" "You really are.") Calmer cast Magic Weapon to temporarily enchant Sequoia's short sword and Zinnia's spear. Zephyr's dagger already possessed such enchantment, and Calmer had a mace of smiting, which he tossed to Zinnia. The werewolves fought with savage ferocity, their claws and fangs tearing into the party—one critical strike left Calmer, in his plate +1, down 18 hit points—but Calmer's radiant guardians materialized around him, burning the creatures with holy light. Zinnia landed a devastating critical blow with his enchanted spear, and together the party brought down both werewolves, standing victorious but battered, with Zephyr reduced to just 8 hit points.

Calmer declared there had better be some magical items in the lair after such a difficult fight. Searching through the room, they discovered a silver service worth 450 gold pieces, which they divided among themselves. More importantly, they found a magical dagger and short sword, which they distributed to those who still lacked enchanted weapons. But it was Zinnia who made the most crucial discovery—a loose flagstone, clearly dug up, that concealed a hidden treasure pile. Beneath it lay 1,600 silver pieces, holy symbols of Saint Cuthbert and Rao, a mace, a jumble of weapons, and most critically, five silver daggers and nineteen silver-tipped arrows. The irony was not lost on them: the very equipment they had needed to fight the werewolves had been hidden in the werewolves' own lair.

Rather than rest in the lair, the party decided to return to Romag and report what they had found. They carefully navigated back through the dangerous corridors, sneaking past the no-longer-frightened wraith in the octagonal room with a group stealth check—Calmer having already spent both of his uses of divine power—and moving silently through the bone-covered hallway where skeletal warriors lay waiting to animate. When they finally reached Romag's chamber, they found Dick Rentsch sitting beside the high priest. Romag was pleased to see them alive, as Dick Rentsch had apparently wagered they would not survive. "Sadly, we came back alive," Zephyr quipped. When the party explained they had encountered werewolves instead of gnolls, Romag turned sharply to Dick Rentsch, who admitted that Alrrem had hired werewolves in the secret chamber as a test of the new recruits.

Romag expressed his approval that the party had not fallen for the angelic trap, recognizing their wisdom and caution. He handed them badges of the Earth Temple, declaring they had proven their worth and would have a place among his followers, before dismissing them because "there are bigger things afoot." That night, the party rested in the bunk rooms with the other guards. When Zephyr's attempt to sell their devotion to the Earth Temple fell flat, Calmer boldly claimed to be a former cleric of Saint Cuthbert who had come to realize "it's all about the dirt." His declaration swayed the other guards, who saw the conversion of a holy man as proof of the Earth Temple's righteousness.

The next morning, Lieutenant Krell appeared with a mission from Romag. The Fire Temple had seized a portion of their level, and the party was to raid them, killing as many as they could without getting themselves killed. He explained that Earth Temple gnolls occupied one area while Fire Temple gnolls held another, and the rivals periodically clashed in brutal combat—opening the doors, charging, killing each other, and going back to square one. The party, having shown more subtlety than the typical gnoll, was tasked with tipping the balance in the Earth Temple's favor. They left to the sound of the guards' Earth Temple chant, screaming the lyrics back with appropriate enthusiasm.

The party moved stealthily to the designated location and entered a chamber that had once been a plush guest chamber but was now dirty and cluttered. Inside was an ogre, and they surprised it and made quick work of it. The ogre had barely managed to shout "You Earth Temple! You die!" before Zephyr and Sequoia cut it down. But as they examined its body, a terrible realization dawned on them—the ogre was a member of the Earth Temple, not the Fire Temple. Someone had set them up, and the most likely culprit was Lieutenant Krell himself.

Furious at the betrayal, the party returned to Romag to explain what had happened. When they arrived, Lieutenant Krell was conspicuously absent, and Dick Rentsch was gone too. Romag at first scolded them for disturbing the High Cleric with petty messages, but listened to their account, and his rage became palpable as he declared he had known the lieutenant was a spy and had kept him under watch. With fury burning in his eyes, Romag commanded the party to bring the traitor to him alive so he could sacrifice him in the Temple of Earth as a warning to all who would dare betray them. The party now faced a new mission: hunting down a cunning spy who had nearly gotten them killed, knowing that the treacherous politics of the Temple of Elemental Evil made trust a dangerous commodity—as the DM put it, "kind of like office politics, except if you get it wrong, you die. Horribly."

- The DM's takeaway: not everybody is to be trusted—the factions live right on top of each other, both working together and trying to subvert each other. "It's kind of like office politics, except if you get it wrong, you die. Horribly."
- The session ended with the party tasked to find and capture the traitorous lieutenant.
- The players said they were enjoying the conspiracy-and-subterfuge style of campaign.

## Locations

### Waterside Hostel

A hostel in Nulb where, in Chapter 16, Dick Rentsch awaited the party and arranged their meeting with Romag. Calmer barricaded the back-room door with a heavy chest and set Zephyr to watch the window while they waited.

### Temple of Elemental Evil

A massive, leprous grey monument to darkness with a ruined tower, obscene carvings, and sealed bronze doors with glowing runes. It exudes a pervading sense of fear with distorted sounds and movements at the edges of vision. The wind moans through gaps in the walls like wails of doomed souls.

- Approached by a rutted, weed-choked road thick with thistles, burrs, thorns, and nettles, showing disguised signs of heavy recent traffic.
- Surrounded by dead trees, unnaturally colored vegetation, and skulls and bones covered in strange fungus.
- The ruined tower juts from the northeast corner of the shattered walls; ravens flap among the ruins.
- Main entrance: four pairs of bronze doors, 23 feet high and 20 feet wide, held by iron chains, every crack sealed with soft iron, and graven with silvery runes. In Chapter 16 the party recalled a story of powerful wizards who sealed the place with mighty magic.
- Extends deep underground; the first two levels are divided among the Earth, Fire, Water, and Air temples.

### Temple Vestibule

The entrance area, passed through in Chapter 16, with reddish-brown slate tiles and plastered walls painted with horrifying scenes of killing, torture, and enslavement. Dim light filters through stained glass, casting revolting colors, and pinkish mineral pillars support arches decorated with bas-reliefs.

### Central Altar Chamber

A chamber containing a central altar of pinkish-white marble, carved with human-shaped depressions and stained dark with old blood. North of the altar is a circular pit surrounded by shards of broken crystal glass. In Chapter 16 Dick Rentsch described this as his 'happy place.'

### Bone-Covered Corridor

A 100-foot long, 20-foot wide passage covered entirely in a jumble of human and humanoid skeletons, many still wearing sundered armor and clutching broken weapons, testifying to a great battle. Gnoll skeletons animated here to attack the party in Chapter 16; on the way back to Romag the DM described an army of essentially infinite skeletons, and the party crossed with stealth checks. It is reached through a secret door that Dick Rentsch opened by knocking on the wall.

### Romag's Chamber

A lavishly decorated chamber where Romag, the high priest of the Earth Temple, resides and conducts meetings with his followers and new recruits.

### Octagonal Room (Air Temple Power Source)

A large octagonal room 15 feet below the normal dungeon floor, illuminated by eerie, milky radiance emanating from the ceiling, walls, and floor. It features a polished gray stone floor with glittering mica, swirling glowing mist, a bronze square and circular shaft above a pit, an alabaster block with knives and a crystal bowl, and crystal braziers emitting sweet perfume smoke. Bronze doors to the north are sealed with iron chains and warning runes. The braziers hang from silver chains; the wraith lurks here.

### Processional Hallway

A 20-foot-wide passage leading from the octagonal room, its walls painted with scenes of gross evil, death, and destruction, and of worshippers leading sacrificial captives, each led by a cleric. Polished brass doors with huge rings line the southern walls. Processions once branched from here toward the Air Temple and toward another temple.

### Angelic Figures' Room

A room with white marble floors and pearly alabaster walls that feels like a place of safety in a storm of evil. A cloudy crystal substance in the east wall brightens to reveal radiant figures. The room is bare and clean, with doors to the west and east.

### Werewolf Lair (Secret Room)

A bare 20-foot square room permeated by a feral odor, with nesting heaps of cloth and pillows by the east wall, bones and litter covering the floor, a large cupboard against the northern wall, and rust-red cloaks hanging on pegs. A hidden flagstone conceals a treasure cache. The secret door, spotted by Zinnia just outside the angelic figures' room, is its only apparent entrance.

### Bunk Rooms

Barracks area where Earth Temple guards and new recruits sleep, a place for rest and interaction with other members of the Earth Temple.

### Ogre's Chamber

A room with polished brown granite slabs, once a plush guest chamber but now dirty and cluttered. It contains cushions and pillows for beds, a half-empty wine barrel, salted fish, spears, and quivers of arrows. The walls are plastered; a shortbow hangs on pegs alongside the quivers, and a chest sits beside a barrel.
