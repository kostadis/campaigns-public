# Chapter 11 A deadly battle

Date: Planting 23, 579 to Planting 24, 579

## Summary

The chapter opens on the aftermath of a lost fight. In Zephyr's account, the battle was "a shit show": Sequoia and Zinnia were knocked out, Calmer healed them, and they tried to run but were attacked by Thalsor. Calmer tried to heal them again, but Thalsor knocked him out. Zephyr saved Sequoia and Zinnia, and then, just as he was about to reach Calmer's body, Thalsor killed him.

Zinnia's entry calls the operation a disaster. The initial attack succeeded, as they overwhelmed the enemy with speed and stealth, but the party lacked tactical awareness of the enemy's assets, in particular the power of the druid (Thalsor) and the golem. He writes that the plan was reckless and that the price was Calmer's death. Thalsor offers a trade: “I know you want your friend back. I want to know who sent you. Give me that information, and I will not mutilate his body.” Zinnia notes that the bastard will mutilate the body anyway, making it impossible to raise him from the dead. But more importantly, Zinnia thinks: “If we let him live, our identities will be exposed to the cult. Someone will connect us to the village elders. Thalsor needs to die.”

Sequoia's entry records the decision. He says, “We have to save Calmer.” Zephyr looks at him and asks “How?” and Sequoia, staring at the wounds, answers “Tomorrow.” Zephyr shouts, “We’ll negotiate tomorrow, Thalsor.” Thalsor screams his plans to kill, mutilate and destroy and promises the retribution of earth and evil, but does not come out of his compound; he is wounded too and does not want to die.

On Planting 24, 579, Sequoia says that Thalsor described all night, in detail, what he would do to the body if they did not show up in the morning, and that the party planned all night. The plan: Thalsor expects them to steal the body and is probably guarding it carefully, so they will use potions of gaseous form to sneak in and attack from close range. Zephyr will take a position to attack with bows, and Zinnia will take advantage of the chaos to strike.

In Zinnia's account of the morning, Thalsor stands in the middle of the yard screaming promises of despicable acts against Calmer. Sequoia, in position with his potion of gaseous form, glides toward Calmer's corpse and Thalsor does not notice the mist. Sequoia materializes, grabs Calmer's body and runs, and Thalsor is shocked. Without Calmer's spells they cannot kill the golem, so they focus on Thalsor. The surprise works: Zinnia's and Zephyr's attacks strike him down quickly. Only a mud mephit remains, and they kill that too. The golem is immobile. Calmer's body lies intact and can be raised.

Next they need what they came for: the chest Lareth wanted and the honey that Pasmarie Clee was looking for. Zephyr says, “These were not some random bandits. Lareth said some random bandits stole a chest. These are cultists of earth. We just solved a problem for him.” Zinnia realises: “The various elemental factions are at each other’s throats. Lareth wants to get rid of the competition. He sent us here to get rid of his enemies. We just made him more powerful.”

The remainder of the chapter consists of three retrospective perspective sections (Zinnia, Sequoia, Zephyr) covering the same stretch from different angles. In Zinnia's, after the skirmish with the Black Earth cultists they secured the stolen honey from Pasmarie Clee and found notes indicating the lost chest was located in a monastery within the mountains. They returned the honey in Sheernobb and received their reward. In Hommlet, to revive Calmer, Zinnia gave up a magical trident as a tithe to Terjon at the Temple of St. Cuthbert, a move that chafed given Terjon's prior dismissiveness. They visited Jaroo, who reiterated the importance of securing the chest, noting that Terjon and his followers might withhold healing magic from non-followers, and Burne and Rufus, who confirmed their interest in the chest, though their priorities seemed more political than purely good.

In Sequoia's section, the lead came from the notes of Romag to Thalsor, confirmed by an unsent note from Thalsor, and the journey to Ostverk was uneventful. They then went to Ostverk and to the tavern "The Cheap Forester", whose keeper, the gnome Robert Deakin, insisted they buy food before he would talk. Zinnia lists the rumours: a powerful chest had been lost to bandits and many were searching for it; Deakin insisted that rumours of a "big evil power" were lies and that they were merely bandits, claiming to know because he fought in the Battle of Emridy Meadows; he theorised that the chest's purpose was to secure a treaty with the Kingdom of Celene, which the Viscounty (Verbobonc) intended to use to wage war on the gnomes and seize their gem mines; the elves at Caer Ostverk and Lord Denelu were rumoured to be willing to pay well for information on the chest; and Deakin said he was related to the Clee family and even had honey mead from the Honey Haven Tavern.

Sequoia's and Zephyr's sections tell the Ostverk visit from their own angles. In the tavern they met Rithelm Drake, a human soldier who initially demanded a drink, then bought the party one on learning they were adventurers, and who recalled an abandoned monastery near Gallows Corner; and Gonnathrad Kevanarial, a skinny High Elf diplomat in a fancy red tunic who had evidently consumed too many adventure stories and was thrilled to be a "quest giver"; Sequoia writes that he almost divulged information about the monastery before recalling his actual duty to find adventurers to hunt bandits. The party hinted at what they knew while withholding specifics, and he issued a letter of permit hiring them to pursue a lead regarding the chest near a monastery by Gallows Corner, worth 100 gold pieces on presentation to Caer Ostverk along with the information. Zephyr says he was eager to leave Ostverk as quickly as possible.
