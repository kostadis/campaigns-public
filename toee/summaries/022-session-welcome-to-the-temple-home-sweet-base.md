# Chapter 22 Welcome to the Temple: Home Sweet Base

Date: 2025-12-24

## Summary

Romag woke the party after eight hours of rest with urgent news. The traitor had been discovered. Lieutenant Krell had fled to the gnoll and bugbear territories, plotting rebellion against the Earth Temple. Romag's spies had tracked him down overnight. The DM made his allegiance explicit: Krell works for the Fire Temple.

Romag outlined a plan to trap the traitor. The commander would bring his troops to guard the entrance, and Earth Temple guards were posted to block Krell's escape. The party, accompanied by Calmer, would go through the dangerous gnoll-infested area and come up from behind. The DM marked the route on the map in yellow. He also made clear that the party did not have to take the mission and could explore elsewhere instead, "eventually, somebody might get cranky at you and wonder where the hell you are."

Over dinner the previous evening, the party had overheard rumors about Romag. He was a new and inexperienced leader trying to prove himself, known for micromanaging and lacking the efficiency and cleverness of his predecessor, who had been promoted to the Deep Temple. They also heard that the Earth Temple's strategy relied on overwhelming enemies with masses of low-level troops rather than the powerful monsters employed by the Fire, Water, and Air Temples.

Sequoia briefly argued that the party should let the chaos inside the temple fester rather than remove the traitor. Zinnia, a trained spy, recalled a master spy's lesson: a dead antagonist you control, whom everyone believes is alive, is more useful than a living one. In the end, Zephyr argued that taking the mission would earn more of the temple's trust, and the party agreed.

The party devised a clever strategy to convince the Earth Temple guards to mount a frontal assault on the gnolls as a distraction. Zephyr's bluff succeeded easily with a Persuasion roll of 24. The human guards harbored deep fears of being replaced by the gnolls and becoming their food.

When the guards advanced into the gnoll territory, disaster struck. Screams of "It's a trap!" echoed through the corridors as the gnolls sprang their ambush, catching the guards in nets and slaughtering them mercilessly. Calmer feared a massacre and stayed back. Zinnia, Zephyr and Sequoia crept forward to investigate and witnessed a horrifying scene of gnolls feeding on the fallen guards.

The party engaged the remaining gnolls in swift combat. Calmer cast powerful magic that summoned spectral guardians to devastate the clustered enemies. As they fought, they heard Lieutenant Krell's voice encouraging the gnolls, revealing his betrayal: "I told you they would try to kill you! … The Fire Temple protects its own."

The party pressed their advantage, cutting through the gnolls with precision strikes. Sequoia dropped one with a single sneak-attack arrow fired through a cracked door.

Krell shouted for his archers to target the cleric and break his concentration. Then he drew a peculiar hand crossbow contraption, a pistol of his own devising, and targeted Calmer directly. He fired twice and knocked the cleric unconscious with devastating shots; Calmer's last words were "NOT AGAIN!" Sequoia quickly revived Calmer with a healing potion, and the grateful cleric thanked him for his loyalty.

Surrounded and desperate, Krell put down his pistol at Sequoia's order and attempted to negotiate with the party. He argued that the Earth Temple used its followers as mere cannon fodder, while the Fire Temple under Alrrem valued power and glory. He offered an introduction to Alrrem and proposed joining forces to destroy the Earth Temple, so that they would be hailed as heroes.

The party rejected his offer, choosing to remain loyal to Romag. They attacked Krell carefully with melee weapons, using non-lethal strikes to subdue him rather than kill him, as Romag had demanded he be brought back alive. After a brief but intense fight, Sequoia delivered the final blow that knocked Krell unconscious.

The party searched Krell's quarters and the surrounding rooms. Krell's quarters had once belonged to an underpriest of the Earth Temple. They discovered considerable treasure, including platinum pieces hidden in a brazier, gold, a three-dragon ante set, and useful supplies like flasks of oil. They also found a secret door leading deeper into the complex. The DM later told the table that their flanking maneuver had saved them. Otherwise the gnolls would have gathered in the other room and come at them from the other direction. He also corrected himself: there had been no bugbears, only gnolls and hobgoblins.

With their prisoner secured, they brought the bloodied and unconscious Krell before Romag, who was immensely pleased with their success. He declared that everyone must witness the consequences of betrayal and ordered the party to attend a ritual sacrifice in the great chamber. Zephyr read Romag's mood and saw a mix of relief, fear, and sadistic glee.

The party was led through ornate corridors of polished brown marble veined with black and inlaid with yellow triangular patterns, lit by burning bronze crescents. They entered a vast ritual chamber with rough-hewn sandstone walls and a floor of dark brown earth, dimly illuminated by phosphorescent lichen. At the center stood a twenty-foot-square pyramid of hard-packed dirt, about six feet high, topped with a stone column bearing carved triangles and bronze manacles. The great sealed doors in the south wall were the magical doors the DM had talked about earlier.

Romag stood atop the pyramid and began chanting to the elder elemental earth god, shoving dirt into Krell's mouth as the traitor screamed about being tricked. Four massive earth elementals rose from the ground at Romag's command and slowly approached the altar. As the crowd chanted and cheered, the elementals tore Krell's body apart in a gruesome display of the Earth Temple's brutal justice before descending back into the earth.

The party carefully blended in with the cheering crowd, feigning enthusiasm for the horrific spectacle to avoid suspicion. Zephyr was "stoic and cheering." After the ritual concluded, they followed Romag and Hartsch, overhearing them discuss how the defeat had cost valuable resources and weakened their plans to overwhelm the Fire Temple. Hartsch (or the commander with them) suggested they could use their new allies, the party, as weapons against the Fire or Air Temple. The party noticed Romag disappearing through a secret door, marked it on their map, and decided to explore the area further.

Their exploration led them to a charred library filled with the burned remains of books on evil philosophy, double-dealing, self-advancement, and treachery. Among the remnants, they found references to the sacred earth triangle and warnings about trials of earthy terrors awaiting the foolhardy in the depths below. They discovered another secret door before continuing their investigation.

Eventually, they encountered four guards who challenged them with javelins. Zephyr briefly blurted "Fire Temple" before correcting himself to "Earth Temple," and the party identified themselves as new Earth Temple recruits. The guards mentioned rumors about Krell's death creating opportunities for promotion, revealing the cutthroat nature of advancement within the temple.

Commander Joradin arrived and questioned the party's presence in his area. Though he acknowledged their status as new recruits, he asserted his authority over that section of the temple and ordered them to return to their quarters. The party was unwilling to accept this restriction. They returned to Romag to question why Joradin could deny them access. Romag was still basking in what he considered a great victory despite the heavy losses. He explained that Joradin was a new recruit brought in by Dick Rentsch who didn't yet understand how things worked in the temple.

Realizing the party needed proper accommodations, Romag granted them the territory they had cleared of gnolls and hobgoblins as their own base within the Earth Temple. He provided them with written permission to explore freely and warned them sternly not to betray his trust. He explained that the Earth Temple's territory extended throughout specific areas of the complex, and the party now controlled a significant portion of it. Zephyr took the yellow areas on the map to be common ground for all the temples.

However, Romag also cautioned that having territory made them targets, as promotion within the temple often came by stepping over the bodies of rivals. The DM congratulated the party on achieving a rank in the Earth Temple. That rank came with obligations: recruiting, attending the ritual sacrifices, and serving as a target for ambitious Earth and Fire Temple rivals.

The party had successfully infiltrated the Temple of Elemental Evil, earned Romag's trust, and established their own base of operations deep within the enemy stronghold. They were positioned perfectly to continue their dangerous mission of espionage and sabotage. The session closed with Merry Christmas wishes all around.

## NPCs

### Romag

The new boss of the Earth Temple who recently took over after the previous boss Barkinar was promoted to the Deep Temple. He is trying to prove himself and is perceived as a micromanager who lacks the efficiency and cleverness of his predecessor.

He assigns the party the mission to apprehend Lieutenant Krell and later conducts a ritual sacrifice. He grants the party their own territory within the Earth Temple as a reward for their service, though he has a skewed perception of victory and defeat.

- He sent spies out overnight to find Krell and frames the mission as cleanup of a mess the party created.
- He insists that everyone witness the sacrifice.
- Zephyr's Insight read showed relief, fear, and sadistic glee.
- After the ritual, he privately admits the losses have weakened the Earth Temple's plan to overwhelm the Fire Temple.
- He grants free passage with the warning "And do not betray my trust."

### Lieutenant Krell

A traitor who betrayed the Earth Temple and allied with the Fire Temple under Alrrem. He holed up with gnolls and bugbears, plotting rebellion. He wielded a peculiar hand crossbow contraption known as a pistol and a scimitar.

He attempted to persuade the party to join the Fire Temple, claiming it values power and glory over the Earth Temple's strategy of using troops as cannon fodder. He was subdued by the party and later sacrificed in a gruesome ritual where earth elementals tore him apart.

- He is tactically aware: he ordered his archers to break Calmer's concentration and to stay out of the Spirit Guardians zone.
- He has armor class 15.
- He laid down the pistol on Sequoia's order.
- During the ritual he screamed "You're a fool! I can help you! I was tricked!"

### Calmer

A cleric and member of the party who expresses concern about the guards being massacred. He casts Spirit Guardians and Toll the Dead during combat, dealing significant damage to gnolls. He was knocked unconscious by Krell's pistol but was revived by Sequoia with a healing potion. After the ritual, he and the party follow Romag and Hartsch and overhear them discuss using the party as a weapon.

- He has armor class 20.
- He did not sneak in with the rogues. He then had to be persuaded to stand at the front as the "meat shield," and grumbled "Grumble, grumble, grr!"
- His last words on going down were "NOT AGAIN!"

### Earth Temple Guards

Low-level human guards who serve the Earth Temple. They are easily persuaded by the party to attack the gnolls, as they are wary of being replaced by gnolls and becoming food. They fall into a trap set by the gnolls, resulting in a massacre.

Later, the party encounters other Earth Temple guards in Joradin's area, who take the party for new recruits and see the sacrifice of one of Romag's lieutenants as a chance for promotion. These four guards were armed with javelins.

### Gnolls

Creatures allied with Lieutenant Krell who set a trap for the Earth Temple guards. They are described as not particularly smart and are found feeding on the cadavers of fallen guards in a gruesome scene. Multiple gnolls are fought and killed by the party.

They used nets in the ambush and fought with bone bows at range.

### Hobgoblin

A creature allied with Lieutenant Krell, noted for having tougher armor than the gnolls. Fought by the party during the confrontation with Krell. It stood between two gnolls as one of Krell's lieutenants. The DM later clarified that the enemies were only gnolls and hobgoblins, not bugbears.

### Hartsch

An adept serving Romag who walks back with him after the ritual sacrifice, along with a lieutenant or commander and several guards. He (or that commander) suggests to Romag that they can use the party as a weapon against the Fire or Air Temple. Romag brought Hartsch along when taking Krell to the ritual chamber.

### Earth Elementals

Four elemental beings summoned by Romag during the ritual sacrifice of Lieutenant Krell. They rise from the ground, move up to the altar, rip Krell's body apart, and then descend back into the dirt.

### Joradin

A commander within the Earth Temple and a new follower recently recruited by Dick Rentsch as part of a recruitment drive. He is protective of his assigned area and denies the party permission to explore it further, ordering them back to their quarters. Romag later dismisses his authority over the party.

He addressed the party by name ("Sequoia, Zephyr, Zinnia") and insisted, "this is my area, and I guard it."

### Dick Rentsch

Mentioned as the individual responsible for running a recruitment drive that brought Joradin into the Earth Temple.

### Barkinar

The previous boss of the Earth Temple who was promoted to the Deep Temple, making way for Romag to take over. At the table the DM called the predecessor "Belsornig"; that was a slip, and the record names Barkinar.

### Alrrem

A figure associated with the Fire Temple whom Krell mentions when trying to persuade the party to join, claiming Alrrem would treat them as heroes if they worked together to destroy the Earth Temple's forces. Krell offered the party "an introduction to Alrrem."

## Locations

### Gnoll and Bugbear Territory

An area where, Romag said, gnolls and bugbears lived (the DM later said there were no bugbears, only gnolls and hobgoblins), considered untrustworthy by the Earth Temple. The party was sent through this territory to come up from behind and apprehend Lieutenant Krell. Multiple rooms were found here including Krell's quarters and storage areas. The DM added room numbers to the map during planning.

### Krell's Quarters

A room that was once the quarters of a fairly important Earth Temple member, with carpets and tapestries covering plain stone walls, a few rugs on the floor, heaps of cloth for sleeping near the walls, a small brazier in the northeast corner, and a small keg of cheap beer in the center. A secret door was discovered here.

The tapestries identify the former occupant as an underpriest of the Earth Temple. The only normal exit is the door in the south wall of the alcove.

### Storage Room South of Krell's Quarters

A room with plain stone walls, doors to the north, east, and west, four heaps of bedding made of rags and torn tapestries by the south wall, two stools near the entrance to the southeast passage, a crude shelf with pottery bottles and mugs, and four spears in the northwest corner.

### Room Further South

A plain room with:
- bare stone walls with a few pegs and three brackets holding unlit torches
- a plain stone block floor
- a rude wooden table against the east wall, just south of the northeastern door, with three stools
- a small keg of water in the northwest corner, with a mound of ordinary-looking dirt beside it
- a south door with a spy hole that can be opened only from inside the room

### Guard Room

A room the party passes through on their way to the ritual chamber.

### Polished Marble Corridor

A corridor sheathed in polished brown marble veined with black, with triangular pieces of polished yellow marble inlaid in the floor forming a definite path. The brown marble shades toward beige northward but deepens to dark chocolate hue southward. Large bronze crescents are staggered at ten-foot intervals on either side, kept fueled and burning brightly.

### Ritual Chamber

A great open area where the principle of elemental evil of earth is served. The walls are rough-hewn sandstone and the entire floor is dark brown earth. Phosphorescent lichen provides dim illumination on walls, ceilings, supports, and arches. Crescents and wall sconces indicate that other light sources are used during ceremonies.

In the center is a twenty-foot square pyramid of hard-packed dirt rising about six feet with a flat top. Stone steps are set in the four faces. Atop it is a stone column with four faces bearing carved triangles, each sporting a pair of bronze manacles dangling from bolted rings. A bronze box rests at the base of the south face of the column.

Three doors are in the south wall, with the center pair being huge bronze valves bearing silvery glowing runes and sealed with soft iron fillings. The runes were chiseled by someone other than the doors' maker. The DM identified these as the magical doors discussed earlier.

### Romag's Secret Door

The secret door Romag used after the ritual. It is marked on the party's map as "Romag Secret Door."

### Charred Library

A large chamber that was once an extensive library, now filled with charred remains of books, broken tables and chairs, and scraps of torn burned scrolls. Remnants of works on the ethos of evil, double-dealing, self-advancement, and treachery were found, along with mentions of the sacred earth triangle and trial of earthy terrors. A secret door was discovered here; it leads south.

### Joradin's Area

An area within the Earth Temple under the command of Commander Joradin, where the party encountered four guards and was denied permission to explore further. It lies behind a door off an eastward passage, and voices could be heard through it.

### Party Territory

The area previously occupied by gnolls and hobgoblins (Romag's "traitorous gnolls and bugbears"), now granted to the party by Romag as their base within the Earth Temple. This includes Krell's former quarters and the surrounding rooms the party cleared out.

- On the map it is labelled "Earth Temple Gnolls" and marked as party territory, within the brown-lined Earth Temple area.
- Zephyr read the yellow areas as common ground for all the temples.

## Spells

### Spirit Guardians

Cast by Calmer during the battle with the gnolls. The spell creates spectral guardians in an area around Calmer that damage enemies who enter or stay within the zone at the end of their turn, and halves their speed. It proved highly effective against the clustered gnolls, dealing significant damage to multiple enemies. The spell requires concentration, which Krell attempted to break by targeting Calmer.

- Its first wave did 14, 11, 11 and 17 damage. Later rounds did 17 damage to each gnoll that failed its save.
- The DM chose it as the substitute for a fireball against the packed gnolls.
- Calmer kept his concentration through an arrow hit and Krell's critical pistol shot. It held until the second shot knocked him out.

### Toll the Dead

A cantrip used by Calmer multiple times during combat. It deals necrotic damage to enemies who fail their saving throws. Calmer used it to kill several gnolls during the battle, and later used it again after being revived from unconsciousness. The DM rolled 2d12 on a gnoll that was already wounded.

### Spiritual Weapon

A spell Calmer considered casting but ultimately did not use because he already had a concentration spell active with Spirit Guardians.
