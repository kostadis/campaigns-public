# Chapter 41

Date: 2026-10-18

## Summary

In the Exhaulted Chamber of High Augury, the party uncovered a secret door hidden within the walls, leading them down a narrow hallway into a forgotten chamber.

Beyond the secret door, the room was dominated by a partially destroyed throne of brown marble veined with black. Both arms had been broken off, the seat was chipped, and a fractured hole marred its back. Rubble and shattered furniture lay strewn across the floor, while the ceiling soared at least thirty-five feet overhead, lost in the shadows of ancient supporting arches. A faint stench of rotting flesh clung to the air. The party moved cautiously through the debris, examining the rubble while deliberately leaving the scattered bones untouched.

It was Zephyr who, with characteristic boldness, settled himself onto the broken throne—and immediately regretted it. Shadows tore themselves free from the darkness and rose from the bodies scattered across the floor, surging toward the party in a wave of cold, draining malice. As the DM explained, “Sometimes you control them, and sometimes you piss them off. This was one of the you-piss-them-off moments.”

Zinnia and Calmer were caught off guard, leaving them momentarily vulnerable. One shadow critically struck Zinnia with a draining swipe, dealing eight points of damage and sapping two points of Strength. Another struck Zephyr for five points of damage and drained three points of Strength; a later attack drained one more. Sequoia, however, was ready, and with a single precise strike obliterated one shadow before it could act again. The rest of the party rallied. Zephyr disengaged and destroyed another shadow with more than thirty points of damage, while Calmer channeled radiant holy energy that the shadows proved vulnerable to. After the final shadows were dispersed, Zephyr asked for his Strength back and the DM granted it.

During the battle, the DM’s digital dice produced an extraordinary run of low rolls—including four twos in succession. Declaring that he no longer trusted the roller, he announced, “The D20 is going into the penalty box. I’m get— I’m reaching for the manual.” Sequoia countered that, given the number of d20s rolled over a lifetime of play, the streak was not as statistically impossible as it felt.

Searching the ruined chamber afterward, the party discovered a remarkable treasure hidden among the rubble: a Ring of Shooting Stars, a very rare magical ring the DM had used only twice in his career. Its powers looked particularly promising against the undead ahead. Sequoia immediately called dibs, and after the group compared everyone’s magical equipment, Zephyr agreed that Sequoia could carry it. When Sequoia complained that Frostbrand had not done much, the DM defended the sword’s discretion: “He’s maintained stealth— he’s maintained operational security.”

After several hours passed and the party had recovered its drained Strength, they turned their attention to the necromancer lurking deeper in the complex. They recognized the destroyed shadows as further “gifts” from that enemy and decided it was time to deal with him before he sent any more. Their route offered three immediate problems: a storeroom full of monsters, a meditation room containing more enemies, and a bronze door inscribed with glyphs of warding that cause damage if touched. Calmer had Dispel Magic available for the wards, but the party first debated whether to strike the necromancer directly or eliminate his lieutenants.

Remembering the consequences of previously leaving a lone skeleton unattended, the group chose to clear the side rooms before confronting the necromancer. They worried that undead might phase through walls and that enemy spellcasters might teleport or blink into the battle, turning a direct assault into a pincer attack. Their plan was to attack the storeroom first, then the meditation room. Calmer would guard the approach with Spirit Guardians while the others struck north. Zephyr also proposed using Thaumaturgy to create a distracting sound.

Moving with practiced stealth, the party slipped into the laboratory storeroom without alerting its occupants. They found six ghouls in total, including two larger and more dangerous dread ghouls. What lay around them was grimmer still. One of the prisoners the party had left behind—part of a group that subsequently disappeared—had been dismembered and laid out in pieces across a bench. Another prisoner had been transformed into a ghoul, recognizable by the remains of prison rags and the shackle galls around its wrists. As the DM observed, “The necromancer has been busy.”

Sequoia struck first, landing a devastating twenty-nine-point Sneak Attack on one of the seventy-five-hit-point dread ghouls and following it with another eight points of damage before disengaging. The blow prompted the DM to protest, “I just spent days preparing this monster!” Zephyr charged in after him and battered the same dread ghoul down to a single hit point, then attempted to dash clear.

The DM initially ruled that the retreat provoked an opportunity attack, which paralyzed Zephyr and exposed him to two further attacks for a total of forty points of damage. After declaring, “You’re dead!” he remembered that the ghouls were still surprised and therefore could not take reactions or attack during that round. Every part of the sequence—the opportunity attack, paralysis, forty damage, and death—was reversed, leaving Zephyr safely withdrawn.

As the normal battle began, Sequoia killed one of the smaller ghouls, Zephyr finished the dread ghoul and slew another ghoul, and Zinnia advanced against the second dread ghoul. Zinnia endured a mass of bites and claws: several missed his armor, but a critical hit dealt eighteen points of damage, and another claw added five more. He succeeded on both Constitution saves and avoided paralysis. Calmer strode in behind the others and cast Spirit Guardians at fourth level, radiating holy power through the undead cluster while guarding the party’s rear.

Sequoia continued cutting through the lesser ghouls, while Zephyr’s attacks briefly faltered against the remaining enemies. Zinnia dealt forty-four points of damage to the surviving dread ghoul, reducing it to one hit point. The creature howled, and the party realized that whatever occupied the meditation room had begun moving toward them. The dread ghoul struck Zinnia twice but could not paralyze him because Zinnia was an elf. A lesser ghoul then attacked Zephyr, dealing three points of damage and successfully paralyzing him.

Calmer closed the fight with his customary composure. “As Calmer likes to say, it’s time for me to clean up the mess,” the DM announced. Spirit Guardians killed the one-hit-point dread ghoul and destroyed the last remaining ghoul after it failed its save. Zephyr’s paralysis expired after the round. The party stood victorious in the storeroom, but the dread ghoul’s howl had been heard, and the inhabitants of the meditation room were already moving toward them.

> **DM:** “The necromancer has been busy.”

Sequoia used surprise and Sneak Attack to tear into one of the toughest enemies in the room:

> **Sequoia:** “I’m gonna do 29 points of damage to it.”  
> **DM:** “I just spent days preparing this monster!”  
> **Sequoia:** “But what do you mean to have more HP? Did I just one-shot it?”  
> **DM:** “Nope. But you weren’t supposed to kill it. You weren’t supposed to cause THAT much damage.”  
> **Sequoia:** “Oh my… 75! Mamma Mia!”

His follow-up attack dealt another eight points before he disengaged.

**The party finds a Ring of Shooting Stars, and Sequoia immediately calls dibs.**

The DM described it as an exceptionally rare find:

> **DM:** “You find a very rare magic item.”  
> **DM:** “A very, very rare magic item. One that I have only used twice in my career.”  
> **Sequoia:** “I mean, I call dibs.”

After checking the party’s existing equipment, Zephyr agreed:

> **Zephyr:** “Yeah, Sequoia can have it.”

**The DM defends Frostbrand’s silence as exemplary operational security.**

When Sequoia complained that his intelligent weapon had not contributed much, the DM offered a different assessment:

> **Sequoia:** “Who hasn’t done much.”  
> **DM:** “What do you mean he hasn’t done much?”  
> **DM:** “He’s maintained stealth— he’s maintained operational security.”  
> **Sequoia:** “Better than most talking swords.”  
> **DM:** “I mean, come on, he’s maintained operational security. What else do you want out of him?”

**A dread ghoul’s dying howl alerts the creatures in the nearby meditation room, signaling that more undead are now moving toward the party.**

After Zinnia reduced the last dread ghoul to one hit point, its cry carried into the adjoining chamber:

## NPCs

### Prince Thrommel

A Paladin Lord and heir apparent mentioned in lore recovered by the party. His full recorded titles are “Grand Marshall of Furyondy” and “Provost of Veluna.” He is said to be a sleeper in an iron coffin on the level below the party’s quarters. Zinnia suspects that the coffin lies within the Eastern Tomb and that Thrommel may not look like a prince when found. Waking him is connected to the prediction that “Verbobonc’s banners come down on the temple.”

### Falrinth

A wizard who, in an earlier session, told the party of a sleeper in an iron coffin in a room on the level below their quarters. He wants word when the sleeper wakes.

### Calmer

A cleric of St. Cuthbert and party member who provides vital healing and radiant magic. He is initially surprised during the shadow ambush and is briefly surrounded, taking ten points of damage and losing four points of Strength before the undead are destroyed. His radiant magic proves especially effective because the shadows are vulnerable to it.

Calmer has Dispel Magic prepared to neutralize the glyphs of warding on the bronze door. During the storeroom assault, he guards the party’s rear and casts Spirit Guardians at fourth level, damaging the clustered undead and finishing the last dread ghoul and ghoul. His closing sentiment is summarized by the DM as, “It’s time for me to clean up the mess.”

### Shadows

Incorporeal undead that rose from corpses in the ruined throne room to drain the strength of the living. They ambushed the party after Zephyr sat upon the broken stone chair. Their draining swipes injured Zinnia, Zephyr, and Calmer while temporarily reducing their Strength. They could hide within the surrounding darkness but proved vulnerable to Calmer’s radiant power.

### Dread Ghouls

Stronger, more resilient versions of standard ghouls with higher armor, substantially more hit points, and the ability to paralyze victims with their claws. Two were encountered in the laboratory storeroom, each beginning with seventy-five hit points. The first was devastated during the surprise assault and finished by Zephyr. The second was reduced to one hit point by Zinnia, then howled loudly enough to alert the meditation room before Calmer’s Spirit Guardians destroyed it.

### Ghouls

Undead creatures found lurking among the dismembered remains of prisoners in the laboratory storeroom. One still wore the rags of a prisoner and bore shackle galls around its wrists, confirming that the necromancer had transformed at least one captive into an undead servant. Their claws could inflict paralysis; one successfully paralyzed Zephyr near the end of the battle, though the effect lasted only one round.
