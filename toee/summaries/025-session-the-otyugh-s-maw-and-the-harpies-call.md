# Chapter 25 The Otyugh's Maw and the Harpies' Call

Date: 2026-02-08

## Summary

The morning dawned with Dren and his Broken Blades mercenaries settling into their newly claimed territory within the Earth Temple. Romag, pleased with the party's previous successes, suggested they continue their expansion by exploring and clearing the areas to the south and east: "since you've done such an incredible job of clearing out the other areas, why don't you go clear out the rest?" The party agreed, eager to further solidify their position within the temple's hierarchy and expand Romag's power base. As Zephyr put it, "Let's clear out one more area for Romag."

Their exploration led them through a passageway to an old wine cellar, where the stale reek of spilled beer, wine, and ale hung heavy in the air. The floor was covered with dark residue, and broken barrels, casks, and kegs lay strewn everywhere. Among the debris, they discovered one small intact barrel. One of the party members, Sequoia, smelled its contents and could tell nothing from the smell. They took a sip and immediately regretted it, barely managing to spit out the liquid at the last minute and avoid being poisoned. They dry heaved for several minutes afterward, as it was "the most disgusting thing you've drank in a very long time."

Moving on, they found an adjacent storage chamber that had long fallen into disuse. Sacks of moldering grain and barrels of rotting flour sat among heaps of broken containers and spilled contents in a jumbled mess. The party continued their systematic exploration, determined to map out every corner of Romag's domain.

Their next discovery proved far more significant. They came upon a door secured with both a door lock and a padlock holding a chain in place. Its condition suggested it was "used with relative frequency." Sequoia skillfully picked the lock, rolling a 20, and the door swung open to reveal a filthy prison chamber. Inside, they found prisoners, all scantily clad, impoverished, and clearly suffering. The males were chained to the wall, and all of them looked terrified upon seeing the party's Earth Temple insignia.

The prisoners explained their circumstances with trembling voices. Most were local merchants who had been captured by raiders, but one stood out. Jorin Pike Kale, a river sailor, said he had been imprisoned by Dick Rentsch and his men as punishment for making a pass at a woman named Dala. They revealed the grim truth that Romag used them either to feed his gnolls or as sacrifices to the Earth Temple. Among the captives were Master Aldous Glimmerfield and Mistress Lenora Glimmerfield, textile merchants from Dyvers, along with their household staff. Four farm boys turned mercenaries, Kalen, Brask, Horgus, and Davine, had also been held. They had been hired to protect the Glimmerfields and had surrendered almost immediately when captured.

The party faced a difficult decision. They wanted to free the prisoners, but the DM made the cost plain: freeing them would certainly anger Romag if he discovered their involvement. Remembering that the temples hate one another, they devised a plan to frame the Fire Temple for the escape. They considered several sources of false evidence: drawing a Fire Temple insignia, gathering insignia from the lower temple's were-creatures or from the Air Temple, or using items they had previously collected from Lareth the Beautiful. Zephyr chose to scatter some of Lareth's Fire Temple belongings, "like maybe like a robe." They also had to contend with Dren's crew in the adjacent territory, who might hear the commotion. Calmer proposed having Sequoia drop in on Dren's crew to see how they were settling in while someone else freed the prisoners. The party decided to have Calmer stand guard at the door while the others freed the prisoners and scattered Fire Temple materials throughout the chamber.

Sequoia unlocked the prisoners' chains while the others kept watch. The prisoners had no idea how to get out, and the only exit the party knew of was the secret passageway. Zephyr explored further and discovered a stairway leading up to an escape route, one of several "escape hatches" from the temple. The party guided the grateful prisoners to freedom, and Jorin Pike Kale thanked them profusely. "I know things," he told them, "and I know when Captain Tolub's pirate ship comes to and from the docks." That knowledge could prove useful. Tolub had previously betrayed the party by sending them to the Hall of the Scarlet Moon in the Kron Hills, where Fire Temple clerics ambushed them. Jorin himself intended to disappear as far away as he possibly could. The Glimmerfields promised a reward of one hundred silver pieces for their rescue. Zephyr rolled a 12 on the deception meant to sell the Fire Temple frame. When he returned, his allies reported that nobody had come out.

The party decided to continue exploring before reporting the prisoner escape to Romag, hoping to give the freed captives "a couple hour head start." Zephyr suggested they tell him "at the end of the day." They discovered several empty armories filled with wooden racks, broken weapons, sundered shields, ruined mail, and scattered skeletons of both humans and gnolls. Cobwebs hung in the corners, suggesting these areas hadn't been visited frequently. Zephyr dubbed the first one the "empty weapons room." In another armory, they found five +1 arrows. As they explored, they began to realize that Romag, for all his bluster and ambition, had left large chunks of the dungeon empty and unused. In the DM's words, "as far as you're concerned, the guy is kind of a clown." Sequoia resolved to tell Romag that he had a lot of territory and that they needed resources to hold it.

Their exploration led them to a passageway that descended to the second level of the dungeon, an area they had previously visited when meeting with representatives of the Air Temple. Using the temple's geography, they guessed that this passage connected somewhere near the mysterious door that concealed an eldritch creature. They chose not to venture down at this time. They also passed a route leading up to the first floor and skirted the area where the ogres had been.

Instead, they continued their systematic clearing of the current level and came upon a room that reeked of filth. The chamber was filled with trash, waste, and bones, its former purpose impossible to determine beneath the accumulated refuse. Zephyr tried to persuade Calmer to investigate the trash pile, rolling a 12. Calmer pointed out that Zephyr was "the one with the high degree of stealth." He insisted they go together, telling Zephyr, "Hell no, you're going in first, boy."

As they approached the refuse pile, the stench hit them first. Then a massive tentacled creature suddenly emerged from the garbage. It was an Otyugh, a disgusting aberration that fed on waste and carrion. Everyone rolled Insight, and Zinnia (22) and Sequoia worked out that it had been sustained by the garbage generated by Romag's crew, but that it viewed the party as far more appetizing than its usual fare. Zephyr declared that this was exactly why Calmer should have checked the trash, since losing him would be way worse than losing Calmer. The DM warned that if Zephyr kept treating Calmer this way, Calmer would someday decide he had better things to do than work with him. The creature's telepathic voice echoed in their minds: "fresh meat. No, rot, good, do eat."

The creature's ambush surprised everyone except Zinnia, whose passive perception was 16. The battle erupted with Zinnia striking first, hitting the AC 14 creature twice for 16 damage. The Otyugh retaliated by lashing out with its tentacles, grappling both Zinnia (13 piercing) and Zephyr (12) in its powerful grip. It dragged Zinnia toward its gaping maw and bit down hard for 14 more. Zinnia failed his Constitution save with a 5, and the poison sapped his strength and cut his maximum hit points by five, leaving him on 8 hit points. Sequoia unleashed a devastating series of attacks, his weapons finding purchase in the creature's flesh again and again: 22, 8, and 6, for 36 in total.

Despite being poisoned and weakened, Zinnia managed to wrench himself free from the Otyugh's grasp, making the DC 13 Acrobatics check even with disadvantage. He then tried to retreat to safety. However, the creature struck him one more time as he fled, rolling a 25, and he collapsed unconscious. Zinnia's player cried "I'm dead," and the DM corrected him: "You're knocked out. You're not dead." Zephyr, still grappled by the tentacles, continued to fight from his constrained position. With Sequoia beside him, he landed a sneak attack for 19 and a second blow for 6. Calmer, clearly annoyed by the situation and Zephyr's earlier insistence that the healer should go first, muttered that "this is why the cleric doesn't go in first because the cleric heals people after they've been hit by creatures." He nevertheless performed his duty and cast a second-level healing spell on Zinnia, bringing him back from the brink of death to 23 hit points. He then strategically positioned himself away from immediate danger.

The Otyugh, frustrated by its prey's resilience, snapped at Zephyr and missed. It then hit Sequoia for five damage and shoved him ten feet away. Finally it slammed its tentacles against both Zephyr and Sequoia, stunning them both with the force of the impact. Both failed the DC 14 Constitution saves (Zephyr rolled a 9, Sequoia a 1) and took 17 damage each. Zinnia, recovered but still suffering from the poison coursing through his veins, attacked at disadvantage and then spent his focus points. His final strike for 11 brought the massive creature down. As it died, Calmer remarked pointedly about the importance of the healer not being in the front lines and cut Zephyr free from the dead creature's tentacles.

Following Calmer's suggestion that "sometimes you could find some interesting treasure in these creatures," they searched through the disgusting refuse for anything of value. Their efforts turned up one hundred eighty silver pieces (45 each) and fifteen gold pieces. They also found a glass vial containing a potion of greater healing that had somehow survived intact inside the creature's body, though it was sticky and smelled terrible. More disturbing was their discovery of a rusted, bent breastplate bearing the symbol of the Fire Temple. Zephyr's Insight check of 17 made the chilling implication clear: Romag sometimes fed his rivals to this creature, using it as a living disposal system for his enemies. The DM likened it to the party having killed "Romag's Sarlacc from Return of the Jedi." The party realized that Romag might not be pleased to discover they had killed his pet monster.

Undeterred, they pressed on with their exploration, entering a new chamber with doors to the north and southwest. As they stepped inside, they heard an ominous click, and a gate suddenly dropped behind them, sealing them in the room. Their passive perception had not been enough to spot the trap. Before they could react, they heard the sound of singing, beautiful, haunting, and utterly compelling. Harpies emerged from the shadows, their grotesque forms combining human-like features with the talons and wings of avian scavengers. The DM compared them to the singers who lure Ulysses toward the rocks in the Odyssey, which Sequoia's player had read at school.

The creatures' notorious song filled the chamber, demanding a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw. Sequoia succumbed to the enchantment, his mind clouded by the harpies' charm. Zephyr realized that this was the song of only one of the harpies, and that although he was immune to its song, the other two could charm him and the rest of the party. The party realized the desperate situation they now faced: trapped in a sealed chamber under threat from the harpies' magical influence, a song that could compel them to follow the creatures heedless of any danger. As the session closed, Zephyr asked whether blocking his ears would help. The DM confirmed it could be done as an action, and Zephyr resolved to do so next time.

### Otyugh

A large tentacled creature that dwells in refuse and ambushed the party in a trash-filled room. It telepathically communicated its desire for 'fresh meat' and was fed by Romag's crew's garbage. The party killed it and found evidence suggesting Romag feeds his rivals to it.

- It has AC 14 and surprised everyone except Zinnia.
- Its tentacles grapple targets, and its bite poisons and reduces maximum hit points.
- Its tentacle slam forces a DC 14 Constitution save or the target is stunned.
- It struck Zinnia as he escaped the grapple, knocking him unconscious.
- The DM likened it to "Romag's Sarlacc."

### Harpies
