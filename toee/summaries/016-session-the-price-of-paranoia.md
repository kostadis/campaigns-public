# Chapter 16 The Price of Paranoia

Date: 2025-09-28

## Summary
The dust had barely settled in the merchant office when Calmer's conjured spiritual weapon struck the final blow against Rannos Davl, leaving both him and Gremag dead on the floor. Zinnia, who had been hovering near death's door, was pulled back from the brink by Calmer's divine healing, the warmth of sacred energy knitting his wounds closed. Calmer's Cure Light Wounds restored 24 hit points. The party stood amid the wreckage of the confrontation, alive but now faced with a far more complicated problem: two dead cultists and a room full of evidence that could bring the wrath of the Temple of Elemental Evil down upon them.

It was the Duke, still bound from his earlier capture, who offered a way out. His first words on seeing the bodies were "oh shit." With a mercenary's calm and a professional's eye, he explained that Rannos and Gremag had not merely been Temple fanatics. They had answered to a shadowy power he called the Deep Temple, a hidden authority that sat above all four elemental cults. He warned that if the Deep Temple sent agents to investigate and found the bodies, the party would be hunted without mercy. He was especially worried that the Temple would cast Speak with Dead on the corpses, so he advised cutting out their tongues and dismembering the bodies. Zephyr volunteered to handle the dismembering.

What followed was a crash course in crime scene cleanup. The Duke negotiated a retainer for his services while barely concealing his amusement at the party's complete lack of experience in such matters. His terms were 100 gold a month, plus 50 gold per job. He asked 120 gold for this first cleaning, Zephyr countered with 90, and they settled on 100. Zinnia and Calmer were dressed in the dead cultists' clothes and sent riding out of town to fake a departure. Zinnia, an elf, could pass as long as he kept his mask on and looked no one in the eye. A few miles out, they were to soak the clothes in blood with a bloody sponge the Duke supplied, leave them there, and slip back unseen. Meanwhile the Duke spent about an hour scrubbing the office, carrying the bodies outside and digging holes. He prepared to tell anyone who asked that Rannos and Gremag had left on urgent business and asked him to keep an eye on the store. While he worked, the party searched the bodies and quarters. They found an amulet of proof against detection, a ring of protection +1, a dagger of venom, three doses of dust of disappearance, a black scarab inscribed "TZGY", 300 gold and 50 platinum. They found no incriminating documents, because the two cultists "weren't that stupid."

Back at the Inn of the Welcome Wench, the party first had to settle a badly shaken Calmer. Zephyr warned him he might get kicked out of "the school" if he kept freaking out. Zinnia then rolled a natural 20 to talk him round, and Calmer thanked him with a backhanded speech about his boils and disfigurement.

The party then spun their cover story to Ostler Gundigoot, the innkeeper, who proved more than willing to believe that the two merchants had fled town with a mysterious chest. Ostler leaned in close and whispered his own long-held suspicions about the pair. He suggested that local authorities, Burne and Rufus, would want to know, and that the party should reach out to Jaroo. Zephyr asked him to spread word that the chest had left the village.

It was then that Elmo, the town's resident drunken buffoon, pulled up a chair and joined them for drinks, which he cheerfully billed to the party. Zinnia and Zephyr saw through him instantly. Behind the slurred words and bleary eyes was a sharp, focused intelligence. Following up with an investigation, Zinnia recognized him for what he truly was: a spy working for the Viscount of Verbobonc.

The party then weighed where they stood. Verbobonc was building the castle to project force, had lost the chest, and was feuding with the gnomes, who claimed the "brigands" were a pretext for bringing in troops. The party realized they were independent contractors who had blundered into being key players in a regional conflict. They resolved to keep the chest, tell Elmo it had gone north with the dead men, and let him know they had seen through him.

The party brought Elmo to a private room and confronted him directly, stripping away his carefully maintained disguise with a few pointed words. Calmer did not join them; he had gone off to his temple to pray. The transformation was immediate. The slouching drunk straightened, his eyes cleared, and he acknowledged their powers of observation with genuine respect, calling Zinnia "masked one." He accepted the party's lie that Rannos and Gremag had stolen the Chest of Rao and fled north "a couple of hours ago." The news alarmed him greatly. He declined Zephyr's offer to chase the thieves, saying his rangers in the north would handle it. He also refused Zephyr's request for troops: if Verbobonc brought soldiers in, the gnomes would view it as an act of war. Instead he charged the party with returning to the Moathouse at first light to destroy whatever evil presence lurked in its depths. He framed them as independent contractors whose anonymity made them invaluable, and Zephyr promised to "speak pay" when the deed was done. Then, as quickly as he had dropped it, he pulled his drunken mask back on and stumbled out into the night, shouting something about needing to relieve himself. The party took rooms and a long rest.

The following morning brought Captain Rufus and Burne to the inn. Both were agents of Verbobonc who had also told the party about the missing chest. The party deliberately told them the same story they had told Elmo, so as not to be caught in two different versions. The two men visibly relaxed on hearing a consistent account. Zephyr and Sequoia, each rolling a 19 on insight, caught the look the two exchanged: they had been testing whether the party would contradict itself. Burne admitted, with some reluctance, that the authorities had been too slow to take the threat of the Temple's reactivation seriously. Both men echoed Elmo's urgent request to clear the Moathouse once and for all, so the area could be declared safe. Lareth the Beautiful still waited in the dungeon below, where they had first met him.

The party set out for the Moathouse and chose to enter through the back, a deliberate show of confidence: "just to show that we have power." Two gnoll sentries wearing some kind of insignia barred the rear entrance, declaring that their master had forbidden all entry. Zephyr answered "We are the masters," and Calmer warned that such creatures could not be reasoned with. That sparked a brief argument over whether the party were the good guys, which ended with everyone settling on chaotic good. The party answered with steel and sorcery, cutting the creatures down in a matter of moments, and made the grim decision to take their heads as a message. When one of Lareth's guards answered their knock, they told him they had come to talk to Lareth and meant business, and he let them through to Lareth's chambers.

Lareth the Beautiful received them with theatrical warmth that barely concealed his predatory intent. He had heard of "some unfortunate deaths in the Earth family" and made no secret of his suspicion that the party was involved, though he seemed almost impressed. He mused that if they had not brought him the chest, he could kill them anyway. When the party revealed they had not brought him the chest, his mood darkened. Zinnia noticed him take the stance of a predator about to swallow its prey, and the guards around the room began to shift into position.

The party attempted to redirect his fury by claiming the Deep Temple itself had stolen the chest, and a group persuasion check succeeded. Zephyr then pointed out that if Lareth belonged to the Deep Temple, he had in effect gained the chest. That gave Lareth pause, though he snapped that he had wanted the chest for himself. When they pushed further, claiming they had told the thieves the chest was meant for him and the thieves had scorned him, his paranoia boiled over. Lareth saw through the deception, rolling a 19 against Zephyr's 20, and his natural paranoia pushed him over the edge. He declared that the party had betrayed him, that they had been given one task and had failed, and that there was no reason left not to kill them where they stood. Zephyr shot back, "The only person here that's backstabbing is you," and that tipped Lareth into violence. The party threw down the gnoll heads in defiance, boasting of every cultist they had slain, as Lareth's guards closed in from all sides and the chamber crackled with the promise of violence. Initiative was rolled, and the session paused there, to resume from the top of the order.

## NPCs

A mercenary with a particular set of skills in disposing of bodies and scrubbing crime scenes. Motivated purely by gold, he negotiates a retainer with the party after the deaths of Rannos and Gremag, his former employers. He is pragmatic, amoral, and surprisingly professional.
- He had done a lot of cleaning for Rannos and Gremag. With them dead he "need[s] new employers" and is branching out toward the more heroic.
- He calls himself "checking out" rather than joining any gang.
- He disliked his former employers' fanaticism about a resurgent Temple.
- Retainer: 100 gp/month plus 50 gp per job; the first job was settled at 100 gp. He can be summoned by asking for him at the inn.
- He stays behind at the store to field questions about the missing merchants.

### Rannos Davl
A fanatic cultist associated with the Deep Temple who was killed by Calmer's spiritual weapon during the confrontation in the merchant office.

A cultist who fought alongside Rannos and was slain by the party during the office skirmish.

The proprietor of the Inn of the Welcome Wench. He is wary of Rannos and Gremag and eager for news regarding the mysterious chest, suggesting that local authorities like Rufus and Burne would want to know about it.
- He sees money to be made in the chest.
- He recommends the party tell Jaroo who has the chest.

A member of the town guard who masquerades as a drunken buffoon but is actually a highly focused and perceptive agent working for the Viscount of Verbobonc. When unmasked by the party, he drops his act and speaks plainly, urging them to deal with the Moathouse threat.

The fighter captain of the Hommlet guard who works alongside Burne and reports to Verbobonc. He questions the party at breakfast about the Chest of Rao and visibly relaxes when their story matches what Elmo already reported.

### Burne
A magic-user and leader in Hommlet who works with Captain Rufus. He admits the authorities were too hasty in dismissing reports of the Temple of Elemental Evil's reactivation and urges the party to clear the Moathouse.

### Lareth the Beautiful
A high-ranking, vain, and paranoid leader within the Temple of Elemental Evil who resides in the Moathouse dungeons. He is obsessed with obtaining the Chest of Rao and views the party's failure to deliver it as a personal betrayal, ultimately calling for their deaths.

- He wanted the chest for himself rather than for the Deep Temple.

A conjured weapon of divine energy wielded by Calmer that delivered the killing blow to Rannos Davl, continuing Calmer's pattern of finishing off key enemies with this spell. Rannos also had a spiritual weapon of his own on the field.

### Cure Light Wounds
Divine healing magic channeled by Calmer to restore Zinnia after he was knocked unconscious during the confrontation with Rannos and Gremag. It restored 24 hit points.

### Chill Touch
A necrotic touch Zephyr delivered by running up to the gnoll guards at the Moathouse back entrance, initiating the combat. The opening Chill Touch missed against AC 15.
