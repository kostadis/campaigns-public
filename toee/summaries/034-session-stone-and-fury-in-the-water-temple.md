# Chapter 34 Stone and Fury in the Water Temple

Date: 2026-06-22

## Summary
The air in the Water Temple crackled with danger as a pack of gargoyles descended upon the party, their stone wings beating silently as they hissed and slashed with razor-sharp claws. The creatures focused their fury on Zinnia and Calmer, darting in to strike before retreating into the air where blades could not easily follow. Zinnia was the only one standing outside the shimmering yellow zone of Calmer's protective spell, and the first gargoyle opened on him with a critical claw and a second hit, 19 damage in all, before flying off. Calmer, ever mindful of his own importance, called out to his companions for protection even as the gargoyles raked at him again and again, insisting that his survival was critical to the group's success.

Zinnia, refusing to be grounded while enemies flew overhead, weighed teleporting with Misty Step and settled on something more spectacular. He spent a ki point to Dash as a bonus action, sprinted across the chamber, and launched himself into the air in a breathtaking leap that the DM likened to Kung Fu Panda, slamming into one of the gargoyles mid-flight with a charge attack. Offered the choice of dropping to the floor and taking the gargoyle's reaction attack or holding on, he chose to hold on, and clung to the creature's back, riding it like a furious storm while it hissed and writhed beneath him. When the moment was right, he drove his weapon home with a 17-point killing blow, then used the creature's crumbling body as a springboard to land gracefully on the ground below, drawing an astonished hiss from the surviving gargoyles. Meanwhile, Zephyr took aim with his pistol from a distance, and Sequoia called upon magic to enhance his own leap, bounding upward to trade blows with the flying stone beasts. The jump also prompted a rules question. The DM ruled that a character can cast only a cantrip and one spell in a round, so Sequoia could not follow Jump with a Silvery Barbs reaction on the same turn.

The battle grew far more dangerous when a massive stone idol lurched to life in the alcove to the south, extending grotesque pseudopods that lashed out at Calmer with terrifying force. In a single round it hit him for 36 damage, including a critical, and left him at 15 hit points. The creature, which the party came to call the Juggernaut, rolled and crashed through the room, reducing the temple's sacred altar to a pile of rubble as it bore down on the group. Calmer, battered but resolute, held his concentration, called upon his divine power to slow the creature's advance, and burned a third-level slot to mend his own wounds back to full, buying precious moments for the rest of the party to regroup.

Sequoia proved himself a cunning fighter, clambering onto the back of a gargoyle and delivering a devastating strike before leaping clear. He talked his way into a sneak attack by arguing the creature couldn't reach him on its own back, then landed 26 damage, and stepped off "Pirates of the Caribbean"-style when Calmer finished the beast. Zephyr darted in and out of the Juggernaut's reach, trading blows and dodging clean out from under its rolling bulk. The Juggernaut, immune to poison and impossible to topple, eventually turned its full wrath on Zephyr, hammering the rogue with a relentless series of blows that left him crumpled and unconscious on the temple floor. Calmer had already withdrawn through a door and shut it behind him. Sequoia acted swiftly, using a magical reaction to blunt the worst of the assault, which cut the damage from 36 to 24. It was not enough to keep Zephyr on his feet, so Sequoia dashed over and dragged him behind a heavy door to safety.

With Zephyr revived to 20 hit points by Calmer's healing touch, the party rallied for a final push against the Juggernaut. Zinnia and Sequoia failed to hold a door shut against a pounding gargoyle, and Zephyr exacted the first measure of his revenge by killing it with a sneak attack. Sequoia then used Zephyr as a deliberate distraction — "the sacrificial lamb again," as the DM put it — positioning him nearby so he could strike the creature from an advantageous angle. He landed a punishing 18-point blow that left the idol teetering on the edge of destruction, then pulled Zephyr back out of reach. Calmer strode forward and delivered the killing strike, and the massive stone construct ground to a halt, its pseudopods going limp as silence finally fell over the shattered chamber.

With the battle won, the party took a short rest and turned to the grim work of looting the fallen. The Juggernaut's body was encrusted with agates and other gems, worth about 500 gold apiece once divided. The DM reminded the party that they had rushed through Canon Belsornig's quarters without searching them. A side room, belonging to the two clerics killed in the first round of an earlier fight, held two gold rings shaped like sea creatures and three spell scrolls. Canon Belsornig's chambers yielded a breathtaking haul of ceremonial treasures: an ivory tray, crystal decanters, silk robes threaded with gold, books of lore, and a trident. More importantly, they recovered powerful magical artifacts from Belsornig's corpse, including a finely enchanted suit of half-plate armor, a mace of smiting, and a ring of free action. Calmer claimed a scroll of Flame Strike for himself. They chose to set the chief cleric's robes aside for a possible future recruit.

Their inventory of riches was interrupted by a knock at the north door. When Sequoia opened it, he found a soft, perpetually damp man in cowled green robes, water beading at his hairline and cuffs as though he had never once been fully dry. This was Brother Eelrich, a surviving cleric of the Water Temple, and he launched immediately into a torrent of flattery, praising Calmer as the glorious new leader of the upper temple and offering his loyalty in exchange for his life. He dangled two valuable secrets: the location of a hidden treasury Belsornig had been hoarding for his own escape, and knowledge of an unsigned contract with a band of trolls that had not yet been finalized. The DM noted that dealing with the trolls was already one of the party's assignments.

The party was skeptical but pragmatic. Calmer handed the negotiation to Zinnia, and Zinnia led it with cool composure, insisting the cleric prove his worth before being granted any formal position. Zephyr watched with barely concealed distrust. The cleric, eager to demonstrate his value, led them to the Fourth Basin. When he asked someone to clear the rubble the creature had left there, Calmer warned it might be a trap, so the party made the cleric do it himself. He navigated the hidden traps and revealed a concealed sump containing a chest brimming with gold, gems, and jewelry that Belsornig had been quietly squirreling away for years. The party divided the windfall and set aside a ten percent share for their ally Dren. When Sequoia suggested evening out the burden, Zephyr declared that as the leader he would pay the bigger share; Zinnia added that Calmer could pay more too, and Calmer, visibly conflicted, called it an excellent idea while giving Zinnia a cross-eyed look. Weighed down with gold, the party agreed it was time to go spend some of it in Nulb.

Brother Eelrich then led them to the chamber of the high priest, a breathtaking room of polished serpentine walls and a crystal dome ceiling that bent and concentrated the pale blue-green light rising from a great oval pool at its center. He explained that the pool held the powerful elemental entity Belsornig worshiped. Heatless green flames burned in bronze crescents along the walls, and a mosaic of a monstrous ten-armed kraken dominated the south wall, its tentacles wrapped around sacrificial victims rendered in dark purple tile. As the party peered into the crystal-clear water, they could see a humanoid figure chained at the deepest point of the pool, and a moment later a telepathic voice filled their minds, urgent and desperate. It was a triton, pleading to be freed so that it could help them survive what was coming, for the kraken depicted on the wall was not merely a decoration — it was about to materialize, and time was already running out.
