# Chapter 34 - 16–17 Harvester 576 CY Broken Deals

Date: 16–17 Harvester 576 CY (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-09)

## Summary
Looking at the map, Furnoc noticed that the Azeri can get to the Efreeti's chamber without the party first killing the salamanders. Surprised, the party decided to ask their new allies — in particular, why not attack through the chamber that has the harginn grues. The Azeri hemmed and hawed, until one of them broke down: "BECAUSE THEY ARE RATS!" Chance, "our local dungeon biologist," pointed out that they are actually snakes, not rats, but the Azeri was on a roll: "They ARE RATS! They breed like crazy. They eat everything!!!!!"

Furnoc interrupted with a rant of his own: "Wait, wait, wait, you're trying to tell us that you played us? And the only reason I found out is that I actually looked at the map?" The Azeri, surprised and sheepish: "Well, it's not my fault you're so stupid to not look at the map first…" Furnoc could not be stopped: "I trusted you, you said trust me when you wouldn't draw on the map the location of the magic circles…" The Azeri hemmed and hawed once more, took a deep breath, and shouted: "No, help until the Salamanders are killed. We tried to make it heroic because that's what you goody-goody two-shoes need to do anything" — and when Chance started to object, "fine that – pointing to Furnoc – goody two shoe needs." Furious, Furnoc stormed off muttering, "Are you even useful to us? Can you cast spells?" Honiel, Chance, Dourakine, and Berc followed him out. The Azeri pleaded with the party to kill the Salamanders, and Furnoc said: "Maybe, but not for you. I don't like being played." The group abandoned the Azeri "to stew on their betrayal," Furnoc very surprised that the conversation had turned so dramatic.

Back to the map, the party headed to room 36, sneaking in the shadows, and found "Nothing there." Chance and Furnoc snuck into the Efreeti's chamber unnoticed. The lavish chamber "is kept cool(er) through four blue ice statues of nude women set around the edges of a large divan" — a safe place from the excess heat of the Fire Node, its walls hung with beautiful tapestries and "furnishings of exquisitely made stonework." "Chance decides he looks intimidating." The Efreeti stood over some paperwork, muttering in a language the party did not understand.

The group went on to rooms 2 and 3. The room to the north held "a portal to the air node that seeps cool breezes and condensation around it," and Brazebul had caged it off "with a malformed brass cage that collects intense heat." The room to the south held a golden jeweled stand with a beautiful bottle, which the group guessed must be an Efreeti bottle; Honiel's detect magic showed it was "of the school of Conjuration." Examining it from afar revealed nothing.

The party spent several minutes working out what to do: using a bag of devouring on the Efreeti, a hallow spell, counterspell, doing nothing. Finally Chance, mentally exhausted, said, "I just don't know what to do," with a deep sigh. At that point the Efreeti showed up. Furnoc: "Hail great Efreeti, and we have heard tales of your wonder and power." The Efreeti pointed his saber at them and said in a deep growling voice, "You have the Orb." The party quietly surrounded him. Furnoc: "Yes, we do." Chance: "It's in this bag right here." Persuaded by Chance's chutzpah, the Efreeti tried to take the bag of devouring. Chance asked, "Do you, by any chance, have the gem of power?" The Efreeti, anxiously pawing at the bag: "That's what the Dragon has." Furnoc: "Open it! Open it!" Chance: "Wait, wait, can you help us with getting it from the dragon?" Furnoc was pulling his hair out. The Efreeti: "No, I gave it to the dragon for safekeeping. Now I want my Orb." Chance shrugged and handed over the bag, muttering, "Fine, Fine." The Efreeti avoided the diabolical trap — "You betrayed me!" — and the fight began.

During the parley, Jacer had carefully moved to the wall to guard the western entrance, as far from the Efreeti as possible: "I will guard this hallway to make sure no one comes in. That's my job!" Chance attacked first and hit the Efreeti (27HP), standing next to Berc. Berc used his action surge against the flanked Efreeti, hitting with his rapier and flame tongue; the flame tongue's fire was ineffective, but Berc still put "some serious hurt on the Efreeti (43HP)." Furnoc used the flanking and his sneak attack bonus (27HP). Honiel cast destructive wave; the Efreeti used his iron will to avoid the worst of it (10HP – from 5d6). Dourakine announced, "Fortunately, you have a magic-user with you. I will use an augmented magic missile to deal with this monster" (6d4 = 26HP), and then: "That is how it is done!"

The Efreeti cast a wall of fire around the group surrounding him, but the party's rings of fire resistance made it a very ineffective attack, and most of them avoided the fire itself. "Jacer survives." Chance ignored the wall of fire and landed "the pointy end of his sword on a critical nerve of the Efreeti (42HP)." Furnoc told Chance to take a step to the right, stepped into the vacated space, and also got "his pointy end on a critical nerve (35HP)". Jacer announced to all that would listen: "The coast is clear, no one attacking this way." Honiel cast thunderbolt and killed the Efreeti, who "bursts into lightning, and then some dust falls to the ground."

Chance asked Jacer, "Can you grab the bottle?" Jacer agreed. There was a trap, and Jacer barely avoided dying (20 HP – 2 from max). Honiel recovered him, and he got up: "I am cool with keep doing this, but perhaps the rogues might want to check for traps before I go further?" Furnoc: "Thanks for the feedback." Jacer: "Not to get too into this, but rogues usually find traps." Furnoc: "Here, take a garnet for your trouble." Jacer: "This is one of the cheaper garnets…" The party carefully searched the path to the bottle, disabling traps along the way. The bottle turned out to be an Efreeti bottle, but "a damaged one that can no longer contain an Efreeti." Jacer was lauded for his heroism.

Back in the Efreeti's lair they found three keys: one unlocking the cage that controls access to the air node, one for the prisoners, and a third whose use is unknown. Jacer suffered one level of exhaustion, as did Honiel. The party used the key to open the cage, flew through the astral plane past "the Orbiting nodes," and landed on top of the mountain in the middle of the air node plateau. Honiel pointed out that this was an excellent opportunity "to trade food and water for potions." But descending the mountain, the group noticed the dragons, and decided to go back to their prisoners in the water node instead of the goblins. They pulled out the water gem, arrived in the water node, and swam slowly to where the prisoners were.

Ammyon, the elven scholar, was delighted to see them and started pestering them with questions about the Efreeti and the node. To get rid of him, Chance handed him the Efreeti bottle. "I will write a treatise to end all treatises on Efreeti bottles!" He babbled in words only other scholars would understand, periodically interrupting people to point out how the artwork on the bottle reflected changes in the decoration of Efreeti containers. Honiel turned to him: "a hallow spell can make this place silent." "Ammyon shuts up."

The party rested and headed back to the fire node, stopping first at the Deva to free him from his curse. The Deva glowed, his wings spread, his chest puffed up, his arms spread, and he said in the nasal, whiny voice of a bureaucrat: "Thank you so much. Thank you so much. Now the Deva council will have to make a decision in a timely manner. Of course, the subcommittee will want more information, which will lead to more debate, but I expect …" He droned on about parliamentary procedure, and the party thought to itself: "perhaps that is why the demons always win."

In room 26, a vandalized library with several cracks in the wall and a room to the south encased in ice, Chance and Furnoc began looking for books of value. A bunch of elemental creatures (mephits) said in a childlike nasal voice: "This is not your room, this is not your room." Furnoc: "Whose is this room?" The mephits: "Gemmel!" Furnoc "shits on their parade": "Gemmel is dead." Their cryptic answer: "He's not alive, and he's not dead." The mephit demanded a small fee to explore, and the party handed over 200GP.

Chance asked, "What is the most interesting book in this library?" The mephit, sing-songy: "The most interesting book in the library is in the room with the ice door that reveals secrets that only the mephits know." "What is up with the ice door?" The mephit looked at the party the way a parent looks at a child asking about sex: "It's magic." "Has the Efreeti been here?" "Oh no, he has not. Poor Brazebul. The one book that had the one thing that he needed to know. But now he is dead." Pressed for their secrets, the mephits offered only: "The real power behind the power behind the power." "And that is?" "How do I know? I never read the book."

The party broke through the ice door and found a book titled "The lament of Tharizdun" by the cleric Wongas. Dourakine recalled that "he is a mad cleric worshipping an insane god." After securing the book, Chance convinced the mephits to show them where Gemmel was, and asked whether Gemmel would hear them if they banged on the door. The mephits: "Gemmel isn't dead and isn't alive. And he doesn't really care. And he's full of evil." With the location of Gemmel's room, the party decided "to defeat the man who is both dead and alive."
