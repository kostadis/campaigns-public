# Chapter 20 - 27 Goodmonth – 7 Harvester 576 CY Discovery and adventure.

Date: 27 Goodmonth – 7 Harvester 576 CY (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-09)

## Summary
Having defeated the Ettins and learned "that the Viscount of Verbobonc was an ally of the Temple and that the Temple was on high alert ready to kill them all", the group, "Short of magic, and worried about the future", went back to Nulb and their home. In the morning they woke to find Lundbeams again. She fixed them with "her steely green eyes and honest face" and said: "Tolub has to go." Furnoc asked why. "Because he's evil, and he's a pirate! And Heironeous would never approve me working for him," she replied. Furnoc asked whether a civil war in Nulb while the Temple still stood was a good plan, and Lundbeams grew thoughtful, reasoning it through aloud: "We need his pirates to protect this town. We don't have enough fighters to do that." If the Temple fell, the world would look very different, "and Tolub may leave on his own or be forced out". After she left, Furnoc remarked: "Whoever knew that a cleric of Heironeous could learn."

The group then went to Otis — "the burly smith who also was an agent of the King of Furyondy, a Knight Bachelor of Veluna, and formerly a watcher of activity in Hommlet" — to tell him about the Viscount. They first showed him "the books titled A, E, F, and W." Otis scanned them and exclaimed: "The prisons, that's where the prison was. How blind we all were. Tearing up every prison in the Flanaess." The answer had been the nodes all along: "The only entrance being through The Greater Temple and the only exit possible with the golden Orb of death." The chapter explains that the Nodes "are elemental pocket planes that Iuz and Zuggtmoy created", and that whichever came first, the Nodes and the Golden Orb of Death are related.

Reading on, Otis remarked, "Well, that's interesting, so that's what happened to Ashrem." He explained: "Ashrem is Alrrem's older brother and was also a high cleric of the air Temple." Then he grew sad: "Poor Jacer." Jacer "was a good man who lost too much and found himself in debt to the wrong people. He was our weaver, and I think you live in what was his house." He had signed up with brigands and was now trapped in the Nodes. Finally Otis smiled at the fate of Sargen, who "came to Nulb looking to join the Temple": "The Temple focused on chaos, Sargen wanted order, I suspect the mix was not as complementary as he thought it would be." He recalled Sargen's boast about process, outcomes and goals: "I remember thinking he was a dead man walking."

Then Otis read the letters between the Viscount and Hedrack, and whistled. Chance asked what he would do with them. "Nothing. It's too soon, we need to wait until after the Temple is defeated." Chance reminded him of his duty to protect the land, and Otis dismissed him: "My job is not to act, but to watch and be ready to act, and now is not the time to act". The party stared, dumbfounded at "the venerable knight afraid of acting". Seeing their faces, Otis realised "He had spent so many years watching history that he had forgotten what it meant to be making history". He turned to them: "The time for watching is past. For too long have I just watched." He walked out of his smithy and called, "Y'dey, suit up, it's time for us to act. Enough with the watching, our moment has arrived." The Canoness came out of her shop, confused. Otis declared: "We have proof of what we knew, the Viscount is a traitor, and he must be dealt with." The Viscount "already knows about the escape of Thrommel and may try other forms of treachery." Otis and Y'dey, "as the truly sworn watchers of Veluna and Furyondy", would go to Chendl to inform "the king and the seven noble houses", and then to Veluna City to tell "the Canon, the  Council of Bishops, and the Celestial Order of the  Moons". He finished: "Iuz deceived us all. The Temple was never defeated. War is coming, and we must be ready."

The party, "remembering too many bards’ tales", worried that Otis might be killed on the road and the evidence lost. He dismissed it: "I am a sworn member of the knights of Furyondy, and a Knight Bachelor of Veluna, in 20 miles, I will be on safe grounds. And, after all, I am not just a smith." When Furnoc asked what would become of the smithy, Otis called over "A scrawny young man of 20" and told him: "Chon your apprenticeship is over, the smithy is yours." With that, Otis and the Canoness Y'dey departed.

The party re-armed at Walter's store and turned to the question of "how do they avoid the massed army at the end of the hallway ready to kill them all?" A frontal assault seemed suicidal. They weighed using passwall to drop in from the 3rd level, or to walk around rooms; the chapter's sentence on a third option breaks off unfinished: "Or, perhaps, an alternative strategy is to lure them out?". With no real resolution, they set out for the Temple.

On the way a half-elven cleric appeared. "I am Deggum," he said, "The right hand of Hedrack." The chapter is unsure who answered — "was it Chance or Furnoc who says" — but the reply was: "I get it, you're here to tell us that you're interested in helping us … for what?" Deggum, "somewhat pleased that his power-mad-hungry attitude is appreciated", declared: "Hedrack is a fool leading us from disaster to disaster. Everything he touches dies." He described The Greater Temple on alert as "an impregnable fortress with magical effects that will defeat groups that are much more powerful than you are", and offered "a secret and a suggestion, in exchange for a promise": "I want you to kill the entire leadership except for me, then I will rebuild the Temple as the last remaining survivor!" Asked whether he would backstab his allies, he clarified: "No. No. I mean, when you attack, I will fight – just not that well." His advice: "You must wait until the Temple is not on alert to attack." Chance suggested appearing in black as Deggum's new allies, but Deggum said it was impossible: "too many have seen you and you are very well known, especially the fish," pointing to Honiel.

Then, almost casually, Deggum asked whether they had found "the golden Orb of death in Falrinth's chambers". Furnoc lied: "What do you mean Falrinth had the Orb of what?" Deggum described "A small 3 inch golden half skull with indentations for gems". Falrinth, an illusionist and "a follower of Lolth's", had told Hedrack he had the Orb, and when he died the Temple searched the ransacked rooms "And we found nothing. Nothing." With the Orb, Deggum said, the path was to wait out the alert, go into the nodes, assemble the artifact and attack The Greater Temple "using the power within it and safe from Iuz. Without the Orb, no one can escape the nodes, and with it, you have enough power to defeat Hedrack." Before he could offer more, "Furnoc decides to attack him only to discover that it was an illusion." Only afterwards did the group realise Furnoc had attacked "before they got a map of The Greater Temple!" "So be it."

The group debated luring the monsters up to the third level, waiting for them to come out, or even a siege, remembering that clerics can cast "create food and water," and that "the nodes are a convenient way to dispose of waste." In the end "they decide to wait seven days, hiding in the dungeon. Curiously, no one finds them. Apparently, Deggum had decided to not tell Hedrack where the group was."

After seven days they went back to The Greater Temple, resisting the temptation to explore while it was off alert, and stuck to the plan: "get into the nodes, reassemble the Orb and defeat The Greater Temple." They passed quickly through the southern promenade (401) into the northern promenade (418), down to the grand corridor, and into The Greater Temple itself: a sixty-foot ceiling like a night sky, walls of smooth black marble or onyx, eight colour-coded corridors, and a dozen gargoyle statues on flying buttresses "sitting and scowling down upon the room's occupants." Furnoc muttered, "Berc, how about we don't try and see if the ugly stone birds are real this time?" North of the entrance, steps rose to a stone altar draped in red silk with "the eight-pointed fire symbol", flanked by two pillars of gold, with a purple curtain behind it, carvings of the four elemental symbols, and monumental statues of Iuz and Zuggtmoy in niches. Two trolls stood guard by the pillars.

The group slunk unnoticed into one of the passages to a node, the brown hallway (Room 420), its walls inscribed with triangles, "large white eyes", basilisks, gorgons and "monsters native to the Elemental Plane of Earth, such as xorns". It led to the portal room (Room 421), triangular, each wall bearing "a baleful red and white eye", with "An enormous magic circle (about 15 feet across)" on the floor. A hill giant guarding it was quickly killed, and "The party, to take no chances, grabs his body and jumps into the elemental node of Earth."
