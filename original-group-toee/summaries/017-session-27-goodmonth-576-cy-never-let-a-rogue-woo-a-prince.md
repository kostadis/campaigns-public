# Chapter 17 - 27 Goodmonth 576 CY Never let a rogue woo a Prince

Date: 27 Goodmonth 576 CY (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-09)

## Summary
Having run from the Temple, the party spent the night resting in Nulb. In the morning they went to Walter Montagu's store to spend some of their loot, and found that "Walter had been stockpiling magic items in anticipation of making some significant sales."

While they were there, Lundbeams came in. The party asked her whether there was friction between her and Tolub over her most recent deputy; she said there was not — "Apparently, Tolub thought that having a pirate work for a cleric of Heironeous was punishment enough." Lundbeams then tried to probe the party about the conflict of having "a chaotic evil pirate king as the mayor of Nulb and a lawful good cleric devoted to justice as sheriff." Furnoc dismissed her concerns at once: "Making Tolub mayor was the best decision we ever made!"

As the party "exchanged staggering amounts of wealth for magic", Walter's brother Faulk came in. He told Walter that he "had prioritized his own wealth over the village's for too long." Walter shrugged and pointed to his new neutral god Beory, and "Faulk told him that there would be a reckoning." Then, noticing Furnoc, the visitor said, "You brought these evil men into our town." Furnoc laughed in his face: "Bringing in Tolub into this town was the best decision we ever made. There's no way we are kicking him out". The scene ends: "Faulk walked out, muttering to himself."

Back at the Temple, the party set off for "the last remaining section of the dungeon". Along the hallway honiel noticed a secret door to a hidden Chapel: black stone draped with white hangings, a small white-painted wooden altar, a reliquary of ashes, a silver votive lamp, and on the altar a snowy cloth with red runes reading "VENERATE THIS SHRINE OF" / "GOOD, THEN HASTE AWAY, ALL YE" / "OF TRUE AND GOOD FAITH!" A silver cross, 2 feet across and 3 feet long, was fixed to the west wall, and in a niche in the north wall stood a silver idol of Pholtus. The chapter sets out who Pholtus is: "Pholtus of the Blinding Light is worshiped throughout the Flanaess, but nowhere more fervently than in the Pale." His portfolio covers Light, Resolution, Law, Order, Inflexibility, the Sun and the Moons — Luna and Celene, which make up his holy symbol — and his clerics' domains are Good, Inquisition, Knowledge, Law, and Sun. By claiming authority over the Natural Order he "has presumed to place himself at the pinnacle of the Greyhawk pantheon", causing friction above all with St. Cuthbert of the Cudgel. His faithful follow "the One True Path", and "Chaos is the most prominent enemy of the faithful."

Remembering "the Lycanthrope trap", the party kept searching, and honiel found another secret door leading west into an Inky Chamber, room 334: a bare hexagonal room, dusty and cobwebbed, with a rag-clad skeleton by the door, "probably some cleric who met his end herein, from unknown causes", and "A vague feeling of unease". The module text calls it empty. "The group is unconvinced of the emptiness" and searched on, finding a black iron coffin by the northwest wall with its iron lid, inlaid with a silver cross, lying beside it, and a forgotten scroll case on the lid. Furnoc "miraculously (rolls a 20)" found an invisible sarcophagus with no top, holding "a vampire corpse, clad in black and appearing hale—except for the stake through its heart."

The scroll read: "Let the Good ones use their newfound strength to seal in the horror of darkness to prove their faithfulness to right and justice. We who wrote this lacked the power to do so." Furnoc, "the mastermind of the group", then remembered another clue, a riddle ending "With the whelp of Mitrik and there until doomsday." The group pieced together only two things: that it was about "the whelp of Mitrik, Prince Thrommel", and that it referred to him being cloaked and hidden. They missed the first line's meaning — "On the third level, in the room with six walls, lies the ninth by succession ruler of Furyondy." The narrator adds, "Perhaps spending some money with Otis might have revealed that information". Nor did they realise the vampire was Thrommel. Luckily, their plan "to stake him even further" was "precisely the way the illusion that was being used to hide Thrommel would be disturbed": "Seems the person who wrote the scroll was trying to give Thrommel a Chance to escape." The proper way to kill a vampire, cutting off its head, "would have, of course, murdered Thrommel."

Using the scroll of strength, the party drove the stake further in, and the vampire form gave way to "a handsome mail-clad human man with golden hair", in a white surcoat quartered with the arms of Furyondy and Veluna and the antlers of the Knights of the Hart, with a gold belt and dagger, a gold chain with an emblem of a crown and crescent moon, a matching gold ring, and his shield beneath his feet.

Thrommel woke confused; neither he nor the party was sure who he was, but he recovered. He asked what had happened at the battle of Emridy Meadows and where his men were. "Furnoc breaks it to him, gently, that 10 years have passed." When Thrommel wondered about his betrothed, Furnoc told him not to worry: "She probably has waited for him for ten years." Thrommel was so touched that he gave Furnoc a ring. Meanwhile "Honiel is smitten" and offered to help Thrommel in any way, and that generosity was rewarded as well. Thrommel called for his sword, "and the mighty artifact known as Fragarach flies into his hand."

"Furnoc then gets greedy. And Honiel falls in love." As Thrommel asked for help getting back to his kingdom, Furnoc began asking about the sword, while Honiel offered her arm to help him anywhere. When Thrommel learned that a pirate lord ran Nulb, he was shocked, and Honiel said at once that Tolub should be deposed. Furnoc, "who is only interested in the gold value of the sword", got into a war of words with Honiel, and an awkward Thrommel told "the Genasi" that Nulb was not part of his kingdom and he could not intervene. Furnoc, weighing "whether he should kill the hope of all good folk for some gold", missed that "Honiel was willing to smash his head open if he tried anything." He reflected that "no-one knows he's here, no-one believes he's alive, and no-one will miss him."

It grew awkward enough that Thrommel asked for his ring back. Furnoc refused, so Thrommel "gives another ring to another member of the group." Finally Furnoc decided that "maybe, killing Thrommel isn't the right move." Honiel led Thrommel out of the dungeon, and the group went back into the museum, room 332: a 30' square room of grim exhibits — heads, skulls, withered hands, flayed skins, banners, crowns, "holy symbols of Good deities", surcoats and tabards, rings and seals, ceremonial maces, and smashed cabinets with bones littering the floor. There, a set of lurkers attacked.
