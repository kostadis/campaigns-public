# Chapter 22 - 7 Harvester 576 CY The sad tales of pirates.

Date: 7 Harvester 576 CY (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-09)

## Summary
The chapter opens where the last left off: inside the earth node, a group of "porcupines of unusual size" is charging the party. While the party fights them, the magically altered body of Romag rises from the ground, a mix of flesh and stone. Romag rants: "Hedrack promised me off this plane if I got the Orb of death. Give me the Orb, and I will let you live. Don’t, and I will kill you and get it for myself." The party tries to reason with him, but he is beyond all reason.

The battle is quick and brutal. Romag uses a banishment spell to send Furnoc and Chance away from the nodes and all the way back to the prime material plane, where Furnoc uses the opportunity to heal himself. Berc injures Romag and breaks his concentration before a minute has elapsed, which brings "an irritated Furnoc and Chance back to the nodes." With Romag dead, the party searches the cavern, and Berc finds a large aquamarine gem. "He, of course, picked it up."

A bright light shimmers around them and the whole party is caught in "a bubble of green/blue light." The bubble lifts them slowly, then accelerates up into the dirt, emerges into the astral plane, arcs over the four elements and hurtles into a ball of water. It speeds through a large body of water and pops into a cave of dirt floating inside the watery expanse — a large internal lake lit by the glowing water and by light-producing fungi on the cavern walls. The narrator observes that if they weren’t trapped in a demi-plane of water "with no way home, probably populated by a series of demonic monsters," it "might not be a bad place to go on vacation." The bubble pops and drops them into a quiet lagoon surrounded by a coral reef, where skeletons and artifacts show that "not everyone who made it to here made it past the corals."

"The Fish, aka the Genasi," quickly dives in and collects the treasure in the lagoon. The rest of the party, in plate armor and carrying packs full of loot, casually swims — "After all, a hero that can defeat dragons and fly to the moon isn't bothered by something as mundane as armor weight in the water..." They swim north into the North Sea, colder, with ice in sight, and on a shelf find skeletal remains with waterlogged possessions, "Curiously, not damaged beyond repair." The most useful are a journal and a magical rapier.

The journal tells the quest of the pirate Bertran to find his lost love Nyria, lost along the banks of the Imeryds river; he sketched a map of where he searched for months. He arrived at the Temple "just as it was being constructed (562)" and entered its service hoping to find her. Bertran was "a freebooter, a rogue, not a bloodthirsty maniac," and the cult was too much even for his practiced cynicism. When he rejected some orders, he was put through a ritual in the Greater Temple presided over by a bulbous demon — Zuggtmoy, the party guesses — in which monsters from the elemental planes are drawn and marched to the corresponding nodes, and sacrifices are then forced through the portals "to appease the monsters." Bertran was sent into the water node with villagers and prisoners; the monsters sent before them attacked, and he escaped the bloodbath. He met other victims, mostly innocents captured in raids or evil men and women caught in the Temple’s power struggles. One entry holds a map: the pirate Merkath Dominos told him of a treasure near the Nyr Dyv in an old abandoned mill, contents unknown, and traded the map for some of Bertran’s food. Bertran survived a long time, pining, until the struggle was too much and he died. His last entry is all about his love for Nyria and how much he wanted to find her one last time. Furnoc is touched; the rest of the group, and Furnoc too, are annoyed that the rapier isn’t powerful enough.

The party moves on to the grotto next to the shelf: fresh, surprisingly warm, transparent water, tropical plants, and "a profusion of beautiful tropical fish" — which turn out to be swarms of quippers. The swarms attack Berc, who leads the charge, allowing his brother Hyramm to cast a cloud of daggers that envelops Berc and the fish. Berc survives, and "thanks his brother profusely for almost killing him." In the corner of the cavern the group finds the symbol of air carved into the ground. Hyramm works out that it is identical to the one the group used to come into the Earth Temple, and that it probably leads to the node of air.
