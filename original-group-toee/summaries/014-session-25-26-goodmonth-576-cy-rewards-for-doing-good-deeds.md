# Chapter 14 - 25–26 Goodmonth 576 CY Rewards for doing good deeds

Date: 25–26 Goodmonth 576 CY (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-09)

## Summary
The Party had just defeated Falrinth and his gang and was heading back to Nulb with "Yip and Faen, two prisoners." On arrival they went straight to Boatmens’ Tavern to talk to Tolub about what to do with them.

Tolub "was delighted to see his old friend Furnoc," so pleased that he told the bartender: "Free drinks for my dear friend and his companions, as I promised". At the same time "he carefully touched his nose," a signal Furnoc correctly read as "give the cheap-ass swill" to his dear friends. Tolub was delighted to hear of Falrinth's demise — "He seemed almost giddy" — realising that now, "modulo this inconvenient Temple, he was the ruler of his domain, wholly protected."

He was less happy to see Yip, a member of Tolub's gang who "had cheated with dice." Yip insisted that all he had done was use Tolub's dice, and Tolub's response was, "As I said, he cheated." "This went on and on." Tolub was tempted to kill Yip, then "remembered he was the Lord Mayor and had a Sheriff," and told the Party to take Yip and put him in jail "under Lundbeams' custody." Faen "was taken into the gang."

The Party found "a confused Lundbeams, who had no idea what she was supposed to do with Yip." Furnoc showed her the 3” golden Orb, "and all she could say was that it was pure evil." As the Party left, she asked Yip "if he would like to join her constabulary."

Next they went to "Canoness Y'dey, aka Mother Screng," for a restoration spell for Hyramm. The Canoness "was unimpressed with the wisdom of Hyramm," "stunned and stupefied that one would be so unwise as to open a magician's closed books." Furnoc argued that it was not that stupid, and the Canoness raised the cost of the restoration. Furnoc tried to renegotiate; the Canoness remarked that she could raise the rate further; Furnoc observed that this would "weaken the forces of good." Hyramm had had enough and said he would pay for the spell "from his share." The Canoness told the Party to come back the next day for the spell.

With Hyramm's restoration assured, they asked what she knew of the golden Orb. She too "recognized it as an artifact of evil" and recommended they talk to Otis, "who knew considerably more." So "the group schlepped over to Otis' smithy."

Otis got excited at the sight of the artifact. He first wanted to know whether the Party had broken any seals, and when they gave him a blank stare, reminded them of "the doors with permanent antipathy." Once every adventurer reassured him the seals were intact, he began to talk. He was delighted: explaining to the Circle of Eight "that the great seals were destroyed would have been very awkward. Never mind the King of Furyondy or the Archcleric of Veluna."

"Iuz and Zuggtmoy constructed the Orb as a unique artifact that they imbued with special powers." Those powers are unknown, except that any good person who possesses it "would lose their goodness," that it makes its possessor someone "evil folks wanted to follow (increase in charisma for evil folks)," and that it provides "immunity from attack from Zuggtmoy." Otis saw in it "the key to destroying the Temple for good": find the missing gems "in the prisons of Fire, Water, Air, and Earth, where Zuggtmoy's followers had hidden the gems," assemble the Orb, breach her prison, and kill her. "Once Zuggtmoy was defeated, the Orb could be destroyed."

He told the Party at once that this was their new mission. Chance responded: "How much?" Otis "was taken aback" — this was for the good of the realm, of Oerth, of all kingdoms; surely that was reward enough. Chance observed: "Killing a demon seems hard work." Otis stared at Chance and offered 8000 GP. Chance looked uninterested. Finally Otis said: "well, then we can raise the price to 12000 GP." "With that offer, the Party said yes!" Meanwhile Furnoc looked at the Orb, "tempted to grab it, and leave his life of goody-two-shoes behind" — "Perhaps the evil of the Orb was seducing the rogue with a heart of gold?"

The adventurers stayed overnight, woke in the morning, got Hyramm restored, and headed back to the Temple. Given the warnings about the prisons, their first stop was "to go and explore the prisons on the map."

In the first jail they found two farmers, John and Jim, who complained that "a hobgoblin, three goblins, and an orc had feasted on their buddy Charlie." "The humanoids only argued that they were hungry." The Party killed the humanoids. In the next prison they found "a gnoll, a bugbear, and two ogres," and slaughtered "those evil prisoners as well."

They then went to defeat a Leucrotta, in a 40-foot domed chamber hewn to look like a bleak natural cave, dimly lit by a sourceless gray light, with a cold, damp breeze, a skeleton in chain mail by a fissure, and "A leering skull atop a low slab of rock" that "seems to taunt you." "The monstrous creatures are defeated very quickly before they can even attack one of the group."

Relieving the monster of its treasure, the Party went to defeat "the Umber Hulks (Room 309)." The approach corridor "suddenly drops off" into an area of 16-foot depth sheathed in "smooth, highly polished bronze," with six-inch ledges along the walls. The Party descended the 16 feet and entered the octagonal main chamber, also bronze-sheathed, with eight small holes in a circle in the ceiling and two arched portals in the north and south walls. "Why the walls were polished bronze became apparent very quickly, as two umber hulks erupted from the ground." "The Party caught in the middle seems trapped." The umber hulks confused Berc, "who attacks Chance with a spell." But the Party avoided further effects and defeated the monsters.
