# Chapter 39 - 18 Harvester 576 CY onward Epilogue

Date: 18 Harvester 576 CY onward (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-09)

## Summary
With Zuggtmoy defeated, the party faces the obvious question of what to do next: whether to take on the assault of The Greater Temple now, or to destroy the Orb first. Iuz and St. Cuthbert have taken their dispute elsewhere, but Iuz may come back and want his revenge, and "Possession of the Orb would make them a permanent target." Looking around Zuggtmoy's prison, they find the portals to the nodes still standing, while the doors that had been sealed with magical runes are no longer closed. After some deliberation they decide to head back, report what they have discovered, see whether the Orb can be destroyed, and see whether they can recruit help for an attempt on The Greater Temple. Furnoc in particular wants to go back to Nulb, where "There is the small matter of reckoning for Tolub." Tolub's tenure as Lord Mayor is about to end abruptly.

In town the party looks for Otis and Mother Screng, only to remember that both have already left. Before they can make another plan they notice a collection of smartly dressed knights and elves in armor, all looking very bored. Their leader clears his throat and introduces himself: "my name is Lord Mattrick, a knight errant of Prince Thrommel." His liege is pledging his allegiance to Jolene of Veluna, and Lord Mattrick has been tasked with getting the party safely to the royal court in time for the Prince's betrothal, where, he says while looking at them with utter disgust, "you will be knighted and made lords of the realm and rewards shall be given." The news dashes Honiel's hopes: "Hers was an unrequited love." The journey will take several weeks, and the armed force is there to protect them. Furnoc is thrilled and asks, "So wherever we go, you have to protect us?" Lord Mattrick explains that the Prince made it very clear the knights were not to assist in any assault on the Temple, since more men would insult his brave heroes by suggesting they could not do the job alone. Furnoc and Chance huddle to find a way to get the knights to follow them into the Temple and soon realize it is futile.

Furnoc offers the knights Otis's home while they wait, only to learn they are already using the house the party had been using. Honiel protests, "but that is our house." Sheriff Lundbeams had told the knights that, as the party's friends, they could use it, and Furnoc groans, "that is precisely what Lundbeams would've done." When Lundbeams wanders in, Chance asks why she let them stay there. Her answer is that they were the party's friends, they were nobles, and they said they were aligned with Prince Thrommel. She even showed them where the key was. Furnoc grinds his teeth: "I would've expected nothing else from you, sheriff Lundbeams." Pressed on believing a prince that "Society thought he was dead for 10 years," Lundbeams claims, "Well, I was one of those who believed that he was still alive!" and then concedes, "Maybe a little bit."

The conversation turns to the Mayor. Chance suggests that maybe "it's time for couples counseling?", and Lundbeams stares at him, unable to parse the idea that she and Tolub are a couple. When Furnoc speaks of the Mayor being out of the picture, Lundbeams reminds him that "we do not depose the Mayor on a whim." Furnoc erupts: it was Lundbeams who first raised replacing the Mayor months ago, and here he is "making a deal with a prince who is assumed to have been dead for 10 years to bring an Army into this town just so I can make you Mayor! I did that for you!" When she asks to speak, he throws up his hands: "Defeating evil goddesses has put me on edge!" Lundbeams stammers in shock, and Furnoc shrugs that they "schlepped through four elemental demi-planes" and killed the goddess. Chance adds his own account of "stabby-stabby and the pokey-pokey and the killy-killy." Impressed, Lundbeams asks whether they really faced the demoness and walked out alive, and both rogues answer together, "in her lair. Yup."

Berc feels it is essential that Lundbeams also know they met Iuz. Furnoc, annoyed, rips into him, asking whether they should also mention that he was possessed by an evil artifact, that they had to kill him so he did not give it to the demoness, and that St. Cuthbert had to show up and raise him from the dead. Lundbeams no longer knows what to believe; she seems to be standing before heroes who defeated a demoness and were raised by a lawful good god while staring down Iuz. The rogues try to walk it back, "nevermind, not important, think of it as deep background and off the record", but her demeanor has changed. She stands upright and declares in a deep, forceful voice, "you are great heroes and have been blessed by the gods, I am yours to command." Furnoc laughs, realizing Berc's blunder has solved their problem.

In her command voice Lundbeams pronounces that "Tolub is a stain on the honor of Nulb." Chance throws out "elections?", and Furnoc is delighted: the party's army will ensure "fair and free elections after the stain on Nulb's honor is removed." Freed from supporting the legal authority, Lundbeams rants that Tolub is a crook, a thief, a liar, and "worst of all, he's still a river pirate!" She describes him as "a disorganized buffoon" who orders taxes collected, forgets he gave the order, tells the person taxed that he never did, and throws people in prison only to release them. She has dealt with criminals who believe in order, but "this man, there is no reason." When she asks for laws so people know what to do on any given day, he tells her to talk to him tomorrow.

Furnoc and Chance, with Lord Mattrick, head toward Tolub's HQ. Furnoc tells Chance he is delighted that Jacer is there, calling it "really good on the job training for him as a warrior." Jacer agrees that he is an intern thrilled to follow them and will just be guarding the rear, but Furnoc tells him "you've earned your stripes" and keeps him at their side for the negotiation. The old river pirate greets them with open arms: "Furnoc! My main man! I am so glad to see you!" Furnoc casually informs him that they killed Zuggtmoy, destroyed the Temple, secured Nulb, found the dead Prince, and have an army. Tolub asks who exactly they killed, and Lundbeams bursts out that Zuggtmoy "was the queen of the fungi!" and that she was sent "to protect Nulb from the likes of her and you!" Furnoc takes her aside to remind her that elections are coming and that "Winning elections is about getting people to like you, not about beating them with a stick or a mace." She steps back with a big thumbs up and a broad toothy smile.

Asked how things are going, Tolub complains that "it's tough being a small business owner," that the sheriff taxes and imprisons the wrong people, and that she is trying to sabotage him, adding, "and you know what Furnoc, I never wanted the job." Told that the most important job of someone in charge is knowing when to step down, he agrees it is "a great idea." Chance asks what new business opportunity he has found, and Tolub reveals he needs "a small upfront investment from some interested people." Furnoc points out that Tolub owes them money from the boat; Tolub replies that he could pay it and stay Mayor, or they could give him what he needs and he would be on his way. Irritated after a day of dealing with a demoness and two gods, Furnoc asks whether he is being shaken down. Tolub insists it is only a farewell gift, "only 20,000 gold pieces," to fund a business buying and selling goods on the Nyr Dyv: "It is a totally legitimate business." Lundbeams rolls her eyes, Furnoc repeats "Totally legitimate," and Tolub swears, "I'm going completely straight—no more piracy for me."

Tolub blames Lundbeams for his lack of money, calling her "a terrible tax collector," and Lundbeams is about to draw her weapon until Furnoc lays his arm on her sword arm. Furnoc then whispers to Tolub that if the party attacks and Tolub fights back, the soldiers they brought will kill him. Tolub looks over Lord Mattrick, who is meanwhile explaining to Chance that any fight would be outside the jurisdiction of the Prince's realm and the knights would stay neutral; Lord Mattrick would in fact be delighted if the party died before reaching the betrothal. Not overhearing this, Tolub revises his position, calling his first figure "more of an opening gambit rather than a hard number." Asked who will leave with him, he answers "my whole gang," then hurriedly corrects it to "my whole cabinet and staff," and Furnoc is pleased that Tolub has learned the language of politicians. Tolub settles: "Fine, I'll take the 10,000. As long as I don't have to pay you the 4000 for dealing with The Greater Temple." Chance mumbles that it depends on what is meant by "deal with," and Furnoc, recognizing a lousy negotiating position, agrees.

In a moment of personal weakness Furnoc tells Tolub, "I am going to miss you. Let's have a drink together." Tolub offers to move goods for them if they are ever on the Nyr Dyv. After Chance hands over the money and Tolub leaves the bar, Furnoc says under his breath, "I hope you take this in the best possible way, but I hope I never see you again." Tolub gives him an inappropriate gesture. Lord Mattrick asks Lundbeams to explain what just happened, and Furnoc tells him no one can. Lundbeams is furious that they bribed "that scoundrel," but is told to focus on her campaign and to treat the money as "an early gift from a long-term supporter." She agrees and offers her slogans: "This is a new era, a good era, a lawful era!" The party suspects the town may not embrace her vision. Asked who could help with an artifact, Lundbeams names Burne and Rufus, local agents of the Viscount of Verbobonc, whose job was to know everything about Zuggtmoy and the Orb and who "happen to have a direct line to the local Bishop of St. Cuthbert."

At Walter's, the party collects items they had purchased. Walter is not particularly happy to see them alive, and when they tell him Tolub is no longer Mayor, he is furious: "He owed me 10,000 gold pieces for some swords, and you let him go?" Saying he is "all about the balance," he demands the goods back or 10,000 gold pieces. Furnoc draws his sword and tells him, "draw your weapon." Walter backs down at once: "This wasn't a demand. This is a sit-down. We are negotiating." Furnoc tells him that next time he should "make deals with better and more reliable counterparties." Chance asks what his next business will be, and Walter is confused, since adventurers will keep coming to Nulb to test themselves against the Temple. When the party says they killed the goddess and are about to destroy The Greater Temple, the merchant hyperventilates: "Tolub walks off with 10,000 gold pieces, my magic items, and you idiots destroyed my gravy train?" Chance asks whether he did not believe in balance, and Walter replies, "Yeah, when the books were balanced in my favor."

Seeing him depressed, Chance and Furnoc propose that he run for Mayor, and Furnoc adds that he "would be a much better choice than Lundbeams." Walter is scornful of "That self-righteous goody-goody two shoes" but concludes that "she could never get elected in this town. She is too good." With the right leadership, he reasons, nobody would need to know the Temple was defeated, adventurers could keep coming, and the Temple might even be offered "to evil cultists that need new headquarters." He already has his pitch: vote for the cleric for the good person, or "vote for Walter" for a steady flow of business. Furnoc wishes him "good luck with the election."

The party rides back to Hommlet with Lord Mattrick and his forces. Burne and Rufus are delighted to see them. Chance shows them the Orb and summarizes the adventure, and the other rogue explains they want to destroy it "so Iuz doesn't keep tracking us down and try to kill us." Burne and Rufus gesticulate wildly and ask in unison, "you found the Orb?" Burne blurts that it has been lost for 10 years and that the Viscount has been asking him to find it and return it the minute it was found. Chance and Furnoc, who know the Viscount is allied with the Temple of elemental evil, refuse in unison: "no, we won't be giving it to the Viscount. We need to destroy it." Told that the reward would be beyond imagining, Chance answers in a somber tone, "Your reward will be a body bag." The Viscount, the party points out, is "a member of the Temple of Elemental Evil."

Asked about Thrommel, Burne had heard the news but took it for "the usual nonsense that comes out of Nulb"; he had sent a note to Otis to investigate, only to learn Otis had left the village wanting "to be a hero again." Chance invites Mattrick into the tower to confirm that Thrommel is alive. The old knight is annoyed at being made bodyguard to "these buffoons," and Furnoc is determined to drag the moment out. Chance produces the papers found in Hedrack's desk implicating the Viscount, and Burne and Rufus are shocked. The local cleric of St. Cuthbert, Calmert, marches in announcing, "St. Cuthbert spoke to me in my prayers! We are to help these men in any way that they require!" Rufus, excited, explains that the artifact has been studied and can be destroyed, and pulls out a tome: "We need a wind of 50 mph of force, a maul from a solid piece of granite, a scorching flame, and some freezing water."

Burne then puts two and two together: Hedrack, head of The Greater Temple, was allied with the Viscount, who therefore withheld the resources needed to build the tower, leaving him stuck in the outpost for 10 years. He declares, "I hired you guys to prove it! I suspected this all of the time!" and notes that the Viscount works for the Arch-cleric whose daughter is to marry Prince Thrommel. Told that taking the evidence to them will make them heroes, he exclaims, "Rufus, we are finally leaving this shit-hole, I mean town," and Burne and Rufus dance and scream for joy.

Jaroo Ashstaff arrives and worries that destroying the artifact might unbalance nature, since "Destruction of artifacts is always a dangerous thing." Calmert, eager to strike at Iuz, calls him "an impossible, sanctimonious tree lover!" The two clerics argue, and the party sneaks out as they are about to come to blows. Chance and Furnoc notice that Jaroo believes the Orb draws its power from the actual planes, and they explain the nature of the nodes and that they are an abomination. Jaroo changes his tune, declares that the Orb must be destroyed as quickly as possible, and offers, "I will cast any spells you need to destroy this thing!" Chance suggests the quarry; Burne growls that they can do it on the castle for all he cares; Rufus objects that it is public land and that they want evidence of their efforts, having been accused of goofing off. The heroes decide to do it at the Temple.

They stand on the Temple's doorstep and cast their spells. Lundbeams and Walter arrive with the villagers of Nulb. Walter points at the heroes, accusing Lundbeams of being aligned with outsiders destroying "our Temple, our business!", while Lundbeams shouts back, "we can be an honest fishing village!" Pushed back 50', she argues the Temple could become "a children's museum or an orphanage!" Furnoc tries to help by advertising its "hydras, sulfurous pools of drinkable water, torture chambers, and cadavers, but no monsters!" Walter screams that "without monsters, there are no adventurers." The party has become political props, and Lord Mattrick keeps a safe distance. The spells are cast, the Orb shatters, the ground shakes, and the heroes run to the villagers just in time as the Temple collapses into the ground. Furnoc thinks, "Lundbeams is not going to win." Lundbeams tells the heroes, "You screwed me! I am never going to be Mayor," admits she only wanted to work for someone who would keep the rules, and proposes that Walter become Mayor and she the sheriff. They hug. Walter turns out to be right: without the Temple, Nulb becomes a backwater fishing village, and without its threat the local Orcish tribes invade and raze the town to the ground.

After a few days of hard riding the heroes reach Mitrik, where there is a big party. Honiel, still upset, gives withering looks to the Princess. Prince Thrommel is delighted to see everyone except Furnoc. Berc is knighted, and Furnoc is outraged that "the one guy who almost turned evil and handed over the artifact to the demoness is knighted!" The Princess remarks, "husband, you were right. This is the one that always puts his foot in his mouth." The Prince declares, "I reward fools," makes them elders of the land entitled to wear "the silver moon of Veluna and the gold crown of Furyondy," and gives each a potion of extra healing, 2000 platinum pieces, and a ring of protection. Dourakine is not rewarded. Honiel, who had the medallion, receives a necklace of prayer beads; Berc, who was given a ring, gets a warhorse of the most exceptional quality. The Prince had meant to give Furnoc a sword of answering, but because "your behavior was unacceptable" it goes to Chance instead, as "an outstanding lesson for you rogue."

Chance presents the constables of Hommlet and their information, and Prince Thrommel flies into a murderous rage at the Viscount's betrayal: "I was trapped in the Temple for 10 years, and that traitor was supposed to be sending troops to free me." When the Prince muses that the Viscount needs replacing, Chance volunteers, "I agree! I am ready to serve!" and explains, "Well, I am related." The other adventurers stare; he had forgotten to tell them he was nobility. In private, the Prince and Chance agree that deposing the Viscount would be a stain on the kingdom, but a resignation in favor of his younger nephew on grounds of failing health would be acceptable to all. Chance is soon to become the new Viscount of Verbobonc, though the Prince reminds him "this is not an opportunity for you to steal from me but to serve me." His lifelong dream is realized.

The chapter closes on each character's fate. Berc is a made man, a knight of the realm with a warhorse. Dourakine, who got no treasure, asks to stay in Chance's town with no taxes, and Chance answers, "I could use a court mage." Furnoc leaves the kingdom in disgust and hires Jacer as his long-term assistant to guard the rear; Jacer says he will keep guarding the rear "as long as he keeps paying me the gold." Honiel sheds a tear and heads into the sea, remembering her dad's advice: "darling, it's better, down where it's wetter." After her grief she finds Tolub's crew, and Tolub is delighted to see her "up until the point the flame strike strikes." She asks the crew, "anybody got a problem with this?", becomes a ruthless pirate, and strikes business deals with Walter, now Mayor of Nulb and open to all trades. Burne and Rufus are reassigned by the new Viscount to Hommlet to build a fortress. A few weeks after the wedding, Otis arrives in Mitrik and complains to Chance, "You ruined my great heroic entry." Told there is a new Viscount, Otis says he worked for the Prince and is leaving, but under Berc's questioning about where he has been, and reflecting that explaining himself to the Prince would not go well, he asks Chance for work. The new Viscount sends him to keep an eye on Burne and Rufus in Hommlet, which suits Otis: "I never want to go back to Nulb for as long as I live." Asked what happened to the Temple, the answer is: "It disappeared into a giant crater."

## Locations
### Zuggtmoy's prison
Where the chapter opens after Zuggtmoy's defeat. The portals to the nodes are still there; the doors once sealed with magical runes are no longer closed.

### The Greater Temple
The objective the party weighs assaulting. Tolub's 4000 was owed for dealing with it, and Chance doubts they have quite "dealt with" it.
