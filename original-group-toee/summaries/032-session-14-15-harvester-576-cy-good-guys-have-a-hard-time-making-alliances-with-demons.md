# Chapter 32 - 14–15 Harvester 576 CY Good guys have a hard time making alliances with Demons

Date: 14–15 Harvester 576 CY (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-09)

## Summary
The chapter opens with the party having "secured the 3rd gem of power, defeated the terrors of the air node," and preparing to head into the fire node — and, as Berc noticed, having "forgot Hyramm frozen in the ice of the air node." They went to the portal near the frost giant and stood on it, and travelled once more through the shimmering tunnel in the astral plane. On the way they saw the ball of air they were leaving, the ball of water, the ball of earth, and found themselves hurtling towards a ball of fire. The tunnel took them safely through the fire and deposited them in a star-shaped room that Dourakine noticed "was in the shape of the symbol of fire."

Taking one of the corridors, the party discovered a Temple of Lolth. Chance remarked, "well, nothing could go terribly wrong if we looked into the Temple and stole any treasure," and Furnoc agreed. Unfortunately Dourakine was distracted, and Honiel "was bored and forgot to dispel magic before the Rogues stole the treasure." Several guards emerged from a corridor covered in darkness, and the party hid. Furnoc and Chance trash-talked Dourakine, remarking that "he hasn't seemed to be carrying his weight the last few battles."

The Drow guards screamed about the fire salamanders defiling their Temple: "How many times have I told you salamanders that you are not to defile my Temple. How many more of you must I capture?" One of the Drow was a mage, whom the party later learned was Nymzt Myund. Nymzt cast Evard's black tentacles in the room, almost trapping Chance and Furnoc, who squeezed away. The party wondered whether to "simulate death throes," and Dourakine told Furnoc: "Perhaps you should imitate dying. After all, you have much practice."

Hidden and unsure, Dourakine — "determined to prove his value" — suggested he could cast fireball on the Drow to disrupt the caster's concentration, though he observed the caster might have a fireball as well. The group collectively bemoaned that the fireball "doesn't damage a room, only the enemy targets." After a lot of debate they agreed on the fireball. It did harm the Myund, who maintained his concentration "through 1st-degree burns, pleasing Lolth greatly." Brushing off the burns, the caster said: "These are not salamanders. Who is violating our treaty? The priestess must learn about this." A drow fighter asked, "Shall we go now?" Nymzt said emphatically, "No!", and then, in a sinister tone, "First we shall kill them because we need information!" The group realized that the Drow use "the "kill and then revive method" of questioning" and stayed hidden.

Staring at a room full of tentacles they did not want to enter, the Drow fighters said, "So what's your next plan Nymzt?" — in a way that sounded a lot more like "What's your next move, genius." The infuriated mage answered: "Are you trying to question my plans? They have escaped my tentacles, but I will use cloud kill and flush out the vermin." While Nymzt prepared the spell, Dourakine attempted a counterspell and failed. Fortunately for the party, cloud-kill also required the mage's concentration — the chapter's sentence ends there, "the tentacles." Honiel was the first hero to react to the poison and cast gust of wind, dispersing the cloud. The mage was furious. Dourakine cast another fireball on the Drow.

The party finally moved into melee and discovered, to their horror, that the Drow fighters were invisible, "rendering flank attacks significantly less deadly." The two rogues were ineffective in their first attacks. On Berc's turn, he saw how everyone was lined up for thunder wave; the net effect was that Furnoc was now within melee range of Nymzt. The mage cast misty step and fled, saying, "I must warn the priestess, and you must defeat the intruders!" The two abandoned fighters screamed, "We shall unleash hell." One turned on Furnoc and wounded him grievously; the second turned on Chance and killed him. Honiel cast dispel magic, making the Drow visible, and Dourakine cast a 4th level magic missile, killing both Drow. Furnoc looked at him: "Damn, this was all you." Dourakine looked haughtily at the dead Chance: "Who was not useful in this combat, eh? Perhaps, the dead rogue?"

The party healed, then debated whether to take the idol of Lolth, concerned that the cleric would be able to find them. Furnoc pointed out that "no one uses divination spells anymore." Honiel was more cautious; Furnoc was annoyed because he did not want to lose any treasure; Honiel assured him they would not forget the idol. After some debate they left it behind.

Exploring on, they discovered the mage's chamber (room 12). By then the heat had affected the party, and Honiel suffered some exhaustion. In the room were the mage's spellbook and a way to make potent magical hallucinogens; the potion gives the imbiber "dominant visions," as the magic-user's many notes explained. The notes included rants about the priestess' devotion to Lolth "and that the demoness will save them" — phrases the party recognized as very traitorous and "Potentially deadly to any drow." The mage was frustrated, believing they needed to find another way to escape and that they had been abandoned to their fate. Further notes revealed that the Drow were sent to find The Orb of Golden Death "nine years ago by the priestesses of the fane of Lolth." They were captured and thrown into the nodes, and had since split up among the nodes, with the bulk occupying the fire node — "Utterly reliant on the cleric for restoration spells to avoid dying from the heat." One last, very useful note: the main entry chamber is neutral territory. The mage was furious about it, because that was the optimal place to take treasure from new arrivals, but "the various powers that carved up the node" had decided it would be best not to keep fighting over that portal. The party retreated to the entryway to rest.

Rested, the group went looking for the mage and entered room 9, where they found "10 or so harginn grues." Like all the other elemental guardians of the gems, these evil fire elemental creatures were there for a gem — "Except these monsters seem distraught." They knew at once that the party had The Orb of Golden Death, and argued among themselves whether to attack. They had lost the power gem; one faction thought that if they secured the other gems and the Orb, "Zuggtmoy's demons will be more forgiving at their next inspection." Another thought it better to let the adventurers find the power gem; the first faction pointed out that the adventurers might then escape, "and they would still be screwed." They bickered for some time, and the party retreated from the room.

"The raiders of the Temple of Elemental Evil" then reached the foul-smelling rectangular room (11), full of mucky volcanic soil sprouting "strange and disturbing looking red luminous fungi." The group recognized some as "demonic fungus of the lower planes, said to be used for the creation of drugs in the Abyss." The chamber was infested with Giant Fire Beetles that avoided the party, and a smaller luminous beetle "of some similar provenance." Silk curtains made from spiderweb showed it was part of the Drow colony.

At the entrance to room F8 hung a chainmail curtain, and Berc used "his in-depth knowledge of the arcane" to realize it was imbued with a ward against evil fey — "(drow are evil fey)." The party was delighted to think a good guy was inside. Furnoc: "Let's go in! These must be good guys." Inside, an attractive woman on plush cushions said in a seductive deep voice, "Hello, how are you?" Dourakine remarked that this was more dangerous than any monster. She introduced herself as Darley: a neutral power in the Temple, who knew about the party's destruction of the nodes and the Temples and that they owned an artifact. Chance showed her a loaf of bread: "Why yes, we have the magical artifact of bread."

Furnoc wanted to know why she was in the Temple, and she mumbled that "everyone has different things that amuse them," and that she found the fire node's petty power plays fascinating. Asked about the powers in the nodes, she named five major forces: the Drow; an Efreeti with "some insane plan to figure out how the nodes were built"; a fire dragon; salamanders "that act as pests that everyone hates"; and a tribe of Azers, who view the whole fire node as an abomination and are determined to kill the Efreeti "because he wants to harness the power of the node rather than destroy it." She pointed out their general locations on the map.

Honiel, who had cast dispel magic before entering, concentrated on Darley. Darley sighed and revealed she was not really a human female but "a cambion demon – half-human and half-demon." She had belonged to a faction that tried to kill Iuz, and, as she put it, "when you are trying to kill a demi-god if you miss, you die." Furnoc: "Ah, so you were banished here?" Darley: "No! When the goody-goody two shoe circle of eight sealed Zuggtmoy in the Temple with their magic, this became the safest place in the entire realms! Iuz can't see into the Temple or nodes!" Furnoc asked whether she knew what would happen if they told Iuz about her; Darley: "Well, you'd be dead, so it doesn't matter." When Furnoc naively asked why Iuz would kill them, Darley answered: "What part of demonic demi-god spawn of a demoness who runs a twisted realm known for its evil, do you not understand?" Chance jumped in: "She's got problems, she's got the information, there is no reason for us to do anything…" Furnoc turned dismissively: "You neutral characters always find a compromise."

Darley set out her terms: "All I want is to be left alone. I want nothing more for you to kill all of the creatures on this level and give me some peace and quiet." In return she would provide all the information they needed. Furnoc was very reluctant to deal with a demonic creature; Chance and Honiel, more pragmatic, pushed back on his increasingly critical stance, and finally Furnoc backed off and let Chance do the talking. Chance learned that the priestess "has a few guards." When Furnoc asked how the harginn grues lost the gem, she answered in one word: "Stupid." Asked who likely had it: "It's unclear, it may be Brazebul, the Efreeti. But I am not sure he does…" She dismissed his megalomania: "Brazebul is all about figuring out how the nodes are built so he can build his own fire elemental plane so he can become a God." Was she worried he would pull it off? "No! These nodes were built with powerful magic, an Efreeti can't figure this out." He was persistent, would expend a lot of energy, and would fail: "He's just annoying, and I want him dead."

Darley then delivered a long monologue on the misery of the fire node: a megalomaniac Efreeti, "a delusional drow cleric and her drugged-out paranoid mage," salamanders breeding like crazy, an "an Azeri dwarf colony that goes on and on and on and on and on and on about how the nodes are some kind of abomination against Fire and the gods of Fire," and "a young dragon that somehow got trapped into this node." If the party killed the factions, the place would be much quieter, and she could stop "pretending to be a helpless female." When Chance pointed out there was a quiet water node, she said: "I like this place. It reminds me of home. This has got the right amount of dank, dark depressing, and hot that makes my demonic side feel good about itself." Furnoc tried to glance around the room, and Darley noticed: "Eyes in front rogue."

The party went to find the Drow priestess, the rogues sneaking in front. They found the priestess and the mage arguing. The Priestess, in the sanctimonious voice of true believers: "Lolth is testing us." The mage, exasperated, waving his hands and pointing at her: "No, she's not. She's not coming. We're f'ed, and these guys may have the artifact." The cleric had convinced herself this was all an elaborate trial of Lolth testing her faith, and that any sign of doubt would bring adverse consequences. "She's gone quite mad." The two rogues used the opportunity to sneak up on her and kill her. The mage saw her die and said, "SEE!!!! I TOLD YOU THERE WAS NO HOPE!." — so intent on being proven right that he carried the argument straight into the massacre of his nominal leader.

Furnoc attacked the magic-user, who, with a prepared action, cast a magic mirror and avoided the same abrupt death. One of the fighters announced he was happy the mistress was dead but would still have to kill the party, and mumbled regretfully something about Lolth not liking it when her clerics are killed. The fighters used their natural darkness to neutralize the rogues' sneak advantage. During the usual round of combat the mage tried to appeal to the party to work together to find the gem, and the party was relieved he was not casting spells. Chance died again. Eventually, "thanks to a timely flame strike and a very wimpy fireball," the Drow were defeated.

Furnoc looked around for the Drow's gold and was disappointed: they had none, having put what little treasure they had into their Temple. He decided now was the time to get it. The two rogues then headed further into the fire node and entered a prison. Furnoc walked out of the shadows and announced they had come "to free the prisoners from the Drow!" The surprised prisoners all blurted out that they were not prisoners of the Drow "but of the Efreeti Brazebul!" The chapter ends as Chance asks, "how did you become imprisoned?"
