# Chapter 26 - 10 Harvester 576 CY Killing Belsornig

Date: 10 Harvester 576 CY (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-09)

## Summary
The party has spent the night at the fire of the good Ettins. On waking they construct "their desperate cunning plan to get Belsornig and the gem of power." The plan is simple: convince Belsornig to come out of his lair, then attack. To deal with fighting in water, which seems to favour the monsters of this node, the party casts "walk on water" to stay dry.

At the lair they call him out. The first step works: Hyramm "used his sweet tongue," convincing Belsornig to come out — "The promise of The Orb of Golden Death was too much of a prize for the cleric." The second step goes less well, because Belsornig is not what they thought. "He was no longer a cleric but a construct made from the magic that powered the nodes."

Berc's Thunder Wave is blocked by a counterspell. On his turn Belsornig casts hold person on the Ettins and Berc. One Ettin avoids it, so Belsornig casts dispel magic and the Ettin sinks into the water — but it immediately realises it can swim under Belsornig and surfaces behind him (the chapter writes "behind him"). His thinking, though he is now a creature of the nodes, "indicated two-dimensional thinking and missed the part where an animal could swim under him." Hyramm answers with the hunger of Hadar, and the evil cleric "met that with a counterspell." Meanwhile the grue keep trying to claw and bite, and every attack is "deflected casually or banged ineffectually against the heroes’ armor."

The held allies recover and surround Belsornig. Desperate, he uses control water to open a fifty-foot-deep trench that the party falls into, staring up at towering walls of water. But Hyramm, "that master of magic and power," was standing on the rock and did not drop into the trench; he casts a spell that does so much damage that Belsornig is "blasted into watery nothingness."

One of the grue tries to flee. Honiel "would have none of that" and casts call lightning, which on a large body of water behaves like a fireball; by centering it carefully she hits only the grue, but at the last moment it submerges enough to get partly out of range. The monster is not interested in fighting. Chance and Berc are disinterested and just want treasure, so the final question of how to kill the creature is left to Furnoc and the Ettins, "the only \*good\* people in the nodes."

With the monsters defeated, the party searches the room where the Gem of Power was said to be, places it carefully in a pouch without touching it, swims to the air node portal, rests, and moves on. The portal carries them through a golden tube out of the rock floating in the water node, briefly into the astral plane, then into a ball of air, through swirling wind, and onto a big rock with a large chunk scooped out of it — a big bowl with "thousand feet tall walls," a towering ice-covered mountain at its centre. They arrive at the top of that mountain as the golden tunnel discharges "a tremendous bolt of lightning into the atmosphere" and disappears. Looking up, they see the astral sky looking down on them, and then they feel "the numbing cold of the howling winds of the air node."
