# Chapter 05 - 22 Goodmonth 576 CY A new, better, happier era of Nulb.

Date: 22 Goodmonth 576 CY (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-09)

## Summary
"After the defeat of Dick Rentsch, and his ally Dala, the Party spent the night on their new boat." When morning came, "the R-Rolling, and Squinty, Grud Squinteye, showed up." Surprised to discover that Dick was no longer in possession, Grud was shown the severed head by Chance — and "Grud appreciated a gruesome severed head like any river pirate." Grud was curious whether the Party was a Temple faction, outsiders, or unaligned. He remarked that the Party had a very nice boat, that it would be a shame if the boat were to go to the bottom of the river, and that perhaps the Party might want to hand it over.

Chance, "always looking for an angle", proposed that the Pirates enter into a business arrangement with Walter Montagu. Grud was dubious, but Tolub was the boss, and it was Tolub’s call. The Party followed Grud to the Boatmens’ Tavern and Nulb Market, and outside they met Tolub: "A corpulent pirate, powerfully built, and clearly the master of all of what he surveyed."

Tolub was delighted to see Dick’s severed head. Dick had been his first mate, then aligned himself with the Temple forces and used his new backers to make life difficult for Tolub — taking a larger and larger cut of the piracy trade, making outrageous demands, and "in general, acting as if he ran the show." Tolub had been around Nulb a long time and had seen Temple leaders emerge and get slaughtered, their allies dying with them; he had learned the hard way "that neutrality in all matters regarding the Temple was a good thing." Seeing Dick’s head was a moment of joy, because he had warned Dick, then resented him, and now got to see his lifeless head. Unfortunately for the Party, and for the negotiations to come, Tolub had really wanted to be the one who cut off Dick’s head. "Tolub was a bit of a sadistic poet", and he was annoyed to have lost "this opportunity for performance art."

Once he got over his joy and disappointment, the negotiations began. Chance proposed that Tolub work with Walter to sell goods to Burne and Rufus. Tolub remarked that Burne and Rufus had been trying to build the castle at Nulb for many years, and that it was interesting that the Viscount, "who had one job", to build the castle and make sure the Temple never got power again, had missed three different revivals of the lesser Temples — "Never mind the fiasco of Lareth the Beautiful occupying the moat house and the increased banditry and piracy in the area." Chance asked if Tolub knew anything. Tolub said no; he just found it interesting.

Tolub was concerned. As an unaligned Pirate he had avoided the factional slaughters that bedeviled the chaotic powers of the Temple, and this deal would clearly align him with the forces of good; "the profits, although real, were not useful if you were dead." Chance proposed that Walter, their ally, could be the cut-out middle man. Tolub had a better idea: Walter could be Tolub’s cut-out. When the Party pointed out that Walter was under their protection, Tolub pointed out "it's kind of hard to protect someone when you are in a dungeon", and he felt pretty secure with his gang, which was now surrounding the Party. He liked the idea so much that, to cement the deal, he asked that their buy-in be the boat. "A token of friendship."

The Party was stunned, and there was a moment of quiet. Then Chance said, "Well, who is in charge of Nulb now? There must be a mayor?" Tolub paused: "Well, I have the most significant gang and am the most prominent merchant, and thanks to you now have no competition, I should be mayor." Chance asked about elections, and Tolub looked at his gang: "Do you think the good folks of Nulb will vote against us?" The bloodthirsty roar convinced everyone that elections would not be a problem.

Grud was horrified. The gang had survived through its neutrality, and now Tolub was trying to be a power in his own right. Grud tried to warn him, but Tolub was liking the idea of being "Pirate King, Savior of Nulb, and Lord Mayor." When Grud warned him about the Temple, Tolub didn’t care; he had a solution to the Temple, the Party. He turned to them: "And you adventurers will solve my Temple problem. You’ll go there and kill everyone and raze it to the ground. That’s what you do"

At which point Chance remarked that as Mayor, he should offer a reward. Tolub, "who understood a shakedown as well as anyone", knew the Party had him where they wanted him. If he didn’t give in, they might not go to the Temple, or worse, go and "reveal just enough to get the new Lord Mayor slaughtered"; with a financial incentive they would be less likely to flee the Temple or betray him. So he made them a deal: "4000 gp for the boat as a reward", 2000 in advance and 2000 when they came back. The Party agreed. "And so Nulb has a new Lord Mayor Tolub, Pirate King of the Nyr Dyv, Savior of Nulb, First of His Name."

With the deal cut, the Party headed out to the Temple. In the first room they explored, Chance took one look at the room's occupants and walked out. Furnoc reminded him that as a member of the Party they had to work together and he had to stay with the Party. Chance didn’t want to; Furnoc reminded him he had to. Chance said: "Fine." The Party then overheard the creatures talk about Iuz — "the great and kind Iuz", the same Iuz who helped build the Temple and had armies to the North massed to invade — who had set this room up "for the Party to get power and help." The Party did not think so. "The angelic creatures droned on…" and the Party just walked out. "The mirror to the angelic creatures went dark when the Party no longer seemed to believe the goodly looking creatures."

The Party headed north into Alrrem’s chambers and plundered all that remained, then went south into the deeper recesses of the dungeon (Rm 203), where they encountered a Troll and a troll chieftain. The Troll in 203 almost killed Chance. When they attacked it, they discovered it wore a ring of fire resistance — "A significant inconvenience given that Trolls must be burned to avoid regeneration." When Berc hit the Troll with his flame tongue and nothing happened, Honiel noticed that the ring on its finger glowed. The Party changed tactics: "First, we knock the Troll down, then remove the ring and then burn him down." "To mock the Troll’s enemies, the room had 20 torches and plenty of oil", all ineffective while the ring stayed on.

The Party then moved to Room 219. "The Troll’s name was Oohlgrist." Both Alrrem and Belsornig had been courting him and his troll tribe, and Oohlgrist was annoyed that, now that the Fire Temple and Water Temple chief clerics were dead, "the pathetic Kelno was the only option on the table." Chance remarked that Kelno had said some disparaging remarks about the Troll. Oohlgrist "didn’t need much motivation" to offer to help the Party defeat Kelno for a ¼ share of the treasure. Furnoc said: "sure we can promise that now…."
