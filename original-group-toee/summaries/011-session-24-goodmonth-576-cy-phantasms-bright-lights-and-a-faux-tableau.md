# Chapter 11 - 24 Goodmonth 576 CY Phantasms, bright-lights, and a faux-tableau.

Date: 24 Goodmonth 576 CY (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-09)

## Summary
The chapter picks up just after the Party "had just eliminated the kingdom of Poor King Scorpp, the hill giant." Room 314 was full of troll and bugbear carcasses, "And the doors to their treasure beckoned."

They searched Scorpp's chamber (317) first: a colorful 30' square room whose walls and floor are mosaics of "weird and depraved scenes," one of them depicting "loathsome fungus," with broken furniture by the fireplace, heaps of rugs and skins, a large barrel, pots, a kettle over dead ashes, and six unlit cressets. The trolls' room looked the same, but Chance noticed that the fungus mosaic they had seen in Scorpp's chamber "was missing in 318." They went on through the bugbears' chambers (315) and the bugbear chief's room (316), both hexagonal chambers of dull ocher stone with empty torch brackets, an iron brazier, a rude table, and six heaps of old cloth. "King Scorpp's kingdom destroyed and plundered," the Party headed south through the closed door and then west.

At the end of the corridor they found a circular room, unused, its floor littered with dried husks and castings, with "Massive webs" high on the ceiling and dust on the strands suggesting the monster that made them "is probably no longer here." In this "oddly abandoned room" they found a trap door, its ring under a small stone flag, with steep, worn, clean steps going down about 15 feet to a passage, and "A small twig broom" at the bottom used to keep the trap door hidden.

The Party marched down the corridor into a 20'x30' room where "a huge pale green basilisk is staring at them." The lead party members were stoned; the rest averted their gaze. As they pondered what to do, the floor became covered with oil. Realising "that this isn't a battle they want to win," they grabbed their stoned members and rushed back down the hallway "averting the blaze." Standing over the trap door of the dusty circular room, they realised "that this was no basilisk": a minute after the encounter the petrified members recovered, and the Party understood "that the phantasmal force had caused them to believe that they had been petrified."

After debate the group chose to explore the southern corridor first. At the end of its 60' length was a circular chamber, some 40 feet across, lit by "four weirdly glowing globular cressets of ancient bronze," and holding nine monstrous statues: a smallish blue dragon, a beholder, a fire giant, a manticore and a medusa "in frozen conversation," and to one side a mummy, an ogre mage, a wight, and a rakshasa. As the Party slowly entered, "the bright lights turn into will-o-wisps and attack the Party at once." Their speed and agility made them hard to hit at first, but over time "the evil creatures guarding this room are snuffed out." Furnoc and Chance wondered whether the statues were creatures petrified by the green basilisk, but realised that a phantasmal force "has a limited duration and could not explain the creatures." The group then examined the monsters one adventurer at a time. "Each petrified monster has a non-petrified component. Some of them are magic. Some of them are trapped." Furnoc was affected twice, and Chance almost died "when some dust of sneezing and choking in an urn spills out." With the treasure secured, they consulted the map and headed northwest.

The northwest passage led into a large room floored with "noisome earth," manure-rich humus from which sprang fungi of every kind, some giving off "a ghostly phosphorescent illumination." A female voice called out a warning to "stand still! Some of the fungi are dangerous. I know the path and will come to guide you", and a comely head wove toward them through the growths. "Turns out that the beautiful face was attached to a Lamia," and she was not alone: 6 Violet Fungi spores surrounded her, weak attackers but a wall that protected her flanks while she cast charm spells unhindered. As she fought, she remarked that "the mistress of Fungi" would not be pleased with anyone entering "her beloved garden," and Chance "correctly infers that this must be Zuggtmoy's garden."

The Lamia first charmed Berc, then used a mirror image. "In this maelstrom of monsters and hell," Honiel's deity gave her a dispensation and let her chain lightning work despite the low ceiling. Just as the battle seemed under control, two more Lamias appeared at the back of the room, preparing mirror images. Before the Party could attack them, Honiel's chain lightning broke the Lamia's concentration, and the two were revealed as "nothing but illusions." Hyramm, about to target them with his eldritch blasts, redirected at the last moment to the real Lamia. "The treachery exposed," the Party focused all its attacks on her and killed her.

With the fungi inanimate, the room was safe to explore. They found "an alchemist lab with equipment to create violet fungi repellant poison and some basic poison that can be applied to a blade," and "a book on Fungi that has a particular demonic twist to it." Of the room's two doorways, the western led to the second level (222a) and the southern "to yet another level of this dungeon." Chance and Furnoc debated going down to the fourth level as a short cut, and after much debate decided not to.

Instead the group left "the Mistress' garden," passed the trophy room, and stopped at a fountain (308), where carved faces "snarl and vomit forth thin streams of water" into a multi-tiered basin. The water smelled evil, but "it turns out that the water is potable."

Before going further east, they took a northern passage they had skipped. At the bend they saw "A sunlit area to the northwest" through a shimmering haze, and, undaunted, went on to find "a horrifying tableau": under what seemed a hot sun and a blue sky only about 20 feet up, four harpies sat in alcoves gazing down at a raised slab of rock on which "a man and a woman" were chained, while "a score of jackals and twice as many giant rats" tried to climb the slippery rock to devour them. Just out of the prisoners' reach lay "a large ring with a key in it, a dagger, a longsword, and a rod of some sort." The prisoners cried out for aid; the beasts took notice; the harpies "see you and open their mouths."

"The party freezes, harpies, this cannot be good." Berc jumped into the fray intending to use thunderwave on many of the creatures, but there was no clear shot that would not also take out the prisoners. "The reproachful look of Chance and Furnoc" convinced him it would be an evil act, since it could kill the prisoners who had called for aid. Berc sighed and drew his sword "to do it the old-fashioned way," "Muttering something about goody-two-shoed adventurers who are too trusting."

"Ah, but if only Berc had listened to his instincts. Because the humans were jackalweres, and the harpies are gargoyles." The battle became a free-for-all: the Jackalweres used their gaze to try to force party members to sleep while the gargoyles and jackals tried to kill the Party. Eventually Berc was surrounded and used his thunderwave "to modest effect," killing several of the remaining jackals and deeply wounding the jackalweres. The Party at last overwhelmed the monsters and ended "this perverse trap."
