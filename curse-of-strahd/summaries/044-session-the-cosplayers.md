# Chapter 44: The cosplayers

Date: Day 27 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The chapter opens with our heroes sitting in Mordenkainen's Magnificent Mansion, smiling at their ally. They know they have gotten "a get-out-of-death-trap card that Strahd could not have anticipated."

Meanwhile, in Castle Ravenloft, Count von Zarovich is talking to Rahadin. Strahd asks, "How does KP have Teleport?" Rahadin answers, "It would appear that the heroes made a deal with the Lich." He explains that "your spell to befuddle him was overcome," that the heroes convinced the Lich "to turn the Amber Temple into a B&B," and that as part of that deal "the Lich gave them access to his spell library." Strahd's reaction is "WHAAAT!" Rahadin observes, "Your lordship, it was a risk to leave the Lich alive." Strahd thinks for a moment that killing Rahadin would be so easy, then decides against it: "A calculated risk. Once we have dealt with these fools, I will deal with the Lich."

Back in the Mansion, KP says that "a simple permanency spell can be cast, coupled with non-detection and Strahd's ability to see our actions will go away." The gnomish wizard looks through the spell books he took from the Lich and finds it missing: "Damn it, I had it in my spellbooks. It is not here." Instead he tells the group, "we shall teleport into the catacombs. Fortunately, we have the crown from Strahd's castle on the junior mage." Someone in the group says, "I have coins from the catacombs." Everyone turns to him and asks whether he really knows which coin is from which place; the answer is "Yes?" KP takes the coin and tells Gaxim, "part of the ritual is you must do a cartwheel." The refusal — "No." — turns out to have come from Wukong. The others take it as a touching gesture of kindness towards the friendly orc, until Wukong explains, "Yes. If he gets injured, then I am more likely to get injured." They stare at him. "Wait, did you guys think it was because I cared?" KP shrugs and teleports everyone into the catacombs.

In the catacombs the party moves past a series of crypts. At the crypt of Artista DeSlop, Court Ceiling Painter, the verdict is "Skip." At the crypt of Lady Isolde Yunk (Isolde the Incredible), purveyor of antiques and imports, Wukong enters and finds a skeleton draped in rags atop a marble slab, surrounded by heaps of old household goods — baskets, braziers, tapestries, candlesticks, chairs, chests, decanters, lamps, scroll cases, tankards and more — none of which looks valuable, with an old chandelier hanging from the domed ceiling. KP remarks that a guy he knew in Vallaki told him "Lady Isolde was the original interior designer," and that "When Barovia was cut off with the mists, her collection became worthless and was buried with her." The crypt of Prince Ariel du Plumette (Ariel the Heavy) gets a "Pass."

At the crypt of Artank Swilovich, friend and member of the Barovian Wine Distillers Guild, Gaxim hears Slerdax say wine and opens the door. A skeleton draped in rags lies atop a marble slab, and around it, covering the entire floor, are thousands of empty wine bottles. Gaxim searches to no avail, but KP points into a corner, and there the half-orc finds a single bottle of Champagne du le Stomp. Gaxim then marches to a door in a short hallway and is hit with a dart, while Wukong gets hit with four poisoned projectiles.

At the crypt of Saint Markovia, "Dead for all time," Wukong is about to turn away in disgust when Marron shouts, "another bone." Gaxim enters. The crypt smells of roses, and the remains on the slab have disintegrated except for one thighbone. When he reaches for the bone, a faint ghostly form appears above the dust and whispers, "The vampire must be destroyed. Use me as your weapon." It fades away. Merrin picks the bone up and notices that it is a mace of disruption.

Then the heroes hear muffled voices, and Wukong sneaks up to see who or what they are. A female voice mocks the plan of finding "some magical artifact that none of the powerful adventurers ever found" and using it to defeat Strahd: "Is that your brilliant plan?" Another female voice answers, "Alexa, I have told you a thousand times, if we wait for adventurers to come and save us, we will be trapped here for eternity." A third voice says, "well, I think we should have waited. They seemed promising." Wukong notices that they are dressed in costumes: a wizard resembling Slerdax, a rogue that looks like Wukong, a barbarian with a Gaxim-like outfit, and a Merrin-like costume. "They are cosplayers," he thinks to himself. He sneaks back and reports, "fools who will die," then recounts the story. "More meat shields?" blurts out Slerdax. Someone in the party cries, "Friends! Let's go have a drink with a person who dresses like Gaxim!" and approaches them with the last known bottle of Champagne du le Stomp. At this point KP remembers that Champagne is the generic term for "Sparkling Ether," a particular brand of alcohol that, thanks to an interplanetary regulatory curse, can only be used by people who make that wine in the ethereal plane.

Alexa, who looks like Merrin, turns to Nadia, who looks like Wukong: "These are the great adventurers that were going to defeat Strahd, and so we didn't have to come here!" The retort comes back: "Just because you have an adventure worshipping fetish." The one dressed as Gaxim says, "We are teetotalers; we don't drink." Faux-Slerdax, though, will take some of the Champagne, and a confused Gaxim answers, "Of course, Slerdax!" KP muses that he has drunk 10-year-old, 20-year-old and even 30-year-old wine, but is "not sure 100-year-old wine will be drunk." Gaxim drinks: "GOOD!" Someone exclaims, "Fantastique, I never taught I would dreenk another bottle of this wine! There are 7." Faux-Gaxim corrects, "Eight of us."

As they sip the last known bottle of Champagne du le Stomp, the conversation turns to what the cosplayers are doing. They know that every adventurer who has ever come here has died, and they parade up and down the castle every night from Barovia. Alexa, who is faux-Merrin, explains that they believe the only way "to see the sun and to not be possessed by the demon is to defeat the demon," and that "if we can find some magical artifact, we will be able to defeat the devil." Merrin shows the sword and the icon: "You mean these two?"

The faux-heroes agree to join the party as squires — staying out of combat, providing food and water, carrying baggage, and most importantly staying out of danger. Faux-Wukong explains, "We learned our lesson from the last group of adventurers that found a group of Barovians who took advantage of their willingness to charge forward." Faux-Gaxim stares at KP: "Wasn't that the magic-user? You were the one that assembled the army to take on Strahd!" KP answers, "Yes, it is something I would do." Apparently KP's original plan was to use an army of villagers to clear out the traps; he did not count on the villagers being turned into various kinds of undead, and soon he was "drowning in a sea of vampire spawn."

The why of the cosplayers' presence turns out to be part insane plan and part coming of age activity, and the door the heroes originally came in has been open for centuries. Alexa explains, "Strahd doesn't care if people come into his castle. If he finds you boring, he will let you explore. If you don't take anything, it's fine." KP looks at them: "so what does he do to people who drink his wine?" All the faux-heroes turn pale, grab their pitchforks a little tighter, and inch closer to Gaxim. "I protect you," bellows Gaxim.

At the crypt of Baron Eisglaze Drüf, opening the door makes the air turn "as cold as the coldest hell they can imagine" — except for Merrin, who doesn't think it's that cold. Every surface inside is covered with thick, brownish mold.

The heroes move back into the main room and find wide stairs in the middle of the eastern wall, descending to a landing flanked by two alcoves, each holding a bronze statue of a warrior with a spear. A soft blue curtain of light flows between them. As they walk through the curtain, KP, Wukong and Ezmerelda are teleported back to the top of the stairs. "Why you not here! Come!" shouts Gaxim. Realizing it is a teleportation spell, KP shouts, "go get the treasure; we are good here!" Wukong takes the opportunity to approach KP about "an excellent business partnership" — "You can be the mage, and I can be the sticky fingers?" KP replies, "I do like a good business partnership."

The heroes explore the tomb of King Barov and Queen Ravenovia: hushed silence, tall stained glass windows on the eastern walls, two coffins on white marble slabs — one marked King Barov von Zarovich, the other Queen Ravenovia van Roeyen — under a vaulted ceiling inlaid with a gold mosaic thirty feet overhead. Gaxim says, "must find treasure!" The good cleric objects, "this is the holy resting place of kings and queens!" Merrin looks out the window and sees an 880-foot drop, with the castle overhead. Gaxim opens the north coffin: a life-sized wax effigy of Strahd's father, King Barov, with the old king's bones in a compartment beneath. "No treasure." The south coffin holds the skeleton of Strahd's mother, Queen Ravenovia, under a tattered white shroud; the magic meant to preserve her remains failed years ago. "No treasure!" Merrin is appalled. Wukong is appalled for different reasons.
