# Chapter 33: The Amber Temple B&B

Date: Days 21–23 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The chapter opens mid-fight: "Our heroes are facing some flameskulls!" The anti-heroes, Slerdax and Wukong, stand as far away as possible, "staring glumly at the destruction of power," and the rogue mutters, "They lack vision." The first flameskull casts a fireball that none of the heroes can dodge, then, seeing its enemies all armed with melee weapons, rises 20’ up a shaft in the center of the room. Gaxim kills one of the remaining flameskulls on the ground and does 28 HP of damage to another, which casts blur and rises up the shaft as well. Merrin casts a silence spell and a healing word. Slerdax, from a distance, casts tidal wave on the two flameskulls in the shaft: one is knocked prone, the other is left at 1 HP but still up the shaft. A magic missile from Ezmerelda hits the prone flameskull without killing it; Wukong kills the prone creature, and Gaxim throws his hand ax and kills the injured one.

Just as the heroes prepare to smash the sarcophagi, three more blurred flameskulls float in from the west through an archway in the main hall of the amber temple, near the anti-heroes. Gaxim, Merrin and Ezmerelda are nearly 60 feet away in a room full of sarcophagi. Slerdax remembers, "Oh right! I remember you need to douse these things in holy water or dispel magic or remove curse, or they come back to life." Gaxim charges 60 feet, and Merrin charges as well. Slerdax casts Tidal Wave on all of the flameskulls, breaking the concentration of one. One undead casts a fireball and Slerdax counters it; that creature rises from the ground in the main hall, out of range of Gaxim's sword. The other casts fireball on all of the heroes and rises too. Merrin is knocked unconscious; "The paladin seizes control and rises with 1 HP."

Ezmerelda, just out of the fireball's range, thinks out loud, "to help that villain Wukong?" and hesitates to run into combat; the chapter then has her notice that "Monsieur Gaxim runs and then casts a 4th level magic missile killing one of the monsters." Wukong moves into the main hall, fires an arrow, and dashes back to cover in the hallway. The friendly giant charges into the hall and throws a pair of handaxes; one hits "for a meager 8 HP," and Gaxim stares at the minor damage, confused. The paladin lays hands on herself and positions her aura to cover as many allies as she can. Slerdax advances, ready to counterspell, but the creatures are nowhere to be seen — and then each casts a flaming sphere and pushes it against Slerdax and Gaxim, who both avoid the bulk of the damage.

Ezmerelda turns to the paladin: "shall you follow me?" / "Yes!" Ezmerelda charges into position. Wukong dashes into the main hall and wounds one flameskull further with an arrow; Marron advances to protect her allies with her aura; Gaxim kills the monster Wukong had injured and throws another hand-ax at the last one. Slerdax's four magic missiles inflict 12 points of force damage, but it survives. Out of spell slots, it uses its fire ray and misses. Slerdax ducks: "FIRE RAY?" The creature flees, and Ezmerelda finishes it off with a magic missile.

That leaves the problem of bodies that "seem to revive from the dead." While wondering how to create holy water, the group recalls that these rooms require a knock spell to open; they are locked with an arcane lock that reseals after 10 minutes. After Marron and Gaxim smash the sarcophagi, the creatures are dumped into room x33b. Slerdax directs Gaxim north into a Wizard's bed-chamber: a white marble bed with its mattress rotted away, golden hawks perched atop the corner posts, and cobweb-covered arcane sigils that were once wards against theft. The wards no longer work, so the heroes march off with "four golden hawks worth 250 GP each." The group takes a short rest in the wizard's bedroom.

Slerdax leads them into the main hall (x5) and has Gaxim open the door in the southernmost wall. It is locked, and Ezmerelda opens it with "yet another knock spell." Beyond lie the central catacombs, smelling of "the horrid perfume of the ancient dead," with stone niches holding human-shaped amber husks, bones and tattered shrouds — the remains of the wizards who once defended the Amber Temple. Later generations of wizards, corrupted by the temple's evil, had smashed the amber sheaths and stolen everything of value. Tall iron candlesticks ignite as Gaxim enters. Inspecting the dead, Marron realizes the Amber temple was crafted to hold the dark vestiges in a permanent prison as part of some ancient ritual, with the wizards guarding it forever — but they "did not account for the power of the vestiges to corrupt."

Slerdax wonders, "did we goof up in breaking the sarcophagi?" Gaxim answers, "amber evil." / "Yes, but did we free the evil? Or did we destroy the evil?" / "Amber Evil! Amber makes Wukong bad!" Marron's attempt to learn more yields "unknown." She rules, "these were not evil wizards. We will not loot their graves. But let us look to see if there are any secret rooms or passageways." Wukong confirms there are none.

Slerdax takes them to the room with the dead vampires and up the stairs into a preserved library, its vaulted ceiling covered by a fresco of "angels being set ablaze in a hell." The sight of the tortured Angel triggers a memory in the Aasimar, and Merrin seizes control. A gold marble staircase spirals down a shaft to the north; six black marble bookcases hold hundreds of tomes, but the ones Slerdax opens have blank covers and empty pages — "Some powerful transmutation magic is at work here." Slerdax opens the secret door to the south, which leads down, then sends Gaxim west (x29) into an empty, dusty room, where Wukong finds yet another secret door to the west.

Behind it is an ornate room with the trappings of royalty, undone by dust and cobwebs, and in its center "a decrepit skeleton clad in tattered robes," red pinpoints of light burning in its eye sockets. "Do I know you?" it asks. / "No." The cleric offers, "Hail friend." It asks, "Why are you here? Who am I?" Merrin realizes that a greater restoration would heal it, but she needs a long rest first, so the group decides to rest in the library. Merrin wants to stake and burn the vampires' bodies first, and a voice answers, "Ah, zee Wolf Pack is beck! How wonderful! But Non, it is not necessaire. The vampeers are but spawn. Once killed, they can not come back from the dead." As they leave, the frail creature asks, "will you come back?" and the cleric reassures it, "of course." Seeing Gaxim for the first time, it asks, "who are you?" — "I Gaxim!" Before sleep, Slerdax asks, "do we think we may be reviving a bad guy?" "Possibly," says Merrin as she falls asleep.

The following day Merrin casts Greater Restoration and the creature transforms in place: "Ah! Ah! My name is Exethanter!" He is "a Lich and master of this temple," confused for centuries, which he thinks "had to do with Strahd! He did something very annoying." Strahd, he says, "treated me rudely." Before the cleric can suggest an alliance, he cuts her off: "Let us not confuse the nature of our relationship, cleric. I am not about to ally myself against Strahd with the first adventurer to walk into my office and cast greater restoration." She tries anyway; he ignores it.

Exethanter gives a history lesson: a secret society of good-aligned wizards built the Amber Temple in the Balinok Mountains "more than two thousand years ago" as a vault for captured evil vestiges and forbidden knowledge, dedicated it to "a god of secrets," and were forced to guard it themselves until the imprisoned evil corrupted them and turned them against one another. He asks whether they smashed the amber sarcophagi. "They were like that when we got here!" mumbles Merrin, but Slerdax, deciding that lying to a Lich is a bad strategy — or perhaps bitter that they were destroyed — admits, "Yes, yes we did." Exethanter sighs that the place will be "a little bit less annoying," but that they have probably "just unleashed all sorts of powerful evils on the world," and shrugs: "no sweat off my back." He ignores Slerdax's request for help against Strahd, asks whether they freed "a vestige in an alcove in the south wall," and when Gaxim nods says, "I hope he isn't too cranky. I did promise to free him, but I never gave a timeline."

He continues his story. He became a Lich; then Strahd showed up demanding to know what was going on, Exethanter told him to "go figure it out himself," and Strahd "made a deal with the Vampyr vestige. Very horrible creature." The wizards were long dead when Exethanter arrived; he breached the wards, "spoke to a vestige trapped in amber, and discovered the secret to becoming a lich," took over the temple, and turned the skulls of its previous defenders into flameskulls under his command. When Strahd came seeking immortality, he communed and forged a pact with the vestiges, sealed with blood when he murdered his brother Sergei; "Strahd transformed into a vampire, and the Dark Powers turned his land into a prison." That prison holds Exethanter too, but he refuses to help: the party might fail and Strahd would turn on him, and "if a group kills Strahd, I will be free regardless of whether I help them or not." Merrin is offended.

When Slerdax asks again whether he can help, the answer changes: "Why yes, my young mage. This is a famous laboratory with powerful magics. And every common spell in existence. Come, come." He shows Slerdax the magic that makes the library's books readable, and, looking over the balcony, notes they destroyed the sarcophagus — but "on the bright side, you did kill the vampires." He finishes his story: Strahd has returned many times seeking escape, but "Strahd is the darkness that sustains them." Asked what spell he wants, the genasi answers, "All of them." Slerdax's jaw drops, and he starts to transcribe spells.

Exethanter muses that he was "always a big fan of them being freed" and wonders whether the evils will escape "the demi-plane of dread. Very, very, very, very interesting." What the party really deprived him of, he says, is "the evergreen entertainment" of watching heroes and antagonists massacre each other here. Gaxim blurts, "that is what we wanted to stop!" Previous groups have reached Strahd's castle, some with "the two artifacts you possess," and Strahd still defeated them; the ones who came here were all killed — "Except this peculiar gnomish wizard," who would not do the rituals and get the artifacts, preferring "some kind of 17th level dimensional chess," and who left behind a book on "The trade opportunities between isolated planes of dread and the abyss."

After a few days, as the group prepares to leave, Gaxim's sword pitches the temple as a tourist attraction. Exethanter, who need not be alone if people come to him, is delighted: "Excellent idea! I could turn this into a wonderful tourist attraction." He would welcome guests and "tell them about the great evils that have been unleashed on the lands by misguided heroes!" The sword adds, "A great family-friendly attraction!" and Merrin announces, "A bed and breakfast!" Merrin asks whether he knows about Vilnius, and the group takes him to the amphitheater, where Vilnius is eating a rat. "An apprentice! I haven't had one in ages!" Vilnius is furious: he has traded one annoying master for an eternal one who can kill him with his gaze. As for cooking, Exethanter decides "that's what wish spells are for."

Outside the Amber Temple, in their furs, the heroes discuss what to do next. Merrin: "Knazaporium probably needs a greater restoration." Gaxim's sword: "Well yeah, there's a reason they call him the mad mage." Exethanter appears with a grouchy Vilnius, who growls and hands over a stack of handbills to distribute in Vallaki — invitations "to the newest Barovian tourist attraction, the Amber B&B." Exethanter thinks aloud: "No experiments on the guests. And this whole Lich aesthetic will have to go," and "Good thing I know how to cast illusion spells." Gaxim's sword asks, "You do have the money?" The Lich stares at the barbarian: "yes, why do you ask?" And with that, the heroes head off.
