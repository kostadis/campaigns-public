# Chapter 18: Dinner at Strahd

Date: Day 11 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
After the inglorious defeat at the hands of the hags, the heroes head back towards Vallaki. On the Old Svalich Road they see a black carriage with no driver, pulled by two black horses. After some discussion they decide that they will, finally, take Strahd up on his offer for dinner. First they take a short rest, and during it they hear Morgantha laughing derisively: "The pies are delicious."

Marron heads to the carriage, and Gaxim and Slerdax follow. Wukong takes a moment to ask, "Are we sure that the contract said no harm?" Marron answers, "He is a demon, but a man of his word." The horses pull the carriage swiftly through the dark night to the castle, "the home of the devil, Strahd von Zarovich." After winding through forest and craggy mountain peaks, the road turns sharply east and Castle Ravenloft towers before them. The carriage stops before twin turrets of broken stone; beyond them a lowered drawbridge of old, shored-up wooden beams spans a fifty-foot-wide, fog-filled chasm. Stone gargoyles grin hideously from the high walls, a rotting wooden portcullis hangs above the entry tunnel, and the main doors stand open, spilling a rich, warm light into the courtyard.

Staring at the gate, Slerdax wonders whether they really need to stay: "what if we left some stinky present and ran?" Gaxim smiles: "mold earth and close door?" Wukong offers: "Cover his house with soiled clothes?" They step into a courtyard of thick, cold fog, lightning and thunder, and enter the main door. Twenty feet inside is a second set of doors; as Gaxim approaches, they suddenly swing open, revealing a grand hall filled with organ music. Unaffected by the mysterious magical doors, Gaxim advances and the rest follow, Wukong in particular not wanting to be far from the barbarian.

In the great, dusty hall, beneath eight stone gargoyles on the rim of the domed ceiling, an elf with brown skin and long black hair descends the wide staircase, "quiet as a cat," in a gray cloak over black studded leather armor with a polished scimitar at his belt. "My master is expecting you," he says. Slerdax replies, "We can tell by the fact that nobody was there to greet us". Gaxim is about to attack, but Marron intervenes and intimidates the friendly giant; it is made clear that this is a polite dinner invitation, not a war. Gaxim is feeling grouchy: the hags defeated his friends and almost killed Wukong, and now "this black bird-like creature" is telling him what to do. "The blackbird is not a friend of Gaxim."

The elf introduces himself: "Welcome to Castle Ravenloft! My name is Rahadin, and I am the Master's Consigliere and his majordomo." Everyone stares. He asks them to follow him to the dining room and to "stay where I say, this castle is not the safest of places." Slerdax protests, "Hey buddy, we have this contract that says we are safe!" Rahadin's reply carries an obvious threat: "As long as you come for dinner, not if you rampage through the Castle." Passing through a vaulted hall with an oiled suit of armor in an alcove, they hear swells of organ music. Gaxim is entranced: "Can we meet the music player?" Rahadin answers, "Why, yes, it's the master."

Rahadin opens the doors to the Dining Hall: three enormous crystal chandeliers, a long table under a white satin cloth laden with roasted beast, roots, herbs, fruits and vegetables, places set with china and silver, and at each place a crystal goblet of amber liquid. The amber liquid is the Champagne du le Stomp from the winery. At the far west wall, between floor-to-ceiling mirrors, a caped figure pounds a massive organ, then stops and slowly turns toward them. Marron detects evil and realizes that there is no vampire within 60 feet; the party is perplexed. The creature smiles, and Marron smiles back, thinking "we must always be smiling". It says, "Please sit down, and eat." Gaxim marches up to shake its hand; it stares at him as if he were a peasant with no right to stand in its presence, and the barbarian, unaware of such societal niceties, tries anyway. It was an illusion.

At that moment Strahd walks in: "it seems I was late for my guests. Rahadin, I told you to get me before they walked in!" Marron detects evil, confirms that this is Strahd, and asks, "why did you call us here?" Strahd answers, "Why? To meet? To learn? But sit, sit!" He presents a chair to Slerdax: "Ah, yes, you like to touch things. And terrorize small children." He says he has not seen one of Slerdax's kind in a very long time and could find employment for one with his "sensibilities and morality". Slerdax answers sarcastically, "I have competitive rates," and the Count, ignoring the snark, tells Rahadin to "please make sure before dinner is out that Slerdax signs a contract."

Strahd turns to Marron: "I was expecting to see the Cleric, but apparently, she is too much of a coward to walk into the demon's nest." Marron says, "What, Cleric?" and Merrin says "Me" in her head, but Marron can't or won't hear. The next line mocks "the absurdity of the person in control thinking that the other doesn't exist" and puts its money "on the Paladin taking control." Looking at Gaxim, Strahd says, "Such an innocent soul." Gaxim answers, "My brother died. Hags made fun of us. Not happy." Strahd offers: "Gaxim, if you were willing to join me, I am sure I could help you deal with the hags, and whoever hurt your brother." Gaxim senses the great evil in Strahd and says, "No." Strahd is surprised. "But enough talking, eat."

As the party drinks with obvious enjoyment, Strahd asks whether they have been to the winery, and when they nod he asks Rahadin, "Which Martikov owns it these days?" The elf answers, and Strahd continues: "Ah yes, Davian. His son lost the gem when he got distracted by a woman. Almost tore the family apart. Sigh. This wine, of course, comes from my cellar because they can't make it anymore." The food is good, the wine lovely, the room beautiful, and for a moment it feels like "an enjoyable end to a horrible day."

Someone asks, "So Strahd, what do you do for fun?" Strahd replies, "I do many things, mage. You have gotten Ireena." The reply comes back, "Aw, come on, man, right to work? Let's just chit-chat." Strahd stares at Slerdax, confused, and describes his pastime: "I bring heroes from the realms, and I torment them, I watch them flail, I watch most of them killed in the intrigues of my people, and a few make it to my castle thinking they can kill Strahd, but they all die."

Marron asks, "What about Knazaporium, the great mage?" Strahd says he came on his own, which was very odd: he wanted to investigate trade routes through the lands of the dark powers and to understand the peculiar magic of the Vistani, thinking it might allow him to create an interplanar economy, and he "muttered something about an artifact in the abyss being used for a similar purpose." Strahd refused his offer for trade — "there would be no trade and no escape" — and his rebellion failed: "It failed. They all fail." Swirling a cup of champagne, Strahd wonders aloud whether he is alive or dead and turns to Rahadin. The majordomo is terrified, not sure what answer Strahd wants: "He's alive. You said you wanted him to be kept alive. To torment him."

Slerdax asks, "why are we here?" Strahd: "Because I am bored, and I need entertainment." Gaxim, clearly morose, says, "I got to meet my brother. That was good." Strahd stares at him, unsure whether to try and kill him now or later: "yes, it was a tragedy. Izek was a soulless monster, committing acts of violence and such brutality." Gaxim is confused.

Strahd decides he has had enough. They are here, he says, "because Ireena is mine, and you stole her from me." Noticing the confusion about her age, he explains: "Ireena is 15 years old. But her soul is that of Tatyana, my love, which Sergei and the dark powers stole from me. I will have her back." The heroes learn the story: before he became a monster, Strahd loved Tatyana, who did not reciprocate; she chose Sergei; he killed Tatyana and Sergei on their wedding day, then made a pact with the dark powers for the chance to find her and make her his. The dark powers torment him: Tatyana comes every 100 or so years, and always she escapes. "You have taken her from me."

"So let me get this straight, dude, you have been chasing after this woman for centuries, and it's our fault?" Strahd is outraged and just barely remembers his vow. He warns them that placing "the bones of St. Andral's" in the church and putting Ireena in it may protect her from his vampires, but "it doesn't protect her from desperate people willing to do my bidding," and that she will have to leave eventually: "I am happy to burn down Vallaki to ensure that when she does, I am there to give her my final kiss." The party realizes that instead of pursuing Tatyana in a 100-year cycle, he plans to make her a vampire.

Marron sees the torment: "have you tried being kind, caring, loving? Burning a city down is not the way to a woman's heart." Strahd asks, "Do you think so?" — and for a moment he is presented with another choice. Then Slerdax blurts out, "It sounds like you are kind of scared of her rejecting you." The anger that is Strahd takes over; he will not be lectured by a lowly mage, and his eyes flash with considerable malevolence. Slerdax pulls the letter out: "we had a deal." The Count stops and remembers who he is, "not a vampire, but Count von Zarovich of Barovia." "Yes. Rahadin, please clear the plates and bring dessert."

With the dinner drawing to a close, Wukong, convinced this is just another shakedown, asks as if bored whether the plan is that they give Ireena to Strahd in exchange for "a ticket home". Strahd answers, "No." In the stunned silence he explains: "That would be boring. I want to corrupt your souls." He wants to see what evil acts they will perform in the name of some high-minded quest, to see them tear each other apart, and "to see if Marron or Merrin will win this battle of wills." Marron answers, "I will." Strahd finishes his list: whether Slerdax "will lose his hands", whether Wukong "will step over his allies' bodies when I offer him a treasure", and "lastly, I want to see Rudolph van Richten fail again and know that he has failed for the last time. That would be entertaining."
