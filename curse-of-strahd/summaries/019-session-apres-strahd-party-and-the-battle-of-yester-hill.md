# Chapter 19: Apres Strahd party and the battle of Yester Hill

Date: Days 11–12 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
As the majordomo ushers the heroes out of Castle Ravenloft, Strahd calls after them from the Dining hall: "It would be a pity if the Martikov's were to lose their winery. You might want to rush back there." Marron tries to shame the Devil — "Shouldn't the Count take care of his Winery?" — and Strahd only smiles and points to his abstinence badge. Slerdax farts loudly on his way out, his way of telling the Count what he thinks; Gaxim, inspired by brave Slerdax, says, "I'll be back." The Count scrunches his nose and growls that he will assume it was "a natural biological process, and not an attack on my person that would violate the terms of this dinner." Slerdax blurts, "It was Rahadin."

Marron then draws the Sunsword and lights it up. The Sunsword, seeing Strahd, takes control of her; it has waited centuries for someone to find it and wants its revenge. Gaxim and Wukong grapple the Paladin, and as she is held, the nightmares of her imprisonment in the hell dimension take over and she screams in an ethereal voice, "You will die, monster!" Strahd stares at the blade: "that is the hilt, my brother's, Sergei's sword." Gaxim, still holding her, smiles: "I have a brother!" She uses that moment to try to squeeze out of his grasp and fails. The chapter gives the line "Khazan said he destroyed the sword. He lied." Marron screams that she wants her revenge on Strahd for stealing the crystal blade. Strahd answers, "No, no, I think not. I don't think you are going to kill me today," and tells the friendly giant to take her out. Slerdax remembers from Arcana 101 at the academy that hurting people sometimes breaks charms: "Gaxim hurt her." Merrin pleads with Marron, "I can take care of this," and is rebuffed: "No, you are not. You are weak. You are a sniveling coward! I protect us, not you!"

Outside the castle the heroes take stock. They know Strahd is coming for Ireena and that the winery is at risk. After some debate Marron insists they go to Vallaki, warn the Mayor, and take Ireena with them; the heroes note that Strahd will be ignoring the winery because he does not drink. It is midnight, and after seeing the daily march of the dead heroes that goes to Castle Ravenloft they decide to stay at the Inn of Barovia. On the way down, Wukong notices they are being followed; rather than tell Gaxim (a fight) or Marron (she might turn into Merrin), he tells Slerdax, who whispers that they should cast find-familiar and spy on the spy. Wukong urges they go on to Barovia — he would rather die in a scary village full of zombies than in a forest with ghosts of other adventurers marching past.

In the Blood of the Vine Tavern, Arik the Barkeep shouts, "Either come in and buy a drink or leave. Last call," and mumbles that this is "the last of my Purple Grapemash No. 3." Several Vistani are at the bar, including Alenka, Mirabel, and Sarovia, the bar's owners and friends of Madam Eva. Marron asks about rogue Vistani who help Strahd. Alenka looks her up and down — "you are not worm food" — and explains that some see Strahd's power and wish to follow it: "They are fools. Strahd is a monster." She spits on the ground and points to Madam Eva as proof that Barovia "doesn't work that way." Because the heroes have found the Sunsword and respect Madam Eva's prophecies, she shares one odd thing: Strahd "has only one true love, a Barovian peasant girl called Tatyana." "Not Ireena?" The heroes make the connection that Ireena might be the re-incarnated Tatyana, which explains his rage when they stole her from him. In the silence, Arik looks at Mirabel, who nods, and demands payment, and the moment passes. As the night drags on they hear Morgantha's words — "pasties, pasties, come and get your pasties" — and the mocking voice makes their blood boil.

After breakfast the heroes head toward Vallaki, planning to stop at the Tser Pool to talk with Madam Eva. Wukong spots the spy following them again, and Slerdax's familiar discovers it has glowing red eyes. Wukong sets a snare trap with Slerdax's help; the wolf is caught and screams in a human voice, and the heroes realize it is half-human. They take their frustration out on Strahd's creature and discover that without silvered or magic weapons they cannot harm him. When it dies, Marron stares at her bloodied sword: "pity, we can't question it."

At Madam Eva's encampment, Gaxim sits with his friends the Vistani while Slerdax, Wukong, and Marron go to her tent. On Slerdax's suggestion they treat her as a trusted advisor and tell her everything. She says, "You want to know about Tatyana. This is a story I have not shared in 400 years," and that "When you make a pact with the Lord of Night, you have to exchange something to get something." Slerdax figures out that she was there at the beginning, when Strahd was a man. "You have a very keen mind, mage," she says, and reveals: "My name is Katarina, and I came to Barovia as a maid in Castle Ravenloft." She tells the old story: Strahd fell in love with Tatyana, but he was "like a statue of cold marble"; Tatyana fell in love with his brother, "the charming, caring, loving Sergei." Strahd planned their wedding, but went mad before the mirror as his brother's best man, killed his brother, drank his blood, and killed the guards; Tatyana fled into the gardens and fell from the walls, and the castle guards killed Strahd. "But they failed. Strahd had made a pact with Dark Powers in his Amber Temple."

When Marron shows her the Sunsword and says Strahd tried to destroy it, Madam Eva is surprised: "This is new." She tells them, "You are not Vistani. We do not give information. We only trade. I will tell you more." She fled the castle to the Vistani and forged a pact with the goddess Mother Night, "trading my youth for the power to undo the evil that Strahd had wrought," and became an ageless crone with the power of magical foresight. She sends her Vistani out in their wagons to bring adventurers to Strahd's domain: "Strahd sees this is a game he plays, but it is my game." The Paladin is shocked — "you bring adventurers to defeat Strahd? Without their consent?" Slerdax asks whether she re-hides the magic items after each failed adventurer; she answers that when the heroes she brings die, the items are lost to her until new heroes come and their fortunes are told. Asked by Wukong about the march of the dead heroes, she says: "Do I regret their deaths? No. They wanted to be heroes. I gave them the chance to be legends." She tells Slerdax, "yes, Ireena is Tatyana, but she will never believe you"; tells Marron what becomes of the re-incarnations; and tells Wukong that Barovia "is a place of torment for Strahd," calling him "a pathetic, all-powerful Vampire that torments a few cities and dead souls." Finally: "You want to know if I will let you leave, and the answer is no." Gaxim pops in to ask for horses, for everyone: "Yes, you can, my friendly giant."

A few hours later they reach Vallaki, where Marron is appalled that nobody is smiling. With Urwin Martikov as Burgomaster, false happiness is gone and the riot damage repaired; no one smiles because smiling recalls the insanity of the former Burgomaster. Marron insists on silvered weapons, and at Thimdul's Armaments they meet Piersym Rowley, an old, grizzled veteran blind in one eye and half-blind in the other, who hates the Burgomaster and gives them a discount. To Gaxim's question about magical weapons, Rowley tells of a legendary armorer in Krezk who is Grygori Sturm's grandfather. At the newly created church of Heironeous, Yeska is delighted to see them, and Milivoj explains that since the defeat of Vargas there has been nothing but joy and happiness, although no one is smiling.

Urwin arrives and complains: "What have I done to deserve such callous behavior? Why are you here?" Marron tells him, "Strahd wishes to destroy the city," because "You stopped smiling" — "Vargas was not lying to you. The smiles kept the Devil away." Urwin resolves, "I shall learn to smile," confirms it must be "The whole town," and orders a mandatory weekly festival where everyone must smile. The chapter observes that in a few weeks Vallaki will have returned to a police state where everyone is required to smile: "Because that is how it must be in Barovia." Marron tells Urwin they will take Ireena; he objects until told that unless she leaves his life is forfeit, then nods, and once the heroes leave he declares that her disappearance will be made known to all. Ireena agrees to go: "I would turn him down again tomorrow. He is a monster." Marron shrugs, "I tried to give him dating advice," and Wukong reports Strahd's reply: "Women can offer no advice on how to woo women." Van Richten, still dressed as Rictavio, asks whether they asked Madam Eva about "the Hall of Bones where the second artifact is hidden," which is hidden in the castle.

After a short rest the heroes set out for the winery and find a massive tree blight attacking the vineyards. Slerdax breaks his staff and destroys the creature, and the Winery is saved. Davian at first is furious and despondent and blames the heroes for the destruction of some vines, until his wife has had enough: "They saved your life, they saved our vineyards, and all you can see is that they didn't save everything? They owed us nothing." The Martikov's devolve into a shouting match laying bare the crimes of three generations while the heroes stand awkwardly aside. The matriarch then thanks them and asks one more favor: "Without the gems, the vineyards will fail. Without the vineyards, there will be no wine. Without wine, the misery of Barovia will be too much to bear."

The heroes head south to Yester Hill, where the druids apparently congregate: a hill of dead grass and cairns of black rock under dark clouds, a towering wall of fog to the west. Marron has a vision of yellow monsters with shiny scales tearing up the ground to build the hill, mounted by men in bright orange vests wearing a hat that says "CAT," and advances convinced some demonic work is at play. Slerdax assumes the cairns are graves. At the top, inside a ring of black boulders, stands a fifty-foot-tall statue of woven twigs and black earth resembling a towering, cloaked man with fangs. Slerdax recognizes it as Strahd and starts to burn it.

Strahd appears — from the mist, invisible, or just out of sight, the chapter says it is unclear. Slerdax calls out, "Hey Count, we are burning this statue because it was an insult to your greatness." Marron cringes. Strahd ignores him and cries, "rise up!", and Druids and Berserkers rise from the ground. "Today you may live, for I must look upon my castle," he shouts; he has not yet noticed Ireena. A ritual is about to take place. The heroes decide that if Strahd wants it to proceed, they must stop it: Wukong hacks at the statue, Marron gallops onto it, Gaxim throws an ax at it, and Slerdax casts burning hands on it. Slerdax at last remembers what Prof. Doepnerium taught: this is a two-way portal spell — the Druids summon some power, and in exchange Strahd sees his old kingdom in all of its glory. Strahd calls, "Tatyana leave those fools. Come and sit with me, and we will see Barovia in all of its glory." Marron grumbles that it's always a good strategy to not use the correct name when trying to convince a woman to find you attractive. "Not." Strahd begs Tatyana to join him, promising to forgive her for choosing Sergei, and howls that only for her is he letting the Druids summon their monster.

Marron attacks the statue; it shatters, and out pops a gem. Strahd is enraged — "His mating ritual has been disrupted." Gaxim grabs Ireena and dashes for the Winery — "We must protect Ireena!" — with Wukong after him. As the Berserkers reach Marron on the hilltop, Strahd says, "You have taken everything from me," and casts a fireball on her: "maybe your spineless cleric can come out and save you!" Marron takes 29 HP of damage and her horse dies. The Sunsword sees Strahd, and in the battle of wills the sword wins (the chapter records "Sunsword rolled a natural 20, beating Marron's 26"). The Sunsword lays on hands to heal Marron and charges toward Strahd.

A druid casts entangle on Wukong and Gaxim, but they guide the horses through it. Wukong briefly convinces Gaxim to stay, hoping to use Ireena as a bargaining chip. Slerdax, seeing Marron possessed and about to die, mumbles "well shit," rides toward her, and — recalling that injuring her would let Marron try to seize control again — casts a thunderwave. The Sunsword/Marron staggers back 15 feet, Marron emerges in control, and Slerdax pulls her onto his horse. Strahd, judging it would be boring to kill them himself and that the powers of the Yester Hill would be upset to be deprived of their vengeance, polymorphs their horse into a frog. Slerdax and Marron fall, and Strahd shouts as he gallops away, "I may not be able to kill you myself, but these will do."

Surrounded by Berserkers and having lost the advantage of being mounted, Marron urges they head east to the forest, but a druid's entanglement catches Slerdax. Gaxim stops, but Ireena, terrified at the sight of Strahd pursuing them, begs him to go, and the friendly giant heads for the Winery; Gaxim and Strahd race toward it and the rest of the heroes lose sight of them. Wukong turns back to save his friends — reasoning that in the worst case he will need allies in Barovia — and an arrow at a druid breaks its concentration and frees Slerdax. Several Berserkers surround Marron but only inflict 9 HP of damage; she misty-steps up to a druid, and Wukong kills that druid with his bow. Slerdax fireballs the Berserkers around Marron; Marron charges another druid, whose thunderwave does not stop her. Slerdax casts another fireball, killing one berserker and badly damaging the other three. "Now it is a general melee."
