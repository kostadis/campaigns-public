# Chapter 4: The mists rise in Barovia

Date: Day 6 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The chapter opens with the party still fighting their way out of a sealed building. Gaxim breaks a 5' section of the wall, and a swarm of spiders covers him – "an Unusual Number of Rodents of Normal Size (UNORANS)". Wukong attacks, and Gaxim tells him, "You brave!" Wukong hits for 13 HP of damage, and Slerdax uses a magic missile that scatters the UNORANS.

In the attic hallway (16) they find that the doors are now blade barriers and the windows are sealed. Gaxim is undeterred: he smashes another wall, and another swarm covers him. Wukong screams "My friend! My hero! My ally!" as he attacks the swarm for seven points of damage. Slerdax burns the rats for seven more, Gaxim attacks recklessly, the rats attack and miss, Wukong misses, and Gaxim kills them.

The party heads down the stairwell. Gaxim smashes the wall into the third-floor balcony (11), and the rats that emerge are promptly killed. Down the sweeping staircase, the upper hall on the second floor (6) is covered in poisonous smoke, and all but Gaxim take two points of damage. The main hall (2) on the first floor is also filled with poisonous smoke, and Wukong and Merrin take five points of damage. Gaxim smashes a 5-foot section of wall leading to Hunter's den (3), and rats swarm over him once more. Slerdax realizes that "only the rooms with fireplaces produce the poisonous smoke" — and fortunately this is the last one. Wukong attacks the rats, Slerdax burns them, Gaxim recklessly attacks the UNORANS, and Merrin heals Wukong for 3 HP. Wukong blows a saving throw but survives, then points Gaxim to a Southwest section of the wall. Gaxim smashes through to (1A), and rats cover him again. Wukong attacks and then heads into the entrance of the building to avoid further damage, Gaxim disperses the rodents, and the party runs through the portcullis.

Outside, the mists surrounding the building are gone, and as they walk the road the building collapses on itself, "leaving nothing but shattered beams." Merrin asks, "so what did we get at all of this?" Gaxim raises an eyebrow and points to the bag. Wukong and Slerdax shuffle through it with a look of horror: many of the items they had found disintegrated or became worthless. The silver plates are tarnished, the books crumbled into dust — "Whatever magic sustained them no longer preserves." About 15 feet away they find a sign: "Do not help the children."

With the mists no longer obscuring the land, they see they are in a clearing surrounded by forest, with a small village (E) only 20 feet to the west, a river (D) to the south, and the road that brought them here to the east. They advance into the village. Tall shapes loom out of the dense fog, the muddy ground gives way to slick, wet cobblestones, and the only sound is "mournful sobbing that echoes through the streets from a distance."

The sounds of sadness cause Merrin to give way to Marron, "the vengeful and somewhat insane Paladin." The sobbing comes from a dark, two-story townhouse (E3), which is barricaded. Marron notices that nobody is coming to the aid of whoever is in so much pain. She grabs her head — "there are voices in my head" — then realizes the voices are coming from outside. Meanwhile Gaxim wanders off and discovers a pub (E1); the rest of the party lets him be. The others take stock: an eerie silence, no one on the streets, claw marks on the doors, and half the houses boarded up and abandoned.

Slerdax, "a true disciple of his Academy," walks up to the house and breaks in. Upstairs, a woman the chapter says "we later find out is mad Mary" sits in the center of the floor clutching a malformed doll, barely recognizing the party. The Paladin tries to intimidate her, to no effect: "Whatever has frightened her is scarier than this Paladin." Slerdax attempts to be kind, and she grabs his hand and tells her tale: "So many years, I spent trying to protect Gertruda. And all was well until she became a teenager and wanted to see more. And just last week, she escaped from the house." Wailing, she cries, "that monster, that monster has taken her." Marron asks, "what kind of monster?" and the old lady, in a hushed tone, answers, "Strahd!" To the question "Who?" she tells the Paladin, "Strahd is a vampire that rules Barovia. The land is his. He can do as he pleases. And whenever he finds a boy or girl that he finds pleasing, he seduces and brings to his home." She tears at her hair and clothes: "for 15 years I hid my daughter from that monster, but he found her, and now she is gone." Asked by Slerdax where Gertruda is, she says, "in his castle!" and points to a castle on a hill (E) just outside of Barovia. After describing her daughter she stops — "I have said too much! If you want to know more, you must ask others." — and, as they are about to leave, gives them the doll: "It belonged to me in my youth, and I gave it to my daughter. The local toymaker, Gadof Blinsky, made it." A tag stitched into its hem bears the words "Is no fun, Is no Blinsky!" When Slerdax asks where everyone is, she waves her hand in disgust: "many were killed by the monsters that roam the streets. Those that were not fled for Vallaki."

Leaving the house, the party notices a building next to Mad Mary’s with a creaking sign reading "Bildrath’s Mercantile." In desperate need of local currency, they go in to sell their gems and jewelry. At a small desk sits "a dour, mean-looking person, middle-aged, balding, and heavy set" who seems to "embody the soul of the town." He greets them with "You are new here!" Marron throws the bag at him; he asks "what do you want for it?", hears "Gold", and offers "I will give you 200." Marron smiles "the smile of an almost psychotic aasimar who is barely in control of her rage" and demands full value; the owner, unimpressed, replies "How about you leave?" Slerdax whispers that there is a 10x markup on everything, and the owner says, "If you want it badly enough, you’ll pay for it." Wukong asks about business, and the owner explains that the Vistani bring him business: "The Vistani are the only ones who can leave and come back from Barovia as they wish. You are not from Barovia." He closes with "You are trapped here like so many in the past. You will pay, or you will leave." The group leaves.

Across the main square they hear Gaxim: "Mead! I drink mead!" He is in the Blood of the Vine tavern, an "once elegantly appointed tavern" grown shoddy, where the barkeep, three Vistani and a man named Ismark Kolyanovich sit by a blazing fire. Gaxim shouts "Me kill monsters! Me drunk!", and Ismark invites the party to his table and orders the giant another mead. He introduces himself as "Ismark the Lesser," since he shares his father's name. Marron demands, "Tell me about Strahd," because "The voices in my head must stop." Ismark explains that "Strahd von Zarovich is a vampire who has had a curse on the land because of a forgotten sin of the Barovians’ ancestors," that those who live here cannot leave even in death, and that Strahd rules from Castle Ravenloft. When Marron mumbles "What do I know about Vampires?", Ismark answers curtly, "Nothing, you know nothing," and explains as if to a child that "A vampire must rest in its coffin during the day." Slerdax interrupts — "Waaait. Did we put two vampires back in their coffins?" — and Marron answers, "No, it was ghosts," adding of Ismark, "This guy is crazier than me." Ismark continues that a vampire can summon wolves and vermin, that Strahd is said to transform into a bat, a wolf, or a cloud of mist, and that he has seen Strahd dominate a man. Anyone who attempts to leave Barovia begins to choke on the fog, and those who don’t turn back perish. Asked about the traders, he says, "You mean the vistani! You should ask them."

Then Ismark makes his request: he needs help to protect "Ireena Kolyana, my adopted sister," whom he believes Strahd desires above all. She is trapped in the burgomaster’s residence (E4), and he wants to escort her to Vallaki, "where Castle Ravenloft’s shadows don’t fall," along the Old Svalich Road. When Gaxim calls for "More Mead!", Ismark answers "Give him more mead, Zsolt!" and explains, "I am but one man." Slerdax quips, "well, we are one man, too," and the party laughs. Marron nods, and the party agrees to help after exploring the town a little more. Ismark warns, "Strahd can see everything and may not wait for us to act."

Marron approaches the three Vistani women, Alenka, Mirabel, and Sarovia; they look at Marron with contempt, and Marron returns the favor. Sarovia, a 40’ish-year-old woman with bright eyes and grey-black hair, says, "Alenka, they have made us money today." Alenka — gaunt, long black hair, a ring in her nose and ear, a tattoo down her left arm, and a sword in very close reach — asks, "trade what trade?" Marron offers "Trinkets?" and shares the material collected from the Death House. The three exchange strange looks and suddenly seem much more interested: "Perhaps tomorrow you come to Madam Eva to have your fortunes read for those trinkets?" When Marron presses, Alenka grips her sword until her knuckles turn white: "Perhaps you go talk to Madam Eva first, and then you ask more questions?" They turn their backs. The chapter then describes the Vistani (singular: Vistana) as flamboyant wanderers in horse-drawn, barrel-topped wagons, each family ruled by its oldest member, who speak "in cryptic, flowing riddles," and who "are merciless when they believe they must be." The party understands that a favorable fortune from Madam Eva would make the Vistani friendlier.

Studying Ismark, the party finds something "borderline grotesque" in how heroic he appears — perfect teeth, toned muscles, perfect jaw — "a hero marked for an extraordinary destiny, or a fool who believes he has one." Asked what is going on in this town, Ismark says, "Many strangers have come to Barovia over the years, but they all die and disappear before long," and whispers, "together we can defeat Strahd!" He praises Vallaki and its Burgomaster, Baron Vargas Vallakovich, "who is a good man!" Asked by Wukong where Madam Eva is, he answers that she is in the Vistani encampment near the Tser Pool, on the way to Vallaki, which is "6 hours" away. Marron decides, "first, we go to the church after we sit and rest." Ismark calls after them as they leave in the dead of night, "Be careful, there are strange things that happen here."

Gaxim has a premonition that visiting a cemetery in the middle of the night is an awful idea and would rather drink mead. Wukong and Marron convince him there is nothing to worry about, and Slerdax settles it by casting a minor illusion of a keg of mead, which Gaxim follows toward the cemetery. In the dark streets they meet a hunched figure in rags pushing a rickety wooden cart, knocking on doors and pitching in a sing-songy voice: "have one of my pastries to escape the horrors of a Barovia for a time!" Gaxim demands a pastry and, before Slerdax can urge caution, eats one; it tastes like mince-meat pie. The old woman introduces herself to Slerdax as Morgantha. She watches Gaxim, seems relieved when nothing happens, and offers "Another?" Gaxim replies "CEMETERY!", and Morgantha hurries off until only a cry for cakes and the rattle of her cart remain.

The church stands atop a slight rise against the roots of the pillar stone that supports Castle Ravenloft, a gray, sagging edifice of stone and wood with a bell tower. Its heavy wooden doors are covered with claw marks and scarred by fire; Marron is convinced wolves made the marks, and Gaxim agrees. Inside, a hall (a) leads to a dark chapel, where a soft prayer is "blotted out by an inhuman scream that rises up from beneath the wooden floor." Slerdax wonders, "would things have been different if we came in the morning?" and Wukong answers, "the windows would have light streaming through into the chapel?" In the chapel (f), among broken pews and dozens of candles, a priest in soiled vestments kneels behind a claw-scarred altar beside the bell-tower rope. From below, a young man’s voice cries, "Father! I’m starving!" The exhausted cleric asks, "Did you hear the noise?", and Marron replies, "I hear many voices."

After much dialogue the priest reveals that his name is Donavich and that the voice is his son Doru. A little more than a year ago, Doru and several other villagers stormed Castle Ravenloft, lured there by a wizard in black robes from a faraway land; the Wizard died by Strahd’s hand, and so did Doru, who returned to his father as a vampire spawn. Donavich trapped him in the church’s undercroft. Marron uses a native ability to detect evil, notices Doru below, and demands, "What is going on with your church?" Donavich laments, "I pray day and night for deliverance, but none comes." Marron says, "Well, as you know, there is no cure for vampirism." Donavich cannot bring himself to send anyone to their deaths. Slerdax suggests, "let’s come back later," Gaxim shouts "Slerdax brave," Marron declares, "I am opposed to vampires and cannot abide their continued existence," and Wukong says, "I go where Marron goes." Realizing the party is determined to send his son to his just repose, the priest shows them the trap door that leads to the area below the altar.

A once elegantly appointed tavern, now shoddy, on the main square. Gaxim drinks here; Ismark and three Vistani are among the few patrons.

Strahd von Zarovich's seat; "All who enter never come back, and if they do, they come back as something very different." Doru and other villagers stormed it in revolt.

### Vallaki
Where those who were not killed fled, and where Ismark wants to escort Ireena. Ismark calls it "a great city"; it is "6 hours" away.

Where Ireena is trapped.

### The church

### Tser Pool
Madam Eva is in the Vistani encampment near it, on the way to Vallaki.
