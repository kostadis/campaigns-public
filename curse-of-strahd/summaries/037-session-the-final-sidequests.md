# Chapter 37: The final sidequests

Date: Days 24–25 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
Armed with their powerful weapons, devices, and spells, and with the light of Argynvostholt "shining like a beacon over Barovia," the heroes ponder what to do next. Gaxim and Merrin, "always focused on the children," raise the matter of the children whose families sold them to the werewolves "to collect money for pasties." The group discusses asking the children whether they would like to be returned to their parents. Slerdax proposes making the children orphans "through summary justice of their parents." But it is not clear how child protective services work in Barovia: perhaps the orphanage would return the children to their parents, and the parents would probably sell them to the hags. The group concludes that "the only real path forward is to kill the hags." Slerdax moans, "Every time we try, they slip through our fingers!" Recognising that the hags lose access to their coven spells when separated, they choose to attack at night.

Gaxim stares at the windmill, "his own personal white leviathan," and then sees Rictavio: "You know things about the hags!" An exchange follows in which Rictavio declares, "I do not work with other men," and is told, "I am a half-orc." The chapter does not tag every line, but someone in the exchange says, "I have been hunting him for so long." Rictavio says, "I am a vampire hunter, not a killer of hags," then pauses and adds, "You need magic that will keep them in the prime material plane." To the answer "I have all spells" he asks "How?", and Slerdax smirks, "I know a guy." Rictavio advises against it: "I wouldn’t bother trying to kill the hags. I would focus on the Strahd." He turns to Ezmerelda for help — "I’ve had this conversation with you." — and she rolls her eyes.

Slerdax and Gaxim step back, grab a glass of Purple Grapemash and get ready for the show. The argument lasts 20 minutes. Ezmerelda opens with, "Saving people is not side quests," and tells Rictavio that "Gaxim has more kindness than you will ever have and has been a champion for saving people, not just pursuing his murderous rampages." Everyone in the tavern stares. Rictavio shouts at her, "bah, I am a lone wolf and never needed you." It is a severe insult. Ezmerelda prepares to curse him, but instead slams a hand ax on the table; Rictavio answers with a dagger, and she storms out of the Inn. She comes back to take the dagger and the hand ax and walks out again. With the bar silent and Stefania praying that the Blue Water Inn won’t be cursed by a Vistani, Ezmerelda steps in once more and flings the dagger at Rictavio, pinning his lapel to the table. In a deadly serious voice she says, "I am not a lone wolf. I found a wolf pack. And it might do you some good if you found your own." Stefania starts pouring wine, and everyone quickly pretends that nothing happened.

The heroes huddle. One group suggests raiding Rictavio’s tower for his treasure, but after some thought they decide to kill the hags. Gaxim asks, "What about the revenants?" The creatures of vengeance keep coming back after they are dead, so they must be secured where they can neither kill themselves nor escape while the heroes deal with Strahd. Merrin proposes, "put them in boxes under a lake!" — awkward, since the coffin maker is dead, "but do-able." The group settles on locking the revenants under the temple in Barovia, where they met their first vampire spawn. Finding them proves unnecessary: "the revenant finds you." On the Old Svalich Road in the middle of the night, the vengeful villains appear, and Jaffa promises vengeance: "All we need to do is kill one of you! And then another! And then still another! And then we will get our revenge on Gaxim!" A few moments later the revenants are tied up. "OUR VENGEANCE IS ETERNAL!" comes the reply, until Gaxim shoves a gag in their mouth. A few hours later the heroes reach the village of Barovia and place the creatures in the basement of the temple of the Morninglord, where they found the tragic Doru. Slerdax casts an arcane lock, and Merrin leaves a sign: "Revenants inside, please do not unlock."

Darkness is coming, and with it Morgantha. "Hide!" screams Gaxim, planning to surprise her. Slerdax remembers something from school: a hag has "a heart of stone that they must clutch to be able to go into the ethereal plane. If we pilfer it, she can’t escape." Gaxim beams; others think "now you figure this out?" Wukong volunteers, and Ezmerelda points out that she has itchy fingers and would love to practice. Merrin likes the idea — "you are a thief!" — to which comes the retort, "NO, I AM NOT! It was a simple misunderstanding." Before they can come to blows, they all hide in the shadows, and Morgantha arrives pushing her rickety wooden cart through the fog, its small wooden wheels rolling across damp cobbles.

Wukong says, "I will have some pie." In the exchange that follows Morgantha squeals in delight, "Your friends have met their doom?", and then offers: "Here have one." When he answers "Okay," Slerdax takes the signal and casts hold monster. Merrin charges up, does a full body search, finds the stone, and runs away. Gaxim attacks the paralyzed hag; Morgantha wails and breaks free; an invisible Ezmerelda attacks; and Wukong finishes her off with his short sword.

Villagers of Barovia run out to see Morgantha dead. One cries, "what have you done? How will I get my pasties?" Another frantically picks up the pasties from the cart. Gaxim shouts, "these are children." The addicted parents don’t care, until Merrin steps forward and explains how children’s bodies are turned into the pasties, and shows them some body parts. The horror hits the villagers at once: they scream at each other, fall down crying, and scream at Merrin. Wukong wonders, "do they need a new mayor?"

Then the three Vistani who own the Blood of the Vine Tavern — Alenka, Mirabel, and Sarovia — emerge. "What have you done?" "Killed evil!" says Gaxim, puffing out his chest. Alenka looks up at Castle Ravenloft: "Is the count dead?" "He’s next!" Mirabel says, "That is what they all say," and Sarovia intones, "some of us will sleep better at night." The heroes head into the Inn to sleep. Asked whether they intend to kill Strahd, Gaxim says, "Yes." As the dead adventurers begin their march up to Ravenloft, Sarovia says, "there can always be new additions to the parade." Gaxim’s answer — "Looks fun! I could do that forever." — raises eyebrows.

The following day the heroes head to where the Martikov’s had said the gem was hidden. On the way they find the corpse of an elf killed in a hit-and-run with a cart. Unable to believe a death in Barovia could be so banal, they spend considerable time looking for the marks of a vampire, the sign of a spell, or violence — "But no, it’s just a hit-and-run."

They continue to Berez, where Baba Lysaga lives. The trail hugs the river and turns to marsh under a thick shroud of fog, past mildewed, caved-in peasant cottages and black clouds of flies "hungry for blood." Across the river, a light flashes amid a dark ring of standing stones. Slerdax, leading today, suggests exploring their own bank first, and they advance into the ruins of Berez. They find a ramshackle hut built on an enormous tree stump whose roots thrust up "like the legs of a gigantic spider," a giant’s skull floating beneath its doorway, and two iron cages of ravens dangling from the eaves. Wukong shares what a guy in Vallaki told him: that Baba Lysaga has a weird relationship with Strahd, and that "Baba" means a grandmother or a nursemaid. Gaxim suddenly remembers, "She stole gem!" and marches in.

Inside, the hut is crammed with furniture, all bolted to the floor except a ghastly wooden crib holding "a small, angelic child," with green light seeping up through the floorboards beneath it. Baba Lysaga smiles: "Who are you," she asks in a creepy voice, "And what do you want?" "Get Gem!" She refuses: "No, that is my gem. I must make sure that my boy," pointing to the baby, "Strahd has a wonderful house to live in! I can never give up my gem." Slerdax asks why the baby is called Strahd. "My precious baby! The one I took care of so long ago." Wukong and Slerdax, "suspicious by nature and touched by the Dark Powers," see through her illusion. Realising it, she turns hostile: "I demand you leave! You are no longer welcome in my house! I am the mommy of Strahd. And Strahd needs the house that moves. And the gem moves house!" Gaxim shrugs and leaves, banging the raven cages with his sword on the way out. The chapter ends on the threat: "Leave, or I shall use your blood for my bath!"
