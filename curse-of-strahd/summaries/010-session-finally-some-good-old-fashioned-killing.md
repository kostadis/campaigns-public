# Chapter 10: Finally some good old fashioned killing.

Date: Day 8 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The chapter opens with the party "lost in a maze of conflicting conspiracies." The Baron of Vallaki claims that Lady Fiona is not to be trusted, and the pentagram beneath her house — guarded by skeletons and populated by individuals who seem to want revenge on Father Lucian — tends to argue in his favor. On the other hand, Ernst claims that Izek has betrayed Ireena and Ismark and has lied about his relationship with Gaxim. For Gaxim the answer is simple: march to the house and kill Lady Fiona, "because that's what Gaxim's brother wants him to do." The rest of the group is uncertain; Lady Fiona has a different relationship with Strahd than the Baron does, and perhaps something could be learned from her. Ernst asks, "Will you stand outside, or will you come in?" The conflicting sets of betrayals turn Merrin into Marron, and the party sighs, because "The cleric is a better and the more reasonable party in a negotiation."

Lady Fiona greets them warmly — "My friends, my friends, it's so good to see you! Please sit down and have dinner with me?" — and gestures to chairs around a coffee table set with hors d'oeuvres. Wukong grabs a chair. Marron will not sit or eat in the middle of a mission. Slerdax walks past the wet bar, pours himself what he thinks is the most expensive booze, and asks, "can you tell us why we are here?" Fiona supposes the Baron has called her "this horrible person who never smiles," and smiles. She calls Vargas, the Baron, and Izek "both brutes," and says that is why Lydia is "such a delicate flower." She asks Ernst, standing near the dining hall entrance, to tell them what has happened. He rolls his eyes and obliges: "Izek's decided that Ireena is his long lost sister. He plans to kill Ismark tomorrow during the festival. Ismark is currently locked in the stockade in the main square."

Marron, who now knows that Izek has been touched by a demonic presence, declares, "Izek is a demonic creature. He must die." Lady Fiona is delighted. She calls the latest of the Vallakovich line "not the worst Burgomaster we have ever had," just "the most insipid with his happiness program," and waits for them to ask about her attempt at an arrangement with the Baron. Slerdax, ever impulsive, tells her, "Lady, you're gonna have to go to the store to buy some fucks, because that's how much I don't care." She laughs — "you are such a hoot!" — and explains anyway: she arranged her daughter Stella's marriage to the Baron's son, but "Victor Vallakovich is as cruel as his father," and Stella "came back insane." Outraged, she stands, centers herself, and sits back down, urging, "You must do something to end this evil!" Marron is unimpressed and asks why Fiona has done nothing, and whether she is "an ally of this evil?" Slerdax adds that they have heard of "a circle of evil" she is associated with.

Upstairs, Gaxim runs up the stairs, irritated. Ernst proffers a glass of wine; Gaxim is uninterested. Someone tells him, "This is private property. Can you come down with us?" Gaxim demands, "Where is this basement!" and is told, "There is none, you half-orc!"

Downstairs in the dining hall, Marron stares impatiently at Lady Fiona, who says she called them here to see whether they would help her "defeat and destroy the Baron," or "perhaps just replace" him, pointing to what was done to her daughter and to Izek on the Baron's payroll, "a callous dangerous brute." Marron learned from the demons she was trapped by how to see the tell-tale signs of liars, and she knows the Lady is telling the truth: everything Fiona said, she believes to be true. Slerdax thinks Fiona is evil. Sensing Marron's confusion, Fiona tells "a prophecy, friend": on her way back from Vallaki she stopped at the Tser Pool Encampment, where the famous Madam Eva read her fortune — outsiders would arrive at Vallaki and "mark a new age," bringing "great ruin, a purge like no other," after which "a great new day would shine through!" Marron is persuaded by the stirring speech. Slerdax wrestles with conflicting thoughts: "What if she is right about the Baron? And what if they are both evil?" Marron, now convinced that Lady Fiona is a messenger from the gods, swears, "I am yours to command! I shall destroy this, Baron!"

The mage presses: "what is in the basement?" Fiona tries to dissemble, but Slerdax won't let her. "Oh dear, you found out about the basement. The four fools on the pentagram? You saw them too?" When Marron asks why the Baron would put a pentagram in her basement, Fiona misses the opening and explains that conspirators against the Burgomaster are scarce in Vallaki, so she "came up with this idea of this book club" — "a demonic cult as a way to find power-hungry crazy people who would work with me for whatever reason." The four people they saw are "nobodies," whose only role is to rally the cult's members to overthrow the Burgomaster: "There was nothing real about this." Marron knows she is telling the truth, and the truth is horrible: Lady Fiona resorted to demonic cults to secure power. Usually Marron would have preferred killing the cultists, but "using them as cannon fodder to create a more just Vallaki isn't that bad." Lady Fiona's rationalizations work.

Upstairs, Gaxim shoves Ernst: "You insulted my brother! You make fun of me! I don't like you!" Ernst replies, "Hey, big guy, I don't want to start a fight. You really need to go downstairs." Gaxim heads down and out the door, and Lady Fiona nods to Ernst to follow him.

Touching Marron — "my beloved Paladin" — Fiona lays out the plan to overthrow Vargas and his entire clan at the festival: the party is to go into the mansion, collect evidence to rally the crowds, and "learn how Victor drove my daughter Stella mad!" Stella comes down the stairs, obviously not well. Fiona raises her fist: "Tomorrow, great things will happen!"

Slerdax, who has been staring at her intently, gets a distinct impression that something is not right and tells Marron, "Don't trust her." Marron is confused: "who is the servant of the demons?" Wukong points at Lady Fiona. Slerdax says, "all of them," and surprises Lady Fiona with chromatic orb for 19 HP of damage, thinking as he attacks, "if I don't get more scales or fangs or something,  then this isn't a bad decision." Slerdax has figured out how to use the Dark Powers as a mechanism to tell whether his acts are good or evil. The Dark Powers are in a state of shock; the matter is sent to committee, currently ruled by a group of "Strict Evil Actors" who believe the act's intent must be evil — "An unknown act can not be evil." Other Dark Powers fume, the "Strict Evil Actors" retort that followers who are not really evil are more like "Dim Powers," and the debate will not be resolved for some time; the committee may change its membership soon, and the more expansive reading of "evil act" will dominate. "Our heroes should worry." Slerdax is relieved. Wukong also surprises Lady Fiona and kills her.

Maids enter and scream. When Slerdax questions their knowledge of the cult, they attempt to lie and fail. Done with "this endless set of evildoers," Slerdax attacks, as does everyone else; his magic missile kills one maid and wounds another. Wukong smiles: "you are one sick twisted bastard. I like it!" Marron follows the wounded maid and kills her. The last remaining maid tries to outrun Marron through the servants quarter, the black vestibule and the storage room, and tries to escape through the kitchen, but Wukong is waiting and kills her with his bow.

Slerdax suggests, "Let's burn the place?" Wukong is appalled: "Treasure? Why not check the place for Treasure first, dumbass?" Marron runs upstairs looking for more people to kill, with Wukong a few feet behind to pick up treasure. Their first stop turns out to be Nikolai Wachter's room; Wukong remembers that's the name of Gaxim's drinking buddies. Slerdax realizes that not burning the house is probably a good idea. Marron walks into the library, a room of mostly bare shelves whose angles seem not quite right. Wukong notices a cat with a key hanging from its neck and kills it with his bow; when Slerdax stares, Wukong asks, "Dude, evil acts get me more power?" Slerdax nods. Marron, thankfully, doesn't overhear. Wukong finds a secret door but, seeing Marron leave, defers. Marron finds what she thinks is Stella's room — an iron-framed bed fitted with leather straps and no other furnishings — then enters the Master bedroom after Wukong unlocks the door.

Above the hearth hangs a family portrait: a noble father and mother, two young sons, and a baby daughter in the father's arms. On the bed lies a man dressed in black, a copper piece over each eye, bearing a striking resemblance to the father in the painting. Wukong screams, "holy shit, she's sleeping with her dead husband." Marron realizes it is a dead body preserved with a gentle repose spell. Before she can leave, Wukong asks, "can we look around and find some treasure? Slerdax, you like to touch shit, something about your academy, can you touch around here?" Slerdax finds a locked iron chest; Wukong opens it and is suffocated with poison. Inside are the bones of a man, which Slerdax concludes bear "the tell-tale markings of someone who was killed by a vampire."

Wukong and the others then go to the secret room behind the bookcase, a dusty, ten-foot-square room where an iron chest rests on the bottom shelf. Using the key, Wukong opens it: 180 ep bearing Strahd's profile, 110 gp, a Wachter heirloom pipe, five deeds to land granted by Count Strahd von Zarovich, Lady Fiona Wachter's manuscript *The Devil We Know*, a forged copy of *The Grimoire of the Four Quarters* by the diabolist Devostas, and an ancient letter to Lady Lovina Wachter from Lord Vasili von Holtz.

With the upper floor explored, it is time to deal with Ernst. Gaxim has been standing outside keeping Ernst pre-occupied. The party tells Gaxim to come in; Ernst follows into a prepared ambush and screams, "What?" Gaxim asks, "we kill Ernst?" Slerdax nods and kills him. A quick investigation of the rest of the house turns up a stifling den with an elk's head over the mantel and windows over dead gardens. Slerdax wants to burn the house down, but Marron points out that "vengeance is only absolute if the blood drips on her scythe!" They head down to deal with the book club members and enter the cellar — a large root cellar with a dirt floor, two staircases, tracks in the earth, and four neatly made cots against the south wall. As they walk in, skeletons burst out of the ground.

## Locations
### Lady Fiona's house
Lady Fiona's home, with a pentagram beneath it guarded by skeletons. The party dines with her in the dining hall, kills her and her maids there, then searches the house — servants quarter, black vestibule, storage room, kitchen, Nikolai Wachter's room, the library, what Marron thinks is Stella's room, the Master bedroom, a secret room, and a den — before entering the cellar.

### The cellar
A large root cellar with a dirt floor, two ascending flights of stone steps across from one another, tracks in the earth, and four neatly made cots against the south wall. Skeletons burst out of the ground as the party enters.

### Vallaki
The town whose politics Lady Fiona schemes in. Ismark is held in the stockade in the main square, and a festival is to take place tomorrow.

### Tser Pool Encampment
Where Lady Fiona says she stopped, on her way back from Vallaki, to have her fortune read by Madam Eva.

## NPCs
### Lady Fiona
Hostess, schemer and founder of the book club. Warm and theatrical ("you are such a hoot!"), she paints the Baron's family as brutes, blames Victor Vallakovich for her daughter's madness, invokes Madam Eva's prophecy, admits inventing a demonic cult to recruit conspirators, and recruits Marron to gather evidence for an uprising at the festival. Slerdax's chromatic orb and Wukong's attack kill her.

### Ernst
Fiona's man in the house. He invites the party in, tells them Izek plans to kill Ismark, deflects Gaxim's search for the basement, and is sent to follow Gaxim outside. He walks into a prepared ambush and Slerdax kills him.

### Stella
Lady Fiona's daughter, who "came back insane" from her arranged marriage to Victor Vallakovich. She comes down the stairs, "obviously not well."

### The maids
Maids who enter and scream after the attack on Lady Fiona. They lie about their knowledge of the cult and fail; Slerdax, Marron and Wukong kill them.

### The dead husband
A man dressed in black lying on the Master bedroom bed, a copper piece over each eye, preserved with gentle repose; he resembles the father in the family portrait. Wukong calls him Fiona's "dead husband."

## Items
### The cat's key
Hung from the neck of a cat that Wukong kills in the library; Wukong uses it to open the iron chest in the secret room.

### The poisoned iron chest
A locked iron chest Slerdax finds in the Master bedroom. Opening it suffocates Wukong with poison; inside are a man's bones bearing the marks of a vampire's victim.

### The secret room's iron chest
Contents:
- A silk bag containing 180 ep, each coin bearing Strahd's stern visage in profile
- A leather bag containing 110 gp
- A wooden pipe passed down through many generations of Wachter patriarchs
- Five scrolls, notarized deeds for land given to the Wachter family by Count Strahd von Zarovich nearly four centuries ago
- *The Devil We Know*, a poetic manifesto by Lady Fiona Wachter on the worship of devils
- *The Grimoire of the Four Quarters*, by the diabolist Devostas (a forgery)
- An ancient letter to Lady Lovina Wachter from Lord Vasili von Holtz, thanking her for her hospitality, loyalty, and friendship
