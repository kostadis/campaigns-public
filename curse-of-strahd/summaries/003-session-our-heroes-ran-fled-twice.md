# Chapter 3: Our heroes ran, fled, twice.

Date: Days 2–6 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The chapter opens on a retreat: "Our brave heroes turned and fled. And spent the night in the attic before they continued their investigations in the dungeon." What follows is three delves into the dungeon, each ending with the party carrying its dead back up to the attic.

In the first delve the party comes back down the stairs, and the chanting has not stopped. Marron proposes returning to the room they had fled from, the dining hall (27): a plain wooden table flanked by long benches, with moldy humanoid bones strewn on the dirt floor, "the remains of the cult’s vile banquets." Wukong investigates a darkened alcove (28), thinking, "There may be a treasure." There isn't, but there is a grick, and Wukong is not surprised. Gaxim recklessly attacks and hits for 9 HP of damage; Wukong shouts, "Well done!" and disengages, not wanting to be near the creature, and Gaxim and Slerdax kill it together. Wukong smiles, "let me go quickly and see what treasure is there." There is none, and the rest of the party glares at the rogue.

The party heads south through the intersection "which almost led to their doom (29)" and into a shrine festooned with moldy skeletons hanging from rusty shackles. In a wide alcove in the south wall stands a painted wooden statue of a gaunt, pale-faced man in a voluminous black cloak, his left hand on the head of a wolf, a smoky-gray crystal orb in his right. Chanting can be heard from the West. Slerdax, "the most intelligent member of the operation," advances and touches the orb. The unofficial motto of his arcane college was "touch everything, grasp everything, interact with everything," a college "not the best at producing high-level magic users who survived encounters with dungeons and monsters," and Slerdax had taken it as his personal ambition to prove that reputation undeserved. "The five shadows that surprise the party and surround them did not enhance the school’s reputation."

Only Wukong saw it coming: "Damn you, Slerdax!" For a moment he weighs killing Slerdax, then realizes he is more likely to live if Slerdax survives, attacks the ghosts and misses. He need not have worried about Slerdax facing the consequences — the ghosts kill him. Wukong injures one ghost and Gaxim another; Marron takes 11 HP of damage and loses 4 points of strength. Wukong decides "that the better part of valor is living," and, "For reasons that are not immediately obvious," picks up Slerdax's body and runs. Gaxim disengages and flees. Marron, surrounded, is killed. Gaxim notices, stops, picks up the body, and follows Wukong up the stairs to the attic. Another day passes; their rations are starting to taste stale, so they head to the kitchen and eat the bland food.

The second delve begins with a change: "Marron returns from demise as Merrin, the just cleric." Wukong asks, "wait, you are a Paladin?" and gets the answer, "No, I am a cleric. I have no idea what you’re talking about." Remembering the bloodlust in the Paladin's eyes, Wukong judges that a cleric who can turn undead is more useful than a vengeful Paladin, and announces, "I am going to be near the cleric." The chapter then has Marron turn to Slerdax: "If you touch anything, I must insist you use this hand cleansing oil."

The party heads north (23) to the crypts. The first crypt (a) is unnamed; the second (b) is empty, but the name on its stone slab is that of Walter Durst. To the West the crypt doors are closed, and Gaxim, with Wukong's help, shoves them open — to everyone's confusion at the rogue's altruism, which he explains: "I’m not going to let this big goof take all the treasure." These are the empty tombs of Rosavalda Durst and Thornboldt Durst. Merrin quickly surmises they belong to the children they found in the attic. The party brings the bones down from the attic and places them in the appropriate coffin, and "At that moment, Rosavalda, who had possessed him, releases him." Merrin apologizes for "her rudeness and bossy behavior"; Wukong shrugs, as does Slerdax: "really? I thought that was how you normally were."

Across from the children's crypts they find Gustav's empty coffin (c) and, next to it, Elisabeth's. Merrin is very disturbed by the empty coffins. Wukong, convinced there must be treasure, touches Elisabeth's empty coffin and is assaulted by centipedes. He "bravely flees," leaving Gaxim to fight; Gaxim recklessly attacks for 13 HP, and the rogue, from his perch out of harm's way, fires his bow and kills the swarm. Adrenaline coursing: "Half-Orc, that was cool! You and I can make a good team, Gaxim! We should do that again." The rest of the party takes note of "the rogue’s faulty instinct for treasure."

Merrin advances carefully (33) into a fancy dining hall: a chandelier above a table, two high-backed chairs, an empty clay jug and two clay flagons, iron candlesticks in two corners with their candles long since melted. West of it is what must have been where the cult leaders slept — a wood-framed bed with a rotted feather mattress, a wardrobe of old robes, iron candlesticks, an open crate with thirty torches and a leather sack of fifteen candles, and a wooden footlocker at the foot of the bed. Wukong, convinced that this time there is treasure, checks for traps, opens the footlocker, and finds "magical items galore!" He clutches them and squeals with glee — and two ghasts jump out of alcoves. Wukong screams in terror, and the chapter wonders whether it is at losing the treasure or at the monsters. The ghasts surprise and kill him, but in death he clutches the magic items "and has a curious smile on his face." Gaxim drags the dead rogue and the magic out toward the hallway that leads to the attic. "Whatever evil power that Durst worshiped has decided that it is not to be Merrin’s day. She is killed." Slerdax grabs the cleric and follows Gaxim, and the party takes a long rest in the attic.

The third delve comes after their fourth night in the attic. Supplies are running low, but the magical kitchen has bland food that replenishes itself every day. "The house will not let them escape. The mists will not let them escape." And they will not die of hunger or thirst. So they go down the stairway (30) toward the chanting. In room (35) ghostly chanting fills the air, and Merrin deciphers it: "He is ancient. He is the land." Thirteen nooks hold bizarre relics that Merrin assumes belonged to some cult ritual, among them a goblin's mummified hand on a loop of rope, a knife carved from a human bone, an orb made from a nothic's eye, a cloak of stitched ghoul skin, a hag's severed finger, the shrunken head of a halfling, and a coffer holding a dire wolf's withered tongue.

The southernmost tunnel slopes down into murky water and ends at a rusty portcullis. Gaxim, who "derives pleasure from feats of strength," breaks it and rushes into room (38), the source of the chanting, which stops as he enters. It is a forty-foot-square room with murky water over most of the floor, dry stone ledges along the walls, a breach in the west wall to a refuse-heaped cave, and a central octagonal dais with a stone altar "carved with hideous depictions of grasping ghouls" and stained with dry blood, rusty chains with shackles dangling above it. The party measures the room — water 2 feet deep, ledges and dais 5 feet high, ceiling 16 feet, chains 8 feet long — and Merrin, from earlier reading, infers that the cultists would shackle prisoners to the chains, dangle them above the altar, cut them open, and let the altar be bathed in blood. A wooden wheel half embedded in the east wall is guessed to lift the portcullis.

While Slerdax stares at the refuse pile and weighs "the lessons of his Academy and the lessons of life," Merrin climbs onto the altar. The chanting rises as thirteen dark apparitions appear on the ledges, black-robed figures holding torches whose black fire seems to draw light in, with voids where Merrin expects faces. "One must die!" they chant. "One must die! One must die!" Slerdax tries to fake them out with the familiar, but the magical aura of the creature disappoints the apparitions and leaves them unmoved. Gaxim is confused. Slerdax suggests, "Hey, maybe if we just walk off the dais, they won’t bother us!" and Merrin shrugs and walks off. The apparitions shout, "Lorghoth the Decayer, we awaken thee!" and a shambling mound emerges from the refuse.

"What should have been a quick defeat turns into a shocking victory because Slerdax has a wand of binding." How such a novice possesses it "is a mystery. Nonetheless, it saves them." When the creature dies the wand disappears, and from the corner of his eyes Slerdax thinks he sees "a man who looks eerily like the statue that had the orb." In the prison room west of the main altar the party finds a dead cultist with a gold ring on his hand. "The monster that Rose and Thorn commanded they kill is dead. All of the mysteries of the house have been explored. Now all that remains is to leave."

But the attic has changed. The window to the South is boarded up, and the door to the North "is no longer a door but a series of slashing blades." While the party experiments with ways to stop the blades, Slerdax, "who likes to touch everything," touches the wall and finds it now brittle; Gaxim smashes it, opening a new passageway, and a swarm of spiders attacks from the broken wall. Slerdax sees that all the doors have been replaced with blades and all the windows boarded up. "The cultists are not happy that both their monster was killed and no sacrifice was made." The chapter ends: "The house apparently is intent on revenge"
