# Chapter 21: The Miraculous Dress Maker

Date: Days 12–13 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The heroes have arrived, once again, at the house of the Burgomaster, and chores await them. Slerdax picks up his bucket and heads to the cows — "Touching things is his thing, and this is a new thing to touch." Wukong takes an ax to a tree and chops it down. Gaxim is about to do the same when he notices Merrin and Ireena heading to the lake, and follows them.

The pool at the north end of the village shimmers and sparkles even under gray skies. Near its shore sits an old gazebo on the verge of collapse, and in it stands a wooden statue of a mournful, bare-chested man, its paint chipped and faded, arms outstretched "as though waiting to be embraced." Ireena has a peculiar glint in her eye and something different in how she carries herself; neither Gaxim nor Merrin notices. She is hearing a gentle voice calling to her. At the pool's edge an image appears in the water: a handsome youth of kind and noble visage, whose sadness turns to sudden joy. "Tatyana!" he says. "It has been so long! Come, my love. Let us be together at last." Ireena gasps, hand on her heart: "My beloved Sergei! In life, you were a prince and a man of faith. We were to be married long ago. Has this blessed pool called your spirit to me?" She reaches toward the surface, and a hand of water rises to take hers.

Gaxim decides the apparition must be evil and tries to grapple Ireena, but she dodges his grasp and reaches out to Sergei (the chapter notes she "rolled a natural 20"). She is pulled into the pool and embraces Sergei beneath the rippling water, and the two begin to fade from view; the chapter says "Neither Merrin nor Gaxim has ever seen a happier couple". Gaxim jumps in after her to save her from whatever evil creature has captured her. Then a peal of thunder shakes the land, the dark clouds coalesce into a terrible visage, and a deep, dark voice from beyond the mountains cries out, "She is mine!" Blue lightning splits the sky and strikes the pool. Gaxim takes 55 HP of damage. Merrin stares, pointing her spear at the scene — "But Marron takes over. The joy of love has weakened Merrin’s hold on the body, freeing the good paladin." As the heroes learn later, "Strahd’s voice and his lightning destroyed a particular blessing the pool had."

Gaxim shrugs at all of this and says, "Chores." He heads back to the Burgomaster's, "terrified of the events", picks up an ax, and helps Wukong finish cutting the tree. Exhausted, they go to eat — and before a single bite they discover a young boy at the dinner table: "Apparently, the dead son is now alive." Slerdax notices that Anna, the Burgomaster’s wife, is trying to hide something, and Dmitri stares at his wife until he can't hold it anymore. "Anna, what have you done?" / "What was necessary!" He turns to the heroes and begs them to help her; apparently she must find a dress. The chapter gives the exchange that follows: "You will die without guards." / "Our town would die without this deal." Then the plea: "I know it is rude to ask, but can you guard my wife as she goes to Vallaki to get a wedding dress for the Abbot? She must get one from the Lady Petrovna or the Lady Wachter!"

The heroes stop chewing. Gaxim blurts out, "Both dead. Gaxim kills Wachter because of evil. Izek kills Petrovna because she evil." Silence falls; Gaxim continues to eat. Anna declares that she doesn't care, that she will go to Vallaki and find a dress the Abbot likes, and the Burgomaster pleads with her and with the heroes. Finally Wukong says, "Enough, I will make a dress." Everyone stares. He pulls down the curtains, takes some sheets and an old dress of the Burgomaster, and marches into a back room. A few hours later — "and a natural 20" — out comes "the most spectacular dress that anyone has seen." Wukong looks at the staring heroes: "I can sew." The Burgomaster is in tears: "my wife, and my son and my town saved! Who would have known that such a hero would come into our village! A hero with the blade, and with the scissors?" Wukong goes to bed.

In the morning the heroes take the dress up to the Abbot. "Where did you get such a dress?" Gaxim gruffly answers, "Wukong dressmaker." The Abbot will take the dress, but asks, "where is the fake bride?" / "Gone." / "Oh, dear. My plans have been damaged." Slerdax asks, "what was your old plan?" The Abbot explains: "My old plan was that Vasilka would become Strahd’s bride. Strahd is eternal. I assumed that with her, Strahd would be happy or at least happier. Then you brought me Ireena, and I had a better plan. I would clone Ireena and then summon Tatyana’s soul into the vessel and give that to Strahd. The real Ireena would have to be disposed of." He pauses, squints at the sky, and continues: "But if Strahd knows that Ireena is gone, then he will not accept Vasilka as his bride. But Strahd is always interested in a new toy, and Vasilka is a very unique toy that he might come to want. I need to find a way to make that happen. This needs thought and preparation." As the party leaves, he calls out, "This dress is divine and deserves a divine boon. I offer the ability to raise from the dead, twice at no cost as long as the dressmaker is with you."

The party heads down and out of Krezk. At the old Svalich road they receive another invitation from Strahd. It says he has pursued Ireena through the centuries and through death itself, that he cannot and will not forgive them for this, but that he wants to meet them again for dinner "to discuss vengeance as civilized men", promising a safe arrival and a safe departure — and should they need a lift, they need only call out.

Further down the road, Wukong once again notices someone spying on them, this time an old man. Slerdax wonders whether the spy could be turned into a friend, walks up, shakes his hand, and asks, "Are you heading in the same direction we are?" The old man is rough-looking, with "a peculiar stench of death about him," and stares intently. "What a coincidence, I am going the way you are. I’ve been searching for herbs in the area, and I noticed you folk. I was wondering what you were up to. And now you are asking me to be your traveling companion." Marron, "the paladin", stares at the herbs (another natural 20) and works out what was bothering her: he is no innocent herb collector but a warlock, "And a powerful one at that", and from his clothes either Strahd or the Dark Powers of Barovia are his patron. The heroes consider killing him quickly and decide against it. Seeing their confusion, he asks, "Perhaps you could give me a lock of your hair to cast a blessing? And I will be on my way." The heroes wisely refuse. After a long stare-down, the old man announces that he has business to attend to in Vallaki.

After considerable debate about where to go next, the party decides on Argynvostholt, where "apparently some enemies of Strahd could become friends. Or so Morgantha told Slerdax." The mansion looms on a quiet promontory high above the river valley: turrets capped with fairytale cones, towers lined with sculpted battlements, a third of the structure collapsed along with part of the roof, and a dark, octagonal tower rising above the rest. Thunder sounds out of the fog, wolves howl in the woods below, and the house stands silent, "seeming like the fossilized remains of some long-dead thing smote upon the mountainside."

Near the castle, a moss-covered statue of a silver dragon of noble bearing sits atop a ten-foot-wide, ten-foot-high cube of granite, looking east toward the mansion, its spiny frill cracked and broken in many places. Gaxim marches up to the main entrance, where flagstone steps climb to tall wooden doors with rusted iron bands and dragon-shaped knockers, and the word Argynvostholt is carved into the lintel. As he stands at the door, the dragon statue opens its mouth, exhales a 60-foot cone of harmless cold air, and closes it again.

The chapter pauses to note that Gaxim had taken the Sunsword from Marron. Gaxim, "a trusting soul", would ask the sword what to do, and the sword — convinced this good soul would give it the revenge it craves — "took it upon itself to protect its wielder from the more reckless of his behaviors." So Gaxim points to Wukong and tells him to check the door. Wukong is surprised — "After all, Gaxim was a meat shield, not a careful hero" — but pushes the unlocked doors open.

The foyer "feels like a king’s tomb": a grand staircase, stone balconies on pillars and arches, a tall faded tapestry of a nobleman in silver armor, six sets of double doors, three alabaster busts of handsome men on marble pedestals and a fourth shattered across the mosaic floor, and two wrought-iron chandeliers hanging "like monstrous black spiders." As Gaxim passes through, a great winged shadow moves across the walls and disappears with a soft bestial hiss — "an ominous shadow of a dragon."

Gaxim turns left into a ransacked, wood-paneled den with a cold, dark hearth and, standing against the north wall, a sarcophagus of black wood with a queen's effigy carved into its lid. As Slerdax follows, a fire erupts in the dead hearth and takes draconic form, hissing and unfurling smoky wings. Slerdax examines it and concludes that some kind of necromantic force is at play. Wukong, seeing no threats, heads east to the dining hall: a twenty-foot-long table with sculpted dragons for legs, chairs carved like folded dragon wings, a softly glowing crystal chandelier, two life-sized statues of knights with dragon-winged helms and shields, rainwater pooling on the floor, and cracked leaded glass doors beside stained glass panels of silver dragons in flight, opening onto a dark, misty room that appears to be a chapel.

Back in the den, as Gaxim is about to strike the fire, the fiery dragon addresses the party: "My knights have fallen into darkness. Save them if you can. Show them the light they have lost!" Then the fire burns out. Slerdax follows Wukong into the dining hall and discovers the chapel: a U-shaped wooden balcony on cracked pillars, a barred door in the north wall, a stone altar carved with a rising sun bas-relief between iron candelabras, and a shattered stained-glass window letting thick fog fill the room. Through the fog, three armored figures kneel before the altar. Given what the dragon told them, the heroes decide not to disturb them.

Meanwhile, Marron explores a dark, moldy storage room with five barrels in wooden braces. Hiding behind the barrels is an injured dusk elf named Savid, and Marron heals him. "Oh, my friends, you have saved me. I am from the Vistani camp outside of Vallaki. I have been searching for the missing girl Arabelle when a wandering mob of needle blights attacked me. I was forced to take refuge here." Asked what he has learned of the place, he answers, "I have lived in Barovia for 400 years, and I know much about this land and its people." When the heroes suggest moving to the dinner table, he brushes them off: "Either you will sit down here, or I will say nothing." He mutters, "this is so exciting. I have someone new to tell my stories." Then he tells the story: "Argynvost was a silver dragon that liked to assume human forms, and Argynvostholt was the dragon’s home." In human guise the dragon led the Order of the Silver Dragon, which sheltered refugees fleeing Strahd's army; Strahd's soldiers slew the dragon, destroyed the order, and sacked the mansion, and Vistani and dusk elves avoid it, "believing that the dragon’s ghost haunts it."

"So he turned them into vampires?" asks Marron. "No, they are revenants. And in Barovia, unlike outside of this cursed land, a revenant will never die until its quest is fulfilled." Asked whether some artifact could save them, Savid stares: "why would you want to do that?" Asked about the Amber Temple, he says only, "You must talk to Kasimir." The heroes remember Kasimir, his tale of woe involving his sister, and the promise they made to go with him to the Amber Temple. Invited along, Savid declines: "No, it is bad luck to be here. I will not stay." Gaxim inspects the barrels, and the sword tells him it has all gone bad, "even the excellent stuff."

Taking special care to avoid the revenants, the heroes explore further and reach the destroyed ballroom, rubble strewn across its pink marble floor among fallen chandeliers and broken chairs, thick webs stretching wall to wall. Moving among them are "too many giant spiders to count!" — 9 in total — and they swarm over the heroes.
