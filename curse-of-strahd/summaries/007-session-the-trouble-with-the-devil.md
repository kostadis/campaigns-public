# Chapter 7: The trouble with the devil.

Date: Days 7–8 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The darkness over the forest dissipates and eight wolves surround the party. Gaxim is excited: the wolf he has been pursuing so long, the one that "will lead him back home", is there. He charges, enraged and recklessly attacking, for eight points of damage. Slerdax casts a chromatic orb for 15 points and kills one wolf. Merrin casts spiritual weapon, uses it on a wolf to the North East of Slerdax and flanks the monster, doing a total of 10 points of damage. Wukong draws his bow on the wolf facing Gaxim and misses, thinking to himself, "Next time," "I will remember to rub my lucky charm." Ismark takes his crossbow: "I will now help my friend to defend us from the wolves! Ireena, stand next to me." He kills the wolf facing Gaxim and misses the wolf fighting Merrin.

Then the wolves all descend on Ismark, four attacks for 20 points of damage. The rest of the party is of no apparent interest to them, and although Ireena stands right next to him, the monsters do not seem interested in her at all; "They seem to be intent on killing her guardian." Gaxim kills a wolf with an 11-point charge. Slerdax casts shatter on the three remaining wolves attacking Ismark: one dies and the other two are grievously wounded. Merrin kills the wolf she is facing. Then Ismark cries, "Ireena, I shall kill these monsters and protect you!", takes his longsword and short sword, and kills the remaining three wolves. The heroes are "impressed, shocked even."

Off in the distance a bat flies off into the darkness. Wukong's arrow pierces its wing and the bat appears to falter, but "the wing miraculously recovers, and the creature continues to travel."

Gaxim puts his ax over his shoulder, grabs a wolf, and announces, "Gaxim kill Wolf. Gaxim, go home." The kindhearted giant begins to head towards Barovia, away from Vallaki. Wukong tries to persuade him that the mists will keep them from traveling, but Gaxim is "too wise to let Wukong’s entreaties confuse him." Merrin tries: "hey, we signed up for a mission, and we're not done." Gaxim answers, "Mission kill Wolf. Gaxim kills Wolf. Gaxim, go home." There will be "no mission creep, Gaxim has spoken." Ismark's appeal — "Friendly giant, you see this beautiful woman, you promised me you would help me get her safely to Vallaki." — falls on deaf ears. Merrin is undaunted and keeps pressing, and in the end Gaxim is convinced that escape through the mists is impossible until all the quests are completed.

The heroes continue down towards Vallaki. The Old Svalich Road turns from a winding path through the Balinok Mountains into a lazy trail descending into a fog-filled valley. They see a walled town near the shores of a great mountain lake whose waters are "dark and still", and a branch in the road leading west to a promontory topped by a dilapidated stone windmill, its warped wooden vanes stripped bare. Merrin wonders why a collection of ravens is squawking so loudly, but decides it is just the meaningless noise of birds. It is getting dark, and rather than spend the night in the mysterious forests of Barovia they push on to Vallaki, deciding that "they will deal with a hag and her pastries later." The woods recede to reveal "a sullen mountain burg surrounded by a wooden palisade", fog pressing against its wall. The road ends at sturdy iron gates with two shadowy figures behind them, and outside the gates a half-dozen pikes bear impaled wolves' heads. Gaxim is excited: "I also killed Wolf!"

The guards are "very fastidious" about the background and reasons of anyone entering. Asked his name, Gaxim says "Gaxim."; asked his occupation, "Gaxim." again; asked his purpose, he answers, "kill Wolf!", sure that these are his people and will appreciate his wolf killing. When Slerdax tries to explain, he learns "that you can never interrupt a bureaucrat with a modicum of power being lorded over an innocent victim": "I was not asking you! I was asking the half-orc." Gaxim replies, "Me Gaxim. You dumb."

Ismark warns Merrin and Slerdax that Vallaki is not a very welcoming place, and Merrin suggests Ismark try to get them through. He tells the guards, "My name is Ismark, and this is Ireena, the daughter of the burgomaster. We come from Barovia seeking safety in the great town of Vallaki." The guard answers, "Do you remember the rules of our town? You must always smile. Our smiles are what keep the Devil away." Ismark asks whether Wukong or Slerdax can speak for the friendly giant; the guard asks Ismark whether he can vouch for Gaxim, and Ismark, unwilling to separate Gaxim from his friends, declines. Slerdax and Merrin get through the questions quickly. Wukong runs into trouble over his profession — "I am the friendly giant’s best friend." — and, when the guards don't buy it, attempts to deceive them about his real job.

Then the guards ask, "what is your opinion of Strahd?" Gaxim: "Gaxim kill Strahd. Gaxim go home." Ismark juts out his chin: "I am the sworn enemy of Strahd! I have spent my whole life preparing to defeat him. And when the time comes, he will die from my hand." Ireena is exasperated, telling him the routine "makes you look ridiculous. Worse, it might attract Strahd’s attention," and that he acts "as if you are some kind of Rudolph van Richten, a legendary killer of vampires." Ismark retorts, "he is a legend, and he does not exist. I, on the other hand, do." The party gathers that this argument has been going on for some time. Slerdax answers, "death to Strahd." Merrin adds, "death to all vampires." The guard is delighted: "excellent, two, checkmarks!" Wukong, "demonstrating an unbelievable lack of tact for a man of many talents", says, "if getting out of the shit hole involves killing Strahd, sign me up for that." The offended guards ask, "what shit hole?", and Wukong backpedals: it "wasn’t meant to be a statement about your fair city." The guards are mollified when he is asked whether he will do what is necessary to defend Vallaki from the enemies of Strahd.

Past the threshold, the guards recite the rules. The name of Strahd is strictly illegal inside, even in written form; he is to be called "The Devil." The town holds a festival each week in honor of the residents' merriment and continued support of the Burgomaster, Baron Vargas Vallakovich; guests need not help prepare, but "your attendance and participation in each and every festival are absolutely mandatory for the length of your stay." The next is "the Festival of the Blazing Sun, will be held in three days in the town square." Breaking Vallakian law means punishment, including banishment from "the only place Strahd has not visited in 100 years", a peace credited to the burgomaster's "program of happiness and festivals"; "All who wish to keep Strahd away must always be happy." The guard smiles.

Ireena thanks the guards for their hospitality — "We have been looking for a safe place." — and suggests to the heroes that they visit the burgomaster to find a safe place to sleep: "He and my father were friends, and such that there Is nobility in this land, I am nobility." Wukong and Merrin reflect that Vallaki of Barovia is very like the city of Vallaki on the Wild Coast — same name, same general layout, curiously different in all the details. Vallaki on the Wild Coast is famous these days, for legend has it that there "the keeper of the knife single-handedly defeated the forces of the abyss through his brilliant tactical and strategic insight", a victory that later led to the defeat of the abyssal armies and the retrieval of the holy relic.

On the way to the burgomaster's mansion the party crosses the main square: limp, tattered garlands, boxes of tiny dead flowers, a row of stocks at the north end holding "several men, women, and children wearing crude, plaster donkey heads", peasants drawing water from a crumbling stone fountain topped by a gray statue of an impressive man facing west. Posted proclamations announce "THE WOLF’S HEAD JAMBOREE!" A brutish man with an arm bearing elongated fingers, long nails and spines appears among guards and has the sign replaced with one for "THE FESTIVAL OF THE BLAZING SUN!" He stares at Gaxim and says, "brother!" Gaxim cries, "We drink!" The man has been looking for his brother "for so long", and asks who the others are. "Friends!" He introduces himself: "I am Izek Strazni. I am the right-hand man of the burgomaster." As he raises his "gruesome demonic hand", everyone around him stands back, terrified. He claims Gaxim: "I lost my brother of so many years ago, my half-brother, of course. He has come back to me." Izek's joy only deepens his guards' fear, while Gaxim is excited to discover he has a brother. It is too late for the burgomaster, so Izek sends them to sleep "in the finest inn of Vallaki as guests of mine", with tea tomorrow. "Mead?" asks Gaxim. "For you, my brother, anything you want." Izek bends down to whisper, "if they do not treat you well, tell me." Gaxim does not pick up on the implicit threat.

The Blue Water Inn is owned by the Martikov Family; the wife Danika runs the bar while her husband Urwin cooks. Ravens perch on its sagging tile roof and a painted sign over the entrance depicts a blue waterfall. The taproom is packed, lit in dull orange, its walls "adorned with wolf heads mounted on wooden plaques." Gaxim wants to sleep, but the heroes take in the local color: two brothers acting like drunk rich boys, a pair of hunters ("wolf-hunters if the pelts and teeth are anything to go by"), and at the bar a colorfully dressed half-elf whose lute marks him as a bard. The barkeep demands, "who are you? And what can I do for you? And more importantly, what can you do for me?" Merrin offers "What you need"; the barkeep asks for a drink together, and Merrin, very law-abiding, agrees "As long as you smile with us." Food and lodging will be "5 SP per night, per lodger", and the party frets over its paltry resources — until Izek, who followed them, shouts, "This is my brother, and these are his friends." The bartender turns pale: "Izek, I didn’t know!" and "They can stay, for… free!" Izek adds, "I expect you to take care of them! I will come to get you tomorrow."

Gaxim declares, "free drinks for everyone!" and heads for the rich boys Karl, and Nikolai Wachter. After the round, Danika announces, "we have no more alcohol." She explains that her husband says a shipment from the Wizard of Wines has been delayed, and that he, the chef, would be delighted to have them look into it. When Merrin admits never having heard of the wizard of wines, Danika asks, "you are not from here?" — "no, I am not." The half-elf bard turns: "where are you from?" Ismark confronts him with his best tough-guy routine — "Do you not know that this man is the brother of Izek Strazni?" — and when asked "Who are you?" answers, "I am Ismark, protector of Ireena." The half-elf suddenly becomes very interested. Gaxim, bored, looks at his empty mug: "I sleep! We go to sleep!" Seeing their meat shield go to bed, the rest follow. Danika's boys take them to their rooms, asking all sorts of questions about the world beyond Barovia. The night passes uneventfully.

In the morning Danika, with "that weird smile that everybody in Vallaki has", tells them, "Izek is outside waiting for you." She then warns Merrin, "Do not trust Izek Strazni." The Baron's henchman recently arrested the local shoemaker, Udo Lukovich, for "malicious unhappiness", and with "some kind of dark magic" burned his house to the ground as punishment for resisting arrest; Udo's family fled to his mother, Willemina Rikalova, "who spends much of her time at St. Andral’s Church."

The burgomaster's mansion is plastered stone scarred where the plaster has fallen away, drapes over every window; inside, framed portraits, a wide staircase with a sculpted railing, a long carpeted hall, and "Bundles of twigs are heaped against the walls." The maids look at Izek as he points to Gaxim — "My brother!" — and quickly turn to clean, smiling the Vallaki smile and saying "how wonderful!" Everyone seems uneasy at a happy Izek. He leads them upstairs, saying the burgomaster "wishes to see Ireena", and that the Burgomaster of Barovia "of course, died because he did not follow the program of the Baron."

In his office Izek presents "my brother" to Baron Vargas Vallakovich, "And here is the woman Ireena that you said I should bring." The Baron stands next to his dog, stiffly smiling, his face twitching "as if he is terrified of what he is really feeling come out"; he stares at Izek "the same way a cow stares at a wolf". He is a middle-aged man with greying hair and black eyes, well-fed, in a brown velour jacket, white silk shirt and red pantaloons, his fine clothes overworn. "Ah, my dear new visitors of Vallaki, how is the city?" "Sleep!" says Gaxim. "All is well," the Baron says nervously, and everyone else just smiles. Slerdax asks why Vallaki is safe when "All the other settlements have been devastated". The Baron starts, "It is my belief All is Well here in Vallaki because we have managed to keep Strahd." Slerdax interrupts, "The Devil?" — "Yes, the Devil! So good to correct me!" Turning manic and pacing, the Baron explains that there is a festival every week, and that anyone who does not attend "and is not happy, who have unwarranted unhappiness, is put in the stocks!" — "And because of our happiness, the Devil has not come for a hundred years!" Slerdax thinks, "I should cast Tasha’s Hideous Laughter on Izek!" He doesn't.

The Baron thanks them for bringing Ireena and tells her, "I demand you stay here!" She agrees politely; Ismark sighs, muttering about the safety of the Church of St. Andral's and the bones of some saint. Izek asks, "what about my brother?" — "If your brother wishes to stay at my house, who am I to say no?" Lydia, the Baron's wife, appears: "Baron, you have brought such guests, and not told me! I have not brought the tea!" Unlike the Baron, she smiles as though she believes smiling will keep the Devil away. Gaxim asks, "mead?", and the Baron apologises that the Wizard of Wines shipment is delayed: "Brother of Izek, whom I found in an orphanage, accept my apologies." Gaxim is sad. Slerdax privately muses, "Barovia’s gender roles are very – well – strong and rigid and … ?" Lydia sighs, "I will get you some tea," and passes the order to a maid.

Over the bitter tea the Baron boasts that "Our program of happiness has kept the valley safe for 100 years!" Slerdax asks, "When did you discover that smiling kept Strahd." The Baron scowls, "We do not say his name!" — "The devil away?" — "It has done so for a hundred years!" — "What happened 101 years ago?" The Baron, irritated, does not know, only that since mandatory happiness, the stockades and mandatory festivals, "the Devil has not shown up." He asks Lydia to agree; caught lost in thought and not smiling, she puts on "the most strained smile she can" and says, "yes dear!"

Then business. As friends of Strazni, the party can do things for him. "There is a woman, Lady Fiona Wachter, who does not attend my festivals and does not smile." The party does not react in horror. "It is said that she worships the Devil himself. I need proof of her alliance with the Devil, so I can bring her down." She is too powerful and he trusts no one, but blood relations of Izek he can trust. Several maids have been lost and he blames her: "Some mysterious evil worship that requires sacrifice." Izek has learned nothing, but the party might quietly find out; "She would not suspect you. When you have what I need, come to Izek, and he will take care of everything." Slerdax asks, "You trust us to do that?", and the Baron suddenly wonders whether he should. Wukong smooths it over — "Baron, what my friend Slerdax was trying to say is that we are surprised with the honor of this trust." — nudges Gaxim into "Wukong Brave!", and promises that "blood is thicker than water, and we will take care of Gaxim’s family." "All is well!" says the Baron. Gaxim is super-excited to work on this mission.

## Locations
### The Old Svalich Road
The road the party follows from the Balinok Mountains down into the fog-filled valley, past a branch west to a promontory with a dilapidated stone windmill, ending at Vallaki's iron gates.

### Vallaki
A walled town by a dark mountain lake, surrounded by a wooden palisade with fog pressed against it, its gate flanked by pikes bearing wolves' heads. Its guards enforce mandatory smiling, mandatory weekly festivals, and a ban on speaking Strahd's name. Wukong and Merrin find it like the Vallaki on the Wild Coast in name and layout but "curiously different in all the details."

### The Town Square
Tattered garlands, dead flowers, a row of stocks holding men, women and children in plaster donkey heads, and a crumbling fountain with a gray statue of a man facing west. Here the Wolf's Head Jamboree notice is replaced and Izek claims Gaxim as his brother.

### The Blue Water Inn
A two-story wooden inn owned by the Martikov Family, with ravens on its roof and a blue waterfall on its sign. The taproom walls are hung with wolf heads. The party lodges here free on Izek's word and learns that the alcohol has run out.

### The Burgomaster's Mansion
A plaster-scarred stone mansion with draped windows, a grand foyer of portraits, and bundles of twigs heaped against the walls. The Baron receives the party for tea in his upstairs office.

### St. Andral's Church
Mentioned only: Willemina Rikalova spends much of her time there, and Ismark mutters about the safety of the Church of St. Andral's and the bones of some saint.

Ireena's guardian, who calls himself "protector of Ireena." The wolves single him out; he takes 20 points and then kills the last three wolves himself, impressing the party. He declares himself "the sworn enemy of Strahd!", argues with Ireena over it, warns that Vallaki is unwelcoming, and confronts the half-elf bard. He sighs when Ireena is kept at the mansion.

Daughter of the burgomaster of Barovia, under Ismark's protection. The wolves ignore her. She mocks Ismark's Strahd routine as one that "might attract Strahd’s attention", thanks the guards, and claims nobility: "I am nobility." The Baron demands she stay at his house, and she agrees politely.
