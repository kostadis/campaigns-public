# Chapter 36: Return to Vallaki

Date: Days 23–24 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The chapter opens with the Dark Powers reflecting on "The irony of the fates". Gaxim, the barbarian, came to the Dark Realms "through an accident of fate to pursue a wolf", has found his wolves, and "now only dreams of revenge on the Hags and Strahd." Kiril and his band of cruel men will die, and Gaxim will live: "A different set of choices might have led to Gaxim being a pawn of Strahd's and not his greatest scourge." The committee on Barovia will have to review the outcome, and there is "the awkward arrival of the exiled powers" that the adventurers and Gaxim freed from Barovia. "The mindless rage of a barbarian and Marron's single-minded desire for revenge was an unexpected combination." The expansion plans had been in place for some time, so the new dark powers will have their own realms and kingdoms. The only remaining question is what to do about these adventurers: "They have come further than any in a very long time."

Wukong kills Kiril. A werewolf named Wensencia sees the death of his pack and his mad pack master and begins, "I was never a fan of Kiril's mad plans to engage the children in Death Matches and blood baths. I was hoping…" — but seeing the bloodlust in Gaxim's eyes, he turns and flees. Davanka flees as well. Ezmerelda tries to maneuver to cast magic missiles against both, but the were-creatures have scattered in the caves; she targets one and injures it. Gaxim announces, "I kill. My job. No, be upset." He kills the first and throws the lighted sword at Davanka, who is injured but not dead. Slerdax casts a 5th level magic missile, and the creature dies.

The group scavenges the bodies and finds 77 ep — "Seems the wolves liked the shiny silver." Wukong then strips the werewolves of their pelts: "It's a gruesome job." Meanwhile Merrin rushes to the children in a chamber that is a shrine to the goddess Mother Night (Z7). Rough-hewn stairs lead down to a torchlit cave where "wide-eyed children stand behind wooden bars and stare at her in terrified silence." There are six wooden cages, their lids weighted with heavy rocks; two are empty, and each of the others holds a pair of frightened children. Between the cages stands a crude wooden statue of a wolf-headed woman draped in garlands of vines and night flowers, an incredible amount of treasure piled around its base, and behind it "two maggot-ridden corpses hang from iron shackles bolted to the wall."

Merrin and Gaxim free the children, who are between the ages of 4 and 12. Ometa (4) and Omm (7) are gnomes "from a world where Gnomes and Giants and Halflings are engaged in a revolutionary war involving equitable redistribution of wealth from the lumpen bourgeoisie to the working classes." Heraclius Ariston (8) and Chrestos Hippolytus (10) came from a land called Meletis, "where the gods of Theros rule." The children came from beyond the mists; some were kidnapped by the Vistani, who traded them with the werewolves, and others were captured by the werewolves. The chapter adds that they all appear to be human and come from "the sword coast" near "Waterdeep." Another four are from the Town of Barovia: Alana (girl 6), Clavdia (girl 6), Balthazar (boy 7), and Bogan (boy 9); apparently the werewolves offered gold to their parents. The heroes remember how the Hags addicted parents to the pasties and seized children when the parents could pay no more, and "Gaxim's bloodlust and rage increases." Last are the two Martikov children, Brom and Bray.

Slerdax studies the surroundings and concludes that some kind of game was played here: "A battle-royal where the reward was lycanthropy." The heroes absorb the horror, then seize the treasure before the altar. Merrin realizes that only a series of lesser restorations will heal the children of what they have seen.

Gaxim leads the group to the back of the cave, where a curtain made of human skin hangs. Behind it a 10-foot-high, 10-foot-wide tunnel with rough-hewn stairs and landings climbs to a secret door, beyond which a twenty-foot-diameter ring of stones dominates a rocky ledge on the mountainside. Inside the ring are spattered blood and small gnawed bones; outside it lie several spears stained with dry blood. Slerdax explains that the shrine was where the children were trained to fight and to provide good entertainment, "but the real battle happened here," and notes that the gnawed bones are children's. The stone ring sits on the mountain above the cave where the heroes entered. Gaxim searches it and finds no treasure.

Heading back down, the heroes find a small child cowering in a cave. Merrin attempts to intimidate it: "The outcome is a disaster." Ezmerelda, "the only human here," tells the group, "you should stay away from human children." She reassures the child and tells the group that he is Kellen. Kellen is hugging a wooden doll that resembles Gaxim painted and dressed as a zombie, etched with a tiny slogan: "In No Fun, Is No Blinsky." Ezmerelda keeps this to herself until the group is in Vallaki; the heroes remember that Vallaki has a toymaker called Blinsky. Kellen is a lycanthrope who has not mastered his powers, and the group decides to take him along with the other children and cure him. Talking with him, they learn he came from Bryn Shander, "in the spine of the world in a place called the Icewind Dales," and was captured by the vistani.

From there the heroes go to a cave with an underground spring (Z4): a gash in the ceiling lets in gray light and cold drizzle, and a pool roughly forty feet across and ten feet deep lies beneath ledges to the north and east. The water is fresh. The crates on the eastern ledge hold heaps of adult-sized clothing, and Wukong uses them to make new fitted clothing for the children. Ezmerelda passes on Kellen's word that all of the werewolves are dead, making this an excellent time to rest. The rest goes badly at first — the heroes come to understand why the treasure was unguarded, as some kind of curse brings nightmares and they cannot sleep. Gaxim goes back to sleep, Ezmerelda does the same, and Merrin, "realizing that nobody wants to go to KP, sighs and rests as well." Rested at last, the heroes use a remove curse spell to free themselves of the curse of Mother Night, a lesser restoration spell to heal the children's trauma, and Merrin uses a greater restoration spell to remove the curse of Lycanthropy from Kellen. One child who had not yet introduced herself is named Jill; she comes from Fairhaven, a town on the Wild Coast.

With the children homeless, Gaxim suggests, "The orphanage!" Merrin scoffs. Although it is evening in Barovia, the heroes brave the darkness with the children in tow. At the Raven River crossroads the revenants attack once more, surprising Slerdax. Their leader shouts, "WE ARE BACK! And we know what to do! Attack everyone but  you!" The group stares at them coolly. "You should be quaking in your boots! YOU SHOULD BE TERRIFIED! We shall have our undying vengeance!"

Gaxim attacks the leader, and Ezmerelda blasts another with a lightning bolt that also hits the trees behind him. A revenant paralyzes Gaxim; the one he attacked is delighted at the chance to hurt him, but Gaxim only takes half. Wukong attacks the same creature. Slerdax, still surprised, takes 30 points of damage from a revenant's sword. Ezmerelda closes on the creature she singed and attacks with her blades while the children huddle in terror. Slerdax casts thunderwave, "but the revenant was waiting for it." Gaxim's will frees him from the paralysis and he glares with bloodlust — but "The plan to destroy his allies, however, is still in effect," and Slerdax is paralyzed by the revenant next to Gaxim. Merrin casts a life transference spell to ensure Slerdax's survival. Wukong kills a revenant, and a ghastly voice echoes, "we will come back with another plan, next time. And it will be a better one." The hold on the mage is broken.

The fight continues. Gaxim deflects 9 HP of an attack, then bounds to the creature attacking Slerdax and critically hits it for 51 HP. Slerdax throws a chromatic orb. When Merrin worries about the children, a remaining revenant says, "I could kill the children. But I am not a monster, not like you!" Slerdax scoffs, "says the ACTUAL monster!" Uncertain how to take that, the aasimar strikes the revenant for 21 HP, then places herself between the remaining "actual monsters" and the children. Wukong strikes for 29 HP, but it is not enough, and Slerdax takes 30 HP of slashing damage from two strokes of the revenant's long sword. Gaxim is annoyed: "How dare you attack Slerdax? You should attack me! That is what I am here for!" The revenant answers, "I want to kill the mage. Slerdax is the one that killed me with that brilliant tactical maneuver of a dimension door!" Gaxim's sword strikes for 33. "You do not understand. I must have my revenge!" It strikes again for 29. "Not today," it says before crumpling to the ground. Gaxim turns on the last revenant and reduces it to 2 HP. An unattributed voice declares, "Bravo! Now, I must practice some 13th-dimensional chess as Monsieur KP urged at his castle! Let me see." Gaxim howls in outrage. "Fine, I will cast a zimple magic missile!" The children are in hysterics until Ezmerelda calms them, explaining that these are not monsters but creatures of vengeance.

A few more miles bring the heroes to the gates of Vallaki, where the two guards who made the deal for the B&B look grouchy and grumpy. Asked how business is, they answer: "Lot of interest, but folks are worried about their personal safety. One thing that would help is if you were to not cause any more destruction to the town. The folks in Vallaki are a little bit spooked every time you folks arrive because something terrible happens. And we get a new mayor." Gaxim wonders aloud if they would like another mayor. "No, he's good. We like him." Gaxim sidles up conspiratorially — "really? You can tell truth to friend Gaxim!" — and gets only "Nah, he's good." On who might be next: "It's one of those funny things. When the mayor dies, and a new mayor shows up, it's never predictable who the new mayor is. It's interesting. It's a part of the charm of Vallaki.  And they always, always, end up working for Strahd." The guards shrug and offer work with "Butterfield and Robinson tour operators", then let them in.

At St. Andral's orphanage, Ms. Belasco is woken and smiles brightly at the heroes: "Why can't somebody deposit orphan children at a reasonable hour?  It's the heroes. The ones that saved the orphanage for good! I'm so sorry to hear about your church, cleric." The chapter then has her doubt them ("Maybe you're not the heroes."), pull out some garlic and shove it in their faces; Merrin takes a bite. Milivoj shouts from inside, "Don't forget the mirror test." She points the mirror at the heroes, verifies four reflections, looks right and left, and says "the scariest words in Barovia, 'Come in.'" The heroes are told, "Do you not understand that in Strahd's realm, you do not just knock on the door in the middle of the night?" Merrin apologizes: "so sorry, but these children need a place to sleep for the night." The answer offers the heroes room as well: "I will make some space in the attic." In the morning, the orphans are excited to see their old friends, though they keep their distance from Slerdax.

At the Blue Water Inn, with the Martikov children in tow, the heroes find Stefania cleaning and trying to get two drunks to leave. She stares grouchily until she notices the children — "My nephews!" — and kisses and hugs them in tears. "The Martikov's are in your debt. Again." Gaxim drops pelts on the ground: "You really did get them," she says, slightly grossed out. "They will come this evening." She pours Gaxim some Purple Grapemash and sends the boys for food: "Brom and Bray, go get these heroes some food!" Her husband cries from the backroom, "oh, Stefania, I wish to sleep!" She answers, "The two boys have been returned. They are heroes. They are heroes of the keepers of the feather. You must feed them, Dag!" Dag screams at his children and nephews to prepare the kitchen, and a good hearty breakfast is served.

Karl and Nikolai Wachter enter and greet the heroes as "the people who gave me my inheritance! It's doing fantastic!" Karl asks Merrin conspiratorially, "but I thought the mayor did not want you in the town." The reply: "That's true. That's his problem? Not ours." Stefania grunts from the back that the heroes "have brought some orphans from the werewolves.  Even this mayor, this piece of shit, has to say, okay!" Later Szoldar and his friend Yevgeni arrive and see the pelts: "Fantastic! You have done an excellent job." When Stefania overhears that the heroes have money, she offers what the Keepers of the Feather have to sell, but warns that "once I show a list, the items that you do not select will be gone, and you will never be able to buy them." The enchantment that hides their treasures from Strahd "is also cursed." The adventurers, pleased, buy powerful magic.
