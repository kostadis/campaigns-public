# Chapter 1: A curious job

Date: Days 1–2 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The last several years have been "exceedingly kind to the city of Fax." Since the drow overlords were defeated and Lundbeams and Tolub rededicated themselves to the town and the Wild Coast, the region has boomed. The streets are clean and well-lit at night, and "the old way of balancing the books via pillaging has been replaced with a more equitable taxation model." Beneath that veneer the city's criminal element prospers, and none more than the owners of the King's Coin, "a well-known establishment for adventurers to find odd jobs of dubious legality."

Over the past few weeks, flyers have been distributed seeking adventurers willing to take on an odd job or two for high pay. Wukong, Slerdax, and the Paladin Marron each obtained a copy and arrived independently. Wukong arrives first and shows his flyer to Gaxim, who sends him to the bar; the bartender sends him to a table in the back of the room. A few minutes later the Paladin sits with the rogue, and finally Slerdax is pointed to the same table. A well-dressed corpulent man seated there motions to an empty seat; when Slerdax tries to ask questions, he demands silence and says the job can only go to a group of four.

Romun, a male drow and co-owner of the King's Coin with his brother Remun, is concerned with the sudden surge of adventure activity this mysterious gentleman is generating. Remun tells Slerdax, "this is the fifth group of adventurers that have sat at that table." Romun asks Gaxim — "a powerful dimwitted half-orc" whose role is "that of bouncer not of a spy" — to go to the table and make sure no unreasonable activity is going on.

Drog Halftooh, a half-orc, sees Gaxim and invites him to sit: "I think there is some coin in this job. It involves violence, not brains. It is well suited for you." Gaxim sits: "money good." The pitch follows. The "secret society of the knife, the butter, and the brioche" is looking for information about a magic user who has gone missing, and is "spreading coins to anyone who will pursue any of their harebrained leads." The society believes some "inter-planar wolves" may be responsible for the disappearance of the gnome. The plan: "go there, kill a wolf, and say that you think you saw a portal." The half-orc tells the bouncer, "this job is even simple enough for you, Gaxim," and tells the magic-user, "bring me some blood, and I will arrange for everything else."

The magic-user introduces himself: "my name is Slerdax, and I am Genasi." An exchange about a Genasi friend ends with Drog offended — "Friendship is not a two-way street" — and asking whether Slerdax is interested. Slerdax nods. Asked why he isn't asking about the money, Gaxim answers, "Gaxim kill," and Slerdax shrugs, "Honestly, I just want to kill someone." Drog asks, "You sure you are a mage?"

The aasimar Paladin is "not sure I am comfortable with the idea of us lying about our findings." Wukong asks her to "suck up your Paladin integrity so we could make some easy money," and Marron concedes that she is short of money, and that "the Knights of the knife the butter in the brioche are always looking for new members." Slerdax asks why the society does not do the deed itself; the answer is that they are desperate to find him quickly, he "has some information that they need," and when a lead is useful "they send their top-guys." Drog shows them the money, 50GP. Gaxim tries to take it — "I take it." — and the half-orc businessman sighs, "no, Gaxim. You have to kill first." The mage uses the distraction to try to steal the coin, but four very lethal-looking associates of the businessman approach: "those tricks may have worked in school mage. But here they will not." Gaxim walks out: "Boss, I leave early. Go, Kill." The pale-white Drow looks even paler: "whatever Mr. Drog wants, Mr. Drog gets!"

The heroes leave Fax, a city cleaned up "since the days of the previous Mayor," with lights on every corner and swept roads — but "the rot of Fax is profound, and always there," and the heroes have found that rot and are trying to profit. Leaving after sunset, they reach woods near Fax. Wukong sums it up: "Easiest 50GP of gold." Gaxim, not smart but possessed of "cunning wisdom", feels something is off; the others ignore him.

The woods are quiet but for the howl of the wolves. A low mist gathers through the night, and by morning the fog turns the trees into "gray ghosts." Gaxim notices, "Trees change." Black pools stand like dark mirrors in the muddy roadway. When Gaxim heads for the forest, the rogue stops him. Slerdax casts produces flame into the mist, which absorbs the fire unchanged. He tells Gaxim, "Touch the fog," but Gaxim is too cunning to fall for it. A firebolt also disappears into the mist. "Stay away from the fog." Gaxim nods: "The mage is not dumb."

The road ahead is the only place with no mist, and the fog swallows the road behind them. They reach high stone buttresses, massive rusted iron gates, and two headless statues of armed guardians whose heads lie among the weeds. The gates swing open, screeching, as the heroes approach. Slerdax puts his hands into the mist — "It feels, misty." Gaxim's night vision is obscured. They go through, catch "the scent of death on the air," and follow the gravel road to a village of tall houses "dark as tombstones", closed-up shops, and a shut tavern.

A soft whimpering draws them to a pair of defenseless children in an otherwise lifeless street. The girl tells Slerdax, "There's a monster in our house!" and points to a tall brick row house with a gated portico, its rusty gate slightly ajar, between abandoned, boarded-up houses. They have only heard its "scary howl" — "Maybe it is a wolf." She asks them to save "our baby brother Walter who is on the third floor," and says, "Our parents keep the monster in the basement." Their parents are "called Gustav and Elisabeth"; the little boy adds, "they like us to call them Mr. and Mrs. Durst." Slerdax stares at the children, then at the house, and marches in. The aasimar's other voice surfaces — "Oh good god, what has that Paladin gotten me into this time," says someone who was Marron — while Gaxim has his own plan: "Go to house. Kill wolf. Get money. Drink mead."

Gaxim fails to smash the portico gate; Slerdax simply pushes it open — it was not locked. In the foyer hangs a shield with a coat-of-arms, "a stylized golden windmill on a red field", flanked by portraits of stony-faced aristocrats. Gaxim takes the shield. Slerdax sees a familial resemblance to the children: "Story checks out." In the main hall, with its longsword above the fireplace and carved panels, Slerdax notices "serpents and skulls inconspicuously woven into the wall designs," and finds no secret doors.

Gaxim charges into the dining room while Wukong waits for screams before following. Gaxim takes the silverware and plates — "Treasure" — while Slerdax finds "twisted faces carved into the tree trunks and wolves lurking amid the carved foliage," and sees that the mist has rolled up to the walls of the house. Wukong calls Gaxim "Grax", is corrected, and steers him toward the kitchen; to the cleric, he observes, "ah, we have a meat shield." The pantry's food appears fresh but tastes bland, and there is no mead. Wukong finds a dumbwaiter. Slerdax casts find familiar and summons a spider; Wukong stops Gaxim from killing it — "no, no, it's a pet!" The spider is "a little bit off" in its eyes, fangs, and size.

The spider rides the dumbwaiter up into the servant's room. Slerdax notices the first and second floors are free of dust and signs of age. Through the spider he presses a button, and Wukong hears a ding in the kitchen. In the upstairs hallway hangs a portrait of the Durst family: what Slerdax surmises are Gustav and Elisabeth Durst with their two smiling children, Rose and Thorn, who have "pink, not grey skin." The father cradles a swaddled baby, "which the mother regards with a hint of scorn." Suits of armor with wolf-headed helms flank the doors, and the carved "dancing youths" are actually fighting off swarms of bats.

Downstairs, the party enters a hunter's den with a stag's head and three stuffed wolves. Wukong picks the locked east cabinet and finds a heavy crossbow, a light crossbow, a hand crossbow, and 20 bolts for each; the north cabinet holds playing cards and wine glasses. In the cloakroom Wukong takes the black top hat and a cloak — "It's pretty." — while Gaxim insists, "Gaxim kill wolf, down!" With no way down, they consider asking the children.

The mist has rolled up to the gates and the road is no longer visible. Gaxim charges into it and five minutes later reappears walking toward the entrance: "Gaxim, walk straight." "The mists do not want our heroes to leave."

Upstairs, past the servants' quarters and a closet of servants' uniforms, the party reaches a study with a mahogany desk, a picture of a windmill atop a rocky crag, and floor-to-ceiling bookshelves. While tabulating the value of the books, Slerdax finds a fake book that opens a secret door. Wukong pitches a gang: "he can be the brawns, I am the face, and you can be the brains!" — with Marron to be "left alone." The secret room holds tomes on fiend-summoning and on the necromantic rituals of a cult called the Priests of Osybus; after an hour, Slerdax infers the rituals are bogus. A skeleton in leather armor sticks out of a clawed-foot chest — a human who triggered a poisoned dart trap. "Tsk. Tsk." "You should have been more careful."

In the dead man's hands is a sealed letter, signed by Strahd von Zarovich, addressed to "My most pathetic servant." It denies being "a messiah sent to you by the Dark Powers of this land," calls its recipients "but worms writhing in my earth," and recounts that the servant "abandoned love for madness, took solace in the bosom of another woman, and sired a stillborn son." It ends, "I much prefer you as you are." The chest holds three blank books, three spell scrolls, the deed to the house, the deed to a windmill, and a will signed by Gustav and Elisabeth Durst leaving everything to Rosavalda and Thornboldt Durst in the event of their parents' deaths. The door closes behind Wukong, who screams for help; Gaxim starts to smash it, and Slerdax opens it with the fake book's mechanism.

The conservatory's alabaster dancers include "carvings of well-dressed skeletons." Still unable to find a way down, the party goes up to a dusty balcony where a suit of black plate armor animates and attacks Gaxim. Gaxim rages for 28 HP of damage; Slerdax's flame does 1 HP; the barbarian hits again: "Gaxim Kill!" The heroes enter the dusty master bedroom, with its silver jewelry box, a dust-covered portrait of Gustav and Elisabeth Durst, and a closet behind a mirrored door that proves empty. Gaxim opens the door to the outside balcony: the strange mist rises around the terrace "into the sky as high as the eye can see."
