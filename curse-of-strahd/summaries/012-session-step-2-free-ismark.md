# Chapter 12: Step 2 free Ismark

Date: Day 9 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
Standing in the hallway, the party reminds itself of the first part of Danika’s master plan: rescue Ireena. Gaxim wastes no time and heads into Izek’s room, where Ireena is held. He tries to tear the ropes, and Ireena screams in pain. Someone asks, "So, Ireena?", and she notices Wukong's disfigurement: "The dark powers have found a willing ally, the rogue Wukong." The reply comes back, "Are you sure you want to have this conversation right now as I am the only thing between you and escape?" Gaxim pulls on the rope again, Ireena screams again, and Gaxim is confused. Wukong sighs, walks up and cuts her free. In her Barovian accent she declares, "Izek is a terrible man. He is a monster!" Gaxim insists, "Izek, Brother!", and the exchange that follows — "No, he’s not your brother! He’s mine! He’s my twin!" / "No, my brother!" — leaves the question unsettled.

With Ireena freed, Gaxim turns to Udo, who is imprisoned, and makes quick work of his chains, shattering them. With all the prisoners free, Ireena asks, "Wukong, where are we to go next? How do we escape?" The answer is, "first, we will go to the attic, and then we will escape." She tries to argue, but Gaxim wants to do what his friend Wukong wants, and the rogue wants the attic. She sighs and glares: "I thought this was a rescue, not a robbery."

Gaxim climbs first, from the master bedroom into the attic: a dusty, twenty-foot-square room under a high-pitched ceiling, its rafters shrouded in cobwebs, empty except for an old table with a lantern. Through the door in the south wall lies the larger attic, full of old, forgotten things draped in white sheets, with a clear footpath through the maze. Slerdax scans it and perceives nothing of any value. Meanwhile Gaxim charges to the door; Merrin tries to warn him, but too late, and a glyph of warding hurts him for 23 HP of damage as he touches it. Gaxim is furious and wants to hurt the door.

Beyond it is a dusty, lamplit study made from old, mismatched furniture: tables strewn with parchment covered in strange diagrams, a freestanding bookshelf holding a collection of bones, a skeletal cat lounging on a pine box and several more skulking about, and — most unnerving of all — three small children standing with their backs turned in the northeast corner. On a stool in the centre sits a thin young man with a premature streak of gray in his dark hair, cradling an open leather-bound book. "Gaxim say door evil!" Gaxim tells him. In a high pitched tone of voice, the young man waves them off: "this is my room. You are not welcome here." When Merrin asks the way out, he answers "yes, downstairs", then recognises Ireena and Udo out of his cell: "This doesn’t seem right to me." Then he pauses: "Wait, you said you wanted to escape? I have just the plan for you."

Asked who he is, he answers, "Victor Vallakovich, the Baron’s son." Before anyone can respond he pulls up the carpet in the corner to show a circle inscribed in the ground: "I have been working on a teleportation circle. It will teleport you where you want. I haven’t quite figured out how to get out of Barovia but anywhere else." He rubs his hands in glee. Merrin suggests Slerdax take a look ("Hmmm, this is a little bit sketchy. Is this some kind of magic?"), and Victor answers, "Of course, it is! I’ve been working on this for some time. I am studying magic!" Merrin suggests experimenting with the cats; Victor argues that they are his pets. Slerdax sees that the circle is deeply flawed: anyone stepping into it and being teleported would be destroyed. Watching the eagerness and stress in Victor's voice, he concludes the aspiring mage knew it didn't work and hoped to use the heroes as test subjects. As the party heads out, Victor shouts, "so you’re not going to use my circle?" Slerdax answers, "it’s a death trap." Victor sighs, "well, there are always some more maids I can experiment on." The party realises that Victor, not Lady Fiona Wachter, was responsible for the missing maids, and Wukong privately decides that Victor perhaps belongs on the kill list — not now, but later.

The heroes climb down the rope from the second floor and stand outside the Baron’s house. "We must go and free Ismark." Wukong answers Ireena, "first we must look at the plan" — and the next step is to free Ismark at the Festival. "To the festival," Wukong agrees. Gaxim mumbles, "The Festival?" and Udo asks, "Are you new to the Vallaki?" Slerdax admits, "I was distracted and have no idea what I was being asked. Can you repeat the question?" Udo explains that the Baron holds a festival every week to encourage folks to smile; attendance and smiling are both mandatory, and sometimes prisoners are executed to encourage everyone to smile more.

Udo announces he will go to the church and begs Ireena to follow: "the church is the only safe place in Vallaki. No vampire has ever entered the church." Gaxim sagely shakes his head: "No bones in church". Udo panics — "what do you mean the bones are gone?" — and cannot accept it: "They are outsiders and know nothing. St Andral will protect us from the vampires!" When Ireena asks "what should I do?", Merrin steps forward: "you should stay with me." Ireena looks at Slerdax and Wukong with fear and disgust and decides, "I shall stay with the good cleric." Gaxim adds, "And Gaxim!" One voice protests, "Honestly, I don’t understand why people keep freaking out when they see me. And when they see Slerdax. It’s just a skin condition that randomly appeared.", and Slerdax asks, "I mean, why do you have to be making fun of me?"

In the main square, where the festival of the blazing sun is to take place, Ismark is bound in stocks on a raised platform, and beside him Izek seems particularly excited about the prospect of killing him. Under threatening skies, unhappy children dressed as flowers lead a procession of sorry-looking men and women carrying a ten-foot-diameter wicker ball, followed on horseback by the Burgomaster and his smiling wife with a sad bouquet of wilting flowers. The ball is hung from a fifteen-foot-high wooden scaffold and splashed with oil, while the townsfolk wear the fakest of smiles. Merrin scans the grounds for any lawbreaker. Before the wicker sun can be lit, the sky tears open in a downpour; "All will be well!" cries the Burgomaster, whose torch goes out as he thrusts it into the sphere.

Gaxim marches towards the platform to free Ismark. Merrin notices that the Burgomaster is not smiling and asks him, "why are you not smiling?", deeply concerned that he is not obeying his own laws in the middle of the crisis. Izek is enraged that the half-orc is coming to free Ismark, and Slerdax and Wukong hang at the fringes of the square with Ireena. Wukong counts 8 armed guards around the platform and thinks, "This is going to end in a bloodbath". Izek sees Ireena and the Aasimar and is enraged: his plans to capture his long lost sister are in tatters. Then a singular laugh erupts from the crowd. "arrest that man!" orders the Burgomaster, and several guards leave the platform for Lars, the man who was laughing.

Gaxim pushes at the guards — "Izek, my brother!" — and Izek shouts back, "He is not my brother! He is a buffoon." Gaxim is upset; his brother hates him. One guard's spear misses him, and Izek points at Ireena: "go get her, you fools!" Two guards move through the crowd towards her, and another guard hurts Gaxim for 5 HP. Merrin intimidates the Baron — "why aren’t you smiling!" — and he quakes: "what is going on, Izek? Help me!!" Seeing the angelic-looking Aasimar judging the Burgomaster by his own laws, the citizens turn on the Baron: "Arrest him!"

Slerdax is about to rush in, but Wukong, worried about losing his meat shield, stops him: "Slerdax, don’t go anywhere. We need to protect Ireena." Slerdax is confused, and the next appeal follows: "Don’t you have some kind of ranged spell? Or can you only make people laugh?" Slerdax considers thunderwave but fears collateral damage, and instead uses a suggestion spell on a guard charging them, convincing him to arrest the Baron for not smiling. The guard shouts, "MY FELLOW GUARDS! The Baron has broken the only rule that protects us from the Devil! Arrest him, and perhaps we can still be safe!" "That was good, Slerdax," Wukong says. Ireena pleads, "Kill Izek! He will kill Ismark out of spite". Wukong weighs it — "I don’t like the plan, but I will go with it." — and his arrow does a measly 5 HP of damage. Izek stares at Wukong: "To me! Protect the Baron! KILL them!" The guards rally to protect the Baron, as Wukong predicted, but the citizens are in open revolt; no more guards can come, and those in the square try to make a safe area around the platform for the Baron and Lady Lydia, while the Baron cries, "ALL IS WELL! ALL IS WELL!"

Within the aasimar, Marron screams to be let loose to avenge the law; to Marron, Merrin’s pathetic whimpering is not how the law is preserved. Merrin stays in control, for the moment. Izek hurls flame at Gaxim for 14 HP of damage, and Gaxim, tears in his eyes, marches on. Lady Lydia cries out, "stop this insanity! The Devil will come!" Gaxim disengages, scales the platform and strikes the rope binding Ismark with his hand-ax. A guard tells Merrin, "back away, you interloper and scoundrel!"; she answers, "I am an angelic being!", and he retorts, "You are a blackguard and a traitor to the lawful law of the land." "FREE ME!" screams Marron in Merrin’s head. The citizens take her side — "NO! She is a hero! She is protecting us from the man who imposes his own laws and can’t obey them!" — and the charmed guard screams, "Seize the Baron, he has betrayed the city with his scowls!" Willemina, Udo’s mother, pierces through the din: "My son was arrested by that man, and his house taken, and he was beaten! How many more have suffered!" Chaos erupts, and the guards form a protective circle around the Baron and Merrin.

Merrin says, "No!", but the Dark Powers intervene: the white feathers molt and the dark feathers of Marron appear. Marron casts a necrotic shroud, frightening several guards, and the Baron and Lady Lydia scramble onto the platform; Marron deliberately chooses not to attack any guard. Slerdax webs several of the fleeing guards. Wukong's arrows at Izek miss, and he wonders what would happen if the Baron — the mayor, the source of Izek's authority — were dead. Izek drops Gaxim to 0 HP. The charmed guard keeps screaming, "Citizens! Guards! Arrest the Baron!" Marron kills the guard who attacked, and Slerdax misses Izek. Wukong reasons that the guards may not fight if the Baron is dead, that no one likes Izek, that the Baron is easier to kill — and that if it was the wrong thing to do, someone could raise the Baron from the dead. Before Ireena can stop him, he fires at the Baron and kills him.

Lady Lydia is shocked and starts screaming. The citizens cheer, "The Baron is dead! We ARE FREE!" Izek, furious, kills Lady Lydia to satiate his blood lust and then attacks Marron, while the guards all shout, "DOWN WITH THE BARON!" Wukong’s gamble pays off. Marron lays hands-on Gaxim, bringing him back from the dead. Wukong explains to Ireena and Slerdax, "I knew this was the right play," comparing it to a boss from his previous employment who relied on a very dangerous monster as his muscle: "Turns out everyone hated the monster, and the boss, so when – someone – killed the boss, everyone turned on the monster." Wukong hits Izek with an arrow, and Izek hits Marron for 13 HP of damage.

Gaxim — confused, sad, and upset — frees Ismark, who grabs a sword. Marron frightens Izek with another spell, and Izek tries to flee. Ismark and Marron hit him, and Gaxim, who doesn’t want to hurt his long lost brother, grapples him and knocks him prone. The crowd cheers, "death to Izek! Death to the Baron! FREE VALLAKI!" The mob now rules the city, and in the distance the Baron’s house is in flames. Ismark and Marron attack the prone Izek and kill him. The inconsolable Gaxim picks up the body, announces "Bury brother!", and marches towards the church — fortunately step 3 of the master plan: go to the church to find the location of the bones, and bring Ireena there so that the Keepers of the Feather can protect her. Ireena tries to console him, but Ismark is too distraught.
