# Chapter 14: All Hail the Lord Mayor Urwin

Date: Days 9–10 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
Danika's plan worked: Ireena was kept from Strahd, "but at what cost." Father Lucian's throat was snapped, Udo's body was drained of blood, Ismark was gutted by claw marks, and many of the Keepers of the Feather are dead. Danika, at death's door, says, "the plan worked." Outside, a furious Strahd bends at one knee, scribbles something on a piece of paper, seals the envelope, and leaves it at the church's door. Merrin observes to no one in particular, "this church is in need of a priest and of a god."

Rictavio, who has sauntered in, remarks, "you got a note, an invitation from the old man." He takes the note and tells Merrin that "it is my learned experience that Strahd is a man of his word. If you promise is that you will go and come back, then you will go and come back." He calls it "a very odd request" and stares intently at Ireena: "There is some connection, with," he says, "her!" Everyone still alive in the church just stares. Rictavio shrugs, drops the note, and says, "If you are to face Strahd, you need the Sunsword. And I need to know if the Vistani are followers of Strahd or not." Urwin jumps in, "perhaps not tonight?" The answer comes back, "Of course not. I, for one, plan to spend the night in here. It is the only safe place in all of Barovia." "The temple of Heironeous is always welcoming to fighters of evil!"

Morning rises over Vallaki, "where the fires of revolution and rage have subsided." Merrin prepares to sanctify the temple to Heironeous, and there is the small matter of the new Mayor. Wukong remarks, "A complete take-over!" The captain of the guards arrives at the church asking for an aasimar with black wings. Merrin shrugs: "mine are white." Urwin, chasing the captain, explains that the captain kept telling him "the story of the white as aasimar that dropped all of its wings and turned into a black aasimar." Merrin is very confused; inside her skull, Marron laughs hard. The captain accuses Merrin — "You attacked my guards!" — and is answered, "They attacked me. They were rogue guards." Slerdax turns it back on him: "YOU are the guards that let the lawful burgomaster die!"

The captain grumbles and walks away, then turns around: "we have a problem." The reply — "No, we don't. We have been smiling continuously!" — makes him snap. "can you drop it with the smiles? They never worked." The smiles, he says, "made that spineless twit the Baron feel like he had agency. No one in Barovia has agency." He lays out what went wrong: "The plan was for you to kill Izek, not the Mayor." Izek saw the plan and killed Lady Lydia after the party killed the Baron. Victor would have been made Mayor, but he was killed in the riots after his "ghastly experiments with the teleportation circle were revealed," and the fathers of the maids that were killed led a mob. "It was a mess."

Merrin blurts, "So you need us to nominate a new mayor?" Slerdax asks, "Why not have an election?" The captain stares incredulously. Urwin intervenes: he is "all for an election, but we need a transition government." The town is split into factions; Lady Fiona's book club, close to Strahd, is a significant voting block; there are still vampires that "may or may not be in the city," and the city needs "a strong, firm hand that can handle the chaos." Merrin tells Urwin he will be one candidate. Urwin protests that the guard would gladly support him in a transitional role, and the Cleric interrupts: "What was the process for replacing Burgomasters? What legal procedures were used?" Urwin admits, "we never really had an electoral process, per-se." Burgomasters were killed or exiled — "a revolt or a coup-d'etat or an assassination or a forced exile…"

Marron cannot resist and shouts in Merrin's head, "they understand how power works, the need to use violence." Under Marron's influence, the idea of combat as the way to pick a new leader suddenly makes sense: "Trial by single combat. Perfect." The response: "Yes, trial by combat! Perhaps more trial by wits! Wits to death. I won because Wachter and the Baron are dead." Slerdax asks, "not even a duel?" Urwin whispers to Merrin, "can you turn into the black thing?" and gets a flat "No." Urwin turns to the guard and informs him that he is to be the transitional Mayor. "Congratulations, Lord Mayor! May your rule be long and fruitful." The party is told, "As long as the mayor of Vallaki, you are free, my heroes, to come through this city," and Merrin answers, "And you are always welcome to my church." The church of St. Andral's has become the temple of Heironeous. Milivoj and Yeska arrange to have their old jobs back. As he is about to leave, one of them fumbles for a title — "your, um?" — and is supplied "Eminence"; the dead need burying and the burials sanctifying, and Merrin agrees. Then Rictavio shows up and reminds them of their promise.

At mid-day the party goes to the camp: a grass-covered hill with low houses built into its sides, elegantly carved woodwork and decorative lanterns, and atop the hill a ring of barrel-topped wagons around a large tent with smoke pouring out of the top, smelling of wine and horses. Slerdax wants to create an illusion of Strahd to flush out any supporters of the Devil; Wukong gives the thumbs up, Merrin isn't thrilled, and since it would be impossible to constrain Gaxim the idea is dropped. A grim, gray-cloaked figure stands in front of one of the houses; he is not Vistani, because he is an elf. Slerdax proposes sending a familiar to look into the tent. Empty wine casks are piled outside; inside, through the smoke of three sputtering campfires, six Vistani lie passed out, and a barely conscious, shirtless teenager hugs the central tent pole, wrists bound and back streaked with blood, while an older, larger man in studded leather lashes him with a horsewhip. A third man in studded leather stands in the bigger man's shadow: "Easy, brother," he says. "I think Alexei has learned his lesson."

Merrin reflects that they have learned everything there is to know: they are evil. Marron objects that whipping an evil person is not evil. Unable to make up its mind, the party asks the elf for information instead. He says, "go to the house of Kasimir. If he thinks you are to be trusted, he will tell you much." The rest — "He does not. He will kill you." — the elf mutters under his breath, and the heroes do not hear it. Asked by Slerdax whether he is in danger, he says, "The dusk elves are always in danger in Barovia. But for more, you must ask Kasimir."

At Kasimir's house three sullen, gray-cloaked figures stand in the lantern light. Slerdax proposes "Information? Treasure?" and they are sent to the back room: "Kasimir will decide." The elf there, presumably Kasimir, has had his ears cut off; he looks "old, grief-stricken, and proud." Slerdax notices his name on a piece of paper on the desk, senses a peculiar connection between Kasimir and the land, and surmises that Kasimir disdains the Vistani — every time he looks towards the encampment, he flinches a little. The mage asks whether he is a Vistani. "No. I am an old friend!" The idea of being friend to these disgusts Kasimir, who spits on the ground: "these Vistani disgust me. They are not like my friend Velikov. He was a real Vistani. A man of honor and of dignity, and not Allied to that monster Strahd." They are "wretched allies of the Devil. He trapped us all here."

When the party says they came to "trap some wolves and collect some treasure," Kasimir laughs and tells the story of the Dusk Elves. They were enemies of Strahd in Barovia, "a foolish thing"; the Devil surrounded them in a final battle and, instead of killing them, trapped them here. "But that is not when our nightmare began." His sister Patrina Velikovna was a concubine of Strahd; for that disgrace to the tribe, he stoned her. Strahd, furious at being deprived of his bride, ordered his minions to butcher all the dusk elf women and children, and "So all would know who was to blame my ears were cut off." Slerdax says they plan to kill Strahd — "It's inevitable." Kasimir proposes they help each other. Patrina "now speaks to me in dreams," claiming that guilt and regret have cleansed her soul. Merrin is disturbed — "I disagree." — while Marron screams, "I agree!" Kasimir: "She lies. The Devil has corrupted her soul beyond redemption. I want to see the Devil dead so she may be rescued from eternal damnation. We are allies in this."

Patrina has given him a clue, but first the party must promise not to hurt these Vistani. The dusk elves have been "trapped here for 400 years," cursed to be the servants of the Vistani and to live as long as they live. He rambles about Velikov, now calling him his brother and "the best of friends." Merrin and Slerdax, realizing that Rictavio will hurt the Vistani, are unsure how to proceed. Kasimir, sensing this, says, "YOU must not do anything. What anyone else does is not your business." Slerdax says, "we won't start anything." It is good enough. Patrina has told him "that there is an Amber Temple, an ancient vault hidden in the Barovian mountains," where Strahd forged his pact with evil powers and discovered how to become a vampire. Merrin and Slerdax both interrupt to ask about Strahd's origin, and Kasimir, miffed, says, "the true story is that we know there was a pact, and nothing else. All I know is that the secret to breaking Strahd's pact and freeing Barovia is there." He asks, "Will you help me find the temple?" As the party leaves, he adds, "I know the vampire killer van Richten wants to know if the Vistani are allied with Strahd," and, with a sigh, "tell him they are."

With a new quest and a new ally, the heroes head to Rictavio's home in the Arasek Stockyard in Vallaki, where a sturdy carnival wagon with peeling paint bears the faded words "Rictavio's Carnival of Wonders." Inside is Rictavio, "And the nature of Madam Eva's fortune becomes clear." Smiling, he tells them — "His unquenchable thirst for vengeance will now be temporarily quenched." He hands them the Sunsword and reminds them that he will pick it up if they fail to kill Strahd. Merrin picks up the sword and gets the impression that it wants revenge on Strahd "for losing its hilt." Rictavio: "I hope you find the other items and defeat him. Or at least wound him. The Baron will have his revenge for my son!"

Back in Vallaki, the heroes eat at the Blue Water Inn and debate what to do next. Danika pleads, "Go get our wine! A revolution with no wine is dangerous to the Mayor!" They agree. First they shop at Thimdul’s Armaments, run by Piersym Rowley, who supplies the guards and, having found the party to be the heroes that removed the hated Vargas, offers them a significant discount. Slerdax asks about other stores, and Danika names an Apothecary owned by Anya Trevali, "a half-elven Dusk Elf"; a General store, "The Dusklight Supply Shop," owned by Bethel; and a Tailor Shop, the "Beast’s Rose" run by Gilli and his wife, Lottie.

The party heads east toward the winery. At an X intersection with a snapped signpost, they determine that Krezk and the Tsolenka Pass lie to the southwest, Lake Baratok to the northwest, Vallaki and Ravenloft to the northeast, and Berez to the southeast. At a second intersection a weatherworn three-armed signpost points north to Krezk, east to Vallaki, and southwest to The Wizard of Wines. The road becomes a muddy trail into a mist-shrouded meadow, where a signpost reads "Vineyard." In a light drizzle the trail skirts a sprawling vineyard, fog taking "ghostly forms" between the rows, and a man in a dark cloak and cowl at the edge of the trees beckons them.

There they meet the Martikov clan: Davian, the patriarch; Adrian, his eldest son; Elvir, the youngest; Stefania, his daughter; and Dag Tomescu, her husband. Davian knows what has happened in Vallaki and is pleased that his son is now Mayor; Adrian is unimpressed with his younger brother, whom he believes fled the winery because he could not do the work. Davian is pleased that Urwin has sent the heroes to help save the winery, which "some evil Druids have seized control of." Gaxim and Wukong wonder about wine; Adrian confirms "three barrels in the loading dock, plus another three in the cellar," with the rest fermenting. The party approaches the winery — an old, two-story, ivy-covered stone building with iron fencing along its roofline, a stable on the east side, and a crumbling well and outhouse to the west. As they reach it, dead vines rustle all around, and "Inhuman shapes emerge from the vineyard, their limbs cracking as they trudge forth through the mist and rain."

The town where the chapter opens and to which the party returns. By morning "the fires of revolution and rage have subsided," and it needs a new Mayor.

The church where the survivors shelter overnight — "the only safe place in all of Barovia," in Rictavio's words. By the chapter's middle, "the church of St. Andral's has become the temple of Heironeous."
