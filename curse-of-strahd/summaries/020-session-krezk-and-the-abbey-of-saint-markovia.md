# Chapter 20: Krezk and the Abbey of Saint Markovia

Date: Day 12 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
With Strahd, Ireena, and Gaxim gone from Yester Hill, the remaining heroes are left to fight the berserkers and druids alone. The large open ground and the enemy's insanity turn the fight into a series of futile charges into fireballs, chromatic orbs, and arrows from Wukong, and when the attackers finally close to weapon range, Marron's armor is "mostly impenetrable." "Yester Hill is, again, calm." The narrator adds that the druids and berserkers are not dead: they await Strahd's summons, because the Dark Powers have constructed "a delightful way to torment Strahd" — in exchange for a glimpse of his old realm, he lets these warriors and druids rampage through Barovia. "But that is a tale for another time."

In the quiet, Marron hears a deep voice carried on the wind: "Long have I waited," it says, "for one who is worthy. My spear hungers for blood. Retrieve it, and rule these mountains in my stead, just like the mighty warriors from the early days of the Whispering Wall." She senses something coming from one of the cairns, approaches it carefully, shrugs, and lifts it. Beneath lie moldy bones and what appears to be a magical spear; the narrator notes that the heroes will later learn it is a blood spear.

With darkness approaching, the heroes head back to the Winery to return the gem they found, and are surprised to find Gaxim there. The Martikovs had spied on the events of Yester Hill in raven form, and with Strahd hot on Gaxim's heel, the Keepers of the Feather jumped into action: the elder Martikov, Davian, told his son Adrian to take Gaxim's horse and ride off, while Gaxim and Ireena hid in the cellar. Davian gives a speech saying he now believes the heroes are the ones the Keepers have been waiting for, destined to free Barovia — and that they need to be elsewhere should Strahd return. During a short rest his wife, Angelika, comes to talk to them: "My worthless husband has finally woken up to the need to take action and not sit on the sidelines. That is good. But we still need the other gem. After you bring Ireena to Krezk, please come back and get the other gem from Baba Lysaga."

With daylight left, the heroes set out for Krezk. At the Raven crossroads (R) a weatherworn signpost points north to Krezk, east to Vallaki, and southwest to The Wizard of Wines. They take the road to Krezk, passing the turn-off from the Old Svalich Road that heads into the mists around Barovia, and climb a rocky escarpment to a gatehouse in a twenty-foot-high stone wall. A bell tolls from a stone abbey clinging to the mountainside high above the settlement — "a welcome change from the deathly silence and oppressive fog to which you have grown accustomed." On the approach Ireena turns to Marron: "Do you think I will be safe here?" / "yes." Then: "And this Abbey will defend me against Strahd." / "Yes."

Four guards in fur hats watch nervously from the parapet. Marron shouts, "We come bringing wine." Gaxim shows the barrel he has carried: "Here barrel! I carried in many places. To Strahd, to the hill with druids, to Winery, to Vallaki and now here." A guard shouts, "call Dmitri." The bourgomaster Dmitri Krezkov arrives — "you are strangers to Barovia." — and when Marron confirms they brought the wine, he smiles ruefully: "You have done my town a great service. I am required by my oath of office and by the natural order as a Barovian noble to bring you into my city." He lets them in and asks for news. Slerdax decides the time has come to reveal all, and after hearing of Strahd, the Druids, and the gems, Dmitri can only say, "Bozhe moi. Bozhe moi."

Wukong notices the Burgomaster is distraught. Marron says, "Smile!" Slerdax asks, "why are you so unhappy?" Dmitri sighs that "the last of my four children died of an illness several days ago, at the age of 14," that given his age they are unlikely to have more, and that this means the end of the Krezkov line — the bloodline that "has kept Krezk safe from the devil and all those who come in and out of Barovia." Marron insists, "try smiling. It's a good technique." The Burgomaster is angered: "You have no children. You do not understand the grief." He is silent the rest of the way, and a coldness settles between him and "the smiling Aasimar."

Beyond the wall, Krezk is a scattering of humble wooden cottages among snow-dusted pines. When Slerdax asks about an Inn, Dmitri explains, "Krezk is a commune. Everyone is expected to do work, and with work, they find a place to sleep. We have chores, and if you do them, you can sleep at my house." Gaxim, fed up, decides to go up to the Abbey; Slerdax touches a few things, decides there is nothing of value, and follows "the good giant." Wukong notices that the path to the Abbey appears unused. Marron thinks now is a good time to kill the vampire: "perhaps Strahd is the new Abbot."

At the Abbey's gates Gaxim pushes the doors open (s6), and two guards rush down from the tower. Otto, a beardless-dwarf-like figure with donkey patches, a wolf's snout, leonine legs and a donkey's tail, mumbles in a donkey's voice: "I am Otto! I am the smartest, wisest, strongest and fastest, most beautiful person I know, and who are you? And why are you here?" Zygfrek, scaled on one side and furred on the other, with a feline eye and cat's-paw hands, snarls, "Why are you looking at me that way? Are you trying to judge me? What are you doing here? What are you here for?" Gaxim offers, "Some pies?" while Slerdax explains they have brought Ireena to the Abbey for safety. The two smile, and Zygfrek declares, "The Abbey will decide. Follow me." Otto takes the pie and starts to eat it.

Studying the creatures, Slerdax remembers the spell book "On the transmutation of persons into beasts and the impacts on society," by "the great Himanth Golious Wellstory" about a wizard called The Sage Moreau, and explains that these are mongrelfolk — humanoids who have undergone, or whose ancestors underwent, horrific magical transformations, their bodies incorporating the features of various beasts, giving them a slow, awkward gait. How they came to be at this Abbey is another mystery.

The heroes pass a graveyard (S7) overlooking the village four hundred feet below, and reach the entrance, where a tarnished copper plaque (S10) reads "The Abbey of Saint Markovia." Otto and Zygfrek bring them inside: "We shall go fetch the Abbot, wait here." In the fog-filled courtyard the guards on the walls turn out to be scarecrows (S11). In the center (S12) stands a stone well with an iron winch; stone sheds with padlocked doors line the perimeter; and chained to one of two posts is a short humanoid with bat wings and spider mandibles. Horrible screams break out from the sheds.

Gaxim goes to examine the well, and Marron uses the Sunsword to light it up. Another mongrelfolk inside — five feet tall and spindly, with three red spider eyes on one side of his face, a frog's foot for a left hand, and a taloned crow's foot — springs up and surprises the barbarian, but is quickly dispatched. Around the courtyard the other mongrelfolk scream, chanting the dead creature's name: "Mishka!"

The Abbot arrives: "A handsome young man in a brown monk's robe" who gently takes "the woman" by her hand, wearing a painted wooden holy symbol depicting the sun, moving "with the grace of a saint." The heroes recall what the Martikovs told them: the Abbey of Saint Markovia, once a convent and hospital, became a madhouse after Saint Markovia and her followers failed to overthrow Strahd; the clergy, isolated and greedy, fought over food and wine until they were killed by each other's hands or driven insane by Strahd's terror. Over a century ago a nameless, strikingly handsome pilgrim from a distant land insisted on reopening it; eternally young, he presides there still, and many villagers suspect "that the Abbot is Strahd in disguise."

"Who are you?" the Abbot asks. "I am Marron. I am a crusader against Evil and Vampires. And I have come seeking power to defeat Strahd." He replies, "You have come late in the day, and you have killed poor Mishka." / "It attacked us." / "That is immaterial. I don't think we can have a proper conversation right now." Asked whether they can stay, with the mongrelfolk screaming through the Abbey, Marron quickly decides, "not tonight." Gaxim, frustrated, asks, "are you good?" The Abbot smiles and shows his symbol of the Morninglord, which Gaxim recognizes from the church in Barovia.

The party presses its case: "This is Ireena. She is not safe anywhere. She needs a safe place. We leave her here." Ireena is dumbstruck: "No!" When Marron explains that Ireena is Strahd's reincarnated girlfriend, the Abbot screams, "No!" — "no, no, no, no, no, no. Ireena is not the true savior of Barovia or Tatyana. That is not the real bride of Strahd. Let me show you who is the real bride." He invites them in, and Merrin whispers to Marron, "come in said the spider to the fly." Gaxim is relieved that finally someone wants them to come in (S13).

Inside, gentle music on a single-stringed instrument trickles down from an unseen master. In the great fifty-foot-square hall, behind a long table, stands a young woman with alabaster skin in a torn and soiled red gown, lost in her own thoughts. "This is the real bride for Strahd. Come here, Vasilka." Gaxim asks, "why Strahd want Ireena?" The Abbot begins, "Ireena is not the real," pauses, squints, and mutters, "this must be some trick of the demon. The Morninglord has spoken to me and said that Vasilka is to be the real bride of Strahd. And if Strahd is to take her, Barovia will be free." Marron recognizes the signs of madness a good person succumbs to when trapped by evil: "This woman is a false creature." Gaxim shrugs, "we leave Ireena, take Vasilka to Strahd." The Abbot answers, "We can't do that yet. She's not ready." Realizing Gaxim does not necessarily have her best interests at heart, Ireena moves closer to Marron. The party concludes that Ireena will not be safe here and that it is not even clear whether the Abbot is an ally.

Lost in thought, the Abbot tells them, "I know how all could be made right, I need a proper bridal gown for Vasilka. With such a gown, Strahd would marry her, and then all of Barovia would be made safe, including your false bride Ireena." Marron suggests, "we know of this wonderful seamstress, Madam Eva. If you were to let us take Vasilka to her, we could get her one." "No need." He scribbles a note with some measurements. Gaxim, not feeling a good vibe, goes back down — for once he thought he had found a house that wanted him in, and it turned out to be evil. Asked "who could provide this dress?", the Abbot answers, "I only speak with the Burgomaster. Ask him."

The heroes arrive once again at Dmitri's house (S3). He smiles and points to an ax and some buckets: Gaxim goes to cut wood, Slerdax takes a bucket to milk some cows, and Marron, with Ireena, heads to the pool (S4) for water.
