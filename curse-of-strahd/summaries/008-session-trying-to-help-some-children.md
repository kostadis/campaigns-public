# Chapter 8: Trying to help some children

Date: Day 8 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The chapter opens in the Baron's study. Izek Strazni turns to the Baron and, with a strong Barovian accent, says "my brother will do as you asked. But perhaps it is wise for Ireena to stay here?" The Baron smiles and agrees: "if Izek says this is the right place for Ireena, then this is the right place. All will be well." Ismark shrugs and whispers something to his friend, but she assures him that there is nothing to fear.

The party now faces too many choices: the Lady Wachter, the hags in the windmill, the Bard in the Inn, the missing shoemaker. None of them is much help in deciding. Gaxim is only interested in what is right in front of him, Merrin and Marron "can't keep track of what's going on because they keep swapping bodies", Wukong has his own agenda, and that leaves Slerdax, "who gets very distracted."

Before they leave, Slerdax, Merrin and Gaxim stare at Izek's arm, trying to divine where it came from. Izek notices, raises the arm in anger, "are you staring at this?", and reveals that his demonic arm is attached to his shoulder. Merrin nods: "I come from the angelic planes, and this gives me pause." The Baron asks whether this is the right time and place for the conversation, but Izek answers anyway: "These foreigners wish to know the truth of Barovia? I will tell them the truth of Barovia. There are dark powers here that reward those that serve." He had lost his arm and had no power, and the dark powers gave him this arm, so that now he has "the power to be the Baron's right arm of justice." The Baron's smile momentarily falters; he explains that the demonic powers "sometimes tempt the just with power", that Izek is one of the just who has been tempted, and that his loyalty "has ensured that all is well." Merrin's eyes open: "I remember that! The demonic powers would do that to tempt the good! Izek must be good. I saw them do that." An uncomfortable silence follows as everyone realizes Merrin is not joking but recounting a personal lived experience.

The Baron's wife whispers to the Baron, and the Baron's expression says the audience is over: "We will take care of Ireena. The time for the hoi polloi to be in the Baron's presence is over." Izek's rage seems to rise as the party refuses to leave, and Gaxim simply gets up and walks out because the place is no longer fun. The party resolves to investigate "the curious case of Udo", who was arrested for "chronic unhappiness" and has disappeared; the arresting officer was Izek. They also hope to learn more about Izek's rage, his demonic arm, and "whether this brotherly re-union is a good thing."

With directions to St. Andral's Church and to the Lady Wachter's house, the party walks into the streets of Vallaki, a city with "that grim look of a forced smile." Just 100 feet past the Baron's residence, Ernst steps out of the shadows: slicked-back hair, the clothes of a parvenu, a large golden ring, a twinkle in his eye, and, most curious of all, tanned skin. "Hey, you. You four, I want to talk to you." Gaxim asks, "You want pie?" Ernst warns them off it — "Don't eat the pie. The pie is bad for you. I think it's made of children, but that's just a story." — and says he works for Lady Fiona Wachter, who noticed their meeting with the Baron. When he raises the arm, Gaxim smiles, "My brother!", and Ernst replies, "I wouldn't trust him. Izek's a psycho." Gaxim gets angry. Someone asks after "the hot broad" ("No, not you, the woman from Barovia. The human, not the Aasimar"), and wonders "Whatever happened to humans? Genasi, Aasimar, Half-Orc." Ernst delivers his invitation: Lady Fiona Wachter "wants to have tea", and "you want to learn the truth behind the Baron's lies?"

Gaxim, enraged, walks away. Merrin stops him: "Hold on, Gaxim. I want to learn more about these demonic powers. I would like to understand more about your brother. Wouldn't you?" The interest in his brother catches Gaxim's attention. Slerdax asks, "Any mead?", and the answer is "Why yes, the best mead in the land. She has her own private stash." The invitation is repeated for tea at Lady Fiona's house, tomorrow, and the party agrees. Merrin explains to Gaxim why this is wise, and he nods, "Brother!" The chapter is blunt about whether he followed: "It's unclear if he understood. Actually, he did not."

At last they reach St. Andral's Church: a slouching, centuries-old stone church with a bulging steeple, cracked stained glass windows of pious saints, and a wrought-iron fence around a garden of gravestones where a thin mist creeps. It is dedicated to the Morninglord and has "the same identical shape as the one in Barovia", because the Morninglord, a god of law and order, sees value in every place of worship having the exact same layout. Inside, Father Lucian is trying to raise the spirits of the desperate. Seeing Merrin, his face lights up: "Ah, a saintly spirit!" Merrin answers "From the mountains of Celestia," and Father Lucian gathers the 10 or so sullen adults and their frightened children: "See, this is a sign from the Morninglord who has not abandoned us!" Gaxim asks "Good priest or bad priest?"; Merrin says "Good," and the Priest welcomes Gaxim as "My friendly giant!"

The Priest introduces Willemina Rikalova, whose son, the shoemaker Udo Lukovich, was taken by "that monster Izek" and imprisoned for speaking out against the Burgomaster. Gaxim's "You bad priest." gets the reply "No, no, my good giant, he burned the house down." Willemina stands with a steely, grim look: "Izek is a soulless monster that burned my house, took my son, and destroyed my family. He will suffer. My son is in the Burgomaster's house. There is great evil in that house." The Priest explains that Udo spoke out against the folly of the Burgomaster, who "thinks that the only reason Strahd has not shown up is that everyone smiles." Asked what happened 101 years ago, Willemina says Strahd goes through periods of activity and inactivity: "He will stay in the castle for 100 years. And then he comes out, tearing the land apart as if he is looking to solve some riddle." The shocked Priest calls this "the ravings of Rudolph van Richten, the Vampire Killer", a legend; Willemina believes he exists, and says that if she knew where he was she "would have already given money to him to kill Strahd." Merrin corrects, "The devil!", and gets "Idle superstition. Barovia is Strahd's domain. He can go where he pleases."

When Slerdax asks why everyone is so unhappy, Father Lucian insists "This is the happiest town in Barovia," and is answered "The saddest Church in the happiest town." In his office he speaks more freely: "Barovia is complicated. Not everyone has a soul." The babies that smile have a soul and the others are "eerily quiet"; "Izek, for example, has no soul." Gaxim perks up — "Brother?" — and Merrin reassures him that something can be done, which Father Lucian seizes on: "yes! Yes! That is why you are here!" Then, lowering his voice: "I thought we were lying to fool the happy giant?" He explains the theory of mists: a finite set of souls in the area, a grown population, and so a shortage. "There is some great evil power here."

In return, he wants a favor. Bones, holy relics, protected the Church, and without them "we have lost everything." Udo may have clues, or it may be his caretaker, Milivoj. The church is "The only safe place that is safe In all of Vallaki" — safe from "The Devil." He does not know whether they are a person's bones. He shows them the crypt, whose door has been shattered by what looks like a shovel; inside they find only evidence that someone with a shovel smashed the door and dug up the bones. Because the crypt door is in the Priest's office, Merrin wonders aloud whether it was an inside job. The Priest paces: "I was the only one who knew about the bones! I was the only one who had the keys!" Then he remembers Yeska, "a foolish boy who works for me in the temple", whom he had calmed by telling him about the bones, and shouts for him.

Merrin worries the boy will be hurt; Slerdax assures her, before Wukong can speak, that this will be a friendly conversation. Merrin has the Priest leave the room, which leaves the boy obviously terrified, and speaks to him in the tone used with a small frightened child. Reassured that no punishment is planned, Yeska confesses: "I told him, I told Milivoj! I didn't mean to." Why? "Milivoj is the caretaker. I thought he knew." Father Lucian tells them Milivoj is at the orphanage.

At the orphanage the matron, a middle-aged woman in grey, plain, sturdy clothes with tired brown eyes, asks "Who are you?" Merrin announces they were sent to bless the children by "Father --- whatshisname?" ("Lucian?" "Why, yes, Lucian!"). The matron is relieved: "Thank goodness, creatures of light! Three of our children have disappeared, and one has died!" Gaxim asks "Souls? Or no souls?", and before the matron can react Merrin sends him outside, where he wanders around looking for the groundskeeper. "Lazlo, Hanz, and Franz have fled, and then Cedric died!" The orphans stream down the stairs and are shooed away with a unison "yes, Ms. Belasco." Merrin leaves Slerdax to talk to the matron while she blesses the children.

Ms. Belasco tells her tale: Cedric was found dead with a locket on his body, impossible to open and covered in demonic art-work. Merrin finds the children bruised and beaten, with no memory of how, only of waking up with the bruises; every night someone wakes up screaming, and Franz and Hanz fled because they were too scared. A young girl of 10 says, near tears, "Milivoj got sick and is dying! I can take you to him!" An adolescent girl, Hedwig, says the rumors are lies: "I know it's because they are jealous of Felix!" Another teenage girl, Ivah, says "ever since Felix came here, things got terrible." Hedwig mocks the story that Felix's family was murdered, his brother killed and his house burned down, and insists "he's just trying to pretend that he wasn't dumped here by some parents that didn't want him."

Slerdax cannot resist casting a minor illusion of a demon, and Merrin casts light on her finger to ward it off. The act draws the attention of a dark power: "Terrorizing children to extract something from them is a selfish, mean, spiteful act." Slerdax's skin becomes cracked and tougher, and he knows that this minor act of selfishness was the cause. The demon did not frighten the children; the Dark Powers Gift did. Merrin leads the children away "from the stranger." Wukong finds Gaxim and brings him in. Ms. Belasco arrives screaming, "You have been TOUCHED BY THE DARK POWERS! WHAT DID HE DO?" When Merrin assures her he is protecting the children, she sobs: "this is how it always begins. You do some minor of an act of evil, they see potential and give you a gift, and before long."

In Milivoj's sparse, dirt-floored bedroom an old shovel lies at the foot of the bed. The sickly young man sleeps fitfully, feverish, with mixed blood bile on his lips and a yellow-tinged, deathly pale pallor. Merrin finds his condition magical and not contagious, and healing magic has no effect. Ms. Belasco pleads, "will you help me with whatever evil is here?", and in her modest office she shares a recent report on Felix's parents: they were indeed murdered, and the killer was never caught.

The locket is a rusted tin circle on an old tin chain with an "X" scratched into the lid. Inside, an inscription in Celestial reads, in Merrin's translation, "Let darkness hide from the gaze of the light." Merrin's light spell does nothing, Gaxim fails to smash it, Slerdax fails to burn it, and "Even radiant damage is ineffective." Ms. Belasco brings two books she has been studying: "Toxic Parenting: The Hurtful Legacy of Corporal Punishment", which she read to understand who might be hurting the children and Felix's strange demeanor, and "Beyond the Morninglord's Sight", a thin primer on demon worship and the basics of Abyssal and Infernal, which Merrin says is full of wrong information. She was trying to translate mysterious runes on the children's beds. "There is some connection to Cedric's death!"

Upstairs, the second-floor hallway has six doors, a ceiling hatch, and a broken, boarded-up window. "He died falling from that window," Ms. Belasco says. On Cedric's bed the party finds runes that say "Dark Dreams", and they go from room to room erasing them while Merrin teaches Ms. Belasco their meaning and how to remove them. In the nursery, among three cribs, a teenage girl hums a lullaby to a baby and another young girl reads on the floor. Ms. Belasco introduces Tessa, shy and overwhelmed: "Cedric was my best friend. He was always cracking jokes. He stole Felix's locket. Felix is weird!" When Merrin asks who Milivoj's friend was, Tessa says "We all were!", and Ms. Belasco protests, "Are you saying that Milivoj brought some evil here? He would never do that!"

Realizing they have not found Felix, the party heads to the dusty, cobwebbed Attic, where a small boy sits in a lone chair by the only window. Merrin tries to talk to him, but Gaxim will not wait and charges. The light dims; dark, putrid smoke pours from Felix's skin, and as the boy slumps forward the shadow takes form, with black claws and a grinning demonic face full of razor-sharp teeth — a semi-corporeal mass of black smoke that bleeds a black, inky, acrid substance. Merrin turns into Marron, who charges. The demon hisses, "Mortal ants; I have seen the shadows of the Great Powers moving through the Dark Seas around this sphere. Your paltry magics are nothing." It swings at Gaxim and misses. Wukong's bow does 5 HP of damage, Slerdax's web fails, and Gaxim, attacking recklessly, does 6 HP of damage, the demon being resistant to non-magical weapons. The chapter ends with the fight under way.
