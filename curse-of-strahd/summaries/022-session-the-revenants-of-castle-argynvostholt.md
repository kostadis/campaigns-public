# Chapter 22: The Revenants of Castle Argynvostholt

Date: Day 13 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
"It is another day in the lands of Barovia." The heroes have come to a castle seeking allies, and so far have found only "the undead and giant spiders." Slerdax decides that fireballs are "a plausible mechanism to exterminate large arachnoid’s", and he is right — the spell is "perfect at very loudly killing living creatures." The noise brings the castle's other residents, who had been quietly praying at the altar, toward the heroes "with swords raised and death in their minds."

Merrin, who "understands only too well that a revenant cannot be killed", warns Wukong to run. Wukong stares at death itself and fires an arrow: "Wukong brave." It is a glancing hit. The chapter steps outside the heroes' knowledge to explain the revenants: they are knights of The Order of the Silver Dragon, who would have been glad to be left in peace, and would have viewed a quiet killing of the spiders as a boon. The fireballs and mayhem instead remind them of Strahd’s final attack on their Order; they take this for another trick by the vampire, and Wukong’s and Slerdax’s appearance "as one of the cursed of Barovia", followed by Wukong's arrow, confirms everything they feared.

The first revenant reaches Slerdax and slashes him twice for 29 points of damage. Gaxim rushes to the door and, with his titanic strength, closes it on the undead, then tells the mage and the rogue, "run!" Merrin advances to the door. Wukong concludes that the good people should fight the good fight and those of questionable morality should run, and shouts, "wait for me, good friend." Slerdax thinks to himself, "Wukong brave." Gaxim and Merrin hold the door against the fallen knights while Slerdax keeps running for the exit.

At the door, Merrin tells Gaxim she has a plan that requires opening it for a moment. The half-orc is uncertain "at what insane plan she has up her wing" but agrees — yet the Sunsword is not so easily persuaded and tells Gaxim not to open the door. Merrin realises the argument is with the weapon, not her ally, and explains to it that she means to cast daylight, which will act as the light they heard about earlier and make the revenants remember their oath. Sergei’s blade, recalling the Knights of the Order of the Silver Dragon — enemies of Strahd, and so friends of the sword — relents and tells Gaxim to open the door. Merrin opens it, casts daylight, and shouts, "remember your oath. We are here to destroy the evil one." The plan has two problems: daylight is not the light they need, and the Knights have decided that their perfect vengeance is for Strahd to stay alive. Merrin adopts a new plan: run. The first revenant paralyzes her, the second paralyzes Gaxim, and the third slashes for 32 points of damage.

Out in front, Slerdax sees a woman with a rapier and a longsword, dressed like a Vistani and sporting a mechanical leg, and thinks, "dammit, another enemy." Instead she introduces herself: "I am Ezmerelda d'Avenir." She says she has been looking for Slerdax and Wukong, that she "was once an ally of the great vampire fighter Rudolph van Richten", and that there are tales he is here and that they are his allies. When they ask her help against the revenants, she is aghast: "you fools! You disturbed the revenants?" Killing them is pointless — "There is no way for them to be permanently killed in Barovia. Every day they will rise from some grave and hunt you down." She offers help now in exchange for an oath to help her defeat the vampire, and Slerdax agrees with a circular argument that ends in "so sure." They tell her of the fiery Dragon that appeared in the fireplace and said that showing the Knights the light would restore their oath. Ezmerelda lays out the plan "as if to a child": "We enter the castle. We kill the revenants. We find the source of the light. We permanently defeat the revenants. We defeat Strahd." Wukong asks whether there is a special way to kill a revenant; she asks if they have a sword and a bow, and when they say yes, answers, "Well, use them." She charges into the castle and Wukong shrugs and follows.

Inside, a revenant fails to penetrate Merrin's armor, but Gaxim is brought to death’s door. Slerdax casts fireball from his perch in the main room ("that is how it is done," Ezmerelda tells Wukong), and Merrin, terrified, still casts healing word on Gaxim. Marron screams at her from within — "you coward. You need me to take care of the messes you get into. Let me out!" — and Merrin refuses: "no, my friends need me, not you. They need a healer, not another sword arm." Merrin hides behind a pillar in the main foyer. Wukong shoots from as far back as he can; Ezmerelda glares at "the coward." The sword pleads with Gaxim to heal himself, fearing its chance at vengeance would go to waste, but Gaxim charges in thinking "save friends" and does 36 points of damage. Slerdax casts haste on himself and a chromatic orb; Merrin casts life transference, bestowing 34 points on Gaxim and taking 17. Wukong kills one of the three revenants with his bow ("Wukong brave."), the hasted mage finishes a second with a critical spell and a magic missile, and Merrin's blood spear, Ezmerelda's rapier and hand ax, Gaxim's light blade (42 points), and Slerdax's chromatic orb and magic missile bring down the last. Ezmerelda rolls her eyes at Wukong's 16 points of damage and wonders whether her former mentor is getting desperate to have allied himself with these characters.

Gaxim asks, "Who are you?" Ezmerelda answers that she is "an apprentice under a one-time ally of the great vampire slayer Rudolph van Richten" and has come to kill Strahd. As a little girl, her family kidnapped van Richten’s teenage son, Erasmus, and delivered him to a vampire: "Even today, years later, I can still hear Erasmus’s pleas for mercy." Van Richten tracked the family down, interrogated her parents rather than harming them, and spared their lives; Ezmerelda witnessed that mercy and was deeply moved. She had seen the party pass her tower and came down to warn them, too late.

Slerdax explores the balconies over the main foyer, with their knight-carved balusters and busts depicting human guises of the Dragon Argynvost. Behind a red velvet curtain he finds an empty alcove whose window looks onto a mausoleum with a marble door engraved "Argynvost" — the name, the heroes remember, of the silver Dragon who created the Order. Merrin looks into a guest room with a hissing hearth. Gaxim says, "Let’s rest," refuses to move to the room with beds ("no, here"), and the heroes rest on the balcony. Slerdax notices the curtain he left open has closed: "It’s Barovia."

On the ruined third floor, a gaunt armored figure slumped on a dragon-carved throne tells them to go away. When Slerdax interrupts its threat, it complains, "I’m not done with my speech. I’ve been practicing it for centuries." It begins again, declaring, "There is no monster I hate more than Strahd von Zarovich," and that Strahd "must suffer eternally in a hell of his own creation". Merrin's question — "so we have to release him from this world and then kill him?" — confuses it; it consults a piece of paper and insists that "Strahd must stay here and be punished eternally in this hell!" Asked what would torture Strahd most, it says Strahd wanted power, fame and recognition, has all of them, "but nobody knows that he exists," and that dark powers have kept the knight alive to ensure Strahd suffers. Its Barovia of old seems to match one of the Wild Coast’s minor kingdoms. Ezmerelda judges, "This creature does not have any information we need. It is too consumed with revenge."

Slerdax passes into a gallery whose stained-glass windows Merrin recognises as Saint Andral, the Morninglord, and Saint Markovia; she is annoyed that her god Heironeous is nowhere to be found. Gaxim enters a study, where a parchment drifts down to his feet; the sword urges the illiterate barbarian to pick it up and to examine the slashed picture above the mantel, which shows the mansion in better days with the top of the chapel tower glowing "like a silver beacon." The sword knows they must go to the chapel’s top. Gaxim waves the paper at Wukong: "read!" It is a letter signed "A" — the silver Dragon's last will and testament — vowing to die "defending that which I love—this valley, this home, and the ideals of the Order of the Silver Dragon," regretting that "my remains will not lie in their rightful place, in the hallowed mausoleum of Argynvostholt," and hoping his spirit will "serve as a beacon of light against the darkness."

Next door, Gaxim ignores the sword's advice to listen first and charges into a war room where skeletal humans in chainmail sit around a table. "Why do you, the living disturb the dead?" Gaxim answers, "we are here to help. Remove curse." Merrin's silver tongue fails, but one knight, moved by the good giant's plea, introduces himself as Sir Godfrey and offers "a tale of woe." After Slerdax's eager questions about seating draw four skeletal hands to their blades, he backs off: "We’ll just stand by the door. That’s fine. The floor’s also good." Godfrey tells how Argynvost, in the guise of Lord Argynvost, built Argynvostholt near the Amber Temple to guard it, founded the Order of the Silver Dragon, and fell when Strahd’s reinforcements overwhelmed the knights; Strahd had the Dragon's corpse stripped to the bone and taken to Castle Ravenloft. Argynvost's death enraged Vladimir Horngaard, who returned as a revenant with other knights under his command. When Strahd became a vampire, the Vistani seer Madam Eva told them Strahd had died only to become a prisoner in his own land, and Vladimir turned the knights to killing anyone who might ease Strahd’s torment. "Our lord must be brought to rest in the mausoleum, and then perhaps my love, Vladimir, will find peace." Then: "I have nothing more to say. And there is nothing more I can do."

On the roof, two spectral warriors in the tower’s turrets attack as Gaxim tries the tower door. Gaxim leaps from parapet to tower and turret, Ezmerelda's firebolt misses ("dammit!"), Merrin's word of radiance kills one warrior — "In the name of Heironeous!" — and Gaxim dispatches the other. Inside, a section of stair collapses under Gaxim; Slerdax uses mold earth to make a stone bridge that proves not well made, and Gaxim pulls Merrin up when she grabs the side. At the tower's peak, among ravens Gaxim tries to talk to, they see Barovia: Vallaki and a lonely windmill to the north and east, a river and marsh to the south, and the Abbey of Saint Markovia to the west. They understand that the light from the painting would shine from here, freeing the revenants. Slerdax's urge to fire a ballista ends with it falling apart. A silver-plated gargoyle shaped like a dragon wyrmling whispers a verse: "The light of Argynvost will beam / And rid this land of gloom." Ezmerelda rages in her head at "les hero et leurs messages secrètes et incomplets!" — she knows they need the Dragon's skeleton.

The heroes weigh their position: pursued by revenants, the way to stop them lies in Strahd’s castle, and "he will know." Slerdax proposes scouting with his familiar, and notes a room of bones in the castle holding one of the three artifacts they need; with the invitation they could walk in and talk to Strahd while he searches in a trance. The sword, through Gaxim, warns, "Strahd know we go." They conclude to use the invitation to enter safely so they need only fight their way out. Slerdax has no more spells, and Ezmerelda insists on a good night's rest and on finishing the exploration first: "Let us finish exploring zis chateaux."

In a circular bedroom beside Godfrey's room, flanked by a brown bear and a dire wolf, the sword determines the bed was shared by two people, which draws a hearty laugh. On the second floor, Gaxim charges into a guest room despite Ezmerelda's plea for caution; she calls him "a person of honeur" and, staring at Wukong, adds "unlike some of you." She teases Wukong as a dressmaker; he answers, "I am not a dressmaker. I do haute-couture," and Gaxim shouts, "An artist!" In the other guest room, a small dragon of ash and smoke bursts from the hissing fireplace, flies upstairs, lands on Vladimir’s throne and disappears; seeing the grouchy knight, the heroes leave.

Heading north between the guest rooms, Slerdax steps into a T-intersection and a stone wall magically appears. The new Gaxim — "who has seen real evil, who found and lost a brother" — reacts in time to end up on the far side with him. Wukong climbs to a tower window, sees four spectral warriors, and decides, "wukong not that brave." Slerdax opens a hole in the wall with mold earth so the others can come through; Gaxim wonders, "Slerdax Brave?" Ezmerelda, now finding Slerdax and Wukong "braver and more honorable than I thought," casts protection from evil on Slerdax. Three more phantom warriors burst through the western door. Slerdax's thunderwave, Ezmerelda's lightning bolt, Merrin's blood spear (each kill sending a surge of power through her), and Gaxim's raging Sunsword — down to 1 and shouting "Not good enough" — destroy them all. In the ruined barracks to the west they find a small wooden coffer of four potions of invulnerability, and Ezmerelda voices what everyone is thinking: "ARGYNVOST! INFORMATION THAT WOULD BE USEFUL! NOT MORE ENEMIES THAT CAN KILL US!"

## NPCs
### Ezmerelda d'Avenir
A woman with a rapier and a longsword, dressed like a Vistani, with a mechanical leg. Once an ally — and, in her own later words, an apprentice under a one-time ally — of Rudolph van Richten, she has come to kill Strahd. She joins the party in exchange for an oath, fights with rapier, hand ax, firebolt, lightning bolt, and protection from evil, and peppers her speech with words like "Merde", "Monsieur", and "zis chateaux". Impatient with riddles and with Wukong, she warms to Slerdax and Wukong after the wall fight.

### The Revenants
Knights of The Order of the Silver Dragon who rose after their Order's destruction. They take the party's fireballs for another attack by Strahd, paralyze Merrin and Gaxim, and are destroyed — though Ezmerelda warns they will rise again.

### The Knight on the Throne (Vladimir)
A gaunt, armored figure on the dragon throne who has practiced his speech "for centuries", hates Strahd, and insists Strahd must suffer eternally. The ash dragon later lands on "Vladimir’s throne."

### Vladimir Horngaard
Per Sir Godfrey, "the greatest of the Dragon’s knights", who returned as a revenant and leads the others; Godfrey calls him "my love".

### Sir Godfrey
A skeletal knight in the war room, moved by Gaxim's plea, who tells the tale of Argynvost and the Order.

### The Sunsword
Sergei’s blade, carried by Gaxim. It argues with Gaxim and Marron, pleads for caution, urges him to read the parchment and study the painting, and warns, "Strahd know we go."

### Rudolph van Richten
Not present. Ezmerelda's account of his son Erasmus and his mercy toward her family moved her deeply.

### Spectral and Phantom Warriors
Two guard the tower with bows; more attack in the wall corridor with force blades.
