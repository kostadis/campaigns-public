# Chapter 6: The fortunes of Madam Eva

Date: Day 7 in Barovia (from `docs/arcs/calendar.md`, ruled by the GM 2026-11-07)

## Summary
The chapter opens in the graveyard. Merrin consecrates the ground that the dead burgomaster is buried in, while Donavich wails about his dead son. Gaxim is unimpressed — "Bad priest! Must die!" — and wanders among the tombstones looking for treasure, anything to break the boredom of Merrin's ceremony.

When the ceremony ends and the heroes walk to the front of the church, they find out why Donavich has gone silent: Doru the vampire lives, and is standing over his father's dead body. Gaxim smiles, walks up to Doru with his hand extended and says, "vampire brave!" Doru, although in sunlight, does not sparkle. Merrin is confused: "why is this vampire that you said you killed still alive?" Ismark, horrified to see Doru and the dead Donavich, an old friend, shouts, "did you put a wooden stake in his body and chop off his head? Did you use holy water?" Merrin shrugs: "I would’ve used carrots. Carrots are what we use in my religion." Slerdax remarks that "the legends I know of vampires say they cannot walk in the middle of the day," and Ismark growls back that "The sun of Barovia is not deadly to the spawn of Strahd or Strahd himself. The vampires walk during the day and the night."

Doru is amused. Whoever approaches him is clearly a fool or suicidal, but being treated as a friend by a meal is worth a few moments to understand. He asks Gaxim, "what are you? Who are you?" — "I, Gaxim. We drink mead!" Slerdax stares: "do you not remember that you killed the vampire?" Gaxim, a simple person, sees only that the priest who tried to betray him is dead and the killer is standing over the body; in his mind Doru did him a good turn, and he should do Doru one. Ismark pushes himself in front of Ireena and screams at the party, "is he insane? He will kill us all! I thought you promise to protect Ireena from Strahd’s toys?"

Slerdax, realizing that Gaxim's values may end in disaster, tries to persuade him that Doru is in fact a wolf that has used magic to look like a vampire. Gaxim wonders aloud, "vampire is the wolf? Wolf killed priest?" The moral dilemma is significant: he owes the wolf a good turn, but if the wolf is not killed he can never go back to the pub, and then things will never be normal again. Doru tries to smooth-talk: "me, a Wolf? Gaxim, surely you believe that is nothing but nonsense." Merrin, who views the undead as the enemy, prepares to fight. Ismark ends the indecision: "if you do not kill this vampire or drive it away, I will consider you pawns of Strahd, and there will be nothing but war between us." Wukong, standing beside Ismark, scratches his head — "so, are we dropping the vampire? Are we going for drinks with the vampire? I am so confused." — and Ireena turns to him: "you promised you would protect me."

Slerdax, "as the brains of the operation", muses privately that "According to the material I studied in the Academy, most encounters with vampires end in fights to the death," that the conversation might make a monograph and a lecture at the Academy, and that, on the other hand, "the vampire could kill them all, in which case that would suck." Someone says, "Gaxim, let’s get the wolf skin," and Gaxim answers, "Gaxim kill Wolf, who killed priest." Slerdax asks whether anyone has the materials to kill a vampire. "Of course I do." Ismark stares at the magic-user with contempt; Merrin is intrigued — "you have carrots?" Ismark wonders whether he has allied wisely with these fools, then reasons that things may be different where they come from: "in Barovia, you need a wooden stake, and we have several."

The fight begins. An enraged Gaxim attacks recklessly; he would have done 19 points of damage, but the vampire resists his weapon and takes nine. Doru slashes at the barbarian, finds him resistant, and grapples; Gaxim breaks free. Merrin casts spiritual weapon, flanks the vampire, and inflicts only six points. Slerdax laughs and casts Tasha’s hideous laughter. Doru doesn't. Ismark, confused and a little horrified, protests: "we do not have to kill him. We simply have to escape. Why are you trying to kill him?" He then fires two crossbow bolts that both miss. Gaxim ignores him and slashes Doru for 4 points. Wukong draws his longbow with a grand flourish — "I shall take care of this creature." — and the arrow hits the top of the church "a good 7 to 10 feet from the vampire's head"; he then "grabs his finger and puts it in his mouth while screaming in pain."

Doru grapples Gaxim and prepares to suck his blood. Merrin does four points of damage. Slerdax casts a chromatic orb for 22 points of acid damage. Ismark fires futilely. The rogue misses again, though this time near the vampire rather than the steeple. Gaxim's ax finds a weak spot in the vampire clinging to him, for 15 points. Wukong takes a deep breath, rubs the birthmark on his neck in the shape of a bow, and hits, piercing the vampire's arm for 9 points. Doru decides "that eternity is more interesting than trying to suck some blood out of the half-orc" and tries to flee. Ismark whoops, thinking the party will let him go, but Merrin follows and lands a glancing blow of five points. Ismark begs the party to stop chasing Doru; Slerdax tries to make the vampire laugh again, but the vampire is wise to his tricks; Wukong's arrow finally strikes, but the vampire's regenerative powers let Doru escape.

Gaxim searches the priest's body and finds nothing. Merrin tells Gaxim to carry the body to the back and cast a ceremony spell while burying him. With the church of the Morninglord empty, Merrin rubs the silver lightning bolts she carries — the chapter writes "he carries" — and wonders whether there is time to make it a place of worship for Heironeous. Ireena, visibly worried after Doru's flight and Donavich's death, asks Ismark whether Vallaki is still the right choice: "There is always the Abbey of St. Markovia near Krezk." The party rests and debates. Slerdax decides they will go to Vallaki and, if it is not safe, consider other options. The road to Vallaki passes the Tser Pool encampment of the Vistani.

The heroes leave the town of Barovia on the Old Svalich road. The road gives way to a twisted, muddy path through the trees, rutted by wagons, until the mist and branches open onto a clearing beside a river that widens into a small lake several hundred feet across. Five colorful round tents, each ten feet in diameter, stand outside a ring of four barrel-topped wagons; a much larger tent near the shore is lit from within, and eight unbridled horses drink from the river. An accordion's mournful strains clash with singing around a bonfire.

Slerdax reminds everyone they are supposed to get their fortunes told. Merrin sighs, privately dismissing it — "another person fooled by the cult of Istus!" — since she knows the future is whatever a hero dedicated to the ideals of Heironeous makes it. Merrin refuses. Gaxim is excited. Slerdax proposes, "let us introduce ourselves and ask for our fortune." A Vistani by the fire calls, "come closer to the fire and join our singing," then squints at Gaxim: "you are the friendly giant, yes?" He answers himself: "yes, you are. Alenka told us to wait for you. We do not have mead, but we have wine." Gaxim grabs a bottle from the ground and chugs.

Asked whether they have questions about the wizard, Merrin says yes, and the storyteller begins: "A mighty wizard came to this land over a year ago." The wizard was a gnome, "A very charismatic gnome", who tried to rally the people of Barovia against "the devil Strahd" and led them to the castle en masse; "When the vampire appeared, the wizard’s peasant army fled in terror. A few stood their ground and were never seen again." When Merrin interrupts to ask what happened to the wizard, the Vistani becomes visibly annoyed, waves his calloused hand near her face, and reminds her he is telling the story on a beautiful night with "the greatest storytellers of all the realms, the Vistani." Gaxim chimes in: "more story!" The Vistani hands Merrin a bottle: "drink, or there is no story." Merrin swallows her pride, drinks a little, and is surprised to find it an excellent wine. Someone declares, "This is not peer pressure, this is hospitality!"

Another Vistani, Dimitar — pink satin shirt, orange velour pants, a ring through an eyebrow — urges, "Alyek, continue your story, we have been waiting for 15 minutes!" An argument of many minutes follows before Alyek relents — "Fine, fine, I will continue." — and blames Dimitar's interruptions for the state of his stories. Dimitar laughs. Alyek continues: "The wizard and the vampire cast spells at each other." Merrin goes pale; Alyek takes offence at being doubted, explains that "A vampire can live for thousands of years, and one as powerful as Strahd can learn," and complains, "You are worse than Dimitar." The battle, he says, flew "from the courtyards of Ravenloft to a precipice overlooking the falls"; thunder shook the mountainside and lightning struck the wizard, and still he stood — "I have never seen such power!" — "But when the devil Strahd fell upon him, the wizard’s magic couldn’t save him. I saw him thrown a thousand feet to his death." Alyek climbed down to the river to search the body for valuables. Gaxim: "Treasure?" — "No! the River Ivlis had already spirited him away. I do not remember the wizard’s name." Merrin asks, "KP?" — "Perhaps." Alyek sends them to Eva and will not speak further until Eva says they are friends of the Vistani. Gaxim insists, "I am a friend!" Alyek agrees, and slips: "And we will fight Strahd. No, I mean, you will fight Strahd," a glorious battle whose defeat he will tell to the next heroes. Gaxim: "Strahd? Strahd Strong! We fight, Strahd?" Alyek explains that the only ones who come to Barovia are those Strahd invites, that they always want to escape, and that "there is no escape without killing Strahd."

In the fortuneteller's tent, magic flames cast a reddish glow over a low table in black velvet and a crystal ball. The crone greets them: "At last, you have arrived!" She knows each of them: Slerdax, "the one from the Academy that teaches you to touch everything"; Gaxim, "who was a bouncer that sat at the wrong table"; Wukong, "the elf born under a blue moon, whose parents forced him to flee from the kingdom of Celene into the Wild Coast"; and "Merrin, is it? Marron is not with us now? I know of your challenging personalities." When Gaxim reaches for the cards, Madam Eva snatches them: "you would dare touch the cards of fate?" She lays three cards from one deck and two from another.

The first card tells of history. When it is revealed as the Paladin, she says, "The treasure lies in a dragon’s house, in hands once clean and now corrupted." The party stares at the card; she stares back, says she misread it because Marron confused her — "I think of vengeance instead of justice" — and tries again: "I see a sleeping prince, a servant of light, and the brother of darkness. The treasure lies with him." The second, a holy symbol of great hope, is the anarchist: "I see walls of bones, a chandelier of bones, and a table of bones — all that remains of enemies long forgotten." The third, a weapon of vengeance, "a sword of sunlight": "A man is not what he seems. He comes here in a carnival wagon. Therein lies what you seek." The fourth names an ally: "Your greatest ally will be a wizard. His mind is broken, but his spells are strong." The fifth will lead them to their enemy; in almost religious ecstasy she says, "I see a dark figure on a balcony, looking down upon this tortured land with a twisted smile."

She walks out with the party and tells the Vistani, "these folks are my friends, do not harm them." With a sly wink she warns the party that not all Vistani can be trusted — "we are smugglers, robbers, and thieves" — then, deadly serious, "and so many are allied with Strahd." Merrin asks where they smuggle from and to: "From beyond the mists." Asked whether the party can go with them, Eva says Strahd is bored, playing an endless game of bringing people into the mists and crushing their will until they join "his procession of the damned", and that "he does not permit you!" Of herself: "I only care for Barovia. I only care that Barovia be free. I am part of Strahd’s game." Of the wizard: "He, too, had his fortunes read." He believed in his magic, faced Strahd without the tools she told him of, and failed. "There is no possibility of escape without the defeat of Strahd."

Slerdax asks Ismark whether any of these ciphers make sense. The answer: "There is a Carnival in Vallaki, I have been told. I think it was Rictavio’s Carnival." As they leave, Merrin asks Alyek how to lessen the dangers on the road to Vallaki, and the Vistani laugh cruelly. Gaxim offers, "Wine?" Dimitar: "How much do you have?" Gaxim shows Morgantha's pies — "Hungry?" — and Alyek recognises them: "These are from Morgantha." Then "Kill children, we fight." / "You would be willing to kill Morgantha." Slerdax: "we tried." Her house, they learn, is along the way "in a windmill (Area O)". "Our windmill!" shouts Gaxim.

The party follows the river to the base of a canyon where a great waterfall spills into a pool under clouds of cold mist, with a great stone bridge spanning the gorge nearly one thousand feet overhead. "This is where the wizard disappeared." Crossing a bridge, they reach an intersection where the dirt road splits, the eastern branch showing patches of cobblestone. Gaxim shouts, "Strahd on balcony!" Ismark: "No!" Gaxim grumbles. Past the intersection are more open gates like the ones they first met on arrival. A little beyond them, Gaxim finds a leather-wrapped bundle stuffed in a hollow log — "treasure!" — but it holds only a drab set of Barovian clothes sized for a human adult. "No treasure!" Ismark draws his longsword and short sword: "We must keep moving. There is danger." Merrin asks what danger. "Werewolves. They leave their clothes when they are in human form. We must go quickly." A little further on they hear wolves, and wolves jump out from either side of the forest.
